**History - Dublin Docklands - Urban Toponymy and Judicial Landscapes**
*Folder: Review / Topologies and Places*

The Dublin Docklands area — Misery Hill, Townsend Street (formerly Lazar Hill) — is read as a "palimpsest" where successive regimes of social exclusion are still legible in the topography and place-names. In 17th-century Dublin, the tidal shoreline ridge became a "zone of exception" where the state deposited its illegible populations: lepers quarantined from the city walls, executed pirates gibbeted to deter incoming sailors, and later the sexually diseased channelled from the Monto red-light district into the Westmoreland Lock Hospital. The review traces how each era renamed and sanitised the locale — "Lazar Hill" to "Townsend Street," the Lock Hospital to "St. Margaret's" — while the social function of the site (management of abject bodies at the urban margin) persisted. The Silicon Docks transformation is framed as merely the latest iteration: the same zone of exceptionalism, now for global capital.

Key concepts: palimpsest, urban toponymy, gibbeting, Lock Hospital, Monto circuit, zone of exception, gentrification

**History - Hmong Diaspora - Vertical Exit and Migration History**
*Folder: Review / Historiques*

The Hmong are framed here as practitioners of "topographical sovereignty": a two-millennia strategy of occupying terrain too karst-fractured and vertical for imperial state-making, thereby existing as "illegible" (Raw/Shèng) subjects outside the tax grid rather than submitting to incorporation as "legible" (Cooked/Shù) taxpayers. The review traces this from the mythologised defeat of Chīyóu's Niǔlí confederacy through the Ming-Qing construction of the Hunan Miao Wall as an internal frontier barrier, through the Great Miao Rebellions (1735–1873) triggered by land cadastration, and into the "Vertical Exit" southward into the Southeast Asian massif after those rebellions failed. The Hmong diaspora — from Guizhou highlands to Minnesota suburbs — is characterised as a "portable architecture of belonging" that now operates through digital cultural revival, including the Pahawh Hmong script.

Key concepts: topographical sovereignty, raw/cooked imperial binary, Miáojiāng, Gǎitǔ Guīliú, Hunan Miao Wall, vertical exit, shatter zone

**Kink Studies - BDSM Biology - Neuroendocrinology of Power and Consent**
*Folder: Review / Kink Theory*

This systematic review synthesises biological evidence arguing that consensual BDSM functions as a complex interplay between the HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) axis and the endocannabinoid reward system. Submissives show cortisol spikes that are simultaneously rewarded by surges in anandamide and 2-AG — physiological stress reinterpreted as pleasurable, facilitating "sub-space." Dominants maintain high vagal tone (the "vagal brake hypothesis"), enabling calm executive regulation of power without cortisol spikes — the distinct "dom-space" construct. BDSM practitioners exhibit elevated pain thresholds and reduced aversive neural activity (fMRI shows diminished pain-processing regions, heightened ventral striatum). Crucially, coercive sadists show testosterone spikes and structural frontal-temporal abnormalities absent in consensual dominants — providing the biological basis for depathologisation of BDSM.

Key concepts: HPA axis, endocannabinoids, sub-space, dom-space, vagal brake hypothesis, depathologisation, pain catastrophising

**Kink Studies - Genital Enhancement - Medical to Aesthetic Consumer Turn**
*Folder: Review / Kink Theory*

Contemporary genital enhancement is argued to be not a coherent medical category but a "heterogeneous contact zone" between sexual medicine, cosmetic medicine, and consumer culture. The central analytical move is a material taxonomy: temporary-absorbable HA gels (partially reversible with hyaluronidase), biostimulatory fillers (PLA, PMMA — longer-lasting, harder to salvage), illicit liquid silicone (FDA-unapproved, prone to migration and catastrophic chronic foreign-body reaction), and surgical implants (prosthetic, partial reversibility only). Market discourse flattens all of these under the generic language of "enhancement" and "confidence," obscuring their radically different risk worlds and complication salvageability. The review concludes that informed consent must be organised around "the shape of failure" for each material class, not around short-term satisfaction metrics.

Key concepts: aesthetic medicalization, material taxonomy, reversibility gradient, salvageability, package aesthetics, consumer-medical reframing

**Oncologiques - Germ Cell Tumors - Systematic Review Relapse Rates After Radiotherapy**
*Folder: Reference / Oncologiques*

A brief clinical reference note distilling a 7-study systematic review on germ cell tumour (seminoma) relapse after radiotherapy. For stage IIA disease, radiotherapy relapse rates range from 0%–10.3% vs 0% for chemotherapy; for IIB, RT produced 28.6% vs 0% for CT (driven by a single retrospective study of mixed IIA–C disease). Five-year relapse-free survival across all stage II disease: ~92% RT vs ~96% CT; for IIA only, 88.7% RT vs 100% CT. The data suggest chemotherapy achieves lower relapse rates for stage II seminoma, particularly IIB, though the evidence base remains small and heterogeneous.

Key concepts: seminoma stage II, radiotherapy vs chemotherapy, relapse-free survival, IIA/IIB disease, clinical evidence quality

**Chinese Historiography - New Qing History - Theorist Map**
*Folder: Reference / Historiques*

A comprehensive scholar-map of the New Qing History (NQH) school and its interlocutors across four camps. The NQH Western revisionists (Crossley's "simultaneity" thesis — Qing emperors claimed co-equal Manchu, Confucian, and Buddhist identity as cosmological fact; Elliott's Banner-system ethnic boundaries; Rawski's multiethnic parallel governance; Millward's hybrid Xinjiang frameworks) argue that Qing success came from deliberate refusal to sinicise. Chinese respondents (Ge Zhaoguang's civilizational-continuity counter; Zhong Han's source-by-source archival rebuttal; Ping-ti Ho's original sinicization thesis) defend the historical coherence of "China" as a category. The map situates NQH against Yuan/Mongol parallels (Allsen, Rossabi, Robinson) and the classical Chinese historiographic tradition (Sima Qian through Guo Moruo), showing structural isomorphisms between each era's sinicization/anti-sinicization debate.

Key concepts: New Qing History, simultaneity, sinicization, Banner system, Crossley, Rawski, Ge Zhaoguang, zhengtong, Lifanyuan

**Comparative Linguistics - Personal Naming Systems - Neo-Classical Aesthetics Across the Sinosphere**
*Folder: Non-Technical / Comparative Linguistics*

A New Yorker-style feature investigating why Korean names "in Hanja" feel more poetic than their Chinese counterparts — and finding the answer in bureaucracy. Korea's Supreme Court Hanja inventory constitutes a "prestige sieve": the allowed-character set is skewed toward virtue/nature/intellect/beauty/affect semantic fields, so the aggregate distribution of Korean name-meanings drifts "literary" by constraint rather than by free choice. NameChart data (2014–2025) reveals a neo-classical wave dominated by open-vowel, sonorant-final syllables (도윤, 서아, 이현) whose phonotactic properties may themselves carry "poetic" affect. The feature extends to China (orthographic visibility accelerates stigma → faster fashion churn), Japan (the 湊/翠 written-reading split; きらきらネーム), and Vietnam (Sino-Vietnamese morphemes like Minh preserve prestige semantic payload across script change), arguing that a common aesthetic cycle operates at the level of morphemic meaning rather than graphic form.

Key concepts: registrable Hanja, neo-classical wave, visibility tax, phonotactic aesthetics, Sino-Vietnamese morphemes, NameChart, semantic prestige sieve

**Queer Theory - Sampat - Sinophone Social-Typing and Reclamation**
*Folder: Non-Technical / Queer Theory*

The essay argues that the Malaysian colloquial word *sampat* (三八) — melodramatic, over-the-top, excessively invested — names a form of adult queer friendship that modern anglophone vocabulary systematically fails to honour. Distinguishing sampat from camp (camp stylises feeling into aestheticised performance; sampat socialises it through fuss, overreaction, and relational excess), the essay shows how the word's untranslatability is itself politically significant: choosing it over "dramatic" is a refusal of full translation that keeps the bond anchored in a Malaysian register of affection and comic excess. The argument threads through: high-context friendship as a "small speech community"; mid-life loneliness in professionally successful lives (legible by function, rarely at native speed); diaspora as primarily a linguistic condition; and why some relationships survive by remaining underdefined rather than being pressed into labels that strip out their density.

Key concepts: sampat/三八, camp vs sampat, high-context friendship, diaspora as language, anti-romance, underdefined attachment, vernacular untranslatability

## Cross-Note Thread

The most insistent thread running across today's ten notes is the politics of **legibility and illegibility** — who controls the categories by which persons, bodies, places, and ethnicities are made knowable, and what persists in the gaps. The Hmong survive for two millennia precisely by being "raw," illegible to the imperial tax grid, retreating into karst topography that functioned as institutional camouflage. Dublin's Misery Hill deposits its own "illegible" populations — lepers, pirates, diseased sex workers — at the literal tidal margin of the city, where, as the Dublin Docklands note observes, "the laws of the central city were relaxed." The New Qing History debate is at its core a dispute about whose reading frame applies to Manchu identity: Crossley's "simultaneity" demands that we treat the Qing emperor as genuinely illegible to any single ethnic category, while Ge Zhaoguang insists on the legibility of Chinese civilizational continuity. The Naming Systems feature is a meditation on how bureaucratic legibility (Korea's Hanja inventory) shapes which meanings get poeticised, and on how the gap between Hangul surface and Hanja depth creates a productive semi-opacity — names that "sound fresh while their semantic payload remains safely classical." The Sampat essay pushes this furthest: the entire argument rests on the claim that *sampat*'s untranslatability is not a deficiency but a resource — the word protects the bond by refusing to resolve it into the cleaner, globally legible categories of "friend," "lover," or "dramatic." And the Genital Enhancement review shows how consumer-aesthetic reframing renders heterogeneous risk worlds "legible" under a smooth, unified vocabulary of "enhancement," with the dangerous consequence that the very distinctions that matter (temporary vs permanently foreign body; reversible vs permanently disfiguring) are the ones the language most actively obscures. Across empire, urban geography, sexology, medicine, and linguistics, these notes repeatedly return to the same question: what survives — and what is lost — when something is finally named.
