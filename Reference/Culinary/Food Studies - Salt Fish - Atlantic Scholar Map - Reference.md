## Dedicated table

| Scholar                              | Key works / field position                                                                                                   | What they add to a salt-fish history                                                                                                         | Best use                                                                                                                                                          | Caution                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bernard Bailyn**                   | *Atlantic History: Concept and Contours*; major institutionalizer of Atlantic history                                        | Makes the Atlantic a legitimate historical unit rather than a set of separate national stories                                               | Use to frame salt cod, bacalhau, Caribbean saltfish, and Newfoundland fisheries as one connected field                                                            | Often tilted toward Euro-American colonial history; less useful for enslaved foodways                          |
| **David Armitage**                   | “Three Concepts of Atlantic History”; distinguishes **circum-Atlantic**, **trans-Atlantic**, and **cis-Atlantic** approaches | Gives a clean spatial method: whole Atlantic system, specific flows, or local societies shaped by Atlantic connections                       | Very useful for structuring the essay: cod as circum-Atlantic system; Newfoundland-to-Caribbean as trans-Atlantic flow; Jamaica or Portugal as cis-Atlantic cases | Typological rather than archival; gives architecture more than content ([DASH][1])                             |
| **Alison Games**                     | “Atlantic History: Definitions, Challenges, and Opportunities,” *American Historical Review*, 2006                           | Provides methodological caution: the Atlantic is useful, but not a smooth single machine                                                     | Use to red-team any over-neat diagram of fish, sugar, slavery, and Catholic markets                                                                               | Her value is conceptual; not specifically a salt-fish or food historian ([OUP Academic][2])                    |
| **Jack P. Greene**                   | British Atlantic, colonial societies, imperial governance                                                                    | Helps compare different colonial systems and avoid making the British Caribbean stand for the whole Atlantic                                 | Use when distinguishing British, Spanish, Portuguese, French, Dutch Atlantic formations                                                                           | Less directly food-focused                                                                                     |
| **J. H. Elliott**                    | Comparative empires, especially British and Spanish Atlantic worlds                                                          | Helps avoid Anglocentric Atlantic history; useful for Iberian Catholic demand and Spanish imperial structures                                | Use for comparing Iberian Catholic markets with British provisioning systems                                                                                      | Not a cod or foodways specialist                                                                               |
| **Philip D. Morgan**                 | Slavery, British Atlantic, African diaspora, plantation societies                                                            | Adds social depth to enslaved life, labour regimes, and plantation societies                                                                 | Use to shift from “salt fish was traded” to “salt fish entered enslaved households and labour systems”                                                            | Salt fish itself may be peripheral in his work                                                                 |
| **David Eltis**                      | Atlantic slave trade demography; SlaveVoyages database                                                                       | Provides quantitative infrastructure for slave-trade scale, routes, ports, and demographic flows                                             | Use to locate salt-fish provisioning beside the measurable forced movement of people                                                                              | SlaveVoyages is about human trafficking routes, not food cargoes; do not overextend it ([slavevoyages.org][3]) |
| **David Richardson**                 | Atlantic slave trade, economic history, mortality, shipping                                                                  | Helps connect shipping, commerce, human cargo, and imperial logistics                                                                        | Use for maritime-capitalist context around provisioning and shipping                                                                                              | Again, not specifically a salt-fish scholar                                                                    |
| **Robin Blackburn**                  | *The Making of New World Slavery*; *The Overthrow of Colonial Slavery*                                                       | Provides large-scale political economy of slavery, capitalism, and empire                                                                    | Use to place salt fish inside the material systems that sustained plantation slavery                                                                              | Big structural frame; may need food-specific supplementation                                                   |
| **Eric Williams**                    | *Capitalism and Slavery*                                                                                                     | Classic argument linking slavery, capitalism, and British economic development                                                               | Use as a foundational but debated frame for plantation political economy                                                                                          | Must be treated historiographically, not as final consensus                                                    |
| **C. L. R. James**                   | *The Black Jacobins*                                                                                                         | Centers enslaved revolt, political agency, and revolutionary Atlantic history                                                                | Use to prevent provisioning history from making enslaved people appear passive                                                                                    | More about revolution and Haiti than food provisioning                                                         |
| **Hilary Beckles**                   | Caribbean slavery, Barbados, reparations, racial capitalism                                                                  | Strong for Caribbean plantation slavery, historical extraction, and reparative framing                                                       | Use when emphasizing that provisioning systems sat inside long-term structures of dispossession and unpaid labour                                                 | Reparations lens can be politically charged; still historically relevant ([Harvard Law School][4])             |
| **B. W. Higman**                     | *Slave Populations of the British Caribbean*; *Jamaican Food: History, Biology, Culture*                                     | Essential bridge from plantation demography to Caribbean foodways                                                                            | Use for Jamaican food history, provision grounds, crops, kitchens, and creole adaptation of imported foods                                                        | Jamaica is not the whole Caribbean; avoid overgeneralizing ([Google Books][5])                                 |
| **Richard S. Dunn**                  | *Sugar and Slaves*; British Caribbean plantation society                                                                     | Gives detailed plantation-world structure: sugar islands, labour, demography, planter power                                                  | Use to contextualize why imported durable protein mattered in plantation economies                                                                                | Food may remain a subtopic rather than the main object                                                         |
| **Richard B. Sheridan**              | Caribbean plantation economy, slave medicine, provisioning, sugar colonies                                                   | Useful for everyday plantation management, health, rations, and colonial economy                                                             | Use for saltfish as part of plantation provisioning and labour reproduction                                                                                       | Needs pairing with enslaved agency and foodways scholarship                                                    |
| **Trevor Burnard**                   | Jamaican slavery, Atlantic slavery, planter society                                                                          | Adds local social history and plantation detail, especially Jamaica                                                                          | Use for connecting macro Atlantic trade to colony-specific slave society                                                                                          | Not primarily about salt fish                                                                                  |
| **Vincent Brown**                    | *The Reaper’s Garden*; *Tacky’s Revolt*                                                                                      | Centers death, violence, war, and resistance in Caribbean slavery                                                                            | Use to prevent food history from becoming benign domestic history                                                                                                 | Saltfish becomes background unless analytically connected                                                      |
| **Stephanie Smallwood**              | *Saltwater Slavery*                                                                                                          | Reframes the Middle Passage and commodification of African captives                                                                          | Use to distinguish human commodification from commodity circulation; sharpens the ethics of “Atlantic trade” language                                             | More about people as commodities than food commodities                                                         |
| **Jennifer L. Morgan**               | Gender, reproduction, slavery, racial capitalism                                                                             | Adds gendered and reproductive labour to plantation analysis                                                                                 | Use to ask who cooked, rationed, stretched, and reproduced labour through food                                                                                    | Less directly about fish; important for household/labour reproduction                                          |
| **Marisa J. Fuentes**                | Archival violence, enslaved women, urban Caribbean slavery                                                                   | Adds methodological caution about silences in archives                                                                                       | Use when discussing enslaved consumers whose own food voices are poorly recorded                                                                                  | More urban/gender/archive-focused than commodity-focused                                                       |
| **Ira Berlin**                       | Atlantic creoles, slavery in mainland North America                                                                          | Useful for cultural formation, African diaspora, and regional diversity                                                                      | Use to avoid treating enslaved populations as culturally homogeneous                                                                                              | Less Caribbean-specific than some others                                                                       |
| **John Thornton**                    | Africa and Africans in Atlantic formation                                                                                    | Restores African agency, political systems, and cultural continuities                                                                        | Use to avoid a Europe-to-Americas-only Atlantic story                                                                                                             | Salt fish is not his central topic                                                                             |
| **Paul Gilroy**                      | *The Black Atlantic*                                                                                                         | Cultural and theoretical frame for diaspora, modernity, memory, movement                                                                     | Use for the afterlife of saltfish as diaspora food, memory, and identity                                                                                          | More cultural theory than archival food history                                                                |
| **Peter Linebaugh & Marcus Rediker** | *The Many-Headed Hydra*; radical Atlantic labour history                                                                     | Centers sailors, dockworkers, enslaved people, pirates, and maritime labouring classes                                                       | Use for salt fish as sailor food, dockside commodity, and working-class Atlantic ration                                                                           | Their radical synthesis is influential but debated                                                             |
| **Marcus Rediker**                   | Slave ships, sailors, maritime labour, Atlantic violence                                                                     | Strong for ships as labour regimes, not just transport vessels                                                                               | Use when discussing salt fish as maritime provision and shipboard food                                                                                            | Not specifically salt cod history                                                                              |
| **Rebecca Earle**                    | Food, colonial bodies, diet, empire; especially Spanish America                                                              | Useful for how colonial food categories mapped onto bodies, race, and civilization                                                           | Use to compare Catholic/Iberian food ideologies with Atlantic commodity flows                                                                                     | More Spanish American diet/body politics than cod-specific                                                     |
| **Judith Carney**                    | *Black Rice*; African crops, ecological knowledge, diaspora agriculture                                                      | Restores African agricultural knowledge and provision-ground agency                                                                          | Use to balance imported saltfish with locally grown foods, African botanical knowledge, and subsistence strategies                                                | Focuses more on rice/agriculture than fish                                                                     |
| **Elizabeth DeLoughrey**             | Caribbean ecologies, provision grounds, postcolonial environmental humanities                                                | Adds provision grounds, ecology, and creolization as active spaces                                                                           | Use to prevent saltfish from crowding out yam, cassava, plantain, breadfruit, and local food systems                                                              | Literary/ecocritical frame; not a conventional economic historian                                              |
| **Sidney Mintz**                     | *Sweetness and Power*; Caribbean anthropology                                                                                | Though not usually classified only as “Atlantic-world,” he is indispensable for linking plantation production, consumption, class, and habit | Use as the method bridge: saltfish as ration/commodity parallel to sugar as export/consumption commodity                                                          | His direct object is sugar, so saltfish application is extrapolative ([Google Books][6])                       |
| **Harold Innis**                     | *The Cod Fisheries*; staples theory                                                                                          | Core for cod as an international economy and Atlantic staple commodity                                                                       | Use to map fisheries, trade routes, markets, empire, and commodity dependence                                                                                     | Weak on enslaved consumption and Caribbean culinary transformation ([JSTOR][7])                                |
| **Mark Kurlansky**                   | *Cod*; *Salt*                                                                                                                | Popular commodity biography; narrative bridge between fish, salt, religion, ecology, and collapse                                            | Use for accessible narrative and ecological afterlife                                                                                                             | Needs correction by archival and slavery scholarship; risks “cod changed the world” overstatement              |

## Condensed scholar roles

| Analytical need                            | Best scholars                                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Define the Atlantic as a field             | Bailyn, Armitage, Games                              |
| Avoid national-cuisine simplification      | Armitage, Elliott, Greene, Games                     |
| Explain cod as commodity system            | Innis, Kurlansky, maritime historians                |
| Explain slavery and plantation capitalism  | Williams, Blackburn, Beckles, Dunn, Morgan, Sheridan |
| Quantify slave-trade context               | Eltis, Richardson, SlaveVoyages project              |
| Recover enslaved agency and foodways       | Higman, Carney, DeLoughrey, Thornton, Mintz          |
| Add gender and archival caution            | Jennifer Morgan, Fuentes, Smallwood                  |
| Add maritime labour                        | Rediker, Linebaugh                                   |
| Add diaspora memory and cultural afterlife | Gilroy, Caribbean food studies, Higman               |
