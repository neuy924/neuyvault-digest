## SURTIME Trial: Comprehensive Compilation of Commentaries, Reviews, and Criticisms

The SURTIME trial (Immediate Surgery or Surgery After Sunitinib Malate in Treating Patients With Metastatic Kidney Cancer) has generated substantial debate in the urologic oncology community. This randomized phase III trial compared immediate cytoreductive nephrectomy (CN) followed by sunitinib versus deferred CN after 3 cycles of sunitinib in patients with synchronous metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC). The trial closed early with only 99 of a planned 458 patients, leading to numerous critiques and interpretations.med-fom-urlgsci.sites.olt.ubc +1

## Trial Design and Methodological Criticisms

**Severe Accrual Failure and Statistical Power**

The most fundamental criticism centers on the trial's dramatic accrual failure. Over 5.7 years, SURTIME recruited only 99 patients—a mere 22% of the planned 458-patient sample size. This necessitated a mid-trial modification of the primary endpoint from median progression-free survival (PFS) to the 28-week progression-free rate (PFR), which required only 98 patients to evaluate. Critics note that overall survival (OS) was relegated to a secondary endpoint, substantially reducing the statistical robustness of the survival findings that would later become the trial's most cited result.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

Multiple factors contributed to poor accrual. Regulatory decisions prevented two European countries from participating, reducing available sites. The trial's complexity—requiring precise coordination of surgery and systemic treatment timing—created logistical barriers. Most significantly, stringent inclusion criteria using unvalidated surgical risk factors rather than WHO performance status created an exceptionally narrow eligible population. This physician-induced selection bias likely excluded patients whom clinicians believed would clearly benefit from CN, as investigators lacked clinical equipoise for such cases.journals.sagepub +3

**Patient Selection Bias**

SURTIME enrolled a highly selected population: 89% had MSKCC intermediate-risk disease, compared to only 47% in the CARMENA trial. The trial specifically selected the most favorable surgical candidates through unvalidated surgical risk criteria. Critically, patients with oligometastatic disease, favorable-risk disease, or those candidates for surveillance strategies were excluded because enrollment required clinical need for immediate sunitinib therapy. This limits generalizability to the broader mRCC population.dailynews.ascopubs +4

## Key Results and Their Interpretation

**Intention-to-Treat Analysis**

The ITT analysis showed no difference in the primary endpoint (28-week PFR: 42% immediate CN vs. 43% deferred CN, p=0.61). However, the secondary endpoint of OS favored deferred CN significantly: median OS was 32.4 months (95% CI: 14.5-65.3) in the deferred arm versus 15.0 months (95% CI: 9.3-29.5) in the immediate CN arm (HR 0.57, 95% CI: 0.34-0.95, p=0.03).pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

Importantly, 98% of patients in the deferred arm received sunitinib compared to only 80% in the immediate CN arm. In the immediate arm, 20% never received systemic therapy due to rapid disease progression post-surgery, poor performance status, surgical morbidity, or cardiac dysfunction (decreased ejection fraction). This differential exposure to systemic therapy became a focal point of post-hoc analysis.journals.sagepub +2

**Per-Protocol Analysis Controversy**

When analyzed per-protocol (excluding patients who didn't follow the assigned treatment), the OS benefit of deferred CN was no longer statistically significant (HR 0.71, 95% CI: 0.40-1.24, p=0.23). This raises the critical question: Does deferred CN itself provide survival benefit, or does the approach simply select patients more likely to benefit through their response to initial systemic therapy? ## The Sunitinib Exposure Hypothesis: A Key Mechanistic Insight

A crucial post-hoc analysis by Abu-Ghanem and colleagues provided mechanistic insight into the OS findings. They demonstrated that **immediate CN impairs the administration, onset, and duration of sunitinib**. Starting with systemic therapy not only resulted in longer drug exposure but also enabled earlier and more profound disease control. Critically, 29% of patients (14/49) in the deferred arm experienced systemic progression before planned CN and, per protocol, did not undergo surgery.journals.sagepub +5

Commentators interpret this as successful patient selection: the deferred approach identified patients with inherent resistance to VEGFR-targeted therapy who were unlikely to benefit from CN. A landmark analysis demonstrated that patients who progressed in the deferred arm before surgery or within 16 weeks after immediate CN had similarly poor survival prognosis, supporting the concept that early progression identifies patients for whom surgery is futile.urology +2

## Surgical Safety: Addressing Prior Concerns

SURTIME provided reassuring safety data that contradicted previous retrospective concerns about performing surgery after VEGFR-TKI therapy. Perioperative grade 3-5 complication rates were similar between arms (22-26%), consistent with non-randomized presurgical sunitinib studies. Critically, wound healing complications were minimal—only two patients experienced superficial complications requiring no intervention (grade 1).
