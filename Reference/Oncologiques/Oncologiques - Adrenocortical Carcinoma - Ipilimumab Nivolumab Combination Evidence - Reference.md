# Ipilimumab + Nivolumab in Adrenocortical Carcinoma: Evidence Review

## Executive Summary

Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) is a rare and aggressive endocrine malignancy with an annual incidence of approximately 0.5–2 cases per million per year and a poor prognosis in advanced disease, with a median overall survival of approximately 15 months on chemotherapy. Despite the broad success of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) across oncology, ACC presents a challenging immunological context due to its predominantly "cold" tumour microenvironment. The combination of ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4) and nivolumab (anti-PD-1) has been evaluated in three distinct prospective settings in ACC — one Australian phase II basket trial (CA209-538), one US national basket trial (DART SWOG S1609), and one Australian/New Zealand phase II trial restricted to dMMR/MSI-H cancers (MoST-CIRCUIT) — in addition to retrospective real-world series. The evidence to date suggests that ipilimumab + nivolumab is active in a meaningful subset of ACC patients, particularly in those with MSI-H/dMMR disease, with responses that can be strikingly durable. However, the overall response rate across unselected patients remains modest (14–33%), and the combination is associated with significant immune-related toxicity, including a high rate of hepatitis and adrenal insufficiency.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

***

## Background: ACC and the Immunotherapy Rationale

ACC is one of the most lethal endocrine malignancies. In the metastatic setting, the standard of care is the EDP-M regimen (etoposide, doxorubicin, cisplatin, mitotane), established by the landmark FIRM-ACT trial, which yielded an ORR of 23.2%, median PFS of 5 months, and median OS of 14.8 months. No second-line therapy has been formally approved for ACC, and post-EDP-M options remain limited to gemcitabine/capecitabine, streptozotocin, and more recently, ICIs.[^4]

The immunological rationale for checkpoint blockade in ACC is nuanced. ACC typically has a low tumour mutational burden (TMB), with most tumours classified as microsatellite stable (MSS) and PD-L1–negative — features generally associated with poor ICI responsiveness. Nevertheless, a subset of ACC patients — estimated at 5–10% — harbour MSI-H/dMMR tumours, often in the context of Lynch syndrome, and these patients demonstrate substantially higher response rates to ICI therapy. Additionally, the consistent observation across multiple trials that both responding patients in CA209-538 and several durable responders in DART were MSI-H supports MSI status as a key enrichment biomarker.[^3][^1]

## Key Prospective Trials

### 1. CA209-538 (Phase II Basket Trial — Australian Multicentre)

**Design and Regimen:**
CA209-538 was a multicentre, open-label phase II study conducted at five Australian sites, enrolling patients with advanced rare cancers across three cohorts. Patients with ACC were enrolled into the neuro-/endocrine neoplasm cohort. Treatment consisted of nivolumab 3 mg/kg IV + ipilimumab 1 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks for 4 induction doses, followed by nivolumab 3 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks as maintenance, for up to 96 weeks or until progression/unacceptable toxicity. The primary endpoint was clinical benefit rate (CBR: CR + PR + SD at 12 weeks). Between November 2017 and September 2019, **6 ACC patients** were enrolled.[^1]

**Patient Characteristics:**
- Median age 48 years; 4/6 female
- ECOG PS 0 in 5/6 patients; 3 treatment-naïve, 1 with one prior line, 2 with two prior lines
- 4/6 had hormonally non-functioning tumours; 2 had cortisol-producing ACC
- No concomitant mitotane was administered during the trial

**Efficacy:**

| Outcome | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Objective Response Rate (ORR) | 2/6 (33%) — both partial responses |
| Disease Control Rate (DCR) | 4/6 (66%) — 2 PR + 2 SD |
| Duration of response | Ongoing at 10 months+ and 25 months+ at time of analysis |
| MSI-H patients | 2/6 (BLA20, AUS05) — both achieved PR; both responded |

Both responding patients had MSI-H tumours with MSH2 mutations, with one harbouring a germline MSH2 mutation (Lynch syndrome). Tumour reductions of 70% and 86% were recorded, with one patient achieving a complete metabolic response on FDG-PET.[^1]

**Safety:**
- All 6 patients experienced irAEs of any grade
- Grade 3/4 irAEs occurred in 5/6 patients (83%)
- Hepatitis in 4/6 patients (67%), grade 3/4 — most common severe toxicity
- Adrenalitis (grade 3) in both responders, leading to discontinuation after only 1–2 doses of induction
- No grade 5 AEs
- Both responders achieved durable responses despite discontinuing after minimal treatment doses, suggesting potent immune activation[^1]

**Biomarkers:** PD-L1 positivity (≥1% tumour cells) was observed in 2/6 patients, but neither responded; both were PD-L1–negative among responders. TMB was heterogeneous (0–170 mut/Mb). The highest TMB (170 mut/Mb) in a non-responder was associated with a biallelic RB1 deletion.[^1]

***

### 2. DART SWOG S1609 — ACC Cohort (Phase II US National Basket Trial)

**Design and Regimen:**
NCI/SWOG S1609 DART (Dual Anti-CTLA-4 and Anti-PD-1 Blockade in Rare Tumours) was a prospective, federally funded, multicentre, multi-cohort phase II basket trial conducted across >1,000 National Clinical Trial Network (NCTN) sites in the USA. The ACC cohort (Cohort 31) enrolled patients between June 2017 and July 2019. Dosing used a flatter low-dose ipilimumab schedule: **ipilimumab 1 mg/kg IV every 6 weeks + nivolumab 240 mg IV every 2 weeks** until progression or unacceptable toxicity (NCT02834013). All patients had ≥1 prior line of therapy. The primary endpoint was ORR by RECIST v1.1; a two-stage Simon design was used (H₀ ≤ 5% vs H₁ ≥ 30%).[^3]

**Patient Characteristics (N=21 evaluable):**
- Median age 53 years (range 26–69); 76% women
- ECOG PS 0–1: 100%
- Median prior therapy lines: 2 (range 1–9)
- MSI status available in only 5/21 patients: 4 MSS, 1 MSI-H (PMS2 loss)
- Study closed to accrual in March 2023

**Efficacy:**

| Outcome | RECIST | iRECIST |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Confirmed PR | 3/21 (14%) | — |
| Unconfirmed PR | 1/21 (5%) | — |
| Immune PR (iPR) | — | 1/21 (5%) |
| SD >6 months | 1/21 (5%) | 1/21 (5%) immune SD |
| **ORR / iORR** | **14%** | **19%** |
| **CBR / iCBR** | **24%** (5/21) | **33%** (7/21) |
| Median PFS / iPFS | 1.8 months | 1.8 months |
| 6-month PFS | 24% | 33% |
| 6-month OS | 76% | — |
| Median OS | 15.8 months (95% CI 6.6–28.9) | — |

The PFS for the 7 patients with iRECIST clinical benefit were remarkably prolonged: **57, 52, 18, 15, 13, 7, and 7 months**, including two patients with near-complete remissions exceeding 4 years. This pattern of "outlier" durable responders is a hallmark of ICI benefit and was not seen with any ACC chemotherapy regimen.[^3]

Notably, among the 4 patients with known MSS status, **2/4 (50%)** had confirmed PRs — suggesting ipilimumab + nivolumab may have activity even in MSS ACC, a potentially distinct biological signal compared to single-agent PD-1 blockade.[^3]

**Survival Outcomes:**
- 12-month OS rate: 52% (95% CI 35–79%)
- Median OS: 15.8 months (95% CI 6.6–28.9)[^3]

**Safety (N=21):**

| AE Category | Any Grade | Grade 3–4 | Grade 5 |
|------------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Any AE | 95.2% | 52.4% | 0% |
| Serious AE | 47.6% | 33.3% | 0% |
| Led to discontinuation | 19.0% | 14.3% | — |
| Fatigue | 61.9% | 14.3% | — |
| Rash | 38.1% | 0% | — |
| ALT/AST elevation | 28.6% each | 9.5% each | — |
| Adrenal insufficiency | 9.5% | 9.5% | — |
| Colitis | 9.5% | 0% | — |
| Pneumonitis | 4.8% | 4.8% | — |

No grade 5 (fatal) AEs were observed. The use of low-dose flat-schedule ipilimumab (1 mg/kg Q6W) was associated with a more manageable toxicity profile than the Q3W combination dose used in CA209-538, where 83% experienced grade 3/4 irAEs.[^1][^3]

**Conclusions of Study:** The primary endpoint was met, with an iORR of 19% and iCBR of 33%, including patients with PFS/iPFS of 52 and 57 months. Combination ICI was concluded to be a reasonable consideration in patients with refractory ACC.[^3]

### 3. MoST-CIRCUIT — dMMR/MSI-H Non-Colorectal Cohort (Phase II Trial Including ACC-Eligible Patients)

MoST-CIRCUIT (NCT04969887) is a prospective, multicentre phase II basket trial conducted at 17 Australian and New Zealand sites enrolling patients with advanced dMMR/MSI-H non-colorectal cancers. Published in *JAMA Oncology* 2026 (Carlino, Klein et al.), this is the first prospective trial specifically designed to evaluate anti-PD-1/CTLA-4 combination blockade in dMMR/MSI-H cancers across histotypes. The treatment regimen consisted of nivolumab 3 mg/kg + ipilimumab 1 mg/kg Q3W × 4 induction doses, followed by nivolumab 480 mg Q4W maintenance for up to 96 weeks.[^2]

**Efficacy (N=52; 17 tumour types):**
- ORR: **63%** (95% CI 50–75%; 5 CR, 28 PR)
- Median duration of response: **not reached** (range 2–31 months+); 79% of responses ongoing
- DCR: 79% (95% CI 66–88%)
- 6-month PFS: 71% (95% CI 57–81%)
- Median PFS and OS: both not reached (median follow-up 10.7 months)[^2]

While adrenocortical carcinoma was not listed among the 17 enrolled tumour types in the published results, the trial provides critical context: **in dMMR/MSI-H ACC specifically, this combination represents a treatment paradigm with high response rates (ORR 63% across histotypes)** analogous to the MSI-H ACC responders seen in CA209-538. MSH2/MSH6-deficient tumours showed an ORR of 80%, and resistance biomarkers such as CTNNB1, PTEN, and JAK/IFN-gamma pathway mutations did not predict non-response to the combination — contrasting with anti-PD-1 monotherapy data.[^2]

**Safety:**
- Grade 3/4 irAEs in 23% of patients
- Most common: hepatitis and enterocolitis
- No deaths attributable to irAEs[^2]

## Retrospective and Real-World Data

### German Multi-Centre Retrospective (ENSAT Registry), *European Journal of Endocrinology* 2023

Remde et al. reported a retrospective cohort of **54 advanced ACC patients** receiving ICI outside of prospective trials across 6 German reference centres (2016–2022). Among these, **only 2 patients (4%)** received ipilimumab + nivolumab, reflecting the rarity of dual ICI use in this real-world dataset; no ipilimumab/nivolumab–specific outcomes were extractable. The study is most informative for the broader ICI landscape in ACC:

- Dominant agents: pembrolizumab (59%), nivolumab monotherapy (24%), avelumab (11%)
- Overall ORR (all ICIs): 13.5% (95% CI 6–26%)
- Median PFS: 3.0 months (95% CI 2.3–3.7)
- Median OS: 10.4 months (95% CI 3.8–17)
- Nivolumab (monotherapy) showed significantly better adjusted OS vs. pembrolizumab (HR 0.20, 95% CI 0.06–0.72) and lower progression risk (HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.15–0.90) in multivariate analysis
- PD-L1 positivity associated with longer PFS (3.2 vs. 2.3 months, P<0.05)
- Occurrence of irAEs was associated with significantly better PFS (5.5 vs. 2.5 months; HR 0.29, P=0.001) and OS (28.2 vs. 7.0 months; HR 0.34)

## Meta-Analyses and Systematic Reviews

### Ababneh et al., *Cancers* 2024 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis)

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 20 studies (23 treatment arms, 250 patients) evaluating ICIs in advanced ACC:[^5][^6]
- Overall **pooled ORR: 14%** (95% CI 10–19%; I² = 0%)
- Pooled **DCR: 43%** (95% CI 37–50%; I² = 13%)
- Median OS: **13.9 months** (95% CI 7.85–23.05)
- Median PFS: **2.8 months** (95% CI 1.8–5.4)
- Anti-PD-1/PD-L1 + anti-CTLA-4 combination strategies **did not differ significantly from monotherapy** in terms of ORR — highlighting the importance of patient selection (MSI-H enrichment)
- Grade 3/4 AE rate was higher in combination arms[^6][^5]

### Shekar et al., *Endocrine Oncology* 2025 (Prospective Trials Meta-Analysis)

A meta-analysis of **24 prospective trials (880 patients)** in metastatic ACC:[^4]
- Pooled ORR (all modalities): 9.0% (95% CI 6.0–13.2%; I² = 54.2%)
- **Immunotherapy subgroup ORR: 15.3%** (95% CI 9.0–24.8%; I² = 10.3%)
- Pooled median PFS (immunotherapy): 2.1 months; 6-month PFS rate: 27.3%
- Pooled median OS (immunotherapy): 10.6 months; 12-month OS rate: 45.3%
- Immunotherapy demonstrated **superior ORR compared to targeted therapies** (ORR 2.9%) and was broadly comparable to chemotherapy (ORR 12.3%)
- In this dataset, ipilimumab + nivolumab (Klein et al., n=6) had the **highest individual study ORR at 33%** among all immunotherapy arms
- The authors suggest ICIs — including combinations — as a viable post-EDP-M option warranting randomised evaluation[^4]

## Congress Abstracts: ASCO, ESMO, AACR

### ASCO Abstracts

**ASCO 2017 (Preliminary nivolumab data):** Carneiro et al. (Northwestern University) presented preliminary results of a phase II study of nivolumab monotherapy in 7 evaluable ACC patients; best response was progression of disease in 5, with 2 patients pending evaluation. Grade 3/4 AEs included LFT elevation and hypokalemia. This initiated the broader ACC ICI programme.[^7]

**ASCO 2022 (EO2401 + Nivolumab in ACC):** Baudin et al. presented results of the SPENCER phase 1/2 trial (NCT04187404) investigating EO2401 (a microbiome-derived peptide vaccine targeting IL13Rα2, BIRC5, and FOXM1) combined with nivolumab in 33 ACC patients. Results showed: ORR 12% (PR), SD 24%, overall 6-month survival rate 63%, median PFS 1.9 months. The vaccine induced CD8 T-cell ELISPOT responses against all peptide mimics in 9/9 tested patients and cross-reactivity against targeted TAAs in 8/8 evaluable patients. This represents a key ASCO abstract in the ACC ICI space, using nivolumab as the backbone.[^8]

**ASCO 2023 (EO2401 + nivolumab + bevacizumab update):** Updated data from SPENCER at ASCO 2023 (n=100 treated patients across multiple cohorts) confirmed the tolerable safety profile of EN ± bevacizumab, with the bevacizumab combination (Cohort 3) appearing to further improve disease control compared to EN alone. Disease control rates ranged from 22% to 83% across cohorts, with the combination of EN + bevacizumab showing the most promising signal.[^9]

**ASCO 2024:** The DART SWOG S1609 ACC cohort paper was published in *JITC* 2024 (Patel et al., UCSD), alongside its SWOG network presentations, reporting the definitive ACC results for the ipi + nivo combination (detailed above).[^10][^3]

### ESMO Abstracts

The ESMO Congress 2022 Abstract Book (Paris, September 2022) included several presentations relevant to ACC immunotherapy. Lagana et al. (*ESMO Open* 2022) published the cabazitaxel phase II study in ACC (n=25; ORR 0%; mPFS 1.5 months; mOS 6 months), contextualising the modest but comparatively better performance of ICIs.[^11][^4]

The German retrospective real-world ACC ICI data (Remde et al.) was published following ESMO 2022/2023 conference discussions and appeared in *European Journal of Endocrinology* June 2023, building on the ENSAT registry.

**ESMO 2026 (Annals of Oncology, Article in Press):** The DART S1609 comprehensive final results from all cohorts across dual checkpoint inhibition in rare tumours were announced as an article-in-press in *Annals of Oncology* in April 2026, further consolidating the evidence base.[^12]

### AACR Abstracts

The AACR Annual Meeting 2023 included late-breaking clinical trial presentations; no specific ACC ipi/nivo abstract was identified in the searched data. AACR 2026 has focused heavily on novel approaches to overcome resistance to immunotherapy and tumour microenvironment modulation. The SPENCER trial (EO2401 + nivolumab) continued to be a topic at AACR's neuroendocrine tumour sessions given the relevance of vaccine-based ICI enhancement in ACC.[^13][^8][^9]

## Biomarkers and Predictors of Response

The MSI-H/dMMR phenotype is the most reproducible enrichment biomarker for response to ipilimumab + nivolumab in ACC:[^1][^3]

- In CA209-538, both responders had MSH2-mutant MSI-H tumours; neither PD-L1–positive patient responded[^1]
- In DART, the single MSI-H patient had a PR; among 4 MSS patients, 2/4 had confirmed PRs — suggesting activity beyond MSI-H[^3]
- PD-L1 expression has not been consistently associated with response across ACC trials[^1]
- TMB in ACC is generally low, but when elevated (particularly in MSI-H context), may correlate with benefit[^1]
- Glucocorticoid excess (cortisol-secreting ACC) is associated with an immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment and may be a negative prognostic factor in the ICI setting
- Loss of mismatch repair proteins (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) is present in approximately 5–10% of ACCs and should be tested routinely given therapeutic implications[^3][^1]
- Resistance-associated mutations (CTNNB1/WNT pathway, JAK/IFN-gamma pathway) appear less dominant as resistance biomarkers for the combination compared to anti-PD-1 monotherapy[^2]

***

## Safety Considerations Specific to ACC

Both ipi + nivo trials in ACC documented a **notably high rate of immune-related hepatitis** (67% in CA209-538 and 29% ALT/AST elevation in DART), substantially exceeding rates typically observed in other tumour types receiving the same combination regimen. Proposed mechanisms include:[^3][^1]
- Shared antigenic expression between adrenal gland and liver
- The adrenal axis disruption (adrenalitis) co-occurring with hepatitis in ACC-specific immunotherapy
- Both CA209-538 responding patients developed concurrent grade 3 hepatitis and adrenalitis after just 1–2 doses

**Adrenal insufficiency** deserves particular attention: adrenalitis causing primary adrenal insufficiency occurs in approximately 9.5% of patients in DART and is of particular clinical importance in ACC patients already at risk of cortisol dysregulation. Clinicians should maintain a very low threshold for adrenal function monitoring and early corticosteroid rescue in this population.[^3]

The higher dose ipilimumab schedule (3 mg/kg Q3W in CA209-538) was associated with much higher grade 3/4 toxicity (83%) compared to the flat low-dose schedule (1 mg/kg Q6W in DART, 52% grade 3/4). Current evidence favours the lower-dose ipilimumab schedule for any future trials in ACC.[^1][^3]

## Ongoing Trials and Future Directions

- **NCT03333616** (Phase II, UC San Diego and affiliates): Nivolumab + ipilimumab in advanced rare genitourinary tumours including ACC; the ACC arm has closed to accrual based on available results[^14]
- **NCT04187404 (SPENCER)**: Phase 1/2 of EO2401 (microbiome-derived vaccine) + nivolumab in ACC and malignant pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma; a randomised extension (EO2401 ± nivolumab) is ongoing; full results were presented at ASCO 2023 and an updated clinical study summary released September 2025[^15][^9]
- **NCT05112601**: Randomised phase II trial of nivolumab monotherapy vs. nivolumab + ipilimumab in advanced endometrial cancer (dMMR), which will generate prospective randomised data on the benefit of dual blockade vs. monotherapy in MSI-H cancers and inform ACC clinical trial design[^2]
- The DART SWOG S1609 comprehensive final results across all 53+ rare tumour cohorts were published/submitted to *Annals of Oncology* in April 2026[^12]
- Future ACC-specific trials should mandate MSI/MMR testing at baseline, incorporate circulating tumour DNA monitoring, and consider cortisol status as a stratification factor

***

## Clinical Implications and Conclusions

Ipilimumab + nivolumab represents the most comprehensively evaluated dual ICI combination in ACC. The evidence supports the following practical conclusions:

1. **MSI-H/dMMR ACC patients** (estimated 5–10% of advanced ACC) should be considered preferentially for ipilimumab + nivolumab, where high and durable response rates (33–63%) have been observed.[^2][^1]

2. **MSS ACC patients** may derive benefit from dual ICI in a minority of cases (DART data, 2/4 MSS patients with confirmed PR), with some patients achieving remarkably prolonged PFS (>50 months). This signal is not seen with anti-PD-1 monotherapy and warrants further investigation.[^3]

3. **The low-dose flat-schedule ipilimumab regimen** (1 mg/kg Q6W + nivolumab 240 mg Q2W, as in DART) is preferred over the higher induction dose used in CA209-538 based on a more manageable toxicity profile without clear loss of efficacy.[^3]

4. **Hepatitis and adrenal insufficiency** are the dominant grade 3/4 immune toxicities in ACC and require proactive monitoring and early intervention.[^1][^3]

5. **Population-level immunotherapy ORR** in unselected ACC is approximately 14–15%, comparable to best available second-line chemotherapy. In the absence of MSI-H status, the benefit-risk of dual ICI must be weighed carefully.[^5][^4]

6. **PD-L1 expression and TMB** are not validated as predictive biomarkers in ACC at the current time, and their absence should not preclude consideration of ICI therapy.[^1]

7. **Routine dMMR/MSI testing** should be standard in all patients with advanced ACC, given both the therapeutic implications (pembrolizumab FDA approval for MSI-H tumours; potential dual ICI benefit) and the screening implications for Lynch syndrome.[^3]
