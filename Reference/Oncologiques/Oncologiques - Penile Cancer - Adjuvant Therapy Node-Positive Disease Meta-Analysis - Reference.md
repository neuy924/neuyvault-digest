# Meta-Analysis of Adjuvant Therapy in Node-Positive Penile Cancer

**Analysis Date:** February 6, 2026

---

## Executive Summary

This meta-analysis evaluated adjuvant therapy outcomes in node-positive penile cancer (pN2-3 disease). All three treatment modalities—radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and chemoradiotherapy—demonstrated statistically significant survival benefits compared to observation. Due to the rarity of penile cancer, all included studies were retrospective. The ongoing InPACT trial (EA8134) will provide the first prospective randomised data.

## Detailed Results by Treatment Modality

### 1. Adjuvant Radiotherapy

**Pooled Analysis:**
- **Hazard Ratio:** 0.486 (95% CI: 0.357–0.662)
- **P-value:** <0.0001
- **Heterogeneity:** I² = 7.9% (low heterogeneity)
- **Number of Studies:** 5 study endpoints from 4 publications
- **Total Patients:** 394

**Interpretation:** Adjuvant radiotherapy reduces the risk of death or recurrence by approximately 51% in node-positive penile cancer. The low heterogeneity indicates consistent treatment effects across studies.

**Included Studies:**
- Tang 2017 (DSS): HR 0.526 (0.298–0.917), n=92
- Tang 2017 (OS): HR 0.588 (0.342–0.990), n=92
- Johnstone 2019 ENE-negative (OS): HR 0.040 (0.002–0.620), n=18
- Johnstone 2019 ENE-positive (DSS): HR 0.480 (0.240–0.970), n=64
- Khurud 2022 (DFS): HR 0.333 (0.150–0.740), n=128

**Clinical Notes:**
- Radiation doses typically ranged from 45-57 Gy in 20-27 fractions
- Extended-field RT showed superior outcomes compared to involved-field RT (Khurud 2023: 2-year DFS 94.1% vs 67.9%, p=0.002)
- Benefit most pronounced in pN3 disease and pelvic node involvement
- ENE-negative patients may derive greater benefit than ENE-positive patients

### 2. Adjuvant Chemotherapy

**Pooled Analysis:**
- **Hazard Ratio:** 0.536 (95% CI: 0.332–0.866)
- **P-value:** 0.0107
- **Heterogeneity:** I² = 0% (no heterogeneity)
- **Number of Studies:** 2
- **Total Patients:** 255

**Interpretation:** Adjuvant chemotherapy reduces the risk of death by approximately 46% in node-positive penile cancer. The absence of heterogeneity suggests uniform treatment effects.

**Included Studies:**
- Sharma 2015 (OS): HR 0.400 (0.190–0.870), n=84 (pelvic node-positive patients)
- Necchi 2019 pN3 (OS): HR 0.650 (0.350–1.200), n=171 (benefit confined to pN3 subgroup)

**Clinical Notes:**
- Regimens included platinum-based combinations (cisplatin/5-FU, TIP, TPF)
- Benefit most pronounced in pN3 disease with pelvic lymph node involvement
- Limited benefit observed in pN1-2 disease
- p53 expression may predict response to chemotherapy (Necchi 2016)

### 3. Adjuvant Chemoradiotherapy

**Pooled Analysis:**
- **Hazard Ratio:** 0.455 (95% CI: 0.260–0.795)
- **P-value:** 0.0057
- **Heterogeneity:** I² = 66.7% (substantial heterogeneity)
- **Number of Studies:** 2
- **Total Patients:** 205

**Interpretation:** Adjuvant chemoradiotherapy reduces the risk of death or recurrence by approximately 54%, showing the largest effect size among treatment modalities. However, substantial heterogeneity suggests variable treatment effects, likely due to differences in patient selection and treatment protocols.

**Included Studies:**
- Chen 2020 N3 (PCSS): HR 0.540 (0.300–0.980), n=77 (SEER database, N3 patients only)
- Khurud 2022 C-CTRT (DFS): HR 0.110 (0.020–0.600), n=128 (concurrent chemoradiotherapy achieved 93% 2-year DFS and OS)

**Clinical Notes:**
- Concurrent chemoradiotherapy showed superior outcomes to either modality alone in pelvic nodal disease
- Khurud 2022 demonstrated that multimodality treatment (C-CTRT) achieved best outcomes in pN3 disease
- Single modality therapy may be adequate for inguinal ENE without pelvic involvement
- Benefit particularly pronounced in N3 disease with pelvic lymph node involvement

## Study Quality and Heterogeneity Assessment

### Heterogeneity Analysis

**Adjuvant Radiotherapy:** I² = 7.9% (low heterogeneity)
- Indicates consistent treatment effects across studies
- Minimal variability in study results

**Adjuvant Chemotherapy:** I² = 0% (no heterogeneity)
- Uniform treatment effects across studies
- High confidence in pooled estimate

**Adjuvant Chemoradiotherapy:** I² = 66.7% (substantial heterogeneity)
- Considerable variability between studies
- Likely due to differences in:
  - Patient populations (N3 subgroup selection)
  - Treatment protocols (radiation dose/field, chemotherapy regimens)
  - Outcome definitions (PCSS vs DFS)
- Pooled estimate should be interpreted cautiously

### Study Limitations

**Critical limitations affecting all analyses:**

1. **Retrospective design:** All included studies are retrospective with inherent selection bias
   - No randomised controlled trial data available
   - Treatment allocation influenced by physician preference and patient characteristics
   - Confounding by indication cannot be excluded

2. **Small sample sizes:** Limited statistical power and generalisability
   - Total of only 854 patients across all analyses
   - Individual studies ranged from 18 to 171 patients
   - Insufficient power to detect moderate treatment effects

3. **Heterogeneous populations:**
   - Variable inclusion criteria (pN2 vs pN3, ENE status)
   - Different staging systems (AJCC 7th vs 8th edition)
   - Geographic variation (Western vs Asian populations)

4. **Variable treatment protocols:**
   - Radiation doses: 45-57 Gy, different fractionation schedules
   - Chemotherapy regimens: VBM, cisplatin/5-FU, TIP, TPF
   - Field size: involved-field vs extended-field RT

5. **Multiple outcome definitions:**
   - OS (overall survival), DSS (disease-specific survival), DFS (disease-free survival), PCSS (penile cancer-specific survival)
   - Combining different endpoints may introduce bias

6. **Publication bias:** Cannot be formally assessed due to insufficient number of studies (<10 per analysis)

7. **Follow-up duration:** Variable across studies (median 8-42 months)

## Clinical Implications

### Evidence-Based Recommendations

**High-risk node-positive disease (pN3, ENE, pelvic nodes):**
- **Strong evidence** supports adjuvant therapy
- All three modalities show significant survival benefit
- **Chemoradiotherapy** may offer greatest benefit (HR 0.46) but with substantial heterogeneity
- Consider patient performance status, comorbidities, and treatment toxicity

**Intermediate-risk disease (pN2, multiple inguinal nodes):**
- **Moderate evidence** supports adjuvant radiotherapy
- Consider extended-field vs involved-field RT based on nodal distribution
- Chemotherapy benefit less clear in this population

**ENE-positive disease:**
- May require intensified multimodality therapy
- RT alone shows limited benefit in ENE+ patients (Johnstone 2019)
- Consider concurrent chemoradiotherapy

**ENE-negative disease:**
- Clear benefit from adjuvant RT (Johnstone 2019: OS improved, p=0.037)
- May not require combined modality treatment

### Treatment Selection Algorithm

```
Node-positive penile cancer post-ILND
    |
    ├─ Pelvic nodes (pN3) → Adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (concurrent)
    |
    ├─ Inguinal nodes with ENE
    |   ├─ Multiple nodes or pelvic extension → Chemoradiotherapy
    |   └─ Limited inguinal ENE → RT or chemotherapy
    |
    └─ ≥2 inguinal nodes without ENE → Adjuvant RT (extended-field)
```

### Future Directions

**Ongoing clinical trials:**
- **InPACT Trial (EA8134/NCT02305654):** Phase III randomised trial evaluating neoadjuvant chemotherapy and adjuvant chemoradiotherapy in locally advanced penile cancer
- Expected to provide first prospective Level 1 evidence
- Will inform future guideline recommendations

**Research priorities:**

## Conclusions

This meta-analysis demonstrates that adjuvant therapy significantly improves outcomes in node-positive penile cancer:

1. **All three modalities effective:** Adjuvant RT, chemotherapy, and chemoradiotherapy all showed statistically significant survival benefits with hazard ratios ranging from 0.46-0.54

2. **Largest benefit in high-risk disease:** Treatment effects most pronounced in pN3 disease with pelvic lymph node involvement or extranodal extension

3. **Adjuvant RT well-supported:** Low heterogeneity (I²=7.9%) and consistent benefit across multiple studies support routine use in appropriate patients

4. **Chemoradiotherapy promising:** Largest effect size (HR 0.46) but substantial heterogeneity (I²=66.7%) warrants cautious interpretation

5. **Evidence quality limitation:** All studies retrospective; prospective data urgently needed

6. **Clinical applicability:** Results support current guideline recommendations for adjuvant therapy in high-risk node-positive penile cancer

**Final recommendation:** Adjuvant therapy should be strongly considered in patients with pN2-3 penile cancer, particularly those with pelvic lymph node involvement or extranodal extension. Treatment selection should be individualised based on disease extent, patient factors, and multidisciplinary team discussion. The ongoing InPACT trial will provide critical prospective validation of these retrospective findings.
