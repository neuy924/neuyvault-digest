# R-CHOP versus R-EPOCH/DA-EPOCH-R: Prospective Trials

## Overview

R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) has been the standard first-line therapy for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) and related aggressive B-cell lymphomas for over two decades. DA-EPOCH-R (dose-adjusted etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, rituximab), developed at the NCI, is a continuous infusion-based, pharmacodynamically dose-adjusted intensification of this platform. A body of prospective trials — predominantly in DLBCL, primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL), MYC-rearranged high-grade B-cell lymphoma, and Burkitt lymphoma — has attempted to define whether DA-EPOCH-R improves upon R-CHOP. The overall finding across unselected DLBCL is that DA-EPOCH-R does **not** improve survival over R-CHOP but is consistently more toxic; in selected subtypes (PMBCL, MYC-rearranged HGBL), single-arm phase II data favour DA-EPOCH-R without a randomised comparator.[^1][^2][^3]

## Detailed Trial Summaries

### 1. Alliance/CALGB 50303 — The Definitive Phase III RCT

This landmark phase III intergroup trial (NCT00118209) is the only fully powered randomised controlled trial directly comparing R-CHOP and DA-EPOCH-R in unselected DLBCL. Between 2005 and 2013, 524 patients with newly diagnosed, previously untreated DLBCL (stage II–IV, plus PMBCL stage I) were randomised 1:1 to six cycles of R-CHOP or DA-EPOCH-R every 21 days.[^4][^5][^1]

At a median follow-up of 5.2 years, **PFS was not statistically different** between the arms (HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.68–1.27; P=.65), with 2-year PFS of 78.9% for DA-EPOCH-R and 75.5% for R-CHOP. Similarly, **OS was not different** (HR 1.09; P=.64), with 2-year OS of 86.5% versus 85.7%, respectively.[^6][^4][^1]

IPI distribution reflected a real-world population: 26% IPI 0–1, 37% IPI 2, 25% IPI 3, and 12% IPI 4–5. Seventy-four percent had stage III or IV disease.[^1]

**Toxicity** was significantly worse with DA-EPOCH-R (P<.001 for all): febrile neutropenia 35.0% vs 17.7%; infection 16.9% vs 10.7%; mucositis 8.4% vs 2.1%; neuropathy 18.6% vs 3.3%. Treatment-related deaths were identical at 2.1% (5 patients per arm).[^6][^1]

**Conclusion:** DA-EPOCH-R should not replace R-CHOP as standard first-line therapy for unselected DLBCL. Correlative molecular analyses (GCB/ABC subtype, MYC/BCL2 rearrangements) were planned separately but did not identify a subgroup with a statistically significant benefit from DA-EPOCH-R in this trial.[^7][^8]

***

### 2. Egyptian Prospective RCT (IJHS 2022)

A smaller prospective randomised trial enrolling intermediate and high-risk IPI DLBCL patients compared DA-R-EPOCH to R-CHOP with subgroup analyses by cell of origin (GCB vs ABC) and double-expressor status (BCL2/c-MYC). Despite the biological hypothesis that DA-EPOCH-R would be superior in high-risk and ABC-subtype disease, there was **no significant improvement in disease-free survival or response rate** with DA-R-EPOCH compared to R-CHOP. Complete remission rates in the ABC subgroup were numerically lower in the DA-R-EPOCH arm (71% vs 86% for R-CHOP), and DA-R-EPOCH was more toxic and complex to administer. The authors concluded that tailoring frontline DLBCL treatment based on IPI or double-expressor status remains an unresolved clinical question requiring further investigation.[^9][^10]

***

### 3. NCI Phase II — DA-EPOCH-R in Primary Mediastinal B-Cell Lymphoma (NEJM 2013)

Dunleavy and colleagues at the NCI conducted a prospective single-arm phase II study of DA-EPOCH-R (without consolidative radiotherapy) in 51 patients with untreated PMBCL. PMBCL was not adequately represented in CALGB 50303 (only 28 patients), making this the primary prospective dataset for this histological subtype.[^11][^7]

With a median follow-up of 5 years (up to 14 years), the results were remarkable: **5-year EFS 93% and 5-year OS 97%** without any mediastinal radiation. Among a concurrent retrospective Stanford cohort of 16 patients, EFS was 100% over a median of 3 years. No late cardiac toxic effects were observed. These outcomes led to widespread adoption of DA-EPOCH-R as first-line therapy for PMBCL in many centres, replacing R-CHOP with radiotherapy.[^12][^7][^11]

A subsequent meta-analysis (Haematologica 2023) of 4,068 patients with PMBCL confirmed an 8% OS benefit for dose-intensive regimens (88% vs 80%; P<0.01) with significantly less radiation use (22% vs 55%), further supporting the biological rationale for DA-EPOCH-R in PMBCL.[^13][^14]

***

### 4. Prospective Multicentre Phase II — DA-EPOCH-R in MYC-Rearranged Aggressive B-Cell Lymphoma (Lancet Haematol 2018)

The NCI-led, 13-centre prospective phase II study (NCT01092182) enrolled 53 patients with untreated MYC-rearranged aggressive B-cell lymphomas, including single-hit (n=19) and double-hit (n=24, with BCL2-R and/or BCL6-R) subtypes. This represents the **only prospective trial in this specific population**.[^3]

With a median follow-up of 55.6 months, the overall response rate was 87% (CR 74%). The **48-month EFS was 71%** (95% CI 57–81%) and **48-month OS was 77%** (95% CI 63–86%) for all patients. Outcomes were similar in single-hit vs double-hit patients (EFS 63% vs 73%; P=0.40). Patients aged ≥60 years fared comparably to younger patients, suggesting acceptable tolerance in older individuals.[^3]

In contrast, multiple retrospective R-CHOP series in MYC-R lymphomas reported 2-year OS of 35–63% and 2-year EFS of 23–25%, though direct comparison is confounded by selection bias. Grade 4 neutropenia occurred on 53% of cycles and febrile neutropenia on 19% of cycles; there were 3 treatment-related deaths (6%), all from infection in elderly high-IPI patients.[^3]

***

### 5. HOVON/SAKK 127 — Phase III RCT: DA-EPOCH-R vs R-CODOX-M/R-IVAC in Burkitt Lymphoma (Lancet Haematol 2023)

This multicentre, phase III open-label randomised trial (EudraCT 2013-004394-27), conducted across 26 centres in the Netherlands, Belgium, and Switzerland, randomised 84 patients (aged 18–75) with newly diagnosed high-risk Burkitt lymphoma without CNS involvement to either DA-EPOCH-R (6 cycles) or R-CODOX-M/R-IVAC (2 cycles alternating). The trial closed early due to slow accrual.[^15][^16]

At a median follow-up of 28.5 months, **PFS was not significantly different** (HR 1.42; 95% CI 0.63–3.18; P=.40): 2-year PFS 70% for DA-EPOCH-R versus 76% for R-CODOX-M/R-IVAC. Two-year OS was 75% and 76%, respectively (HR 1.21; P=.65). However, **DA-EPOCH-R was significantly less toxic**: fewer infections, fewer transfusions, and shorter hospitalisation in the R-CODOX/R-IVAC arm. The authors concluded DA-EPOCH-R is a valid, less toxic alternative for high-risk Burkitt lymphoma without CNS involvement.[^17][^18][^16]

***

### 6. AMC Trial 034 — R-EPOCH in HIV-Associated Lymphoma (Haematologica 2010)

The AIDS Malignancy Consortium (AMC) 034 trial was a randomised phase II study in HIV-associated B-cell lymphoma (74% DLBCL), randomising 106 eligible patients to concurrent R-EPOCH (arm A: rituximab given with each EPOCH cycle) versus sequential EPOCH followed by weekly rituximab (arm B). This trial was not a direct R-CHOP vs R-EPOCH comparison but evaluated optimal rituximab scheduling within the EPOCH backbone.[^19][^20]

Concurrent R-EPOCH achieved a CR/CRu rate of 69% versus 53% for sequential EPOCH→R (P=0.005 for arm A vs 50% null hypothesis). Grade 3–4 febrile neutropenia was similar (16% vs 15%) and no excess toxicity with concurrent rituximab was observed. A response-adapted strategy allowing 4 cycles for those achieving early CR showed comparable 2-year EFS (~78–85%) versus 5–6 cycles. These data supported the recommendation for concurrent rituximab with EPOCH in HIV-associated lymphoma and informed the design of CALGB 50303.[^20][^19]

## Clinical Interpretation and Synthesis

### DA-EPOCH-R Does Not Improve Outcomes in Unselected DLBCL

The totality of prospective evidence—anchored by CALGB 50303—establishes that DA-EPOCH-R is **not superior to R-CHOP** in unselected DLBCL, and carries substantially higher toxicity burden. The hope that subgroup analyses (GCB vs ABC, double-expressors) from CALGB 50303 would reveal a benefiting subgroup has not been realised in published reports. Current expert consensus limits DA-EPOCH-R use to specific subtypes with high-level supporting data.[^5][^21][^8][^7][^1]

### DA-EPOCH-R Is Preferred in PMBCL

In PMBCL, DA-EPOCH-R has become a widely adopted standard at many centres, based on the NCI phase II study showing 93% EFS and 97% OS at 5 years without radiotherapy. No randomised trial exists comparing it directly to R-CHOP in PMBCL, as the 28 PMBCL patients in CALGB 50303 were too few for meaningful subset analysis. Retrospective comparative data and the meta-analysis support the superiority of dose-intensive approaches (predominantly DA-EPOCH-R) over R-CHOP alone in PMBCL. This remains an international randomised trial priority.[^7][^11][^13]

### DA-EPOCH-R Is the Preferred Regimen for MYC-Rearranged HGBL/DHL

For high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC/BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangements (double-hit/triple-hit), DA-EPOCH-R is the NCCN-recommended preferred regimen based on the Lancet Haematology 2018 phase II data. No randomised comparison to R-CHOP exists specifically in this population; the CALGB 50303 DHL cohort was too small for subset analysis. Historical R-CHOP retrospective data in MYC-R lymphomas showed 2-year OS of 35–63%, significantly worse than the 77% OS at 4 years with DA-EPOCH-R.[^22][^23][^3]

### Burkitt Lymphoma: DA-EPOCH-R Is a Valid, Less Toxic Alternative

The HOVON/SAKK 127 trial—the first randomised trial in adult Burkitt lymphoma—showed DA-EPOCH-R achieved equivalent PFS and OS to R-CODOX-M/R-IVAC, with substantially less toxicity. This has not been compared to R-CHOP in a prospective trial; R-CHOP alone is generally considered inadequate for Burkitt lymphoma due to the high proliferative index.[^16][^17]

### Absence of Randomised Evidence in Key Subtypes

A critical gap in the evidence base is the absence of a randomised trial comparing R-CHOP to DA-EPOCH-R in:
- PMBCL (the IELSG 37 and IELSG 53 trials address the RT question but not regimen choice)
- MYC-rearranged HGBL/double-hit lymphoma
- Double-expressor DLBCL[^2][^24]

Retrospective series consistently suggest DA-EPOCH-R may be superior in these subtypes, but selection bias limits interpretation. Prospective randomised evidence in these biomarker-defined populations remains the highest research priority.[^2][^3]
