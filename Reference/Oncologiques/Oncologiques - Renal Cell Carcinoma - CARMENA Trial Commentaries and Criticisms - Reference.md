## CARMENA Trial on Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma: Comprehensive Analysis of Commentaries, Reviews, and Criticisms

## Executive Summary

The CARMENA (Cancer du Rein Métastatique Néphrectomie et Antiangiogéniques) trial fundamentally challenged decades of surgical practice in metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC), demonstrating that sunitinib monotherapy was noninferior to cytoreductive nephrectomy (CN) followed by sunitinib. Published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2018, this phase III randomized noninferiority trial enrolled 450 patients over nine years and reported median overall survival of 18.4 months with sunitinib alone versus 13.9 months with nephrectomy plus sunitinib (HR 0.89; 95% CI 0.71-1.10). While practice-changing, the trial sparked intense debate regarding patient selection, external validity, and applicability to modern immunotherapy-based treatment paradigms. This analysis synthesizes expert opinions, methodological critiques, and clinical implications to provide institutional-grade insight into one of oncology's most controversial contemporary trials.dailynews.ascopubs +2

## Trial Design and Main Findings

## Study Architecture

CARMENA employed a noninferiority design comparing upfront CN followed by sunitinib (arm A) versus sunitinib alone (arm B) in patients with synchronous intermediate- or poor-risk metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma. The trial utilized a noninferiority margin of 1.20 for the hazard ratio, with a planned enrollment of 576 patients. However, accrual challenges led to premature closure after the second interim analysis with only 450 patients enrolled over nine years (2009-2017)—a rate of approximately 0.7 patients per site per year.ascopubs +3

Patients were stratified by Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC) risk groups, with 44.4% poor-risk patients in the CN arm and 41.5% in the sunitinib arm—proportions significantly higher than typical clinical practice. All patients required biopsy-confirmed clear cell histology, ECOG performance status 0-1, and were candidates for immediate systemic therapy.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

## Primary Results

At median follow-up of 50.9 months, the trial demonstrated noninferiority of sunitinib alone:pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

- **Median OS**: 18.4 months (sunitinib) vs 13.9 months (CN + sunitinib)
- **Hazard ratio**: 0.89 (95% CI 0.71-1.10), below the 1.20 noninferiority margin
- **Progression-free survival**: 8.3 months (sunitinib) vs 7.2 months (CN + sunitinib) onclive +1
- **Objective response rate**: 29.1% vs 27.4% urologytimes +1

Subgroup analyses by MSKCC risk category showed consistent results across intermediate-risk (23.4 vs 19.0 months) and poor-risk (13.3 vs 10.2 months) populations.dailynews.ascopubs +1

## Updated Analysis

Extended follow-up at 61.5 months confirmed initial findings while revealing important subgroup distinctions. Reclassification using International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium (IMDC) criteria showed 58.6% intermediate-risk and 41.4% poor-risk patients. Critical post-hoc analyses identified potential CN benefit in highly selected populations, particularly those with only one IMDC risk factor, though these findings carry inherent limitations of unplanned analyses.ascopubs +1

## Major Criticisms and Controversies

## 1\. Patient Selection Bias and External Validity

**The Fundamental Critique**

Multiple analyses questioned whether CARMENA enrolled a representative mRCC population. A National Cancer Database (NCDB) comparison revealed systematic differences between trial participants and real-world U.S. practice:kidney.uroonco.uroweb +1

The trial recruited patients with median tumor burden of 14.2 cm across metastatic sites versus 8.6 cm for the primary tumor—suggesting metastatic disease dominance that would inherently favor systemic therapy over local control.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih

**Selection Bias Mechanisms**

Poor accrual (0.7 patients/site/year over 9 years) suggests physician-induced selection bias toward poorer surgical candidates. Investigators lacking clinical equipoise may have preferentially excluded patients with low-volume, indolent disease who would clearly benefit from CN. This created a self-fulfilling prophecy: enrolling predominantly poor candidates for surgery demonstrated that surgery doesn't help poor candidates.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

As one external validation study concluded: "The CARMENA trial recruited patients with higher metastatic burden than those seen in US practice, which may have affected the trial outcomes". This fundamentally limits generalizability to patients with favorable tumor-to-metastasis ratios who historically derive maximum CN benefit.auajournals +1

## 2\. Trial Contamination and Protocol Deviations

**Substantial Crossover**

Nearly one-third of patients did not receive their allocated treatment:dailynews.ascopubs +1

- **Sunitinib arm**: 17% underwent secondary CN (40 patients) pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2
- **CN arm**: 6.7% never received nephrectomy; 22.5% never received sunitinib ascopubs
- **Sunitinib arm**: 4.9% never received sunitinib ascopubs

This contamination biases toward noninferiority. Particularly significant was the secondary CN cohort, who achieved median OS of 48.5 months versus 15.7 months in those without surgery (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.22-0.54)—one of the most striking signals in the entire dataset. While this represents selected responders, it demonstrates CN's potential value in appropriate contexts.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

**Delayed Systemic Therapy**

In the CN arm, sunitinib initiation required 4-6 weeks post-surgery for convalescence. Given that 22.5% of CN patients never received sunitinib—presumably due to perioperative complications, disease progression, or death—the trial inadvertently tested whether nephrectomy could completely derail systemic therapy rather than whether it adds value to systemic therapy.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

## 3\. Risk Stratification and Poor-Risk Patient Overenrollment

**The Poor-Risk Paradox**

The inclusion of 41-44% poor-risk patients represents a fundamental design flaw. Large retrospective datasets had already demonstrated that poor-risk patients derive minimal CN benefit, with median OS around 10 months regardless of surgery. Including substantial poor-risk populations essentially stacked the deck against demonstrating CN benefit.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +3

Post-hoc IMDC analysis revealed the poor-risk cohort had median OS of 10.2 months with CN versus 13.3 months with sunitinib alone—confirming retrospective evidence that surgery may harm these patients by delaying effective therapy. As one commentary noted: "This suggests physician-induced selection bias towards poorer surgical candidates prior to inclusion in CARMENA".pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

**Intermediate-Risk Heterogeneity**

Updated analysis distinguished intermediate-risk patients by number of IMDC factors:uhra.herts +1

- **One IMDC factor** (time from diagnosis to treatment <1 year only): Median OS 30.5 months (CN) vs 25.2 months (sunitinib), HR 1.24 \[0.81-1.90\], not significant
- **Two IMDC factors**: Median OS 16.6 months (CN) vs 31.2 months (sunitinib), HR 0.61 \[0.41-0.91\], p=0.015

This striking reversal suggests CARMENA inadvertently enrolled heterogeneous intermediate-risk patients, with the two-factor subgroup potentially including chemoresistant biology or higher occult burden not captured by risk criteria. However, these post-hoc findings require cautious interpretation given lack of prespecification and multiple comparison issues.reddit +1

## 4\. Metastatic Burden and Oligometastatic Disease

**Defining Tumor Burden**

CARMENA reported median two metastatic sites with high organ-specific burden (79% lung, 36% bone, 35% lymph nodes). Yet 33% of patients had only one metastatic site—a potentially oligometastatic population where aggressive local control might provide substantial benefit.ascopubs +2

The number of metastatic sites showed counterintuitive results: patients with one site had median OS of 23.6 months (CN) versus 22.7 months (sunitinib), HR 1.08 \[0.75-1.57\]. Post-hoc analysis concluded "number of metastatic sites is not helpful to define good candidates for surgery"—yet this contradicts established oncologic principles that oligometastatic disease responds to multimodality therapy.ecancer +3

**Primary Tumor Size Implications**

Retrospective analyses demonstrate small primary tumors (≤4 cm) associate with improved survival in metastatic ccRCC (10-31% relative risk reduction per 1-cm decrease). CARMENA's median primary tumor of 8.6 cm versus 14.2 cm metastatic burden represents a 1.65:1 metastasis-to-primary ratio—populations where systemic disease dominance would naturally favor medical therapy.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## 5\. Timing of Surgery: Deferred Cytoreduction

**The SURTIME Complement**

The SURTIME trial (Immediate Surgery or Surgery After Sunitinib Malate in Treating Patients With Metastatic Kidney Cancer) compared immediate versus deferred CN, both with sunitinib. Though severely underpowered (99 of 458 planned patients), median OS favored deferred CN: 32.4 months (95% CI 14.5-65.3) versus 15.0 months (95% CI 9.3-29.5) with immediate surgery.med-fom-urlgsci.sites.olt.ubc +2

SURTIME recruited predominantly MSKCC intermediate-risk patients (89%) versus CARMENA's 47%, suggesting appropriate patient selection yields different conclusions. The deferred approach allowed identification of 29% of patients with rapid progression before planned surgery, sparing them morbidity.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +3

**Exposure-Duration Hypothesis**

Post-hoc SURTIME analysis revealed immediate CN impaired sunitinib administration, onset, and duration. Median sunitinib exposure was longer with upfront systemic therapy, enabling earlier disease control and identification of progression prior to surgery. This supports a paradigm where systemic therapy serves as a "litmus test" for CN candidacy.journals.sagepub +2

Complementary CARMENA data showed 17% of sunitinib-alone patients eventually underwent deferred CN at median 11.1 months, with strikingly favorable 48.5-month median OS. While this represents selected responders, it demonstrates deferred CN's potential after demonstrating systemic therapy responsiveness.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

## 6\. Statistical and Methodological Concerns

**Noninferiority Margin Controversy**

The 1.20 hazard ratio margin generated methodological debate. Critics argued this liberal margin allowed declaring noninferiority despite potentially clinically meaningful differences. The 95% CI upper bound of 1.10 fell below 1.20, establishing statistical noninferiority—but the point estimate favored sunitinib alone (HR 0.89), raising questions about whether superiority rather than noninferiority was demonstrated.ascopubs +2

Noninferiority margins should preserve substantial fractions of active control effects. With CN previously demonstrating 3-10 month survival improvements in the interferon era, a 1.20 margin might allow unacceptable effect loss. However, others counter that pragmatic margins balance ideal preservation with achievable sample sizes.fda +2

**Premature Closure and Power**

Stopping at 450 of 576 planned patients after the second interim analysis reduced statistical power. While the noninferiority test succeeded, subgroup analyses became exploratory rather than confirmatory. The slow accrual itself suggests recruitment challenges reflecting real-world equipoise issues.tcr.amegroups +2

## 7\. Surgical Quality and Perioperative Outcomes

**Operative Morbidity**

CN carries substantial morbidity: perioperative mortality 0-13%, major complications 3-36%. In CARMENA, 22.5% of CN patients never received sunitinib—likely reflecting perioperative complications, prolonged recovery, or early progression. This "opportunity cost" of surgery becomes critical when effective systemic therapy exists.amj.amegroups +2

One analysis noted 29.5% overall complications and 6.1% high-grade complications (Clavien-Dindo 3-5) in CN patients, with 1.4% perioperative mortality. Surgical volume significantly affected outcomes, suggesting CARMENA's 0.7 procedures/site/year might not reflect high-volume center results.amj.amegroups +1

**Surgical Technique Variability**

CARMENA did not mandate standardized surgical approach, quality metrics, or perioperative care pathways. With enrollment across multiple countries and centers of varying volume, surgical technique heterogeneity could confound results. High-volume centers typically achieve superior outcomes, yet most CARMENA sites performed fewer than one procedure annually.dailynews.ascopubs +1

## 8\. Evolving Treatment Landscape and Applicability

**The Immunotherapy Paradigm Shift**

CARMENA concluded in 2017—the same year CheckMate 214 demonstrated ipilimumab/nivolumab superiority over sunitinib for intermediate/poor-risk mRCC. By publication (2018), standard-of-care had shifted to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) combinations, rendering sunitinib monotherapy obsolete for most patients.dailynews.ascopubs +1

CheckMate 214 showed 42% complete responses with combination ICI versus 1% with sunitinib. These unprecedented response rates—including potential for "functional cure" in select patients—fundamentally alter CN calculus. As one commentary noted: "The role of CN in patients receiving immunotherapy or novel agents must be evaluated".pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

**Biological Differences by Therapy Class**

The mechanism of CN benefit may differ between immunotherapy and targeted therapy. Proposed mechanisms include reducing tumor-derived immunosuppressive factors, decreasing tumor antigen load, removing sanctuary sites, and improving performance status. If CN primarily synergizes with immune responses, CARMENA's TKI-based design may have missed the population where surgery matters most.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

Real-world evidence in the ICI era continues supporting CN in selected patients, particularly those with favorable performance status and limited metastases. A post-hoc CheckMate 214 analysis suggested patients with prior nephrectomy derived greater ICI benefit than those with primary in situ—though this comparison suffers from selection bias.kidney.uroonco.uroweb +2

## 9\. Exclusion of Key Populations

**Non-Clear Cell Histology**

CARMENA mandated biopsy-proven clear cell RCC, excluding non-clear cell subtypes that comprise 15-20% of renal cancers. Non-ccRCC often responds poorly to systemic therapy, potentially making CN relatively more valuable. Sarcomatoid dedifferentiation—present in ~5% of RCC and associated with 5.9-month median survival—was also not specifically addressed, though such patients likely fell into poor-risk categories.cancertherapyadvisor +2

**Favorable-Risk Disease**

By requiring intermediate or poor IMDC risk, CARMENA excluded favorable-risk patients. This population—characterized by good performance status, low metastatic burden, and indolent biology—traditionally derives greatest CN benefit. The trial therefore cannot inform management of approximately 20-30% of mRCC presentations.urologytimes +1

**Metachronous Metastases**

CARMENA enrolled synchronous metastatic disease (present at initial diagnosis). Patients developing metachronous metastases months to years post-nephrectomy represent biologically distinct populations, often with better prognosis. While prior nephrectomy patients could enroll if metastases developed subsequently, the trial primarily addressed synchronous presentations.nature +1

## Upfront CN Should Be Considered For:

- Favorable-risk patients with resectable primary and limited, stable metastatic disease
- Patients with dominant primary tumor burden (large primary, small/few metastases)
- Oligometastatic disease (≤3 sites) with all disease potentially controllable
- Symptomatic primary tumors (bleeding, pain, paraneoplastic syndromes)
- Intermediate-risk patients with one IMDC factor and low metastatic burden (weak recommendation based on post-hoc data)

## Upfront Systemic Therapy Should Be Prioritized For:

- Poor-risk patients (≥4 IMDC factors)
- High metastatic burden (multiple sites, bulky disease)
- Intermediate-risk patients requiring immediate therapy
- Patients with symptomatic metastases
- Brain metastases or other immediately life-threatening disease
- Medical comorbidities increasing surgical risk

## Deferred CN Should Be Considered For:

- Near-complete or complete response at metastatic sites after systemic therapy
- Initially unresectable primary becoming resectable after treatment response
- Development of symptomatic primary during systemic therapy
- Residual primary as only site of measurable disease after metastatic CR

## CN Should Generally Be Avoided In:

- IMDC poor-risk with >3 risk factors
- Rapid systemic progression on initial therapy (indicates chemoresistant biology)
- Prohibitive surgical risk
- Patient preference for medical management
- Unresectable primary tumor

## Conclusion

The CARMENA trial represents a watershed moment in mRCC management, ending the reflexive practice of upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy for all metastatic presentations. Its demonstration that sunitinib alone was noninferior to surgery plus sunitinib fundamentally challenged surgical dogma and appropriately shifted treatment paradigms toward prioritizing systemic therapy in intermediate and poor-risk disease.

However, CARMENA's limitations—including patient selection bias, high metastatic burden enrollment, poor accrual, substantial crossover, and conduct in the now-obsolete TKI monotherapy era—preclude sweeping conclusions that CN has no role. Rather, the trial's legacy is more nuanced: it established that surgery can harm patients with high metastatic burden and poor prognosis, validated systemic therapy prioritization in most cases, and emphasized the critical importance of patient selection over blanket approaches.

The striking 48.5-month median survival in patients undergoing deferred CN after systemic response suggests surgery retains value when deployed judiciously. Emerging evidence in the immunotherapy era, including real-world data supporting CN in selected ICI-treated patients and ongoing trials combining SBRT or CN with modern doublet therapy, indicates the field continues evolving beyond CARMENA's framework.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

Ultimately, CARMENA transformed CN from default treatment to a precision medicine decision requiring multidisciplinary evaluation, validated risk stratification, consideration of tumor burden and biology, and integration with increasingly effective systemic therapies. This represents genuine progress—even as debates continue about exactly which patients benefit from a surgery that, in appropriate candidates, may still contribute meaningfully to long-term survival and potentially cure.
