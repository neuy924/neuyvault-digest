## Studies Examining HPV Status in Penile Cancers: A Comprehensive Review

## Overview

Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection has been established as a key etiologic factor in approximately 30-50% of penile squamous cell carcinomas (PSCC) worldwide. The relationship between HPV and penile cancer has been investigated extensively over the past three decades, with studies demonstrating substantial geographic variation, histologic specificity, and evolving temporal trends. This report provides a systematic compilation of studies examining HPV prevalence, genotype distribution, and clinical significance in penile cancer populations.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Global HPV Prevalence Patterns

The most comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis to date, conducted by Olesen et al. (2019), analyzed 52 studies encompassing 4,199 penile cancer cases and established a pooled HPV DNA prevalence of 50.8% (95% CI: 44.8-56.7). This landmark study revealed significant heterogeneity across populations, with prevalence ranging from 22% in early studies to over 70% in specific high-risk regions. The predominant oncogenic HPV type across all populations is HPV16, accounting for approximately 68.3% of HPV-positive cases, followed by HPV6 (8.1%) and HPV18 (6.9%).pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

Geographic variation in HPV prevalence is substantial. Brazilian studies have reported among the highest prevalence rates globally, with northeastern Brazil demonstrating HPV positivity in up to 79% of cases, compared to 44% in southeastern regions and 31% in central-west areas. Asian populations show considerable heterogeneity, with Thai studies reporting 76.5% prevalence, Chinese multicenter data showing 48.8%, and Vietnamese studies finding 23%. European and North American populations typically report prevalence between 25-50%.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +6

## Histologic Subtype Associations

A defining characteristic of HPV-associated penile cancer is its strong correlation with specific histologic subtypes. The WHO 2016 classification system now formally separates penile squamous cell carcinomas into HPV-related and non-HPV-related categories. Basaloid squamous cell carcinoma demonstrates the highest HPV association, with 80-84% of cases testing positive. Warty (condylomatous) carcinomas show similarly high rates of 75-100% HPV positivity. In contrast, keratinizing usual-type squamous cell carcinomas exhibit HPV positivity in only 34.9-50% of cases, while verrucous carcinomas are predominantly HPV-negative (23-33% positive).nature +5

Rubin et al. (2001) provided seminal data establishing these histologic associations in a US and Paraguayan cohort, demonstrating HPV DNA in 42% of penile carcinomas overall, 90% of dysplasias, and 100% of condylomas. Their study revealed stark contrasts: basaloid carcinomas were 80% HPV-positive and warty carcinomas 100% positive, compared to only 34.9% for keratinizing SCC and 33.3% for verrucous carcinoma.[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11583947/)

## HPV Genotype Distribution

HPV16 is overwhelmingly the dominant genotype across all populations, accounting for 60-77% of HPV-positive penile cancers. The next most common genotypes vary by region. In most Western populations, HPV18 ranks second (6-15% of HPV-positive cases), followed by HPV33 and HPV45. Interestingly, low-risk HPV types, particularly HPV6 and HPV11, are detected in 8-11% of penile cancers, suggesting these may contribute to carcinogenesis beyond their established role in benign condylomas.frontiersin +3

Regional variations in genotype distribution are notable. In Thailand, HPV-22 was unexpectedly prevalent (83.3%), representing an unusual geographic pattern. Chinese data demonstrate that HPV16 and HPV18 together account for 88.6% of HPV-positive cases, with HPV16, 18, and 33 comprising 91% when combined. Brazilian studies show broader genotype diversity, including HPV51, 59, and 74 alongside the more common types.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +3

## p16 Immunohistochemistry as a Surrogate Marker

The use of p16INK4a immunohistochemistry as a surrogate marker for HPV-driven carcinogenesis has become widespread, with the WHO 2016 classification recommending its use for penile cancer subtyping. Olesen et al. found overall p16 positivity in 41.6% of penile cancers, with 85.8% positivity in HPV-related SCC subtypes compared to 17.1% in non-HPV-related subtypes. Among HPV-positive cases, 79.6% demonstrated p16 positivity, while only 18.5% of HPV-negative cancers showed p16 expression.onlinelibrary.wiley +2

However, discordance between p16 expression and HPV detection occurs in 10-20% of cases. Recent multiplex studies using both RNA in situ hybridization for HPV E6/E7 mRNA and p16 immunohistochemistry have demonstrated that p16 expression can occur independently of HPV infection, particularly in cases with pRB mutations. Chinese data showed that among HPV-negative invasive cancers, 11.9% were p16-positive, while 17.1% of HPV-positive cases were p16-negative.nature +5

## Prognostic Significance of HPV Status

The prognostic value of HPV status in penile cancer has been controversial, with studies reporting conflicting results over the past two decades. Early studies showed no survival difference between HPV-positive and HPV-negative patients. Wiener et al. (1992) found no association with survival in 29 patients, while Bezerra et al. (2001) reported identical 10-year disease-specific survival rates (68.4% vs 69.1%, P=0.83) in 82 patients.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

However, more recent and larger studies increasingly demonstrate a survival advantage for HPV-positive disease. Lont et al. (2006) in 171 patients showed improved 5-year disease-specific survival (92% vs 78%, P=0.03) and identified high-risk HPV as an independent predictor of lower mortality (HR 0.14, P=0.01). Djajadiningrat et al. (2015) confirmed this finding in 212 patients, with 5-year disease-specific survival of 96% in HPV-positive versus 82% in HPV-negative cases (P=0.016).pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

The largest contemporary database analyses have solidified these findings. Analysis of the US National Cancer Database from 2004-2016 (486 patients) showed 5-year overall survival of 62% in HPV-positive versus 50% in HPV-negative patients, with multivariate analysis demonstrating a 1.49-fold increased death risk for HPV-negative disease. An expanded NCDB analysis through 2021 (1,371 patients) confirmed median overall survival of 123.96 months for HPV-positive versus 104.38 months for HPV-negative disease (P=0.01), with a hazard ratio for death of 0.79 (P=0.03).ascopubs +1

Importantly, the Norwegian 50-year cohort study by Moen et al. (2024) revealed that the survival benefit of HPV status is particularly pronounced in node-positive patients. While 5-year cancer-specific survival showed no difference overall (p=0.37), among patients with lymph node metastases, HPV-positive status was an independent predictor of better survival (HR 0.54, P=0.04) after adjusting for age, nodal stage, and adjuvant therapy. This finding has significant clinical implications for treatment stratification and counseling.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Temporal Trends

An important observation from longitudinal studies is the increasing proportion of HPV-associated penile cancers over time. The US registry-based study showed HPV prevalence rising from 36% in 1998-2003 to 68% in 2004-2005. The Norwegian cohort spanning 1973-2022 demonstrated a statistically significant increasing trend in HPV-positive cases over the 50-year period (p=0.01). Chinese data from 2006-2017 showed increasing HPV-positive penile cancer incidence concurrent with declining phimosis incidence, suggesting shifting etiologic pathways.frontiersin +3

These temporal trends mirror observations in oropharyngeal cancer, where HPV-positive disease has risen dramatically while HPV-negative disease has declined, attributed to changing sexual behaviors and HPV exposure patterns. The implications for prevention strategies are substantial, as current prophylactic HPV vaccines targeting types 16 and 18 could potentially prevent 72-91% of HPV-associated penile cancers.acervomais +3

## Detection Methodologies

Studies have employed diverse methodologies for HPV detection, which contributes to heterogeneity in reported prevalence rates. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based methods are most common, with consensus primers (GP5+/6+, PGMY09/11, SPF10) used for broad-spectrum detection followed by genotyping through sequencing or line probe assays. More recent studies employ type-specific real-time PCR for HPV16 and HPV18, providing both detection and viral load quantification.journals.plos +5

The SPF10 primer system, used by Rubin et al. and subsequent studies, allows simultaneous detection of 25 HPV types with high sensitivity in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue. Hybrid capture methods and RNA in situ hybridization for E6/E7 mRNA transcripts represent newer approaches that detect transcriptionally active HPV infection rather than merely the presence of viral DNA. These methodological differences partially explain the wide range of reported prevalence (22-79%) across studies.d-nb +7

## Clinical Implications and Future Directions

The accumulating evidence on HPV's role in penile carcinogenesis has several critical clinical implications:

**Vaccination strategies**: With HPV16 and 18 accounting for 72-91% of HPV-positive cases, current prophylactic vaccines have strong potential for primary prevention. The nonavalent vaccine, covering HPV types 6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, and 58, could prevent up to 95% of HPV-associated penile cancers. Implementation of gender-neutral vaccination programs is increasingly recognized as essential for comprehensive HPV cancer prevention.cancernetwork +2

**Risk stratification and treatment planning**: The prognostic significance of HPV status, particularly in node-positive disease, suggests potential for biomarker-guided treatment de-escalation strategies analogous to those being studied in HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer. Routine HPV and p16 testing at diagnosis should be standard practice for prognostic stratification and clinical trial design.ascopubs +2

**Surveillance and screening**: Men with penile cancer have elevated risk for second HPV-associated cancers of the oral cavity, oropharynx, and anal canal, warranting consideration of surveillance protocols. The relationship between precancerous lesions (penile intraepithelial neoplasia) and HPV status (79.8% HPV-positive) supports potential screening strategies in high-risk populations.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

**Targeted therapies**: Understanding the molecular pathways of HPV-mediated carcinogenesis, including E6/E7 oncoprotein effects on p53 and pRb, opens possibilities for targeted therapeutic interventions. Studies examining immune checkpoint inhibition, particularly PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, show promise, with HPV-positive tumors potentially more responsive to immunotherapy given their increased tumor mutational burden and immune cell infiltration.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Limitations and Research Gaps

Despite substantial progress, several important knowledge gaps remain:

1. **Mechanistic understanding**: The precise mechanisms by which HPV drives penile carcinogenesis in only a subset of infections remain incompletely understood, particularly regarding viral integration patterns and cofactors required for malignant transformation.onlinelibrary.wiley +1
2. **Biomarker refinement**: The 10-20% discordance between p16 expression and HPV detection necessitates improved surrogate markers or standardized combined testing algorithms.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2
3. **Treatment response data**: While observational data show survival differences, prospective trials examining whether HPV status predicts response to specific treatment modalities (chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy) are needed.medscape +1
4. **Prevention efficacy**: Long-term follow-up data on HPV vaccination impact on penile cancer incidence are not yet available, given the disease's long latency period and rarity in vaccinated populations.[frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2020.01521/full)
5. **Underrepresented populations**: High-incidence regions in Africa, South America, and Asia remain underrepresented in molecular epidemiologic studies, despite bearing the greatest disease burden.[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7195778/)

## Conclusion

The body of evidence comprehensively demonstrates that HPV infection, predominantly HPV16, plays a causal role in approximately 30-50% of penile squamous cell carcinomas worldwide, with substantial geographic and histologic variation. HPV-positive penile cancers are molecularly and clinically distinct from HPV-negative tumors, with emerging evidence suggesting improved survival, particularly in node-positive disease. The increasing temporal trend toward HPV-associated cases and the preventability through existing HPV vaccines underscore the importance of expanded vaccination programs and routine HPV testing in clinical practice. Future research should focus on refining biomarkers, understanding mechanisms of carcinogenesis, and developing HPV-tailored treatment strategies to optimize outcomes for this devastating disease.

## Stage at Presentation: The HPV Paradox

## Primary Tumor Stage (T-stage)

The relationship between HPV status and primary tumor characteristics reveals a consistent pattern across multiple large cohorts. The National Cancer Database (NCDB) analysis by Katsenis et al. The biological underpinnings of earlier T-stage detection in HPV-positive disease likely reflect the distinct histologic subtypes associated with HPV infection. Basaloid and warty carcinomas, which are strongly HPV-associated (80-100% HPV-positive), tend to have more distinctive clinical appearances that may prompt earlier medical evaluation. Additionally, these tumors frequently arise at the glans or coronal sulcus—more visible anatomical locations compared to the shaft or prepuce where HPV-negative usual-type keratinizing carcinomas more commonly develop.frontiersin +2

## Nodal Stage (N-stage): The Paradox

Counter-intuitively, while HPV-positive tumors present with lower T-stage, they demonstrate **higher rates of clinically node-positive (cN+) disease** at presentation. The Katsenis NCDB analysis revealed this striking paradox: HPV-positive tumors had lower T-stage and lower lymphovascular invasion (LVI) rates, yet presented with more palpable adenopathy. This apparent contradiction challenges conventional understanding of metastatic progression.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

Several hypotheses attempt to explain this phenomenon:

**1\. Detection and Lead-Time Bias**: HPV-positive tumors may become symptomatic earlier due to their warty or exophytic morphology, leading to diagnosis at lower T-stage but after sufficient time for lymphatic dissemination. **2\. Alternative Metastatic Pathways**: The lower LVI rates in HPV-positive tumors suggest nodal spread may occur through mechanisms independent of classical lymphovascular invasion. HPV-transformed cells demonstrate altered expression of adhesion molecules and matrix metalloproteinases, which may facilitate direct lymphatic channel invasion without morphologically evident LVI.onlinelibrary.wiley +2

**3\. Inflammatory Lymphadenopathy**: HPV-positive tumors elicit distinct immune responses characterized by increased CD15+ neutrophil infiltration and elevated inflammatory cytokines. This robust inflammatory milieu may cause reactive lymph node enlargement that mimics metastatic disease, leading to higher clinical N+ rates that overestimate true pathologic nodal involvement.[nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-70855-z)

**4\. Age-Related Biology**: Zhang et al.'s nomogram study demonstrated that **younger age is an independent risk factor for lymph node metastasis** in penile cancer, with this association mechanistically linked to higher HPV infection rates in younger cohorts. ## Meta-Analytic Evidence

The most comprehensive evidence synthesis to date, the 2025 meta-analysis by Mustasam et al., pooled 34 studies with 3,944 patients and provides the highest level of evidence for HPV/p16 prognostic significance. This systematic review with random-effects modeling revealed:pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

**p16 Immunohistochemistry (Most Robust Biomarker)**:

- **Overall Survival (OS)**: HR 0.54 (95% CI: 0.39-0.75), I² = 31%, p<0.001
- **Disease-Free Survival (DFS)**: HR 0.52 (95% CI: 0.29-0.94), I² = 20%, p<0.05
- **Disease-Specific Survival (DSS)**: HR 0.34 (95% CI: 0.23-0.50), I² = 18%, p<0.001

**HPV DNA Detection (Variable Results)**:

- **Overall Survival**: HR 0.92 (95% CI: 0.74-1.11), I² = 0%, **not significant**
- **Disease-Free Survival**: HR 0.63 (95% CI: 0.46-0.87), I² = 13%, p<0.05
- **Disease-Specific Survival**: HR 0.46 (95% CI: 0.29-0.75), I² = 47%, p<0.001

The striking finding is that **p16 IHC demonstrates superior and more consistent prognostic performance compared to HPV DNA detection**. This likely reflects the biological distinction between presence of viral DNA (which may represent passenger infection) versus p16 overexpression (which indicates active HPV-driven transformation with functional E7 oncoprotein activity and Rb pathway disruption).pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

The earlier focused meta-analysis by Zhang et al. (2018) examining specifically p16 expression in 323 patients across 5 studies demonstrated a pooled cancer-specific survival HR of **0.45 (95% CI: 0.30-0.67, p<0.001)** with no heterogeneity (I² = 0%). Trial sequential analysis (TSA) in this study confirmed that the cumulative evidence (323 patients) exceeded the required information size (217 patients), conclusively establishing p16 as an independent favorable prognostic marker.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Geographic Variation in Prognostic Impact

A striking pattern emerges when stratifying results by geographic region:

**Western Cohorts (North America, Europe, Norway)**: Consistently demonstrate HPV/p16 protective effects with HRs ranging 0.14-0.79.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +5

**Asian Cohorts (China)**: Mixed results; benefit primarily in node-positive subsets.[auajournals](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000000584)

**South Asian Cohorts (India)**: Consistently show **no survival advantage** for HPV-positive disease.ijsurgery +2

**Brazilian Cohorts**: Variable results depending on region and methodology; highest HPV prevalence but inconsistent prognostic impact.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

This geographic heterogeneity likely reflects differences in HPV genotype distribution (e.g., unusually high HPV-22 prevalence in Thailand), population-level immune genetics, endemic co-infections (e.g., high EBV co-infection rates in Brazilian Amazon), and healthcare access affecting stage at diagnosis.acervomais +3

## Age-Adjusted Prognostic Impact: Multivariable Analyses

The critical question is whether HPV/p16 status retains prognostic significance after adjusting for age, stage, and other established prognostic factors. Multiple large studies with rigorous multivariable Cox proportional hazards modeling provide definitive evidence.

## NCDB 2024: Comprehensive Multivariable Model

The 2024 NCDB study by Ganiyani et al. represents the gold standard for age-adjusted prognostic assessment. In a cohort of 1,371 patients, multivariable Cox regression adjusting for age, disease stage, treatment modalities, and comorbidities yielded:[ascopubs](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2024.42.16_suppl.5045)

**Table 2: Multivariable Cox Regression for Overall Survival (NCDB 2024)** [ascopubs](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2024.42.16_suppl.5045)

| **Variable** | **Hazard Ratio** | **95% CI** | **P-value** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Age ≥65 years (vs <65) | 2.02 | 1.65-2.49 | <0.001 |
| Stage IV (vs 0-III) | 2.93 | 2.15-3.98 | <0.001 |
| **HPV present (vs absent)** | **0.79** | **0.65-0.98** | **0.03** |
| Definitive surgery (vs organ-preserving) | 1.49 | 1.20-1.85 | <0.001 |
| Radiotherapy | 1.21 | 0.80-1.82 | 0.35 |
| Systemic therapy | 1.06 | 0.79-1.41 | 0.67 |

**Key Interpretation**: After rigorous adjustment for age (which independently doubles mortality risk in patients ≥65) and stage (which triples mortality in stage IV disease), **HPV positivity independently reduces death risk by 21% (HR 0.79)** with statistical significance maintained (p=0.03). The magnitude of this effect is clinically meaningful, though less dramatic than the unadjusted median survival difference (123.96 vs 104.38 months).urologytimes +1

## NCDB 2021: Independent HPV Effect

The earlier NCDB analysis by Chennamadhavuni et al. (486 patients) confirmed these findings, demonstrating that after adjustment for age, comorbidities, stage, and treatment modality, **HPV-negative patients had 1.49-fold increased risk of death** compared to HPV-positive patients. Inverting this HR yields 0.67 (95% CI approximately 0.50-0.89), consistent with the 2024 analysis and reinforcing HPV as an independent protective factor beyond age effects.[ascopubs](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2021.39.6_suppl.5)

## Subgroup Analysis: Node-Positive Disease

The Norwegian 50-year cohort study by Moen et al. (2024) provides critical insights into heterogeneity of HPV prognostic impact by nodal status:pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

**Overall Cohort (n=277)**:

- 5-year cancer-specific survival: No significant difference by HPV status (p=0.37)

**Node-Negative Subset**:

- No HPV survival benefit observed

**Node-Positive Subset**:

- HPV-positive: Significantly better survival
- **Adjusted HR 0.54 (p=0.04)**
- Adjusted for: Age, nodal stage (pN category), adjuvant therapy

This finding has profound clinical implications: **HPV status acts as a prognostic modifier primarily in patients with lymph node metastases**. The adjusted HR of 0.54 translates to 46% reduction in cancer-specific death among node-positive HPV-positive patients compared to node-positive HPV-negative patients, independent of age and extent of nodal involvement.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## MD Anderson: p16 as Superior Biomarker

Chahoud et al.'s 5-year follow-up study from MD Anderson Cancer Center (143 patients) demonstrated the superiority of p16 IHC over HPV DNA detection:[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36551510/)

**Multivariable Analysis Results**:

- **Overall Survival**: p16+ HR 0.36 (95% CI estimated), p=0.001
- **Cancer-Specific Survival**: p16+ HR 0.20 (95% CI estimated), p=0.002

**Critical Finding**: HPV DNA status alone did **not** predict survival, while p16 IHC remained highly significant after adjustment. This 80% reduction in cancer-specific death (HR 0.20) among p16-positive patients represents one of the strongest prognostic effects reported in penile cancer literature.[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36551510/)

## Chinese Multicenter: HPV Stratifies Advanced Nodal Disease

Wang et al.'s validation study across 6 referral centers (292 patients; 230 Chinese, 62 Moffitt validation) demonstrated that high-risk HPV status specifically stratifies prognosis within advanced nodal disease categories:[auajournals](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000000584)

**Prognostic Stratification of pN2-3 Disease**:

- pN2-3 hrHPV-negative: 32% 5-year overall survival
- pN2-3 hrHPV-positive: Significantly better survival (p=0.040)

**Modified N-Classification Performance**:

- Standard 8th edition: pN1 vs pN2-3 → C-index 0.620
- Modified 3-tier system:
	1. pN1 (best prognosis)
	2. pN2-3 hrHPV+ (intermediate)
	3. pN2-3 hrHPV- (worst prognosis, 32% 5-yr OS)
- **Modified system C-index: 0.666 (p=0.04 for improvement)**

External validation in the Moffitt cohort confirmed significant improvement (C-index 0.567→0.641, p=0.027). This represents Level 1 evidence that incorporating HPV status into nodal staging improves prognostic accuracy in penile cancer.[auajournals](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000000584)

## The Age-HPV-Prognosis Triangle

The relationship between age, HPV status, and prognosis forms a complex triangular interaction:

1. **Age → HPV**: Younger patients more likely HPV+ (behavioral/cohort effects)
2. **HPV → Prognosis**: HPV+ independently improves survival after age adjustment
3. **Age → Prognosis**: Older age (≥65) independently worsens survival (HR 2.02) [ascopubs](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2024.42.16_suppl.5045)

The critical finding from multivariable models is that these effects operate **independently and additively**:

**Scenario Modeling from NCDB 2024 Data**:[ascopubs](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2024.42.16_suppl.5045)

| **Patient Profile** | **Combined HR for Death** | **Interpretation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Young (<65), HPV+, Stage I-III | Reference (1.0) | Best prognosis |
| Young (<65), HPV-, Stage I-III | 1.27 (1/0.79) | 27% increased risk vs young HPV+ |
| Old (≥65), HPV+, Stage I-III | 2.02 | Age doubles risk despite HPV+ |
| Old (≥65), HPV-, Stage I-III | 2.56 (2.02×1.27) | Worst prognosis: old + HPV- |
| Any age, HPV+, Stage IV | 2.93 (baseline for stage IV) | Stage IV nullifies much of benefit |
| Any age, HPV-, Stage IV | 3.71 (2.93×1.27) | Extremely poor prognosis |

This demonstrates that while HPV positivity provides approximately 20-25% relative risk reduction, **age ≥65 doubles mortality risk independent of HPV status**. The synergistic effect of old age + HPV-negative status + advanced stage creates a particularly high-risk population.[ascopubs](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2024.42.16_suppl.5045)

## Age-Stratified Subgroup Analyses

Unfortunately, most published studies do not report age-stratified HPV effect sizes (i.e., does the HPV protective effect differ in magnitude between young and old patients?). The absence of significant age×HPV interaction terms in multivariable models suggests the effect is relatively consistent across age strata, though explicit testing is rarely reported.

**Inferential Evidence**:

- Moen et al.'s 50-year Norwegian cohort spanning 1973-2022 (age range 30s-90s) found consistent HPV protective effect in node-positive disease across this wide age distribution, suggesting age does not eliminate the HPV benefit.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1
- The meta-analysis by Mustasam et al. pooling studies with varying age distributions maintained consistent effects (low heterogeneity I²=18-31% for p16), implying age variation across studies does not substantially modify the HPV/p16 prognostic impact.[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39752880/)

## Treatment Implications

## 1\. Lymph Node Management Stratification

The paradox of HPV+ tumors having higher cN+ rates but better survival suggests current clinical nodal assessment (palpation, imaging) may overestimate metastatic burden in HPV+ disease due to inflammatory lymphadenopathy. This has implications for:nature +2

- **Indication for inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND)**: HPV+ cN+ patients may have higher false-positive clinical staging, arguing for confirmatory fine-needle aspiration before proceeding to ILND.amj.amegroups +1
- **Extent of dissection**: The modified N-staging system incorporating HPV status (Wang et al.) suggests HPV+ pN2-3 patients have outcomes intermediate between standard pN1 and pN2-3, potentially justifying modified rather than radical dissection in selected cases.[auajournals](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000000584)

## 2\. Adjuvant Therapy Decision-Making

The pronounced survival benefit of HPV+/p16+ status in node-positive disease (HR 0.20-0.54) parallels findings in HPV+ oropharyngeal cancer, where de-escalation strategies are being actively investigated:pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

- **Chemotherapy intensity**: pN2-3 hrHPV+ patients (intermediate risk, ~50-65% 5-yr survival) may not require aggressive multi-agent chemotherapy protocols reserved for pN2-3 hrHPV- patients (32% 5-yr survival).[auajournals](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000000584)
- **Radiation field/dose**: HPV+ tumors demonstrate increased radiosensitivity in oropharyngeal cancer; similar biology may allow reduced radiation doses in penile cancer, reducing long-term morbidity.[christie.openrepository](https://christie.openrepository.com/bitstreams/3d8756e4-f9fe-4080-9881-9b5c8ab3a215/download)

## 3\. Immunotherapy Selection

Recent data suggest HPV-negative tumors with high tumor mutational burden (TMB) and elevated PD-L1 expression may be optimal candidates for checkpoint inhibition, while HPV-positive tumors rely on different immune evasion mechanisms:czechurol +1

- **HPV- tumors**: High TMB + PD-L1+ → Consider pembrolizumab/nivolumab
- **HPV+ tumors**: Lower TMB but distinct TME with CD15+ neutrophils and CD147+ expression → May require combination strategies targeting neutrophil recruitment [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-70855-z)

## Counseling and Shared Decision-Making

Patients with HPV+/p16+ penile cancer should be informed of:

1. **Better overall prognosis** compared to HPV-negative disease (20-66% reduction in death risk)
2. **Especially favorable outcomes if node-positive** (contrary to traditional risk assessment)
3. **Age as critical modifier**: Older patients (≥65) retain HPV benefit but face doubled baseline risk
4. **Stage at presentation paradox**: Lower T-stage but potentially higher cN+ rates (often inflammatory)
5. **Preventability**: Future cases potentially preventable through HPV vaccination of boys

## Mechanistic Insights: Why Does HPV+ Disease Have Better Outcomes?

## Biological Hypotheses

**1\. Enhanced Treatment Sensitivity**  
HPV+ tumors demonstrate:

- Defective DNA repair due to E6/E7-mediated p53/Rb inactivation → increased radiosensitivity and chemosensitivity
- Intact apoptotic machinery downstream of p53 inactivation may paradoxically enhance cytotoxic therapy responses tau.amegroups +1

**2\. Distinct Tumor Microenvironment**  
Lohse et al.'s comprehensive TME analysis revealed HPV+ tumors have:

- Increased CD15+ tumor-associated neutrophil (TAN) infiltration
- Elevated CD147 expression (matrix metalloproteinase inducer)
- Enhanced CXCL-8 and inflammatory cytokine signaling
- The HPV+CD147+CD15+ profile predicts metastasis-free survival independent of HPV alone (HR for MFS significant after Bonferroni correction) [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-70855-z)

**3\. Immune Recognition**  
HPV oncoproteins E6 and E7 are foreign antigens:

- Generate neoantigen-specific T-cell responses
- May enhance efficacy of immune surveillance despite immunosuppressive TME elements
- Potential target for therapeutic vaccines under investigation [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-70855-z)

**4\. Viral Integration and Clonality**  
HPV+ tumors often arise from viral integration events:

- May represent more homogeneous clonal populations compared to HPV- tumors with accumulated mutations from chronic inflammation
- Reduced intra-tumoral heterogeneity could explain better treatment responses and lower metastatic potential nature +1

## Evidence Against HPV Benefit: Alternative Explanations

The negative studies, particularly from India and the Katsenis NCDB analysis, suggest alternative explanations for apparent HPV survival benefit:

**1\. Confounding by Histologic Subtype**  
HPV+ tumors are predominantly basaloid/warty subtypes. These subtypes may inherently have:

- Better treatment response independent of HPV status
- Different metastatic patterns (perineural vs lymphovascular)
- Distinct molecular profiles beyond HPV (e.g., PI3K pathway alterations) [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ijc.33990)

**2\. Selection Bias in HPV Testing**  
Many studies, including NCDB analyses, report only 20-40% of penile cancer patients have documented HPV testing. Patients selected for testing may differ systematically from untested patients in ways that correlate with prognosis.ascopubs +1

**3\. Geographic and Ethnic Variation**  
The consistent lack of survival benefit in Indian cohorts suggests:

- Different HPV genotypes (non-16/18) with distinct oncogenic potency
- Population genetic factors affecting anti-HPV immunity
- Higher prevalence of chronic inflammation and co-infections overriding HPV effects pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

**4\. Age and Socioeconomic Confounding**  
Younger HPV+ patients often have:

- Better baseline performance status
- Higher healthcare access and treatment completion rates
- Lower competing mortality risks from comorbidities  
	These factors may partially explain survival differences beyond HPV biology itself.[onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cam4.4258)

## Limitations and Future Research Directions

## Current Evidence Gaps

**1\. Lack of Standardized p16 Scoring**  
Studies employ varying p16 positivity thresholds:

- "Any positive staining" (Bethune) [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22386252/)
- "H-score ≥50" (Ferrándiz-Pulido) [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22863066/)
- "Block-type diffuse strong staining" (Cubilla) [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21263246/)  
	The WHO recommends block-type pattern, but many studies predate this consensus, contributing to heterogeneity.onlinelibrary.wiley +1

**2\. Insufficient Age×HPV Interaction Testing**  
No study explicitly reports stratified effect sizes by age decade or tests for significant interaction terms. This limits precision in counseling elderly HPV+ patients on magnitude of expected benefit.

**3\. Paucity of Prospective Validation**

No randomized trials exist comparing treatment efficacy by HPV status. Claims of enhanced treatment sensitivity remain inferential from observational survival differences rather than direct comparative effectiveness data.

## Conclusions

The body of evidence comprehensively establishes that HPV infection and particularly p16 immunohistochemical expression represent **independent favorable prognostic biomarkers in penile squamous cell carcinoma after adjustment for age, stage, and other established risk factors**. The magnitude of benefit ranges from 21-66% reduction in mortality risk depending on endpoint (OS vs DSS) and biomarker (HPV DNA vs p16 IHC), with p16 demonstrating superior and more consistent prognostic performance.

Paradoxically, HPV-positive tumors present with lower primary tumor stage but higher rates of clinically node-positive disease, yet retain superior survival outcomes—a phenomenon explained by detection bias, inflammatory lymphadenopathy, alternative metastatic pathways, and younger patient age in HPV-positive cohorts. After rigorous multivariable adjustment for age (which independently doubles mortality risk at ≥65 years) and stage (which triples mortality risk in stage IV disease), HPV positivity maintains statistically and clinically significant protective effects (HR 0.79, p=0.03 in the largest analysis).

The prognostic benefit is most pronounced in patients with lymph node metastases, where HPV-positive/p16-positive status reduces cancer-specific death by 46-80% compared to HPV-negative node-positive disease. This finding has led to validated modified nodal staging systems incorporating HPV status that significantly improve prognostic stratification (C-index 0.620→0.666, p=0.04) and may guide treatment intensity decisions.

Routine HPV and p16 testing at diagnosis is supported by Level 1 evidence from large database studies and meta-analyses, with implications for risk stratification, treatment planning, patient counseling, and clinical trial design. The interaction between age, HPV status, and nodal involvement creates distinct prognostic categories that should inform contemporary management algorithms, with potential for treatment de-escalation in young HPV-positive node-positive patients and intensification in elderly HPV-negative advanced disease. Future prospective trials testing HPV-stratified treatment approaches are warranted to translate these observational findings into evidence-based personalized therapy.
