## Giant Cell Glioblastoma: Biology and Prognosis

## Overview and Classification

Giant cell glioblastoma (gcGBM) is a rare histological variant of IDH-wildtype glioblastoma, accounting for approximately 1% of all GBM cases. First described by Schmincke in the early twentieth century and termed "giant-celled glioma" by Meyer in 1913, this entity is now recognized as a morphologically distinct subtype of grade 4 diffuse glioma. Under the WHO 2016 classification, gcGBM was listed alongside gliosarcoma and epithelioid GBM as one of three morphological variants of IDH-wildtype GBM. In the 2021 WHO CNS5 classification, the term "glioblastoma" is now strictly reserved for IDH-wildtype diffuse astrocytic gliomas (grade 4), and while gcGBM is no longer formally separated as a named subtype, it remains a recognized histological pattern within the GBM category.ncbi.nlm.nih +4

## Histopathology

gcGBM is defined by a predominance of bizarre, multinucleated giant cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Key histopathological features include:pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2

- **Giant cells**: Large (average diameter >50 μm), often containing >5 nuclei, frequently lobulated or bizarre in morphology. A threshold of >30% multinucleated giant cells has been proposed to define "true" gcGBM, correlating with a distinct molecular profile.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih
- **Necrosis**: Present in ~88% of cases (geographic > palisading), consistent with GBM grade.
- **Microvascular proliferation**: Present but often less prominent than in conventional GBM; glomeruloid structures are occasionally seen.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih
- **Reticulin network**: An occasionally abundant reticulin fibre network and focal sarcomatous areas have been described.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1
- **Well-circumscribed borders**: gcGBM shows minimal invasion (<2 mm) compared with classic GBM, contributing to higher rates of gross total resection.ncbi.nlm.nih +1
- **Low OLIG2 expression**: Significantly lower than conventional GBM (7% vs. 77%), suggesting reduced stemness characteristics.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih

## TP53 as a Driver Event

The near-universal TP53 alteration in gcGBM is the defining molecular hallmark. TP53 mutation is considered an early, possibly initiating, event in gcGBM gliomagenesis, promoting genomic instability through failure of centrosome duplication control and cell-cycle arrest. This contrasts sharply with classic IDH-wildtype GBM, where the EGFR–CDKN2A–TERT axis dominates.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Telomere Maintenance: ATRX and the ALT Pathway

In gcGBM, particularly tumours with >30% giant cells, ATRX alterations are common (~53%) and mutually exclusive with TERT promoter mutations. ATRX loss is associated with the alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) mechanism—a homologous recombination-based process more typical of IDH-mutant gliomas. This finding places gcGBM in an intermediate or "hybrid" position between primary (IDH-wildtype) and secondary (IDH-mutant) glioblastomas.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Aurora B Kinase and the Giant Cell Phenotype

Temme et al. (2010) demonstrated that Aurora B kinase (AURKB) mRNA and protein levels are significantly elevated in gcGBM compared to classic GBM. No AURKB gene mutations were identified; rather, the overexpression is transcriptional. Critically, ectopic overexpression of Aurora B induced multinucleation *only* in TP53-mutant glioma cells (U373-MG), not in TP53-wildtype cells (U87-MG), and silencing p53 in wildtype cells increased the fraction of multinucleated cells, an effect further augmented by Aurora B overexpression. This indicates that the giant cell phenotype arises from a synergistic interaction between loss of p53 function and dysregulated Aurora B levels, leading to failed cytokinesis and polyploidization.academic.oup

Recent work has extended this concept, demonstrating that multinucleated giant cells (MNGCs) in GBM divide by **"neosis"** —an atypical, non-mitotic cell division involving cytoplasmic pinching without DNA condensation, spindle poles, or acto-myosin contractile rings. Aurora kinases A and B are specifically upregulated in dividing MNGCs, and pharmacological or genetic inhibition of aurora kinases abrogates neosis and prevents GBM recurrence in orthotopic mouse models.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih

## DNA Damage Vulnerability

Ogawa et al. (2019) showed that gcGBM cells harbour significantly more DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) than conventional "monotonous small" GBM, with 76% of gcGBM cells positive for γH2AX compared to 15% in conventional GBM. Nearly all large cells (93%) demonstrated severe DSBs. The authors proposed that gcGBM cells have an *innate vulnerability to DNA damage*: the absence of TERT promoter mutations (which facilitate DNA DSB repair and escape from senescence) and low OLIG2 expression (reflecting reduced stemness) result in proliferating cells that readily accumulate DSBs, undergo mitotic slippage, and become polyploid—hence the "giant cell" morphology. Importantly, many Ki67-positive large cells are simultaneously γH2AX-positive, meaning Ki67 positivity in gcGBM does not necessarily reflect true proliferative capacity.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih

## Tumour Mutation Load and Immunotherapy Potential

Most gcGBMs have a **low tumour mutation load** (TML; mean ~4.2 mutations/Mb). However, exceptional cases with very high TML have been identified, driven by POLE hotspot mutations (e.g., p.P286R) or MSH2 mutations. These ultra-mutated cases show loss of mismatch repair (MMR) protein expression and may represent candidates for immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy. This has potential clinical significance, as gcGBMs with high TML and POLE mutations have also been identified in the TCGA dataset, and a clinical response to PD-1 blockade has been reported in a GBM patient with POLE germline mutation.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih

## Epidemiology and Clinical Features

Compared with conventional GBM, gcGBM shows a distinctive clinical profile:ncbi.nlm.nih +1

- **Younger age at diagnosis**: Median 51 years (gcGBM) vs. 62 years (GBM); 27.5% present before age 40.
- **Gender**: ~1.5:1 male predominance, similar to GBM.
- **Location**: Most commonly temporal (46%) and frontal (31%) lobes; no significant temporal predilection over GBM.
- **Size**: Median ~4.2 cm, similar to GBM.
- **Resectability**: More circumscribed borders facilitate higher rates of complete resection (51% total lobectomy/GTR vs. 35% for GBM).ncbi.nlm.nih

## Prognostic Factors

In the SEER analysis (n=171 gcGBM), multivariate Cox modelling demonstrated that gcGBM histology independently conferred a survival advantage over GBM (HR = 0.76; 95% CI 0.59–0.97), even after adjustment for age, tumour size, extent of resection, and radiotherapy. This advantage persisted when patients <40 years were excluded (HR = 0.71; 95% CI 0.53–0.95), mitigating concerns about misclassification with pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma (PXA). Significant prognostic factors include:ncbi.nlm.nih

- **Age**: Strongly adverse for ≥60 years (HR 3.68 vs. <40 years).ncbi.nlm.nih
- **Extent of resection**: Cancer-directed surgery vs. biopsy alone (HR 0.30).ncbi.nlm.nih
- **Adjuvant radiotherapy**: No RT associated with HR 4.51.ncbi.nlm.nih
- **Adjuvant temozolomide + RT**: Associated with significantly longer PFS (32.9 vs. 13.1 months).augusta.elsevierpure
- **Giant cell content >30%**: Trend toward better OS (25.3 vs. 14.3 months, p = 0.136).pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih
- **TP53 mutation**: gcGBMs with TP53 mutation had significantly better OS than TP53-wildtype GBMs (combined series median 21.3 months; log-rank p = 0.002).pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih
- **Long-term survival**: 5-year survival is achievable in >10% of gcGBM patients, compared to ~3% for conventional GBM.ncbi.nlm.nih

## Biological Explanations for Improved Prognosis

Several interrelated mechanisms likely underlie the relatively favourable prognosis:

1. **Circumscribed growth**: The well-demarcated borders with minimal invasion facilitate more complete resection.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1
2. **DNA damage vulnerability**: The inherent susceptibility to DSBs and mitotic slippage means many tumour cells enter a senescence-like state rather than continuing aggressive proliferation.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih
3. **Reduced stemness**: Low OLIG2 expression and absence of TERT promoter mutations suggest fewer glioma stem-like cells capable of driving recurrence.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih
4. **Polyploidization as a "dead end"**: Giant cells accumulate severe DNA damage and undergo polyploidization/senescence, effectively removing them from the proliferative pool despite Ki67 positivity.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih

## Differential Diagnosis

The main differential diagnostic consideration is **pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma (PXA)**, which shares pleomorphic giant cells but is BRAF V600E-mutant (~60–70%), typically occurs in younger patients, has xanthomatous change and eosinophilic granular bodies, and carries a WHO grade 2–3 designation. Absence of BRAF V600E mutation and the presence of TP53 mutation help distinguish gcGBM from PXA. MGMT promoter methylation status can also assist in this distinction.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +1

## Summary

gcGBM is a rare but clinically significant variant of IDH-wildtype glioblastoma defined by multinucleated giant cells, near-universal TP53 mutations, unusually frequent ATRX loss (especially in high giant-cell content tumours), low EGFR amplification and CDKN2A deletion, and an innate vulnerability to DNA double-strand breaks. The synergistic interaction between p53 loss and Aurora B kinase overexpression drives the multinucleated phenotype through failed cytokinesis. Although the overall prognosis remains poor, gcGBM patients have a significantly better median survival and higher rates of long-term survival compared with conventional GBM, supporting aggressive surgical and adjuvant therapy. Rare ultra-mutated cases harbouring POLE mutations may represent candidates for immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.

## Practical Takeaways for Giant Cell GBM

- **Best‑supported regimen:** Maximal safe resection followed by RT+temozolomide should be considered standard for gcGBM, mirroring conventional GBM, with evidence for a substantial PFS benefit and overall better prognosis than classic GBM.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih +1
- **Bevacizumab:** May be considered for recurrent disease or suspected radiation necrosis as in other GBM subtypes; current gcGBM‑specific evidence is limited to isolated case experience.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih +2
- **Evidence gaps:** No randomized or large comparative datasets exist specifically for gcGBM regarding RT fractionation, TMZ intensity/duration, or bevacizumab; all practice is extrapolated from general GBM with supportive but underpowered gcGBM series.
