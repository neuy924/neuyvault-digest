# Apalutamide vs Enzalutamide vs Darolutamide in Prostate Cancer: A Multi-Angle Clinical Debate

## Executive Summary

Apalutamide (Erleada; 240 mg/day), enzalutamide (Xtandi; 160 mg/day), and darolutamide (Nubeqa; 600 mg BID) are second-generation androgen receptor inhibitors (ARIs) that have transformed prostate cancer management across disease spectrums. No head-to-head Phase III randomised controlled trial has directly compared all three agents, leaving clinical choice dependent on patient phenotype, disease setting, comorbidity, and tolerability priorities. Across indirect comparisons and real-world data, all three show similar magnitude of efficacy in nmCRPC, whereas divergence in mCSPC data, toxicity profiles, drug-drug interactions (DDIs), and approved indications increasingly inform differentiated selection.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

***

## 1. Regulatory Landscape and Approved Indications

Understanding the approved label for each agent is critical because off-label use carries regulatory, reimbursement, and liability implications.

| Drug | nmCRPC | mCSPC (doublet + ADT) | mCSPC (triplet + ADT + docetaxel) | mCRPC |
|------|--------|-----------------------|-----------------------------------|-------|
| Apalutamide | ✓ (SPARTAN) | ✓ (TITAN) | — | ✗ |
| Enzalutamide | ✓ (PROSPER) | ✓ (ARCHES/ENZAMET) | — | ✓ (PREVAIL/AFFIRM) |
| Darolutamide | ✓ (ARAMIS) | ✓ (ARANOTE; FDA June 2025)[^5][^6] | ✓ (ARASENS) | ✗ |

**Enzalutamide holds the broadest label**, being the only agent FDA-approved across nmCRPC, mCSPC, and mCRPC. Darolutamide uniquely holds an indication for triplet therapy with docetaxel + ADT (ARASENS), and received standalone mCSPC approval (without docetaxel) in June 2025 based on ARANOTE. Apalutamide lacks an mCRPC label, which limits its use in that sequencing context.[^7][^8][^9][^5][^6][^10]

## 2. Pivotal Trial Efficacy Data

### 2.1 Non-Metastatic CRPC (nmCRPC)

The three cornerstone trials — ARAMIS, SPARTAN, and PROSPER — compared each agent to placebo in men with nmCRPC and PSA doubling time ≤10 months.[^1]

| Trial | Drug | MFS HR | Median MFS (months) | OS HR | Median OS (months) |
|-------|------|--------|---------------------|-------|--------------------|
| SPARTAN | Apalutamide | 0.28 | 40.5 vs 16.2 | 0.78 | 73.9 vs 59.9 |
| PROSPER | Enzalutamide | 0.29 | 36.6 vs 14.7 | 0.73 | 67.0 vs 56.3 |
| ARAMIS | Darolutamide | 0.41 | 40.4 vs 18.4 | 0.69 | 83% vs 77% (3-yr OS) |

All three significantly improved OS over placebo. In network meta-analyses, OS efficacy was highest for darolutamide (HR 0.69 for death, p=0.003), though statistical methodology and population heterogeneity across trials limit these conclusions. Enzalutamide and apalutamide showed essentially equivalent MFS (OR 1.02; 95% CI 0.72–1.45 for Enza vs Apa). In the PSA-DT ≤6 months high-risk subgroup, one NMA suggested the highest nmCRPC efficacy for enzalutamide.[^11][^12][^2][^3][^1]

### 2.2 Metastatic Castration-Sensitive Prostate Cancer (mCSPC)

This is the setting with the most controversy, emerging real-world comparative data, and meaningful differentiation.

| Trial | Drug | Regimen | rPFS HR | OS HR | 4-yr OS rate |
|-------|------|---------|---------|-------|--------------|
| TITAN | Apalutamide | APA + ADT | 0.48 | 0.65 | 65% vs 52% |
| ARCHES | Enzalutamide | ENZA + ADT | 0.39 | 0.66 | ~66% vs 53% at 5yr |
| ARASENS | Darolutamide | DARO + DOCE + ADT | — | 0.68 vs DOCE+ADT | — |
| ARANOTE | Darolutamide | DARO + ADT | 0.54 | 0.81 (not yet sig.) | — |

Apalutamide (TITAN) and enzalutamide (ARCHES) have closely matched OS hazard ratios (0.65 vs 0.66). ARANOTE demonstrated significant rPFS improvement with darolutamide + ADT doublet (HR 0.54; p<0.0001), including in both high- and low-volume subgroups; the OS data (HR 0.81) are immature given the trial was not powered for OS.[^13][^14][^15][^16][^17]

Darolutamide's ARASENS triplet data (OS HR 0.68 vs docetaxel + ADT) supports its use in high-volume mCSPC alongside chemotherapy. A MAIC comparing ARCHES vs ARANOTE doublets suggested a greater rPFS benefit for enzalutamide over darolutamide in doublet therapy for mHSPC, though matching-adjusted indirect comparisons carry inherent limitations.[^18][^19][^8][^20]

### 2.3 Metastatic CRPC (mCRPC)

Only enzalutamide is approved here. PREVAIL demonstrated OS HR 0.71 and rPFS HR 0.19 in chemotherapy-naïve mCRPC. AFFIRM established benefit in post-docetaxel mCRPC. Neither apalutamide nor darolutamide has regulatory approval in this setting.[^9][^21][^22]

## 3. Real-World Comparative Efficacy Debate

### 3.1 Apalutamide vs Enzalutamide in mCSPC

Multiple retrospective analyses have examined this comparison. A large J&J-sponsored real-world study (n=3,719; 2024–2025 data) using IPTW methodology found apalutamide associated with a statistically significant 23% reduction in all-cause mortality vs enzalutamide at 24 months in mCSPC. A separate analysis from J&J (February 2026) reported a 51% reduction in the risk of death (HR 0.49; 95% CI 0.30–0.83) for apalutamide vs darolutamide in mCSPC.[^23][^24][^25][^26]

**Critical caveat**: Both analyses were sponsored by Johnson & Johnson (apalutamide's manufacturer), and neither is a prospective randomised trial. Differences in patient selection, prior treatment, co-administered therapies, and unmeasured confounding substantially limit causal inference. A published letter to the editor in *Advances in Therapy* (2025) highlighted that since enzalutamide — unlike apalutamide — is approved for mCRPC, patients initiating enzalutamide for mCSPC may inherently represent a different clinical profile, confounding survival comparisons.[^7]

### 3.2 Darolutamide vs Enzalutamide/Apalutamide in nmCRPC

The DEAR study (2023 GU Symposium) and a JAMA Network Open cohort study (2024) both found that darolutamide patients had significantly lower rates of treatment discontinuation and progression to mCRPC compared with enzalutamide and apalutamide in real-world nmCRPC. In the DEAR trial, discontinuation/progression occurred in 37% with darolutamide vs 51% with both enzalutamide and apalutamide. Japanese real-world data showed higher label-dose adherence for darolutamide (78%) vs enzalutamide (58%) vs apalutamide (62%), with lower progression rates.[^27][^28][^29][^30]

**Mechanistic rationale**: The superior real-world adherence for darolutamide likely reflects its more favourable tolerability profile (see Section 4), which translates into fewer treatment disruptions rather than true pharmacological superiority per se.[^31][^28]

***

## 4. Safety and Toxicity: A Multi-Dimensional Analysis

### 4.1 Central Nervous System Effects — Darolutamide's Clearest Advantage

The most consistently reproduced differentiator among the three agents is CNS toxicity, driven by differences in blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration.[^32][^33]

**BBB penetration hierarchy (brain/blood ratio in rat QWBA studies)**:
- Enzalutamide: ~0.765 (high)
- Apalutamide: ~2-fold lower than enzalutamide (moderate)
- Darolutamide: ~0.074 — approximately **10-fold lower than enzalutamide** and **26–46-fold lower than apalutamide/enzalutamide** at brain level[^33][^34][^35]

This pharmacokinetic distinction directly manifests in clinical outcomes:[^36][^32]
- **Seizures**: Enzalutamide and apalutamide carry a black-box/contraindication concern; darolutamide allowed enrolment of patients with prior seizure history in ARAMIS — none experienced a seizure on darolutamide[^32]
- **Falls**: Darolutamide vs placebo: 4.2% vs 4.7% (essentially equivalent); significantly higher falls with enzalutamide[^36]
- **Cognitive impairment**: Dizziness (4.5% vs 4.0% placebo), cognitive disorder (0.4% vs 0.2% placebo) with darolutamide — near-identical to placebo[^36]
- **Fatigue**: Enzalutamide worst (34–36%); apalutamide intermediate; darolutamide reported fatigue at 5.6% (even lower than placebo at 8.1% in ARANOTE)[^14][^36]

A 2023 JAMA Oncology systematic review and meta-analysis confirmed an increased risk of cognitive toxic effects, fatigue, and falls with second-generation antiandrogens as a class, highlighting that effect was strongest with enzalutamide.[^37]

The ODENZA crossover trial (n=249 mCRPC patients, median age 72) directly compared darolutamide and enzalutamide: 48.5% of patients preferred darolutamide, 40.0% preferred enzalutamide, and 11.5% had no preference (p=0.92 — no statistically significant difference in preference). Reasons favouring darolutamide included reduced fatigue and improved quality of life; a modest improvement in episodic memory acquisition was noted with darolutamide.[^38][^39][^40]

### 4.2 Skin Toxicity — Apalutamide's Significant Burden

Rash is a distinctive and clinically important adverse event of apalutamide, representing a major differentiator:[^41]

- **Apalutamide**: ~25% overall rash incidence (grade ≥3 in ~5%); relative risk vs placebo 20.8x in SPARTAN[^42][^43][^41]
- **Darolutamide**: ~3% rash; relative risk vs placebo 1.7x in ARAMIS[^44][^41]
- **Enzalutamide**: Intermediate; not the dominant toxicity concern

Apalutamide rash onset occurs at a median of 83 days, resolves in ~78 days with antihistamines/corticosteroids, but recurs in ~50% of re-challenged patients. In the SPARTAN trial, rash led to treatment discontinuation in some cases. Rare serious skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis) have been reported with apalutamide.[^45][^42]

### 4.3 Cardiovascular and Metabolic Toxicity

All three agents — particularly through ADT co-administration — carry cardiovascular risk, but with some agent-specific differences:

- **Apalutamide**: Hypertension in 25% (14% severe grade); grade 3–4 ischemic heart disease 2.6% and ischemic cerebrovascular disorders 1.6% in SPARTAN; arterial thromboembolism in 4%[^46][^42]
- **Enzalutamide**: Major composite cardiovascular adverse event risk HR 1.22 (95% CI 1.01–1.48; p=0.04) compared to non-treated in Medicare data[^47][^48]
- **Darolutamide**: Favourable cardiovascular profile; fatigue incidence even lower than placebo in ARANOTE; limited specific cardiovascular signal in Phase III data[^14]

All three require monitoring and cardiovascular risk factor optimisation (hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia).[^49][^46]

### 4.4 Fracture and Bone Health

Fractures are an important concern in a population on ADT, already at heightened fracture risk:

- **Apalutamide**: Fracture relative risk 4.13x vs placebo in SPARTAN; 9% fracture rate in TITAN; grade 3–4 fractures in 3%[^50][^41]
- **Darolutamide**: Fracture relative risk only 1.045x vs placebo in ARAMIS — markedly lower than apalutamide[^41]
- **Enzalutamide**: Fracture data not systematically reported in PROSPER (fracture rate was not a primary safety endpoint)[^41]

This is particularly relevant in the elderly nmCRPC patient who may already have osteopaenia or osteoporosis from prolonged ADT and must be co-managed with bone-targeted agents.

### 4.5 Summary Toxicity Comparison

| Toxicity Domain | Apalutamide | Enzalutamide | Darolutamide |
|----------------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| CNS/seizure risk | Moderate (BBB penetration) | Highest | Lowest (minimal BBB) |
| Fatigue | Moderate | Highest (34–36%) | Lowest (≤ placebo in ARANOTE) |
| Cognitive effects | Moderate | Highest | Lowest |
| Falls | Elevated | Elevated | Placebo-equivalent |
| Skin rash | Highest (~25%, RR 20x) | Intermediate | Lowest (~3%, RR 1.7x) |
| Fractures | Highest (RR 4.1x) | Not well defined | Near-placebo (RR 1.04x) |
| Hypertension | 25% (14% severe) | Common | Less pronounced |
| Cardiovascular | Ischemic events ~4% | HR 1.22 composite risk | Most favourable profile |

## 5. Drug-Drug Interaction (DDI) Landscape

Prostate cancer predominantly affects men aged ≥65, who typically have multiple comorbidities and take 5–9 concomitant medications.[^51][^52]

**CYP enzyme induction/inhibition profile**:[^53][^54]
- **Apalutamide**: Strong CYP3A4 inducer and CYP2C19 inducer (via N-desmethyl apalutamide); also induces CYP2C9, BCRP, and P-gp — potentially reduces exposure of numerous co-medications
- **Enzalutamide**: Strong CYP3A4 inducer; moderate CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 inducer — similar DDI concern
- **Darolutamide**: Minimal CYP induction; however, **strong BCRP inhibitor** (can increase plasma concentrations of BCRP substrates, e.g., rosuvastatin) — a distinct but more targeted DDI risk[^53]

**Real-world DDI analysis (Optum Clinformatics database, n=1,515 patients)**:[^52]
- 30 potential DDIs (pDDIs) identified per 100 co-medications for both apalutamide and enzalutamide
- Only **2 pDDIs** per 100 co-medications for darolutamide
- 58% of apalutamide patients had ≥1 pDDI; 54% of enzalutamide patients; only **5% of darolutamide patients**
- Four apalutamide pDDIs were rated Category X (contraindicated co-administration)[^52]

This is highly clinically relevant for elderly men on anticoagulants (warfarin is a CYP2C9 substrate; strongly induced by apalutamide and enzalutamide), anticonvulsants, cardiovascular medications, and statins. Darolutamide's minimal CYP induction confers a significant practical advantage in the polypharmacy patient, though its BCRP inhibition requires attention to statin dosing (e.g., rosuvastatin AUC increases).[^51][^53]

## 6. Sequencing, Cross-Resistance, and the Resistance Paradigm

### 6.1 AR-V7 and Class-Level Resistance

AR splice variant 7 (AR-V7) — a truncated AR lacking the ligand-binding domain — is constitutively active and confers primary resistance to all three agents (and abiraterone), as it cannot be targeted by drugs that bind the C-terminal ligand-binding domain. AR-V7 prevalence rises from ~4% at initial mCSPC to >30% in post-treatment mCRPC. Cross-resistance among ARIs is the rule, not the exception.[^55][^56][^57][^58][^59]

### 6.2 Sequential ARI Use

A 2025 multi-institutional retrospective study (n=56 nmCRPC patients) found significant cross-resistance when sequencing novel ARATs:[^60]
- Median treatment duration with second ARAT: 7.0 months vs 14.0 months with first ARAT (p=0.038)
- PSA decline ≥50% in second-line: only 25% vs 73% in first-line (p<0.001)
- No PSA response in second-line: 45% vs 11% in first-line (p<0.001)

Nonetheless, a smaller Japanese retrospective study (n=9) suggested darolutamide may have residual activity after enzalutamide/apalutamide failure, given its structurally distinct scaffold — with 55.5% achieving PSA response ≥50% and a median PFS of 6 months. This remains hypothesis-generating and does not justify routine sequential ARI use.[^61]

**Practical implication**: Using enzalutamide in mCSPC does not preclude subsequent darolutamide use in principle, but cross-resistance limits efficacy. Conversely, choosing apalutamide or darolutamide for nmCRPC/mCSPC preserves enzalutamide as the only approved option for mCRPC, which has clinical relevance in treatment planning.

## 7. Patient Population-Specific Considerations

### 7.1 The Elderly Patient With Polypharmacy

This is arguably the most clinically important selection dimension given that the median age in all three pivotal trials exceeds 70 years. Darolutamide dominates this scenario:[^62][^51]

- Near-placebo CNS adverse event rates — critical for patients at baseline risk of cognitive impairment, delirium, and falls[^62][^32][^36]
- Dramatically fewer DDIs (5% vs 54–58% with enzalutamide/apalutamide)[^52]
- No seizure concern — eligible for patients with seizure disorders[^32]
- Favourable fracture profile[^41]

Real-world data in Japanese elderly nmCRPC patients confirmed consistent efficacy across age groups with darolutamide, with favourable tolerability in those ≥75 years.[^63]

### 7.2 High-Volume Fit mCSPC — The Triplet Scenario

In fit patients with high-volume mCSPC who can tolerate docetaxel, darolutamide + ADT + docetaxel (ARASENS) provides the only prospective Phase III evidence for triplet benefit with an ARI (OS HR 0.68 vs docetaxel + ADT), with ≥75% of control arm patients receiving subsequent ARPIs — meaning the triplet benefit is seen even against an active subsequent therapy backdrop. PEACE-1 establishes the triplet principle with abiraterone, but among oral ARIs, only darolutamide has proven triplet data.[^19][^8][^20]

### 7.3 Patients With Prior Seizure History

Only darolutamide allowed enrolment of patients with prior seizures in its Phase III trial. Enzalutamide and apalutamide are generally contraindicated in patients with history of seizures or predisposing CNS conditions. This makes darolutamide the default choice in patients with epilepsy, CNS tumours, prior stroke, or medications that lower seizure threshold.[^32]

### 7.4 Patients With Significant Dermatological History

Patients with history of severe drug reactions, inflammatory skin conditions, or those for whom quality of life is strongly driven by skin health should preferentially avoid apalutamide, given the 25% rash incidence with 5% grade ≥3, and the rare but documented risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis.[^42][^45]

### 7.5 Patients Who Require mCRPC Treatment After mCSPC Progression

If there is a genuine clinical likelihood of needing approved mCRPC therapy after mCSPC, enzalutamide is the only ARI with this indication. However, sequential ARI use is limited by cross-resistance (discussed in Section 6), and abiraterone or taxane chemotherapy represent more rational second-line options in most scenarios after ARI failure.[^22][^9][^7]

### 7.6 Patients With Cardiovascular Comorbidity

All three agents require cardiovascular monitoring, but darolutamide's safety profile in ARANOTE (adverse events comparable to placebo) is particularly reassuring. Apalutamide's 4% cerebrovascular event rate and 2.6% grade 3–4 ischemic heart disease rate require explicit discussion with patients who have established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. The DDI burden of apalutamide and enzalutamide with anticoagulants (warfarin, rivaroxaban) and antiplatelets is clinically significant in this patient subset.[^46][^53][^52]

## 8. Quality of Life and Patient Preference

Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) was maintained in all three pivotal Phase III trials through the study period, with no statistically significant differences from placebo in the FACT-P total score in TITAN (apalutamide) and ARAMIS (darolutamide). The ODENZA crossover trial provides the most direct patient-preference data, showing no statistically significant preference difference between darolutamide and enzalutamide (48.5% vs 40.0%; p=0.92), though more patients preferred darolutamide due to reduced fatigue and better perceived quality of life. A modest improvement in episodic memory was associated with darolutamide.[^39][^64][^40][^38][^14]

Patient-reported outcomes from real-world settings consistently demonstrate that higher rates of treatment adherence with darolutamide translate to better disease control, reinforcing the concept that tolerability is a surrogate for effectiveness in this context.[^28][^31][^51]

***

## 9. Economic and Access Considerations

In the United States, all three agents are similarly priced at approximately $7,500–$8,500 per month. NICE health technology assessment (HTA) data suggest that with Patient Access Scheme (PAS) discounts, darolutamide + ADT costs are similar to or lower than apalutamide + ADT for mCSPC. Cost-effectiveness analyses in Chinese healthcare systems have modelled all three agents against ADT alone in mHSPC, highlighting that agents with broader doublet/triplet evidence will likely dominate reimbursement decisions. In systems with restricted formularies, enzalutamide's longer market presence and broader label (including mCRPC) may offer formulary advantages, while newer approvals for darolutamide doublet in mCSPC (June 2025) will drive formulary access negotiations.[^65][^66][^67]

## 10. Probabilistic Framework for Clinical Decision-Making

In the absence of head-to-head randomised data, Bayesian and probabilistic reasoning helps navigate uncertainty.

### 10.1 nmCRPC Setting

**Efficacy probability**: Near-equivalent for all three in MFS; darolutamide may have slightly superior OS signal in indirect comparisons (~60–65% probability of being ranked highest in OS per NMA). However, the magnitude of difference is small and within indirect comparison uncertainty.[^2][^3]

**Tolerability probability**: Darolutamide is >70% likely to be the best-tolerated agent based on consistent mechanistic, clinical, and real-world data; darolutamide discontinuation rates are consistently ~14–20 percentage points lower than enzalutamide or apalutamide.[^27][^31][^28]

**Practical recommendation probability**: For a typical nmCRPC patient (elderly, multiple comorbidities), darolutamide is the preferred choice in ~65–70% of clinical scenarios; in high-risk/short PSA-DT patients without CNS concerns, enzalutamide may offer comparable or superior efficacy with some evidence supporting its use in the ≤6-month PSA-DT subgroup.[^3]

### 10.2 mCSPC Doublet Setting

**Efficacy (OS HR approximation)**:
- Apalutamide: HR ~0.65 (TITAN)
- Enzalutamide: HR ~0.66 (ARCHES)
- Darolutamide (doublet): HR ~0.81 — not yet statistically mature for OS (ARANOTE)[^14]

**Practical probability**: Apalutamide and enzalutamide have more mature mCSPC doublet OS data; for mCSPC doublet without docetaxel, apalutamide and enzalutamide are essentially co-preferred (~45% each), with darolutamide as a compelling tolerability-driven alternative (~10% of selections in current practice) — likely to increase as ARANOTE OS data mature and the June 2025 FDA approval propagates through guidelines.[^5][^6]

### 10.3 mCSPC Triplet Setting

Darolutamide + ADT + docetaxel (ARASENS) is the only ARI with proven triplet efficacy data in Phase III. Darolutamide is the clear preferred choice for fit high-volume patients eligible for docetaxel. Probability of being the preferred agent: ~90%.[^8][^19]

### 10.4 mCRPC Setting

Enzalutamide is the only approved ARI option; probability of selection = 100% among the three agents for this indication.[^9][^22]

## 11. Unanswered Questions and Emerging Debates

1. **Does the real-world OS benefit of apalutamide vs enzalutamide/darolutamide in mCSPC reflect genuine pharmacological superiority or selection bias?** The J&J-sponsored studies are methodologically rigorous (IPTW, pre-specified protocols) but not randomised. An independent, prospectively designed head-to-head trial is needed.[^25][^23]

2. **Will ARANOTE OS data, when mature, equalise the mCSPC doublet landscape?** Given ARANOTE's HR of 0.81 for OS (trending but not significant), longer follow-up may reveal significant OS benefit — particularly given darolutamide's consistently better tolerability driving superior adherence.[^15][^14]

3. **Can sequencing darolutamide after enzalutamide/apalutamide provide meaningful benefit?** Limited retrospective data suggest 55% PSA response rate, but prospective validation is absent.[^61][^60]

4. **What role do biomarkers (AR-V7, AR amplification, BRCA2) play in ARI selection?** Currently no biomarker predicts differential benefit among the three agents. AR-V7 confers resistance to all three; PARP inhibitor combinations are relevant for BRCA-mutant disease.[^57][^58]

5. **How will dose-reduction strategies affect outcomes?** A rapid review found that dose reduction of all three agents is practised in real-world settings; within each dose subgroup, darolutamide maintains lower progression rates.[^68][^29]

6. **Does darolutamide's BCRP inhibition require systematic statin management protocols?** This emerging safety signal for darolutamide's BCRP inhibition (increasing rosuvastatin and other substrate concentrations) warrants standardised monitoring guidance.[^53]

## 12. Synthesis: A Decision Framework

| Clinical Scenario | Preferred Agent | Probability / Rationale |
|-------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| nmCRPC, elderly, polypharmacy | Darolutamide | ~70%; DDI safety, CNS profile, fracture risk |
| nmCRPC, seizure history | Darolutamide | ~99%; only eligible agent |
| nmCRPC, severe skin conditions | Darolutamide or Enzalutamide | ~80%; avoid apalutamide rash risk |
| nmCRPC, PSA-DT ≤6 months, fit | Enzalutamide | ~55%; NMA efficacy signal in ultra-high risk |
| mCSPC, doublet, fit | Apalutamide or Enzalutamide | ~45% each; most mature OS data |
| mCSPC, doublet, elderly/polypharmacy | Darolutamide (since June 2025) | ~60%; DDI, tolerability advantage |
| mCSPC, triplet with docetaxel | Darolutamide | ~90%; ARASENS only proven triplet ARI |
| mCRPC, any setting | Enzalutamide | ~100%; only approved ARI |
| Post-APA/ENZA progression in nmCRPC | Darolutamide (limited data) | ~55% PSA response; cross-resistance limits benefit |
| Patient prioritises QOL/fatigue | Darolutamide | ~65%; ODENZA, real-world data |

***

## Conclusion

The three second-generation ARIs occupy overlapping but meaningfully differentiated niches in prostate cancer treatment. Enzalutamide's broadest label (spanning nmCRPC through mCRPC) and longest track record make it the reference comparator; however, its CNS penetration, seizure risk, fatigue profile, and high DDI burden are genuine clinical liabilities, particularly in older patients. Apalutamide delivers competitive mCSPC doublet efficacy (TITAN OS HR 0.65) but is uniquely burdened by skin toxicity, cardiovascular events, and the heaviest DDI profile among the three. Darolutamide's structural distinction — minimal BBB penetration, favourable CNS/fatigue/fracture/DDI profile — translates into superior real-world adherence and tolerability, and its triplet data (ARASENS) and new doublet mCSPC approval (ARANOTE, June 2025) have substantially expanded its clinical footprint. For the majority of patients — who are elderly, have comorbidities, and face long treatment horizons — tolerability is as important as efficacy, and on that dimension, darolutamide holds a consistent, evidence-based advantage.
