# PCNSL phase 2-3 trials published from 2001 to 10 May 2026

**Scope.** Adult immunocompetent primary CNS lymphoma (PCNSL), published prospective phase II, randomized phase II, phase III, and selected phase I/II studies with a phase II efficacy cohort. The focus is treatment trials with reported clinical results. Trials mainly involving secondary CNS lymphoma, retrospective series, pure protocols without outcome data, case reports, and phase I-only studies were excluded unless they are explicitly flagged in the borderline section. Publication window: 2001 to 10 May 2026.

**Caution.** PCNSL trial nomenclature is inconsistent: some papers report protocols, some report primary randomized results, and some report later long-term analyses. Where the same trial has multiple publications, I list the publication most relevant to results and note long-term updates. "NR" means not reached; "not found" means not found in the indexed source checked, not necessarily absent from all databases.

**Abbreviations.** Ara-C, cytarabine; ASCT, autologous stem-cell transplant; BTKi, Bruton's tyrosine kinase inhibitor; CR, complete response; CRR, complete response rate; EA, etoposide plus cytarabine; EFS, event-free survival; HDC, high-dose chemotherapy; HD-MTX, high-dose methotrexate; ITT, intention-to-treat; LD-WBRT, low-dose whole-brain radiotherapy; ND, newly diagnosed; ORR, overall response rate; OS, overall survival; PFS, progression-free survival; PR, partial response; R/R, relapsed/refractory; WBRT, whole-brain radiotherapy.

## Synthesis

The highest-level randomized adult PCNSL evidence over this period supports HD-MTX-based combination induction and discourages routine standard-dose WBRT when effective consolidation alternatives exist, particularly in younger fit patients. IELSG20 established the value of adding cytarabine to HD-MTX. IELSG32 established MATRix as a leading fit-patient induction standard. G-PCNSL-SG-1 and later consolidation trials shifted practice away from indiscriminate standard-dose WBRT because OS gain was not clear and neurotoxicity was substantial. PRECIS and IELSG32 consolidation results show ASCT and WBRT are both active, but long-term cognitive toxicity makes standard-dose WBRT unattractive in many younger patients. Alliance 51101 supports stronger PFS with HDC-ASCT than EA consolidation, without an early OS separation.

For elderly or frail patients, the evidence base remains less definitive. PRIMAIN, Nordic, ANOCEF-GOELAMS, and MARTA show that reduced-intensity, age-adapted, or carefully selected transplant strategies can be feasible, but toxicity and competing mortality remain central. These trials should not be extrapolated uncritically to very frail patients or to patients with renal impairment.

Relapsed/refractory PCNSL trials are mostly small, single-arm, and biologically informative rather than definitive. Temsirolimus, lenalidomide-rituximab, pemetrexed-based regimens, and BTK inhibitors show activity, but median PFS is often short. BTK inhibitors, especially tirabrutinib and ibrutinib, changed the salvage landscape, but resistance, infections, and durability remain limiting. CAR-T data in PCNSL remain early but increasingly credible.

The 2024-2026 first-line literature shows a rapid expansion of BTK inhibitor and PD-1 inhibitor combinations, especially from China. Response rates are often very high, but many studies are single-arm, small, short follow-up, and use response-based primary endpoints. These should be treated as hypothesis-generating until randomized data show improved PFS/OS and acceptable neurotoxicity/infectious toxicity.

## Practical interpretation

1. The most practice-changing trials remain randomized: IELSG20, G-PCNSL-SG-1, IELSG32, HOVON105, PRECIS, JCOG1114C, Alliance 51101, and NRG/RTOG1114.
2. MATRix followed by thiotepa-based ASCT remains the cleanest evidence-backed approach for young/fit patients where transplant is feasible.
3. Standard-dose WBRT has disease-control activity but is penalized by neurocognitive toxicity, especially in older patients.
4. Low-dose WBRT improves PFS in NRG/RTOG1114, but OS and neurocognitive maturity should guide adoption.
5. Elderly PCNSL is not one disease population: fit elderly, frail elderly, renal impairment, and transplant-ineligible patients require separate evidence interpretation.
6. BTKi/PD-1 combinations look promising but are not yet replacements for randomized evidence-backed induction/consolidation standards.
