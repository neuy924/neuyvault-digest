# Cetuximab in Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma: Clinical Trials

## Overview

Cetuximab, a chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), has been a central agent in HNSCC therapy since the landmark Bonner trial established a survival benefit over radiotherapy alone in 2006. Its role has evolved substantially: while initially heralded as a paradigm-shifting radiosensitizer and first-line agent in recurrent/metastatic disease, subsequent trials have clarified important limitations, particularly in cisplatin-eligible and HPV-positive populations. The table below catalogues key completed and ongoing trials.[^1][^2][^3]

## Key Contextual Takeaways

**Cetuximab + RT (LA-HNSCC, cisplatin-ineligible):** The Bonner trial remains the bedrock of this indication. NRG-HN004 confirms cetuximab+RT is superior to durvalumab+RT for cisplatin-ineligible patients, reaffirming the indication.[^9][^10][^2]

**Cetuximab is NOT a cisplatin substitute in cisplatin-eligible patients:** RTOG 0522 showed no benefit from adding cetuximab to cisplatin-RT. ARTSCAN III and both HPV-de-escalation trials (RTOG 1016, De-ESCALaTE) confirmed inferiority to cisplatin.[^7][^5][^12][^16]

**R/M first-line:** EXTREME established cetuximab+platinum-5-FU as a standard, but KEYNOTE-048 demonstrated pembrolizumab-based regimens are superior, particularly for PD-L1 CPS ≥1. EXTREME/cetuximab-based therapy remains an option where immunotherapy is contraindicated.[^19][^21]

**Post-ICI setting:** Cetuximab monotherapy shows modest activity (ORR ~13–21%) post-ICI. Combining with monalizumab failed (INTERLINK-1). Rational biomarker-selected combinations (e.g., palbociclib in CDKN2A-altered, HPV-unrelated HNSCC) are under active investigation.[^27][^22]
