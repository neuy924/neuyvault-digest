# BEP in Germ Cell Tumours: Bleomycin Dose Density and Clinical Outcomes

## Executive Summary

BEP (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) remains the cornerstone of curative chemotherapy for metastatic germ cell tumours (GCTs), achieving cure in >90% of good-risk, ~85% of intermediate-risk, and ~50–70% of poor-risk patients. Bleomycin's contribution to this regimen is firmly established across multiple randomised trials: omitting or substantially reducing it compromises tumour control, particularly when fewer cycles or lower etoposide doses are used. Conversely, administering bleomycin carries a cumulative pulmonary toxicity risk (1–40% depending on dose and risk factors) that remains the principal driver of bleomycin-related death in clinical practice. The emerging paradigm focuses on optimising dose *density* — shortening cycle intervals from 21 to 14 days using G-CSF — to improve outcomes in intermediate- and poor-risk disease, with the ongoing international Phase III P3BEP trial (NCT02582697) designed to definitively answer this question.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]

***

## Standard BEP Regimen: Bleomycin Schedule and Dosing

The standard BEP regimen, as used in landmark trials and contemporary practice, comprises:[^9][^10]

- **Cisplatin** 20 mg/m² IV days 1–5 (100 mg/m²/cycle)
- **Etoposide** 100 mg/m² IV days 1–5 (500 mg/m²/cycle)
- **Bleomycin** 30,000 IU IV on days 2, 9, and 16 (or days 1, 8, 15); total **90,000 IU per cycle**
- Cycle length: 21 days; 3 cycles for good-risk, 4 cycles for intermediate/poor-risk disease

The **relative dose intensity (RDI)** of bleomycin, defined as actual delivered dose-intensity divided by planned dose-intensity, is the critical parameter under study. In Japanese cohort data, the average RDI of bleomycin was 0.95, while that for etoposide and cisplatin was 0.97, demonstrating that full dose delivery is achievable in most patients with appropriate G-CSF use. The maximum recommended cumulative dose is 300,000 IU in patients under 40 years of age to minimise pulmonary fibrosis risk.[^11][^12]

***

## Foundational Evidence for Bleomycin's Role

### Randomised Trials: Bleomycin vs. No Bleomycin

Two pivotal randomised trials established that bleomycin is an indispensable component of BEP when fewer than four cycles are given:

**ECOG Trial (Loehrer et al., JCO 1995):** 178 patients with minimal- or moderate-stage disease were randomised to cisplatin/etoposide (PE) with or without weekly bleomycin (30 IU/week × 9 weeks) over three cycles. The BEP arm demonstrated significantly superior outcomes:[^8]
- Disease-free status: 94% (PVP16B) vs. 88% (PVP16)
- Failure-free survival: 86% vs. 69% (P = 0.01)
- Overall survival: 95% vs. 86% (P = 0.01)
- Conclusion: **Bleomycin is an essential component of 3-cycle therapy** in good-prognosis patients[^13][^8]

**EORTC GU Trial (de Wit et al., JCO 1997):** 419 patients with good-prognosis nonseminomatous disease were randomised to BEP (bleomycin 30 mg weekly) vs. EP over four cycles, with etoposide at 360 mg/m²/cycle (lower than current standard). The BEP arm showed:[^14][^1]
- Complete response: 95% (BEP) vs. 87% (EP); P = 0.0075
- No statistically significant difference in time to progression (P = 0.136) or survival (P = 0.262) after 7.3 years' median follow-up
- Two bleomycin-related pulmonary deaths occurred in the BEP arm
- Interpretation: With a substandard etoposide dose, bleomycin cannot be safely deleted; however, the survival equivalence suggests the picture may differ with full-dose etoposide[^15]

**Key insight from the AGCTG reduced-dose trial:** In 166 good-risk patients randomised to standard BEP ×3 vs. a modified 3-day BEP (bleomycin 30 units on day 1 only per cycle = 90 units total vs. 270 units, plus reduced etoposide), the modified arm showed significantly inferior OS (HR 0.22, 95% CI 0.06–0.77; P = 0.008). This underscores that **simultaneous reduction of both bleomycin (to 30% of standard) and etoposide dose is detrimental**.[^16]

### Bleomycin Dose Reduction and Survival Impact

Retrospective real-world data confirm dose-intensity matters. A study demonstrating reduced-dose BEP (<75% standard-dose bleomycin and <50% etoposide dose) was significantly associated with poorer 5-year OS compared with standard-dose BEP. A Mexican multicentre cohort (n=179, intermediate and poor prognosis) found that bleomycin dose modifications — largely due to drug shortage — had a **deleterious impact on both PFS and OS** by multivariate analysis; only 42% of patients received the full Bleo360 (90 IU/cycle ×4) dose, and the median bleomycin dose was 67.5 IU/cycle.[^17][^18]

A 2026 ASCO systematic review (JCO 2026 suppl.) specifically evaluated the impact of bleomycin dose omissions from BEP ×3 in NSGCT, examining whether omitting one or more doses preserves cure rates in the good-risk setting, with results suggesting omission may be survivable in highly selected patients — though formal meta-analytic conclusions require full publication.[^19]

## Cycle Number and Schedule: The 3-Day vs. 5-Day Evidence

### Three vs. Four Cycles of BEP

**Indiana University / SECSG (Saxman et al., JCO 1998):** 118 patients with good-prognosis (minimal/moderate stage) disease were randomised to 3 vs. 4 cycles of BEP. At median follow-up of 10.1 years:[^20][^21]
- No significant difference in overall survival (P = 0.80) or disease-free survival (P = 0.93)
- 98% of patients with HCG ≤1,000 mIU/mL alive without disease at 5+ years
- Elevated HCG >1,000 mIU/mL remained an independent predictor of adverse outcome (P <0.001)

**EORTC/MRC Factorial Trial (de Wit et al., JCO 2001):** 812 patients with good-prognosis disease were randomised in a 2×2 design to 3 vs. 4 cycles AND 5-day vs. 3-day schedule. Bleomycin 30 mg was given on days 1, 8, 15 during cycles 1–3 in all arms:[^22][^23]
- 2-year PFS: 90.4% (3 cycles) vs. 89.4% (4 cycles); difference −1.0% (80% CL −3.8%, +1.8%) — **equivalence demonstrated**
- 3-day vs. 5-day: 2-year PFS 89.7% vs. 88.8% — **equivalent efficacy**
- Quality of life favoured 3 cycles over 4; no QoL difference between 3-day and 5-day schedules[^24]
- **Conclusion: 3 cycles of BEP with etoposide 500 mg/m² is the standard for good-prognosis GCT**[^22]

**GETUG T93BP (Culine et al., Ann Oncol 2007):** 257 patients with good-risk NSGCT randomised to 3×BEP (etoposide 500 mg/m²; 3BE500P) vs. 4×EP (etoposide 500 mg/m²; 4E500P). Bleomycin in the BEP arm administered weekly as standard:[^25]
- Favourable response: 124/124 (3BE500P) vs. 122/257 (4E500P); P = 0.34
- 4-year EFS: 91% vs. 86% (P = 0.135); 5 vs. 12 deaths (P = 0.096)
- Trial was underpowered; **3BE500P remains standard of care** pending larger confirmatory data[^25]

***

## Bleomycin Infusion Schedule: Bolus vs. Continuous Infusion

**TE-3 Trial (Shamash et al., Ann Oncol 2017):** Phase III randomisation of 212 men with IGCCCG good-prognosis metastatic GCT to conventional BEP (weekly bleomycin 30,000 units IV bolus ×3 per cycle) vs. a single 90,000-unit infusion over 72 hours on day 1 of each cycle. Primary endpoint was CT-assessed pulmonary toxicity:[^26]
- No significant difference in lung toxicity by CT imaging between infusional and bolus arms
- PFS outcomes were equivalent between the two approaches
- Provided reassurance that alternative administration schedules (frontloading bleomycin during hospitalisation) are efficacious and safe[^27][^26]

This trial underpinned subsequent modified regimens, such as bleomycin administered on days 1, 3, and 5 of the 5-day hospitalisation cycle.

***

## Modified Bleomycin Administration Schedules

### Days 1, 3, 5 Schedule (Sun Yat-sen University, 2025)

A large retrospective single-institution study (n=274 adult males) at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center evaluated BEP with bleomycin 30 IU IV on days 1, 3, and 5 (concentrated within the hospitalisation window) instead of standard days 1, 8, 15:[^27]

| IGCCCG Risk | Favourable Response Rate | 5-yr PFS | 5-yr OS |
|-------------|--------------------------|-----------|---------|
| Good | 91.07% | 93.75% | 99.05%[^27] |
| Intermediate | 84.13% | 87.57% | 92.84%[^27] |
| Poor | 52.63% | 43.38% | 55.96%[^27] |

Key safety findings: grade 3–4 neutropenia in 38.69%, febrile neutropenia in 6.2%, and only 4 patients (1.46%) developed pulmonary toxicity — with **zero deaths from pulmonary toxicity**. Poor-risk survival was inferior to contemporary IGCCCG Update data, likely reflecting higher proportions of retroperitoneal and mediastinal primaries in the Chinese cohort.[^27]

### Day 1 Only Per Cycle (Modified BEP, ISJ 2019)

A tertiary-care study of 49 patients (India) used a modified BEP where bleomycin was given at 30 IU on day 1 only per cycle (10 IU/body/week planned intensity), with cisplatin 20 mg/m² days 1–5 and etoposide 100 mg/m² days 1–5:[^28]
- Overall response rate: 81.2%
- Bleomycin-induced pulmonary toxicity: 4.1% (clinically evident)
- Febrile neutropenia: 10.4%
- Lower toxicity contributed to better compliance; efficacy was described as comparable[^28]

### Aurilio et al. Modified BEP (American Journal of Clinical Oncology, 2020)

A retrospective analysis using bleomycin 15 U IV bolus on day 1 and continuous infusion 10 U over 12 hours on days 1–3 found survival outcomes and tumour response not inferior to those in published standard BEP studies, with no pulmonary toxicity deaths.[^27]

## Dose-Dense BEP: The Accelerated Approach

### Rationale

The dose-density hypothesis posits that shortening the inter-cycle interval from 21 to 14 days — enabled by pegylated G-CSF — increases the relative dose density (RDD) of all three agents, potentially overcoming tumour cell repopulation. The planned RDD in 14-day BEP vs. standard 21-day BEP is 1.5.[^29][^30]

### Phase I/II Evidence

**Rimmer et al. (Br J Cancer, 2011):** The first systematic dose-dense BEP Phase I study enrolled 16 patients (11 intermediate, 5 poor prognosis) receiving 4 cycles of 3-day BEP every 14 days with pegfilgrastim:[^29]
- Mean overall RDD: 1.38 (median 1.46; range 0.72–1.5)
- CR after chemotherapy alone: 13%; CR after chemotherapy + surgery: 56%; PR with normalised markers: 25%
- Estimated 5-year PFS: **81%** (95% CI 64–100%) at median follow-up 4.4 years
- Mucositis and haematological toxicity were more severe than standard BEP but manageable
- Conclusion: Accelerated BEP tolerable; warranted a Phase III RCT[^29]

**ANZUP Phase II (Ann Oncol 2014):** 43 patients (12 poor, 16 intermediate, 15 good prognosis) received accelerated BEP with cisplatin and etoposide q2w ×4 cycles and bleomycin 30,000 IU weekly ×12 doses (total bleomycin dose equivalent to standard):[^31]
- Feasibility: 86% of patients completed the schedule as planned
- CR rates: 33% (poor), 81% (intermediate), 100% (good prognosis)
- 2-year PFS: 50% for poor prognosis[^31]
- Long-term follow-up updated in 2016 confirmed durability of responses[^32]

**Phase II by Tryakin et al. (ASCO 2014):** 61 patients (36 intermediate, 25 poor prognosis; IGCCCG) received every-2-week ddBEP (cisplatin 20 mg/m² days 1–5, etoposide 100 mg/m² days 1–5, bleomycin 30 IU days 1, 3, 5 ×4 cycles, G-CSF days 6–10). Dose reductions occurred in bleomycin (7.7% of cycles), etoposide (8.9%), and cisplatin (6.4%). No toxicity-related deaths; 72% started cycle 4 within 1 week of schedule. Efficacy results forthcoming at time of reporting.[^33]

## Ongoing Phase III Trial: P3BEP (ANZUP 1302/AGCT1532)

The P3BEP trial represents the definitive international attempt to validate dose-dense BEP:[^5][^6][^34]

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Design | Open-label, randomised Phase III, 2-stage seamless |
| Population | Age 11–50 years, intermediate/poor risk metastatic GCT (testis, ovary, retroperitoneum, mediastinum) |
| Arms | Standard BEP q3w ×4 vs. Accelerated BEP q2w ×4 (then bleomycin ×4 weekly) |
| Bleomycin dose | 30,000 IU weekly ×12 in both arms (total bleomycin dose preserved) |
| G-CSF | Pegfilgrastim 6 mg day 6 or filgrastim daily |
| Stage 1 endpoint | Favourable response (n=150) |
| Stage 2 endpoint | 2-year PFS (n=500 total) |
| Sites | 21 ANZ, 22 UK (Cambridge CTC), 148 USA (COG) sites |
| Accrual (as of Sept 2024) | 321 participants enrolled[^5] |
| Safety interim | No safety concerns identified[^5] |

**Key design note:** The accelerated arm preserves total bleomycin dose (12 weekly doses) while compressing the cisplatin and etoposide cycle interval. This isolates the dose-density effect of the cytotoxic backbone, allowing a clean assessment of whether dose density — rather than total dose — drives improved outcomes.

## Poor-Prognosis Disease: Dose-Intense Strategies

Standard BEP ×4 achieves 5-year survival of only ~50% in IGCCCG poor-risk patients. Multiple intensification strategies have been tested:[^35]

### BOP/BEP Alternating (Br J Cancer 2004)[^36]
43 patients with intermediate/poor prognosis received dose-intense alternating BOP (bleomycin, vincristine, cisplatin)/BEP ×3 cycles:
- Complete response rate: 58%; 7% rendered disease-free by resection of viable residual tumour
- 3-year OS: 81% (95% CI 66–91%); 3-year PFS: 72% (95% CI 56–83%)
- 86% of patients completed all planned courses; bleomycin-induced pulmonary toxicity was the most significant non-haematological toxicity[^36]

### CBOP/BEP (MRC TE23, Eur Urol 2015)[^37]
Randomised Phase II trial of intensive CBOP/BEP (carboplatin, bleomycin, vincristine, cisplatin, then BEP) vs. concurrent patients on standard BEP for poor-prognosis nonseminoma. The intensive induction with CBOP/BEP demonstrated promising response rates warranting further investigation.[^37]

### GETUG 13 (Lancet Oncol 2014; mature results ASCO 2016)[^38]
263 poor-prognosis patients received 1 BEP cycle, then stratified by tumour marker decline at day 21:
- Favourable decline (n=51): continued standard BEP
- Unfavourable decline (n=203, randomised): dose-dense intensification (paclitaxel-BEP + oxaliplatin ×2, then CIB ×2) vs. standard BEP

At 5.6 years' median follow-up:
- 5-yr PFS: 60% (dose-dense) vs. 47% (BEP); HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.43–0.97); P = 0.037
- 5-yr OS: 70.4% vs. 60.8%; HR 0.69 (P = 0.12 — non-significant)
- Late neurotoxicity resolved by year 2; no grade ≥2 toxicity thereafter[^38]
- **GETUG 13 established marker-decline-guided intensification as a preferred approach for poor-risk NSGCT**

### EORTC 30974 (Ann Oncol 2011)[^39]
Phase III comparing standard BEP ×4 vs. sequential high-dose cisplatin, etoposide, ifosfamide (VIP) + stem-cell support as first-line therapy for poor/intermediate risk. This study **could not demonstrate superiority of high-dose chemotherapy in the first-line setting** over standard BEP, in contrast to the second-line HDCT benefit.[^39]

## Bleomycin Pulmonary Toxicity: Risk Stratification and Cumulative Dose

Pulmonary toxicity (BPT) is the principal dose-limiting toxicity of bleomycin in BEP:

- Overall incidence in clinical cohorts: **5–40%** depending on population and monitoring intensity[^4]
- Cumulative dose >550,000 IU: BPT incidence 17%, fatal BPT 10%[^3]
- Cumulative dose <450,000 IU: BPT incidence ~5%[^3]
- In a large Indian intermediate/poor-risk cohort (n=223), 30 patients (13%) developed bleomycin-induced lung toxicity; **5 (16.7%) died from BPT**; 56.7% were symptomatic, 60% required steroids, 13.3% required ICU admission[^4]

**Risk factors for BPT**:[^3][^4]
- Older age (>40 years)
- Active or former smoking history
- Pre-existing pulmonary disease
- Renal impairment (reduced bleomycin clearance)
- Cumulative dose >300,000 IU
- Prior thoracic radiation
- High-flow supplemental oxygen (anaesthetic risk)
- Lung metastases at baseline

In clinical practice, BEP with extensive pulmonary disease typically warrants substitution with VIP (vinblastine/etoposide, ifosfamide, cisplatin) to avoid bleomycin in the context of pre-existing pulmonary compromise and planned post-chemotherapy thoracotomy.[^40]

## Clinical Practice Implications

**1. Preserve bleomycin dose intensity in good-risk patients receiving 3 cycles.** The evidence is clear that reducing bleomycin dose or omitting doses is detrimental when only 3 cycles are given with substandard etoposide. With full etoposide (500 mg/m²/cycle), the margin is somewhat wider, but dose reductions remain inadvisable without compelling clinical necessity.[^13][^8]

**2. Bleomycin cannot be omitted in 3×BEP but may be substitutable in 4×EP.** If bleomycin must be stopped (early pulmonary toxicity signs, renal dysfunction, or patient refusal), completion with EP ×4 is a reasonable alternative for good-risk disease, as ECOG data suggest the harm of omission is most pronounced in the 3-cycle setting.[^41][^42]

**3. Cumulative dose capping at 300,000 IU (age <40) is essential practice.** Scheduling bleomycin on days 1, 8, 15 as originally designed delivers 270,000 IU over 3 cycles or 360,000 IU over 4 cycles — well within safety thresholds for most patients. Modified schedules (days 1, 3, 5) concentrating bleomycin within the hospitalisation window appear efficacious and may reduce outpatient burden without increasing pulmonary risk.[^12][^9][^26][^27]

**4. Dose-dense BEP is investigational and should not displace standard BEP outside clinical trials.** The Phase I/II data are promising — particularly the 5-year PFS of 81% in intermediate/poor-risk patients at RDD 1.38 — but equivalence or superiority over standard BEP in a Phase III powered for PFS remains to be demonstrated. P3BEP will provide this answer.[^29]

**5. Bleomycin shortage represents a real-world clinical threat.** The Mexican cohort demonstrating inferior PFS/OS with dose-modified bleomycin (largely due to drug shortage) is a stark reminder of bleomycin's non-substitutable role and the importance of supply chain security for curative-intent regimens.[^18]

**6. GETUG 13 strategy is preferred for poor-prognosis patients with unfavourable marker decline.** Marker-adapted intensification (switching from BEP to dose-dense paclitaxel-BEP + oxaliplatin at day 21) significantly improved 5-year PFS (60% vs. 47%), establishing a role for early response-adapted treatment intensification over continued standard BEP in this high-risk subset.[^38]
