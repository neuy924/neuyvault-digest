# 東洋史 — Major Theorists & Theories

### Related Articles
[[Japanese Historiography - Toyoshi - Periodization Empire and Postcolonial Epistemology - Reference]]

## Reference Table

| Theorist | Dates | School | Major Theories & Concepts | Primary Focus | Critical Legacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **白鳥庫吉** Shiratori Kurakichi | 1865–1942 | Tokyo Imperial University · Tokyo School | **Scientific Philology** — Applied German historicist source criticism to Asian texts; demythologized Chinese foundational legends as ideological fabrication. **Reactive China Thesis** — China's civilization forged through interaction with northern nomadic peoples; Inner Asia, not the agrarian core, drove East Asian historical change. **Steppe-Agrarian Binary** — East Asian history structured by tension between nomadic steppe polities and sedentary agrarian Chinese states. | East Asian historiography; Inner Asian peoples; Chinese mythology | Founded 東洋史 as academic discipline. Steppe-agrarian binary became default framework; later critiqued for reducing Inner Asia to a foil for China. |
| **内藤湖南** Naitō Konan | 1866–1934 | Kyoto Imperial University · Kyoto School | **Tang-Song Transition Thesis** — Tang = aristocratic, medieval, closed; Song = meritocratic, commercial, urban, popular. Rupture as significant as any in world history. **Chinese Kinsei (近世)** — China entered its own early modernity c. 1000 CE, centuries before Europe; endogenous modernity, not Western-dependent. **Structural Periodization** — Social and economic formation over dynastic succession; introduced social history into a court-history dominated field. | Chinese social & economic history; periodization; cultural history | Most influential single theorist in 東洋史. Tang-Song thesis broadly accepted descriptively; its "modernity" interpretation contested as Eurocentric in inverted form (Mizoguchi critique). |
| **宮崎市定** Miyazaki Ichisada | 1901–1995 | Kyoto University · Kyoto School | **Extended Tang-Song Modernity** — Systematized Naitō's thesis with deep economic history; Song monetization and urban culture constituted genuine proto-modernity. **Keju as World's First Meritocracy** — Civil examination system (科挙) was a revolutionary social technology more open than contemporary European feudalism. **Mongol Interruption Thesis** — Yuan conquest derailed Song's modernizing trajectory; China's failure to sustain early modernity was external contingency, not civilizational deficiency. **World-Historical Comparison** — China must be understood through genuine comparative world history, not as exotic Other. | Chinese economic history; examination system; comparative world history | Definitive study of the examination system. Mongol interruption thesis criticized as rescue device; meritocracy thesis complicated by Hymes/Hartwell local elite studies. |
| **桑原隲蔵** Kuwabara Jitsuzō | 1871–1931 | Kyoto Imperial University · Kyoto School | **China-Islam Maritime Networks** — Tang-Song port cities (esp. Quanzhou) were cosmopolitan nodes in Eurasian trade; Muslim merchants were structural participants in Chinese economic life. **Open China Thesis** — Chinese civilization historically porous; Tang Chang'an hosted Nestorian, Manichaean, Zoroastrian, and Muslim communities. **Multi-source Methodology** — Integrated Arabic and Persian sources with Chinese dynastic records; established case for reading Chinese history through non-Chinese sources. | Maritime trade; China-Islam connections; Song-Yuan port cities | Foundational for maritime Asia studies. Pu Shougeng study foundational for maritime China history; anticipated the 海域アジア turn by half a century. |
| **津田左右吉** Tsuda Sōkichi | 1873–1961 | Waseda University · Independent | **Radical Source Hypercriticism** — Confucian classics are retrospective political constructions, not historical records; Zhou philosophy projected onto fabricated antiquity. **Confucian Canon as Ideology** — Classical texts were tools of political legitimation; the sage-king tradition was manufactured. **Applied to Japanese Sources** — Extended hypercritical method to Japanese ancient texts; resulted in wartime prosecution, revealing political stakes of source criticism under nationalist regimes. | Chinese & Japanese ancient history; textual criticism; classical canon | Most rigorous methodologist of the prewar generation. Better at dismantling than constructing alternative frameworks; hypercritical method remains influential in philological history. |
| **羽田亨** Haneda Tōru | 1882–1955 | Kyoto Imperial University · Kyoto School | **Multiethnic Empire Theory** — Tang and major "Chinese" dynasties were multiethnic, institutionally composite formations; Tang's ruling house, military, and culture substantially non-Han. **Sogdian Network Thesis** — Sogdian Iranian merchants were primary agents of Silk Road exchange; Tang cosmopolitanism was Sogdian-mediated, not simply Chinese openness. **Inner Asia as Historical Subject** — Steppe and Central Asian peoples must be studied on their own terms, not merely as backdrop to Chinese history. **Eurasian Corridor Framework** — The Inner Asian corridor was itself a historical unit generating political formations and cultural syntheses irreducible to any single civilization. | Central Asia; Tang multiethnic empire; Sogdian networks; Islam-China connections | Institutional founder of Central Asian studies in Japan. Direct precursor to New Qing History; Sogdian thesis confirmed by Dunhuang and Turfan manuscript archaeology. |
| **西嶋定生** Nishijima Sadao | 1919–1998 | Tokyo University · Postwar Tokyo | **East Asian World System** — Japan, Korea, Vietnam, and China formed an integrated civilizational zone defined by four shared elements: Chinese writing, Confucianism, Buddhism, tributary system. **Tributary System as Architecture** — Tribute system was structural backbone of East Asian international order, conferring legitimacy and creating centripetal pull toward Chinese institutional forms. **Silk as Political Currency** — Silk functioned as political-economic instrument in early China; heqin silk-for-peace agreements with Xiongnu were rational political economy. **Peripheral Agency** — Peripheral states manipulated the tributary system strategically; the system had a center but peripheries were not passive. | Early Chinese economic history; East Asian international order; silk economy | East Asian World framework became default for pre-modern East Asian international relations. Critiqued for sophisticated Sinocentrism — defining the East Asian world entirely through Chinese cultural elements; marginalizes Southeast Asia, Tibet, and steppe worlds. |
| **岡田英弘** Okada Hidehiro | 1931–2017 | Tokyo University of Foreign Studies · Revisionist | **"China" as Constructed Fiction** — "China" as a continuous 5,000-year civilizational entity is retrospective nationalist ideology; actual history = series of discontinuous, often nomad-founded polities governing the north China plain. **Conquest Dynasties as Protagonists** — Yuan, Qing, and substantially Tang were conquest empires, not "Chinese dynasties"; sinicization was an instrumental governance tool, not civilizational gravity. **Mongol Empire as World-Historical Pivot** — Chinggis Khan's empire was the single most consequential event in premodern world history; Chinese history is a provincial chapter in the Mongol story. **Nationalist Historiography as Territorial Politics** — Chinese historical claims to Tibet, Xinjiang, Mongolia rest on fabricated continuity narratives. | Mongol Empire; Manchu Qing; anti-Sinocentric revisionism; Inner Asian empires | Most radical revisionist in the tradition. Anticipates New Qing History; important methodological points undermined by association with Japanese nationalist circles. |
| **溝口雄三** Mizoguchi Yūzō | 1932–2010 | Tokyo University · Postwar Tokyo | **China as Method (方法としての中国)** — Chinese historical experience should generate its own analytical concepts; China is a source of theory, not a case study illustrating Western categories. **Immanentization of Li (理)** — Principle (li) shifts from Song transcendence to Ming-Qing immanence, constituting a distinctively Chinese transformation of subjectivity and moral agency. **Gong/Si (公/私) as Non-Liberal Public** — Chinese public/private distinction does not map onto European liberal theory; gong carries cosmological weight, not aggregation of private interests. **Against Sympathetic Eurocentrism** — Even Naitō's generous "early modernity" thesis measures China against European developmental templates; requires full epistemological reversal. | Late Ming thought; Chinese philosophy as theory; epistemology of East Asian studies | Most epistemologically radical figure in the tradition. "China as Method" became a touchstone for global postcolonial historiography. Risk of essentialism; politically appropriable by authoritarianism. |
