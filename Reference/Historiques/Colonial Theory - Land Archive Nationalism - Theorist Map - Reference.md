### 1. Land, sovereignty, settler colonialism

| Theorist                    | Core idea                                                                                                           | Best use                                                                                          |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Patrick Wolfe**           | Settler colonialism is a structure, not an event; its core logic is elimination of Indigenous sovereignty for land. | Australia, North America, Indigenous Taiwan, Palestine/Israel, Manchukuo, Xinjiang by adaptation. |
| **Lorenzo Veracini**        | Settler colonialism tries to disappear as colonialism by naturalising settler sovereignty.                          | Comparing settler formations and settler self-narratives.                                         |
| **Aileen Moreton-Robinson** | The “white possessive”: sovereignty, property, and racial entitlement are fused in settler societies.               | Australia, property, possession, Indigenous sovereignty.                                          |
| **Glen Coulthard**          | Liberal recognition can reproduce colonial power when land and sovereignty remain untouched.                        | Recognition politics, multiculturalism, autonomy without restitution.                             |
| **Audra Simpson**           | Indigenous refusal interrupts settler sovereignty; refusal is a political stance, not absence.                      | Indigenous sovereignty, refusal, border-crossing nations.                                         |

**Best for your cases:** Indigenous Taiwan, Manchukuo, Xinjiang settlement/infrastructure, Tibet sovereignty.

### 2. Empire, intimacy, affect, archive

| Theorist             | Core idea                                                                                                 | Best use                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ann Laura Stoler** | Empire works through intimacy, family, sexuality, archives, affect, anxiety, and durable imperial debris. | Xinjiang households/surveillance; Tibet religion/memory; Taiwan Japanese/KMT subject-making; Manchukuo ideology. |
| **Michel Foucault**  | Power works through discipline, surveillance, biopolitics, population management, and normalisation.      | Schools, prisons, public health, surveillance, census, sexuality, security.                                      |
| **Lisa Lowe**        | Liberal freedom was constituted through slavery, colonialism, migration, and imperial intimacy.           | Linking empire, race, labour, migration, liberalism.                                                             |
| **Frantz Fanon**     | Colonialism damages subjectivity, body, language, recognition, and political agency.                      | Humiliation, assimilation, anti-colonial psychology, racial embodiment.                                          |
| **Homi Bhabha**      | Mimicry, hybridity, ambivalence; colonial authority is unstable because imitation is never exact.         | Assimilation, colonial education, elite mimicry, ambivalent subjecthood.                                         |

**Best for your cases:** Xinjiang and Tibet intimate governance; Japanese Taiwan; Manchukuo’s staged imperial modernity.

### 3. Classification, ethnicity, state legibility

| Theorist            | Core idea                                                                                                                       | Best use                                                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rogers Brubaker** | Ethnicity is not a fixed group but a category, event, claim, and process of “groupness.”                                        | Avoiding crude blocs like “Han,” “Uyghur,” “Tibetan,” “Manchu.”              |
| **James C. Scott**  | States simplify complex societies to make them legible; people resist through evasion, hidden transcripts, and local knowledge. | Frontiers, nomads, hill peoples, religious communities, everyday resistance. |
| **Nicholas Dirks**  | Colonial rule hardens flexible social categories and makes them appear traditional.                                             | Census, ethnography, legal classification, caste/minzu analogies.            |
| **Thomas Mullaney** | PRC minzu classification produced official ethnic categories through state-socialist ethnology and fieldwork.                   | Chinese ethnic classification, minzu politics, Xinjiang/Tibet.               |
| **Stevan Harrell**  | Chinese civilising projects frame minorities through developmental hierarchy.                                                   | Han–minority relations, ethnicity, civilising centre/periphery.              |
| **James Leibold**   | Chinese nationalism and ethnic policy are shaped by minzu classification, race, empire, and modern state-building.              | PRC ethnic politics, Xinjiang, Tibet, nationalism.                           |

**Best for your cases:** Xinjiang, Tibet, Manchuria, Qing/PRC borderlands.

### 4. Nationalism, imagined community, historical narrative

| Theorist               | Core idea                                                                                                                | Best use                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Benedict Anderson**  | Nations are imagined communities produced through print capitalism, shared time, maps, censuses, museums, and schooling. | Chinese, Japanese, Taiwanese, Tibetan, Manchu national imaginaries.   |
| **Partha Chatterjee**  | Anti-colonial nationalism often reproduces colonial categories while claiming inner cultural sovereignty.                | Nationalism under colonial constraint; elite nationalist discourse.   |
| **Dipesh Chakrabarty** | European categories of modernity are indispensable but inadequate; Europe must be “provincialized.”                      | Critiquing linear modernisation narratives.                           |
| **Prasenjit Duara**    | Nations retrospectively capture history; Manchukuo used authenticity and sovereignty to legitimise empire.               | Manchuria, Manchukuo, East Asian nationalism.                         |
| **Hannah Arendt**      | Imperialism, race-thinking, bureaucracy, and statelessness helped generate modern domination.                            | Bureaucracy, statelessness, race-thinking, totalitarian continuities. |

**Best for your cases:** Taiwan nationalism, Manchukuo legitimacy, PRC national integration narratives.

### 5. Resistance, subaltern agency, refusal

| Theorist                             | Core idea                                                                                                            | Best use                                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **James C. Scott**                   | Everyday resistance: foot-dragging, evasion, hidden transcripts, noncompliance.                                      | Local resistance under strong states.                            |
| **Ranajit Guha / Subaltern Studies** | Colonial and nationalist elites erase subaltern agency; peasant/local actors have their own political rationalities. | Recovering local agency, rebellion, rumours, non-elite politics. |
| **Gayatri Spivak**                   | Elite representation often silences subaltern voices; “speaking for” is politically fraught.                         | Archive critique, voice, mediation, intellectual ethics.         |
| **Audra Simpson**                    | Refusal is a political act against absorption into settler recognition.                                              | Indigenous sovereignty and refusal.                              |
| **Fanon**                            | Anti-colonial struggle remakes the colonised subject psychologically and politically.                                | Liberation movements, embodied struggle, colonial violence.      |

**Best for your cases:** Tibetan religious persistence, Uyghur cultural survival, Indigenous Taiwanese sovereignty, Manchurian/local collaboration-resistance ambiguities.

### 6. Development, capitalism, infrastructure, extraction

| Theorist                   | Core idea                                                                                                            | Best use                                                                   |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rosa Luxemburg / Lenin** | Imperialism as capitalist expansion, monopoly, finance, resource extraction, and incorporation of peripheral spaces. | Railways, mines, plantations, markets, imperial political economy.         |
| **David Harvey**           | Accumulation by dispossession: capitalism expands by enclosing land, privatising commons, and reorganising space.    | Infrastructure corridors, land grabs, resource frontiers.                  |
| **Tania Murray Li**        | Development defines deficiencies and intervenes to “improve” populations, often depoliticising dispossession.        | Poverty alleviation, resettlement, rural development, improvement schemes. |
| **Foucault**               | Governmentality: populations are managed through expertise, welfare, risk, and optimisation.                         | Development as technical rule.                                             |
| **Stoler**                 | Development can become colonial common sense and produce imperial duress.                                            | When improvement rhetoric masks domination.                                |

**Best for your cases:** Xinjiang development/security; Tibet infrastructure/tourism; Manchukuo industrialisation; Taiwan colonial modernity.

### 7. Violence, exception, militarisation, death-worlds

| Theorist            | Core idea                                                                                                                   | Best use                                                                |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Achille Mbembe**  | Necropolitics: sovereignty includes the power to expose populations to death, injury, abandonment, or unlivable conditions. | Militarised occupation, camps, death-worlds, carceral frontiers.        |
| **Giorgio Agamben** | State of exception: law creates zones where people are included through exclusion from full protection.                     | Camps, emergency rule, martial law, suspended rights.                   |
| **Arendt**          | Bureaucracy, race-thinking, statelessness, and imperial violence feed modern domination.                                    | Race bureaucracy, total domination, statelessness.                      |
| **Wolfe**           | Elimination can be physical, legal, cultural, demographic, or institutional.                                                | Genocide-adjacent analysis without reducing all elimination to killing. |
| **Fanon**           | Colonial violence is bodily, psychic, spatial, and political.                                                               | Anti-colonial violence and subject formation.                           |

**Best for your cases:** Xinjiang detention/security debates, Tibet militarisation, Taiwan martial law, Manchukuo war empire.

### 8. East Asia and China-specific historians / theorists

| Theorist            | Core idea                                                                                              | Best use                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Thomas Mullaney** | PRC minzu classification as a state-socialist classificatory project.                                  | Xinjiang, Tibet, ethnic classification.      |
| **James Leibold**   | Chinese nationalism, race, and ethnic governance are historically entangled.                           | PRC borderlands, minzu politics.             |
| **Stevan Harrell**  | Han–minority relations often operate through civilising and developmental hierarchy.                   | Chinese frontier governance.                 |
| **Prasenjit Duara** | National history is retrospectively constructed; Manchukuo staged sovereignty and authenticity.        | Manchuria/Manchukuo.                         |
| **Louise Young**    | Manchuria was central to Japan’s total empire: military, economic, ideological, and social.            | Japanese Manchuria.                          |

**Best for your cases:** Taiwan and Manchuria need these more than generic postcolonial theory alone.

## Most useful master grouping

For your project, I would not organise by theorist first. I would organise by **analytic problem**:

| Problem                                                        | Main theorists                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Who controls land and sovereignty?**                         | Wolfe, Veracini, Moreton-Robinson, Coulthard, Simpson |
| **How does rule enter family, school, body, archive, affect?** | Stoler, Foucault, Fanon, Lowe, Bhabha                 |
| **How are ethnic/national categories made?**                   | Brubaker, Mullaney, Dirks, Harrell, Leibold           |
| **How is nationhood imagined and historicised?**               | Anderson, Chatterjee, Chakrabarty, Duara              |
| **How do people resist or refuse?**                            | Scott, Guha, Spivak, Simpson, Fanon                   |
| **How do development and infrastructure govern?**              | Tania Li, Harvey, Luxemburg/Lenin, Foucault           |
| **How does violence become normalised or exceptional?**        | Mbembe, Agamben, Arendt, Wolfe, Fanon                 |
