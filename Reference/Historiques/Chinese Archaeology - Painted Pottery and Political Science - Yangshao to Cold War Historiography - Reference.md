## 英文摘要 — Abstract (English)

This conversation traces the intellectual and political history of Chinese prehistoric and Bronze Age archaeology from its Neolithic foundations through the epistemological ruptures of the twentieth century. Beginning with the material culture of the Yangshao Neolithic complex and its regional sub-phases, the discussion moves through the theoretical frameworks developed to interpret that evidence — from Johan Gunnar Andersson's diffusionist hypotheses and the nationalist counter-mobilisation they provoked, through the empiricist culture-historical archaeology of Lǐ Jì and the Academia Sinica Anyang team, to the competing polycentric models of Sū Bǐngqí and Zhāng Guāngzhí. Central to the analysis is the observation that Chinese archaeology has never operated as a politically neutral discipline: Republican nationalism, Marxist historical materialism, Cold War institutional division, and contemporary civilisational longevity claims have each conscripted the same empirical material for divergent ideological programmes. The 1949 political rupture emerges as a decisive epistemological break, splitting the archaeological establishment across the Taiwan Strait and generating radically divergent interpretive frameworks from a shared material foundation. The conversation identifies a persistent suppressed variable across all frameworks examined — ecological, demographic, and subsistence-economic causation — as a recurring blind spot in a field dominated by cultural, political, and theoretical concerns.

## 主要主題 Zhǔyào Zhǔtí — Major Themes

**1. 多元起源 Duōyuán Qǐyuán — Polycentric Origins Debate**
The central tension throughout: was Chinese civilisation generated from a single Yellow River cradle or from multiple simultaneous regional florescence events? Every topic addressed — Yangshao sub-phases, Sū Bǐngqí's theory, Zhāng Guāngzhí's interaction sphere, PRC vs. Republican interpretation — orbits this question.

**2. 考古學政治化 Kǎogǔxué Zhèngzhìhuà — Politicisation of Archaeology**
Archaeology in China has never operated as a purely empirical discipline. Republican nationalism, Marxist stage theory, PRC civilisational longevity claims, and Taiwan institutional conservatism all conscripted the same material evidence for competing political programmes.

**3. 外來影響與本土回應 Wàilái Yǐngxiǎng yǔ Běntǔ Huíyìng — Foreign Influence and Indigenous Response**
Andersson's diffusionism provoked a defensive indigeneity movement that paradoxically reinforced Central Plains centrism. The pattern — foreign framework triggering nationalist counter-mobilisation — recurs structurally across the entire period.

**4. 理論框架的演變 Lǐlùn Kuàngjià de Yǎnbiàn — Evolution of Theoretical Frameworks**
Culture-historical empiricism (Lǐ Jì) → Marxist historical materialism (Guō Mòruò) → regional systems theory (Sū Bǐngqí) → interaction sphere model (Zhāng Guāngzhí) → post-processual pluralism. Each framework was simultaneously intellectual advance and political instrument.

**5. 制度連續性與斷裂 Zhìdù Liánxùxìng yǔ Duànliè — Institutional Continuity and Rupture**
1949 split the archaeological establishment across the Taiwan Strait. Same collections, same sites, radically divergent interpretive trajectories. The rupture's consequences — isolation of Taiwan scholars from new field data; mainland scholars constrained by ideology — shaped the discipline for four decades.

**Yangshao Culture — Overview** | #T1 #T2 #T3 | 🔭 [archaeology] |

---

仰韶文化 (Yǎngsháo wénhuà) is a Neolithic archaeological culture of the Yellow River middle reaches, c. 5000–3000 BCE. Named after the type-site at Yangshao village, Henan, discovered by Johan Gunnar Andersson in 1921 — the first prehistoric culture identified in China.

**Geography:** Core zone spanning Henan, Shaanxi, Shanxi; extensions into Gansu, Qinghai, Hebei.

**Subsistence:** Millet agriculture (*Panicum miliaceum*, *Setaria italica*) as primary base; pig and dog domestication; hunting, fishing, gathering supplementary.

**Material signature:**
- Painted red-on-buff/red-on-cream pottery — geometric, fish, human-face, deer motifs; wheel-absent, hand-built
- Semi-subterranean round/square pit-houses in nucleated villages (e.g., Banpo 半坡, Xi'an)
- Woven textile impressions on ceramics; silk filament evidence at Xishuipo
- Bone tools, polished stone axes, jade precursors

**Social structure:** Evidence debated — likely segmentary lineage-based communities; cemetery data at Banpo suggests possible gender/age differentiation but not pronounced hierarchy. Later phases (Miaodigou 庙底沟 sub-phase, c. 4000–3500 BCE) show wider cultural diffusion, possibly prestige-network expansion.

**Significance:** Ancestral to later Longshan 龙山 culture; sits at origin-debates for Chinese Bronze Age civilisation and proto-Huaxia ethnogenesis discourse.

---

Yangshao is not monolithic — Chinese archaeology partitions it into regional traditions and chronological phases:

---

### Core Sequence (Central Plains / Guanzhong)

| Phase | Date BCE | Key Sites | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banpo 半坡 | 5000–4000 | Banpo, Jiangzhai | Red ware, fish motifs, round pit-houses, moated villages |
| Miaodigou I 庙底沟 | 4000–3500 | Miaodigou, Xipo | Rose/petal motifs, expansion westward & northward; widest spatial reach |
| Xiwangcun 西王村 | 3500–3000 | Xiwangcun | Transitional to Longshan; painted ware declines |

---

### Western Extension (Gansu–Qinghai Corridor)

Technically post-Yangshao but derived — sometimes grouped as **Gansu Yangshao**:

- **Majiayao 马家窑** c. 3300–2050 BCE — spiraling black-on-orange pottery, most elaborate painted ceramic tradition in prehistoric China
- → sub-phases: Majiayao → Banshan → Machang

---

### Northern Variants

- **Dadiwan 大地湾** (Gansu) — predates canonical Yangshao; earliest millet evidence ~5800 BCE; painted pottery precedes Banpo
- **Hougang I 后岗一期** (Hebei/Henan border) — eastward variant, contacts Dawenkou culture

---

### Southern/Lower Wei River

- **Qujialing 屈家岭** contact zone — Yangshao traits merge with middle Yangtze Neolithic traditions here; fuzzy boundary

---

### Key Interpretive Point

Miaodigou I is the pivotal phase — its pottery motifs spread across ~1M km², leading scholars (e.g., Su Bingqi 苏秉琦) to propose it represents an early **interaction sphere** rather than a single ethnic/political unit. Some read it as a prestige-goods network; others as migration/colonization.

## 蘇秉琦 (Sū Bǐngqí, 1909–1997)

China's most influential 20th-century archaeologist. Rejected unilinear "Central Plains cradle" 中原中心論 (Zhōngyuán zhōngxīn lùn) orthodoxy.

## 區系類型論 Qūxì Lèixíng Lùn
**"Regional Systems Typology Theory"**

Proposed ~1970s–80s. Core argument: Chinese civilisation emerged **polycentric** — multiple independent regional cultures developing in parallel, each with distinct trajectories, occasionally interacting.

---

| # | Region | 地區 | Key Cultures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central Plains | 中原區 | Yangshao, Longshan |
| 2 | Shandong | 山東區 | Dawenkou 大汶口, Longshan 龍山 |
| 3 | Yangtze Delta | 江浙區 | Hemudu 河姆渡, Liangzhu 良渚 |
| 4 | Middle Yangtze | 湘鄂區 | Daxi 大溪, Qujialing 屈家嶺 |
| 5 | Northeast | 燕遼區 | Hongshan 紅山 |
| 6 | Southeast Coast | 東南區 | Various regional cultures |

---

### 三部曲 Sān Bùqǔ — Three-Stage Developmental Arc

蘇秉琦 proposed civilisation formation follows:

**1. 古國 Gǔguó** — "Ancient state"; chiefdom-level polities, ranked society, no writing
→ e.g., 紅山 Hóngshān (Liaoning jade-ritual complex)

**2. 方國 Fāngguó** — "Regional state"; territorially defined polities, proto-dynastic
→ e.g., 良渚 Liángzhǔ (Zhejiang; monumental earthworks, jade hierarchy)

**3. 帝國 Dìguó** — Unified empire; Qin/Han model

He argued 古國 appeared c. 5500–5000 BP — **predating Xia-Shang-Zhou** by millennia, challenging dynastic-centric historiography.

---

### 滿天星斗 Mǎntiān Xīngdǒu
**"Stars Filling the Sky"**

His famous metaphor: Chinese civilisation origins resemble a sky full of stars — many simultaneous glowing centres — not a single sun radiating outward from the Yellow River.

Contrasted with the older 黃河中心論 (Huánghé zhōngxīn lùn) — Yellow River centrism.

---

### Significance & Critique

**Impact:**
- Legitimised regional archaeology programs outside Henan/Shaanxi
- Enabled 紅山, 良渚, 三星堆 (Sānxīngduī) to be framed as *civilisations*, not periphery
- Influenced UNESCO 良渚 World Heritage arguments

**Critiques:**
- Region boundaries somewhat arbitrary; defined partly by modern provincial logic
- 三部曲 schema risks teleology — projecting empire as endpoint
- Interaction/diffusion mechanisms between regions undertheorised
- Later scholars (e.g., 張光直 Zhāng Guāngzhí) proposed networked **interaction sphere** models as complement

## 中原中心論 — Core Claim

Yellow River middle reaches (Henan/Shaanxi core) = singular cradle of Chinese civilisation. All other regions derivative, peripheral, or recipient of Central Plains diffusion.

---

## 起源 Qǐyuán — Intellectual Origins

### 1. Classical Textual Tradition (pre-modern)
- 三皇五帝 (Sānhuáng Wǔdì) mythic geography centred on 中原
- 《史記》(Shǐjì) — Sima Qian's dynastic frame anchors legitimacy in Yellow River basin
- 華夷之辨 (Huá-Yí zhī biàn) — civilised centre vs. barbaric periphery binary; spatial hierarchy baked into classical cosmology
- 正統論 (Zhèngtǒng lùn) — "orthodox succession" theory; legitimate rule flows from Central Plains lineage

### 2. Early 20th Century — Nationalist Archaeology
- 1928: 殷墟 (Yīnxū) excavations, Anyang — spectacular confirmation of Shang dynasty in Henan
- Institutionally reinforced Central Plains priority
- 中央研究院 (Zhōngyāng Yánjiūyuàn) — Academia Sinica digs concentrated in Henan
- **安特生 Āntèshēng** (Andersson) paradox: his Yangshao discovery was *used* to anchor Chinese origins in Yellow River even while his "painted pottery westward diffusion" hypothesis implied outside contact — provoking nationalist backlash

### 3. Andersson's 彩陶西來說 Cǎitáo Xī Lái Shuō
- Andersson (1923) proposed Yangshao painted pottery derived from Central Asian/Western sources
- Chinese scholars (especially 梁思永 Liáng Sīyǒng, 李濟 Lǐ Jì) mobilised against this — defending indigenous origins
- Ironic result: defence of indigeneity **reinforced** Central Plains centrism as the locus of that indigenous origin

---

## 政治化 Zhèngzhìhuà — Political Instrumentalisation

### Republican Period (1912–1949)
- 中華民族 (Zhōnghuá Mínzú) — unified national identity project required singular cultural origin
- 黃帝 (Huángdì) Yellow Emperor cult revival; anchored in 中原
- Archaeology conscripted to prove continuous civilisational presence against Japanese imperial claims of Chinese cultural inferiority
- 疑古派 (Yígǔ pài) — "Doubting Antiquity" school (顧頡剛 Gù Jiégāng) challenged mythic history but didn't displace geographic centrism

### PRC Period (1949–1980s)
- Marxist unilinear evolutionism (primitive → slave → feudal → socialist) mapped onto Central Plains sequence
- Henan/Shaanxi sequences = the ladder; regional cultures = rungs or deviations
- 夏商周斷代工程 (Xià-Shāng-Zhōu Duàndài Gōngchéng) — Xia-Shang-Zhou Chronology Project (1996–2000): state-funded, politically pressured to confirm textual dynastic sequence archaeologically; critics noted Central Plains bias in site selection and interpretation
- Minority-region archaeology systematically underfunded relative to Yellow River basin projects

### 統一多民族國家 Tǒngyī Duōmínzú Guójiā
- "Unified multi-ethnic state" official ideology creates tension:
- Needs peripheral cultures acknowledged (legitimises current territorial claims over Xinjiang, Tibet, Northeast)
- But needs them subordinated to Han-centred civilisational narrative
- 中原中心論 serves Han ethnic primacy; its partial relaxation serves territorial legitimacy — political utility pulls both directions simultaneously

## 張光直 Zhāng Guāngzhí (1931–2001)

Born Běijīng; family fled to Taiwan 1946. Studied under 李濟 Lǐ Jì (Academia Sinica, Taiwan) — direct intellectual lineage to early Chinese scientific archaeology. Emigrated to USA; PhD Harvard 1960; faculty Harvard & Yale; eventually Harvard Peabody Museum curator.

Uniquely positioned: trained in Chinese classical scholarship AND Anglophone anthropological archaeology (processualism, systems theory). Fluent in both traditions — rare for his generation.

Key works:
- *The Archaeology of Ancient China* (1963, four editions) — standard Western reference for decades
- *Art, Myth and Ritual* (1983) — shamanism, bronze iconography, political power
- *Shang Civilization* (1980)

Returned intellectually to PRC archaeology from the 1970s onwards as Sino-US relations thawed; bridged Taiwan/PRC/Western scholarship institutionally.

## 兩種模型介紹 — Model Introductions

### 蘇秉琦：區系類型論
Core logic: **differentiation**. Chinese prehistoric space contains discrete regional systems, each with independent developmental trajectory. Civilisation = multiple autonomous florescence events. Interaction exists but is secondary to regional integrity. Metaphor: 滿天星斗 — each star burns independently.

### 張光直：相互作用圈 Xiānghù Zuòyòng Quān
**"Interaction Sphere"** — borrowed and adapted from Joseph Caldwell's 1964 Hopewell Interaction Sphere concept (American archaeology). Core logic: **integration**. Regional cultures across Chinese Neolithic are linked through sustained exchange of prestige goods, ritual knowledge, iconographic conventions, and élite networks. Shared elements are not proof of common origin or diffusion from one centre — they are products of **horizontal interaction** across a vast connected zone.

First systematically applied in his 1986 paper: argued a Chinese Interaction Sphere existed by at least 4000–3000 BCE connecting cultures from Manchuria to Guangdong.

---

## 比較對照 — Comparison

### Fundamental Ontology

| Dimension | 蘇秉琦 | 張光直 |
|---|---|---|
| Primary unit | Region 區系 | Network / sphere 圈 |
| Analytical focus | Internal regional sequence | Inter-regional linkage |
| Cultural boundaries | Relatively bounded, definable | Permeable, overlapping |
| Driving force | Endogenous regional development | Exogenous exchange & interaction |
| Spatial metaphor | Stars 星斗 | Web / sphere 圈 |

---

### On Shared Cultural Traits

**蘇:** Shared traits (e.g., jade use, painted pottery motifs) explained by **parallel evolution** within regions or by one region influencing another through contact — but each region retains distinct identity. Miaodigou expansion = one regional tradition spreading.

**張:** Shared traits = evidence of **sustained network participation**. Jade ritual complexes appearing simultaneously in 紅山 (Northeast), 良渚 (Yangtze Delta), 凌家灘 Língjiātān (Anhui) not coincidental — reflect élite interaction sphere where prestige items, cosmological knowledge, and ritual practices circulate horizontally. No single origin required.

---

### On Civilisation Formation

**蘇 — 三部曲 teleology:**
古國 → 方國 → 帝國
Civilisation emerges *within* regions, staged developmentally. Hongshan = ancient state on its own regional trajectory. The endpoint (empire) is implicitly the synthesis of regional florescence.

**張 — Networked transformation:**
No clean stage sequence. Élites across regions participate in shared ritual-political economy; this *network* is what eventually gets consolidated. Bronze Age states (Shang especially) emerge by **capturing and centralising** pre-existing interaction sphere networks — controlling jade, bronze, shamanic knowledge flows. Power = control of the network, not just regional dominance.

---

### On the Central Plains

**蘇:** Central Plains = one region among six, albeit influential. Explicitly decentralises it — political intervention against orthodoxy.

**張:** More nuanced but arguably re-centres: the Shang state is his primary object of analysis, and Shang = Yellow River basin. His interaction sphere *explains how* Shang could synthesise so much — but Shang remains the analytical culmination. Critics note this partially reinstates Central Plains priority through the back door.

---

### Methodological Roots

**蘇:** Typological, stratigraphic — classical culture-historical archaeology. Empiricist. Theory emerges inductively from ceramic sequences.

**張:** Anthropological archaeology — systems theory, structural anthropology (Lévi-Strauss influence visible in *Art, Myth and Ritual*), comparative civilisation studies. Explicitly theoretical. Brings Weber, Marx, and American processualism into dialogue with Chinese material.

---

### Weaknesses — Each Model

**蘇秉琦:**
- Region boundaries underspecified — partly administrative/modern
- 三部曲 teleological; empire as inevitable endpoint
- Interaction mechanisms between regions underdeveloped
- Risks reifying regional cultures as ethnically bounded

**張光直:**
- Interaction sphere boundaries equally vague — what's inside/outside?
- Borrowed concept (Hopewell) may not translate cleanly to Chinese scale/complexity
- Élite-network focus marginalises subsistence economy, ecology, non-élite agency
- Shang-centric culmination sits uneasily with polycentric premise

---

### Synthesis Attempts

Later scholars treat the models as **complementary scales**:
- 蘇's regions = nodes
- 張's sphere = the edges connecting nodes

嚴文明's 重瓣花朵 is partly a political compromise but also a genuine attempt to nest regional integrity *within* a connected whole — Core Plains as densest interaction node, not sole origin.

## 安特生 Āntèshēng (1874–1960)

Johan Gunnar Andersson. Born Knista, Örebro County, Sweden. Geology PhD Uppsala 1901. Early career as polar researcher — participated in Otto Nordenskjöld's 1901–03 Antarctic expedition. Appointed Director of the Geological Survey of Sweden 1906. Reputation: rigorous field geologist, opportunistic intellectual, institutionally well-connected.

---

## 來華 Lái Huá — Arrival in China

Recruited 1914 by the newly established Chinese Republic as **Mining Adviser** 礦政顧問 to the Ministry of Agriculture and Commerce. Mandate: locate exploitable mineral and coal deposits for industrialising state. Arrived Beijing; established working relationships with nascent Chinese geological community including 丁文江 Dīng Wénjiāng and 翁文灝 Wēng Wénhào — founders of modern Chinese geology.

Operated under Beiyang government patronage; institutionally protected but dependent on Chinese governmental goodwill — a tension that would later complicate his legacy.

---

## 周口店 Zhōukǒudiàn — Dragon Bone Hill

### Initial Discovery
1918: Andersson investigating fossil sites near Beijing. Local pharmacies sold 龍骨 lónggǔ ("dragon bones") — ground as traditional medicine — actually fossil mammal bones. Traced supply chains to limestone karst deposits at 周口店 Zhōukǒudiàn, 42km southwest of Beijing.

Recognised scientific potential immediately. Identified the site as a significant Pleistocene fossil locality. Began preliminary excavations.

### Palaeoanthropological Significance
1921: Andersson found quartz fragments at Zhōukǒudiàn inconsistent with local geology — interpreted as possible evidence of human activity. Famously declared to colleague Otto Zdansky: *"I have a feeling that there lie here the remains of one of our ancestors."*

Zdansky's subsequent excavations (1921–23) recovered two hominin teeth, published 1926. This triggered the systematic excavations by 步達生 Bùdáshēng (Davidson Black) and eventually 裴文中 Péi Wénzhōng's 1929 discovery of the first complete 北京人 Běijīng Rén (Peking Man / *Homo erectus pekinensis*) skullcap.

Andersson did not discover Peking Man directly — but his geological intuition and institutional mobilisation made the discovery chain possible.

---

## 仰韶 Yǎngsháo — The Defining Discovery

### Excavation
1921: While surveying Henan for minerals, local contacts directed Andersson to 仰韶村 Yǎngsháo Cūn, Mianchi County. Recognised painted pottery sherds eroding from loess deposits. Negotiated access; excavated with Chinese assistant Liu Changshan.

Recovered: painted red-and-black pottery, polished stone tools, bone implements, ash deposits — unmistakably a prehistoric settlement site. First scientifically excavated Neolithic site in China.

Published: *An Early Chinese Culture* (1923), Geological Survey of China Bulletin.

### Institutional Impact
Established that China had a prehistoric past recoverable through scientific archaeology — not merely textual antiquity. Forced Chinese intellectual establishment to confront deep prehistory as a domain of inquiry separate from classical historiography.

Triggered founding of Chinese prehistoric archaeology as a discipline — though Chinese scholars would rapidly move to control and redefine it.

---

## 彩陶西來說 Cǎitáo Xī Lái Shuō — The Painted Pottery Hypothesis

### The Argument
Andersson noticed striking visual similarities between Yangshao painted pottery and Neolithic painted ceramics from Anau (Turkmenistan), Tripolye (Ukraine), and Susa (Iran). Proposed 1923: Chinese Neolithic painted pottery tradition derived from westward diffusion — ultimately connected to a broad Eurasian painted pottery complex stretching from Eastern Europe to East Asia.

Implied: Chinese civilisation had partial external origins. The Yellow River basin was a *recipient* as well as generator of cultural innovation.

### Reception — Nationalist Backlash
Catastrophic reception among Chinese scholars. Interpreted as:
- Denial of Chinese civilisational autochthony
- Implicit alignment with Japanese imperial narratives of Chinese cultural inferiority
- Continuation of Western diffusionist assumptions (Elliot Smith, Perry) that civilisation spread from Near East

李濟 Lǐ Jì, 梁思永 Liáng Sīyǒng, and others methodically challenged the ceramic parallels — arguing typological similarities were superficial, chronologies didn't align, and independent invention was more parsimonious.

By 1930s the hypothesis was largely abandoned — subsequent radiocarbon dating confirmed Yangshao predates proposed Western parallels in several cases, decisively refuting the diffusionist sequence.

### Unintended Legacy
The mobilisation *against* 彩陶西來說 accelerated development of rigorous indigenous Chinese archaeology — 李濟's Anyang excavations (1928–37) partly driven by imperative to demonstrate China's independent Bronze Age. The wrong theory productively shaped the right discipline.

---

## 甘肅考察 Gānsù Kǎochá — Gansu Surveys

1923–24: Andersson conducted extensive surveys through Gansu and Qinghai — partly following painted pottery distribution westward to test his own hypothesis. Collected enormous quantities of material including:

- 馬家窯 Mǎjiāyáo painted pottery (identified but not yet named as distinct culture)
- 齊家文化 Qíjiā wénhuà (Qijia culture) materials — copper/bronze objects, absence of painted pottery
- 辛店文化 Xīndiàn wénhuà materials

Shipped thousands of objects to Sweden — now held at Museum of Far Eastern Antiquities (Östasiatiska museet), Stockholm. This removal became a persistent grievance in Chinese archaeological historiography; negotiations over repatriation occurred intermittently through 20th century.

Andersson's Gansu sequence — though stratigraphically imprecise by modern standards — established the first framework for understanding the Gansu-Qinghai Neolithic corridor, later refined into the Majiayao/Banshan/Machang sub-phase sequence.

---

## 離華與晚年 Lí Huá yǔ Wǎnnián — Departure & Later Life

Left China 1925 as political climate shifted — Nationalist movement increasingly hostile to foreign researchers controlling Chinese cultural patrimony. Returned to Sweden; became director of the Museum of Far Eastern Antiquities 1926, position he held until 1939.

Continued publishing on Chinese prehistory from Stockholm. *Children of the Yellow Earth* (1934) — popular account of his China fieldwork, widely read in Europe.

Later years involved in organising the Swedish collections and corresponding with Chinese colleagues as relations permitted. Died Stockholm 1960 — living long enough to see Peking Man's significance fully established, though the original fossils themselves had mysteriously disappeared in 1941 during Japanese occupation.

---

## 歷史評價 Lìshǐ Píngjià — Historical Assessment

Andersson occupies genuinely ambiguous position in Chinese archaeological historiography:

**Acknowledged:** Discoverer of Yangshao; instrumental in Zhoukoudian chain; introduced scientific field methods; established China had recoverable prehistoric past.

**Criticised:** 彩陶西來說 diffusionism; removal of material to Sweden; operating within colonial-era framework of foreign expertise extracting knowledge/objects from China.

**Current PRC framing:** Grudging credit for discovery, emphasis on Chinese scholars who corrected and superseded him — particularly 李濟, 裴文中, and the Anyang excavation teams. Andersson as necessary but flawed precursor, quickly transcended.

The deeper irony: his wrong theory (西來說) and his right discovery (仰韶) both served, in opposite ways, to consolidate the Central Plains centrism he himself had complicated.

# 李濟 Lǐ Jì (1896–1979)

## 傳記 Zhuànjì — Biography

Born Zhōngxiáng, Húběi. Classical education followed by study at Tsinghua's preparatory program for US-bound students. BA Clark University (psychology/sociology); PhD Harvard 1923 — **first Chinese person to receive a Harvard anthropology doctorate.** Dissertation: *The Formation of the Chinese People* — physical anthropology, craniometry, racial classification. Already wrestling with questions of Chinese ethnic origins from a scientific rather than textual frame.

Returned China 1923. Joined 清華大學 Qīnghuá Dàxué faculty. Encountered Andersson's Yangshao work; recognised need for Chinese-led scientific archaeology to counter foreign narrative control. Connected with 中央研究院 Zhōngyāng Yánjiūyuàn (Academia Sinica) upon its founding 1928 under Nanjing Nationalist government. Appointed head of its archaeology section — position that made him institutional patriarch of Chinese scientific archaeology for a generation.

Retreated to Taiwan 1949 with Academia Sinica collections and staff. Continued at National Taiwan University; trained subsequent generation of Taiwan archaeologists. Died Taipei 1979 — never returned to mainland; never saw Anyang again after 1949.

---

## 西陰村 Xīyīncūn — First Independent Chinese Excavation

### The Dig
1926: Lǐ Jì excavated 西陰村 Xīyīncūn, Shanxi — a Yangshao culture site. Strategically significant: **first prehistoric site excavated entirely by Chinese archaeologists** without Western direction. Explicit nationalist-scientific statement — Chinese scholars could conduct rigorous field archaeology independently.

### The Silkworm
Recovered a half-cut silkworm cocoon — apparently sliced by a sharp implement. Lǐ Jì argued this constituted evidence of sericulture (silk cultivation) in Neolithic Yangshao context, potentially pushing Chinese silk origins back millennia. The identification was contested — later analysts debated whether the cut was ancient or post-depositional. Regardless, the claim fed directly into narratives of autochthonous Chinese civilisational achievement.

### Methodological Contribution
Xīyīncūn introduced systematic grid excavation, stratigraphic recording, and artifact cataloguing methods drawn from Lǐ Jì's Harvard training — establishing standards that would define subsequent Chinese field archaeology. The excavation report became a methodological template.

---

## 殷墟 Yīnxū — Anyang and the Shang Dynasty

### Background
Oracle bones 甲骨文 jiǎgǔwén had been appearing in antiquities markets since 1899, traced to fields near 安陽 Ānyáng, Henan. Scholar 王國維 Wáng Guówéi demonstrated through textual analysis that inscriptions recorded divination records of late Shang kings — potentially locating the legendary capital 殷 Yīn archaeologically. This was the site Lǐ Jì's team would excavate.

### Fifteen Seasons (1928–1937)
Academia Sinica conducted fifteen field seasons at Yīnxū before Japanese invasion halted work. Lǐ Jì directed overall program; individual seasons led by rotating team members. Scale was unprecedented in Chinese archaeology — large trenches, royal tomb complexes, palace foundations, workshops, residential areas systematically uncovered.

### Royal Tombs 王陵 Wánglíng
The eleven large cruciform shaft tombs in the royal cemetery 侯家莊 Hóujiāzhuāng yielded staggering material: bronze ritual vessels, jade objects, lacquerwork, bone carvings, chariots, horse burials, and mass human sacrifice deposits. Single tombs contained hundreds of sacrificial victims — decapitated, bound. Scale of ritual violence exceeded anything previously documented for Bronze Age East Asia. Lǐ Jì's careful recording of burial contexts established that Shang royal ideology was built on systematic sacrificial violence at a scale requiring substantial organisational infrastructure.

### Bronze Vessels & Craft Production
Anyang bronzes — including 司母戊鼎 Sīmǔwù Dǐng (now called 後母戊鼎 Hòumǔwù Dǐng), at 832kg the largest known ancient bronze object — demonstrated extraordinary metallurgical sophistication. Lǐ Jì documented workshop areas showing production was palace-controlled, specialised, and organised as a state industry rather than dispersed craft production. This fed into his broader argument that Chinese Bronze Age represented an independently achieved civilisational peak, requiring no Western diffusionist explanation.

### Answering Andersson
The Anyang evidence was Lǐ Jì's decisive rebuttal to 彩陶西來說. Here was unambiguously sophisticated, complex, literate Bronze Age civilisation in the Yellow River basin — with no credible Western prototype. The oracle bone script 甲骨文 was demonstrably ancestral to later Chinese writing with no evident external origin. Lǐ Jì argued stratigraphy and typology showed continuous indigenous development from Neolithic through Shang. The diffusionist hypothesis died at Anyang.

---

## 理論貢獻 Lǐlùn Gòngxiàn — Theoretical Contributions

Lǐ Jì resisted grand theoretical systems — empiricist by temperament and training. His lasting theoretical contributions were methodological and institutional: rigorous stratigraphic excavation, systematic artifact typology, integration of physical anthropology with material culture analysis. He trained virtually every significant Chinese archaeologist of the mid-20th century — his influence operated through people as much as publications.

His *Anyang* (1977), published late in life in English for Western audiences, remains a synthetic account of the excavations combining archaeological and textual evidence — unusual methodological integration for the period.

# 裴文中 Péi Wénzhōng (1904–1982)

## 傳記 Zhuànjì — Biography

Born Fēngnán, Héběi. Geology and biology degree, Peking University 1927. Joined Geological Survey of China immediately upon graduation — assigned to 周口店 Zhōukǒudiàn excavations under French palaeontologist 德日進 Dé Rìjìn (Pierre Teilhard de Chardin) and Canadian anatomist 步達生 Bùdáshēng (Davidson Black). Young, relatively junior researcher when he made the discovery that defined his career.

Later studied in France under 步日耶 Bùrìyē (Henri Breuil) — leading Palaeolithic archaeologist; returned China 1937. Remained on mainland through 1949; continued at Institute of Vertebrate Palaeontology and Palaeoanthropology (IVPP) under PRC. Unlike Lǐ Jì, Péi navigated the communist transition and sustained a mainland career — though suffered during Cultural Revolution. Died Beijing 1982.

---

## 北京人 Běijīng Rén — Peking Man Discovery

### The Morning of December 2, 1929
Péi Wénzhōng was supervising excavations in Cave 1 at Zhōukǒudiàn when, in late afternoon fading light, workers exposed a hominin skullcap embedded in consolidated cave breccia at approximately 40m depth. Péi made the decision — against protocol — to extract it himself rather than wait for senior supervision, reportedly wrapping it in cloth and paper and carrying it back to Beijing personally.

The specimen: an almost complete *Homo erectus* calvaria (skullcap), cranial capacity approximately 915cc — later designated 北京直立人 Běijīng Zhílì Rén. Davidson Black announced the find internationally; global sensation followed. Péi published the initial description in *Bulletin of the Geological Society of China* (1929).

### Significance of the Discovery
Peking Man pushed confirmed human habitation of East Asia to approximately 400,000–700,000 BP (modern dating; estimates have shifted). Crucially for Chinese intellectual context: demonstrated that the ancestors of humanity had lived in China independently of any African or European centre — a point of enormous national significance. China was not a latecomer to human prehistory; it was a primary locus of human evolution as then understood.

The multi-regionalism vs. out-of-Africa debate would later complicate this — but in 1929, Peking Man seemed to confirm deep indigenous human roots in Chinese soil.

---

## 理論：舊石器文化 Jiù Shíqì Wénhuà — Palaeolithic Framework

### Building Chinese Palaeolithic Archaeology
Beyond the skullcap, Péi Wénzhōng's systematic excavations at Zhōukǒudiàn over subsequent years recovered the material basis for understanding Peking Man's behaviour: thousands of stone tools (chopper-chopping tool industry — notably absent of Acheulean handaxes, later termed the **Movius Line** problem), burned bones interpreted as evidence of fire control, and dense faunal assemblages.

Péi argued the stone tool assemblage represented a distinct East Asian Palaeolithic tradition — not derivative of Western Acheulean industries. This fitted neatly into the broader pattern of Chinese scholars arguing for independent regional development, now extended deep into the Pleistocene.

### 山頂洞人 Shāndǐngdòng Rén — Upper Cave Man
1933: Péi excavated the Upper Cave at Zhōukǒudiàn, recovering anatomically modern human remains (Upper Palaeolithic, c.30,000–10,000 BP), decorated perforated ornaments, and ochre — evidence of symbolic behaviour. Demonstrated Zhōukǒudiàn as a multi-period site spanning from *Homo erectus* through modern humans — extraordinary stratigraphic depth for a single locality.

---

## 化石失蹤 Huàshí Shīzōng — The Disappearing Fossils

The original Peking Man fossils — accumulated across seasons of excavation, representing over 40 individuals — disappeared in December 1941 during Japanese occupation of Beijing. The US Marine detachment guarding the fossils for shipment to the United States was captured at Qinhuangdao following Pearl Harbor; the fossils vanished and have never been recovered.

Péi Wénzhōng, who had remained in Beijing during the occupation, was interrogated about the fossils' fate. Various theories persist: buried somewhere in Beijing; lost at sea; in a Japanese collection; in private American hands. The disappearance remains one of archaeology's most consequential unsolved mysteries. Cast replicas survive; the originals do not.

---

# 安陽發掘團隊 Ānyáng Fājué Tuánduì — The Anyang Team

## 梁思永 Liáng Sīyǒng (1904–1954)

### Biography
Son of reformist intellectual 梁啟超 Liáng Qǐchāo — grew up surrounded by the most sophisticated Chinese intellectual discourse of the early 20th century. BA and MA Harvard; studied under Alfred Tozzer and Roland Dixon — rigorous Boasian anthropological training. Returned China 1930; joined Academia Sinica archaeology section under Lǐ Jì.

Physical constitution was fragile — suffered tuberculosis chronically; spent significant periods bedridden yet continued scholarly output. Died 1954, aged 49, having never fully recovered health. Remained on mainland after 1949 — unlike Lǐ Jì, did not evacuate to Taiwan.

### 後岡三疊層 Hòugāng Sān Diécéng — The Hougang Stratigraphy
1931: Liáng Sīyǒng excavated 後岡 Hòugāng site, Anyang vicinity. Identified a three-layer superimposition in a single stratigraphic column: Yangshao (bottom) → Longshan (middle) → Shang (top). **The Hougang Triple Stratification** was a decisive empirical contribution — demonstrating for the first time in a single excavation that Yangshao, Longshan, and Shang represented a chronological sequence rather than coeval regional variants.

This resolved a major outstanding debate: Longshan and Yangshao had been thought by some to be contemporary regional cultures rather than sequential. Hòugāng proved the developmental sequence stratigraphically, providing the empirical backbone for a unilinear developmental narrative of North Chinese Neolithic into Bronze Age. Ironically this finding, while scientifically rigorous, also reinforced Central Plains sequence as the master chronology — contributing structurally to 中原中心論.

### Typological Work
Liáng Sīyǒng developed systematic ceramic typology methods for the Anyang assemblages — classifying vessel forms, decorative motifs, and paste compositions to establish relative chronologies within the Shang sequence. His typological frameworks were used by subsequent researchers for decades.

---

## 董作賓 Dǒng Zuòbīn (1895–1963)

### Biography
Born Nányáng, Hénan. Self-taught classical scholar; joined Academia Sinica 1928 despite lacking formal Western academic credentials — recruited for classical Chinese expertise rather than scientific training. Represented a different intellectual model from Lǐ Jì and Liáng Sīyǒng — philologist rather than anthropologist.

Followed Academia Sinica to Taiwan 1949. Taught at National Taiwan University; continued oracle bone research until death.

### 甲骨文 Jiǎgǔwén — Oracle Bone Scholarship
Dǒng Zuòbīn's contribution was the decipherment and periodisation of oracle bone inscriptions recovered at Anyang. He developed a five-period chronological classification of oracle bone script styles — correlating calligraphic features, vocabulary, and divination topics with specific Shang reigns. This 甲骨斷代 jiǎgǔ duàndài (oracle bone periodisation) system allowed inscriptions to be dated within the Shang sequence and cross-referenced with excavation contexts.

His monumental *甲骨文斷代研究例* Jiǎgǔwén Duàndài Yánjiū Lì (1933) established oracle bone studies as a rigorous discipline. By correlating inscription content — royal names, ritual cycles, astronomical records — with archaeological stratigraphy, Dǒng effectively created a relative chronology for late Shang that anchored the archaeological sequence in textual history. The integration of 地下材料 dìxià cáiliào (underground materials) with 地上文獻 dìshàng wénxiàn (above-ground texts) became a defining methodological principle of Chinese archaeology — later theorised explicitly by 王國維 Wáng Guówéi as the 二重證據法 Èrchóng Zhèngjù Fǎ (Double Evidence Method).

---

## 石璋如 Shí Zhāngrú (1902–2004)

### Biography
Extraordinarily long-lived — died aged 102. Student of Lǐ Jì; participated in Anyang excavations from early seasons. Followed Academia Sinica to Taiwan; taught at National Taiwan University for decades. Outlived virtually all his contemporaries; his memory became a primary oral history source for the Anyang excavation period.

### Field Documentation
Shí Zhāngrú's contribution was meticulous field recording — architectural remains, pit features, spatial distribution of artifacts. His documentation of Shang palace-temple foundations at Anyang established the spatial organisation of the royal centre: rammed-earth 夯土 hāngtǔ platform foundations arranged in orientated complexes suggesting cosmologically organised ceremonial architecture. This spatial analysis informed later interpretations of Shang ritual geography and the relationship between architectural space and political power.

---

## 集體貢獻 Jítǐ Gòngxiàn — Collective Achievement

The Anyang team collectively produced what remains the foundational dataset of Chinese Bronze Age archaeology. Their achievement was not merely discovery but **methodological institution-building** — establishing that Chinese archaeology could meet international scientific standards while being directed entirely by Chinese scholars, answering the implicit challenge of Andersson's era.

The forced interruption of 1937 (Japanese invasion) and the 1949 political division — splitting the team between Taiwan and mainland — fragmented institutional continuity. PRC archaeology resumed Anyang excavations in the 1950s under new political frameworks; Taiwan preserved the original collections and senior scholars. The same sites, the same materials, interpreted through increasingly divergent political-intellectual frameworks across the Taiwan Strait for the next four decades.

## 框架 Kuàngjià — Framing the Problem

1949 is not merely a political caesura — it is an **epistemological rupture** in Chinese archaeology. The same sites, same artifact classes, same oracle bone corpus became objects of fundamentally different interpretive frameworks depending on which side of the Taiwan Strait the scholar sat. The divergence operates at multiple levels simultaneously: theoretical, methodological, ideological, and institutional.

---

# 民國時期 Mínguó Shíqī — Republican Period Interpretation (pre-1949)

## 理論基礎 Lǐlùn Jīchǔ — Theoretical Foundations

The Academia Sinica team operated within **culture-historical archaeology** as practised at Harvard and in the Boasian anthropological tradition. Core assumptions:

- Archaeology recovers cultural sequences through stratigraphy and typology
- Cultures are identified by artifact assemblages; change explained by diffusion, migration, or independent invention
- Minimal theoretical superstructure imposed on empirical data
- Chronology-building and cultural classification as primary goals

Explicitly **anti-diffusionist** in reaction to Andersson — but not anti-empiricist. Lǐ Jì's empiricism was almost aggressive; he distrusted grand theoretical systems, including Marxist ones he would later encounter from a distance.

---

## 文明成就論 Wénmíng Chéngjiù Lùn — Civilisational Achievement Framework

The dominant interpretive lens was **demonstration of indigenous civilisational achievement.** Anyang evidence was read primarily as proof:

- China achieved Bronze Age civilisation independently
- Shang state represented sophisticated political organisation, ritual complexity, and technological mastery without external stimulus
- Chinese civilisation was autochthonous, continuous, and comparable to any world civilisation

This was archaeology in service of **cultural nationalism** — not crude propaganda but genuine scholarly conviction shaped by the political context of semicolonial humiliation and Japanese aggression. The implicit audience included Western scholars (proving China's place in world civilisational history) and Japanese imperial narratives (refuting claims of Chinese cultural dependency).

---

## 商代社會性質 Shāngdài Shèhuì Xìngzhì — Shang Social Character

Republican-period scholars did not systematically theorise Shang social formation in structural terms. Lǐ Jì and Dǒng Zuòbīn were primarily concerned with:

- Establishing Shang as historical (not legendary) dynasty
- Correlating oracle bone king lists with 《史記》Shǐjì records
- Documenting material culture with maximum precision

When social interpretation appeared, it was broadly **feudal** in a loose classical Chinese sense — 封建 fēngjiàn as hierarchical politico-ritual order, not as Marxist mode of production. Shang kingship was analysed through ritual and political lens: the king as supreme diviner, controller of ancestral communication, ritual centre of a hierarchically organised polity.

Human sacrifice was documented with clinical precision by Lǐ Jì's team — scale, context, skeletal treatment — but interpreted primarily as **ritual practice** within a cosmological system, not as evidence of class exploitation or mode of production.

---

## 文字與文明 Wénzì yǔ Wénmíng — Script as Civilisational Marker

Dǒng Zuòbīn's oracle bone periodisation operated within a framework that treated 甲骨文 as direct window into Shang royal consciousness — divination records, ritual calendars, military campaigns read as historical documents. The interpretive approach was **philological-historical**: connecting archaeological material to textual tradition, validating classical historiography through underground evidence.

王國維's 二重證據法 Èrchóng Zhèngjù Fǎ (Double Evidence Method) — using newly discovered materials to illuminate and verify transmitted texts — was the methodological ideal. Underground evidence and above-ground texts mutually illuminate; neither dominates.

---

---

# 中華人民共和國時期 — PRC Period Interpretation (post-1949)

## 理論基礎 Lǐlùn Jīchǔ — Marxist-Leninist Framework

PRC archaeology operated under mandatory **historical materialism** as theorised by Marx, Engels, and Morgan, codified through Stalin's *Dialectical and Historical Materialism* (1938) and Morgan's *Ancient Society* (1877) as mediated through Engels' *The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State* (1884).

The universal stage sequence was non-negotiable:

**原始社會** Yuánshǐ Shèhuì (Primitive/Communal) → **奴隸社會** Núlì Shèhuì (Slave Society) → **封建社會** Fēngjiàn Shèhuì (Feudal Society) → **資本主義** Zīběn Zhǔyì (Capitalism) → **社會主義/共產主義** Shèhuì Zhǔyì/Gòngchǎn Zhǔyì (Socialism/Communism)

Every archaeological culture had to be located within this sequence. The empirical question — where does Shang fit? — was simultaneously a **politically charged theoretical question.** Getting the answer wrong had professional and personal consequences, particularly during the Anti-Rightist Campaign (1957) and Cultural Revolution (1966–76).

---

## 奴隸社會論爭 Núlì Shèhuì Lùnzhēng — The Slave Society Debate

The central interpretive controversy of PRC Bronze Age archaeology: **was Shang a slave society?**

### 郭沫若 Guō Mòruò — The Dominant Position
Poet, playwright, oracle bone scholar, and — crucially — President of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and intimate of Mao Zedong. Guō Mòruò had argued since the 1930s (from Japan, in Marxist exile) that Shang represented **slave society** 奴隸社會 — China's equivalent of Greek and Roman antiquity in the Marxist universal sequence.

Evidence marshalled:
- Mass human sacrifice at Anyang = slaves killed for royal burial
- Oracle bone references to 衆 zhòng (masses) and 臣 chén performing agricultural labour = slave labour force
- Bronze production requiring coerced specialised labour
- Sharp material distinction between royal tombs and commoner burials = class society based on slave ownership

This position became **orthodox** under PRC because it satisfied the Marxist sequence AND because Guō's political position made challenging him professionally dangerous.

### 范文瀾 Fàn Wénlán & 翦伯贊 Jiǎn Bózàn — Feudal Alternatives
Some scholars argued Shang was already **feudal** 封建 rather than slave society — pushing slave society back to earlier periods or eliminating it from Chinese history entirely. This was heterodox and periodically suppressed; the slave society orthodoxy for Shang-Zhou was dominant through most of the Maoist period.

### 問題 Wèntí — Problems with the Framework
The slave society framework imposed several interpretive distortions:

- 人祭 Rénjì (human sacrifice victims) reinterpreted as **slaves** regardless of osteological or contextual evidence suggesting prisoner-of-war, lineage member, or retainer status
- Agricultural labour organisation read through slave-mode lens despite oracle bone evidence suggesting more complex tributary/corvée arrangements
- Periodisation debates became ideologically loaded — scholars self-censored empirical conclusions that conflicted with required stage assignments
- Comparative evidence from other Bronze Age societies (Near East, Aegean) selectively deployed to validate Chinese fit with universal sequence

---

## 考古學與民族主義 Kǎogǔxué yǔ Mínzú Zhǔyì — Archaeology & New Nationalism

Post-1949 nationalism differed from Republican nationalism in structure but not intensity:

**Republican nationalism:** Prove China equals the West; civilisation is indigenous and ancient; refute diffusionism.

**PRC nationalism:** Prove China follows universal historical laws (Marxist sequence) while demonstrating that Chinese civilisation is the longest continuous civilisation in the world — 上下五千年 shàngxià wǔqiān nián (five thousand years of continuous history). Archaeology must extend that timeline and fill it with progressive content.

Practical consequences:
- Pressure to find ever-earlier evidence of civilisational markers (cities, bronze, writing, state) — pushing Xia dynasty archaeology aggressively
- 夏商周斷代工程 Xià-Shāng-Zhōu Chronology Project (1996–2000) as late expression: state-funded effort to assign absolute dates to pre-Shang dynasties, driven partly by desire to demonstrate Chinese civilisation predates or equals Near Eastern timelines
- Regional cultures (Hongshan, Liangzhu) increasingly claimed as part of Chinese civilisational origin — territorial and ethnic politics embedded in prehistoric interpretation

---

## 文化大革命 Wénhuà Dàgémìng — Cultural Revolution Rupture (1966–1976)

Archaeology nearly ceased as organised discipline. Key impacts:

- Excavation halted at most sites; researchers sent to 幹校 gànxiào (cadre schools) for 再教育 zàijiàoyù (re-education)
- Museum collections threatened; some destroyed
- Scholars with pre-1949 training or Western educational backgrounds particularly targeted — 舊知識分子 jiù zhīshifènzǐ (old intellectuals) as class enemies
- Notable exception: politically significant discoveries continued — 馬王堆 Mǎwángduī (1972, Han tombs) excavated partly because Zhou Enlai personally intervened to protect the site; its international prestige value overrode revolutionary iconoclasm

Interpretive consequence: a decade of enforced theoretical stagnation followed by a generation of scholars whose training was disrupted or deformed. Post-1976 rehabilitation of archaeology required simultaneously recovering empirical standards AND negotiating which Marxist frameworks could be quietly relaxed.

---

## 後毛澤東時期 Hòu Máo Zédōng Shíqī — Post-Mao Reinterpretation (1976–present)

### 理論鬆綁 Lǐlùn Sōngbǎng — Theoretical Relaxation
Post-1978 Reform era allowed gradual loosening of mandatory Marxist stage theory. Key shifts:

- Slave society framework for Shang quietly abandoned by most working archaeologists by 1980s — replaced by more cautious characterisations of Shang as **early state** or **complex chiefdom confederacy**
- 蘇秉琦's regional systems theory rehabilitated and celebrated — offered theoretical pluralism without explicitly repudiating Marxism
- Western processual and post-processual archaeology concepts gradually imported — systems theory, agency theory, landscape archaeology

### 文明探源工程 Wénmíng Tànyuán Gōngchéng — Civilisation Origins Project
State-funded program launched 2001 (Phase I); continuing. Explicitly tasked with identifying the origins of Chinese civilisation using multi-disciplinary methods — archaeology, ancient DNA, isotope analysis, remote sensing. Politically framed as establishing China's place among world's earliest civilisations.

Interpretive shift from Anyang-centric Bronze Age focus toward much earlier Neolithic complex societies — Liangzhu, Hongshan, Lingjiatan — as proto-civilisational. Civilisation definition itself became contested: PRC scholars increasingly rejected Western four-criteria model (writing, cities, bronze, state) as Eurocentric, proposing alternative criteria that better fit Chinese evidence (jade ritual systems, monumental earthworks, social hierarchy without necessarily requiring writing).

This was simultaneously genuine theoretical innovation and political manoeuvre — redefining civilisation to ensure China qualifies earliest.

# 臺灣詮釋 Táiwān Quánshì — Taiwan Interpretations (post-1949)

## 連續性 Liánxùxìng — Continuity with Republican Period

Academia Sinica Taiwan maintained direct institutional and personnel continuity with pre-1949 scholarship. Lǐ Jì, Dǒng Zuòbīn, Shí Zhāngrú continued working with the original Anyang collections (moved to Taiwan 1948–49). No mandatory theoretical framework imposed — empiricist culture-historical approach continued.

Lǐ Jì explicitly and repeatedly rejected Marxist stage theory as ideological distortion of archaeological evidence. His *Anyang* (1977) engages Western scholarship — Kwang-chih Chang / 張光直, American processualism — but maintains empiricist foundations.

## 孤立問題 Gūlì Wèntí — The Isolation Problem

Taiwan archaeology operated in a paradox: custodians of the foundational Shang collections, but cut off from the actual sites. New excavations on the mainland — post-1950s Anyang resumptions, new Bronze Age discoveries — were inaccessible. Taiwan scholars worked increasingly from closed datasets while mainland archaeology generated new material continuously.

This produced a gradual scholarly divergence: Taiwan interpretations became more philologically and typologically refined within the existing corpus; mainland interpretations increasingly driven by new field data but constrained by political frameworks.

張光直 — institutionally American, intellectually bridging both traditions — was uniquely able to synthesise because he was beholden to neither Taiwan's institutional conservatism nor the mainland's ideological requirements.

## 深層張力 Shēncéng Zhānglì — Deep Tension

Both traditions share a structural problem: **archaeology conscripted for national narrative.** The Republican team proved indigenous civilisation against Western diffusionism. The PRC team proved Marxist stage progression and civilisational longevity. Post-1978 PRC archaeology proves five-thousand-year continuous civilisation for contemporary geopolitical purposes.

The empirical material — bones, bronzes, oracle bones, rammed earth foundations — remains; the interpretive overlays shift with political weather. What neither tradition has fully sustained is archaeology genuinely insulated from national identity imperatives — a problem not unique to China but nowhere more structurally embedded.
