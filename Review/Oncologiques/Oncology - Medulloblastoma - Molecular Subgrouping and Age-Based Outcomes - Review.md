### \[Empirical Fact\]

- **WNT (Wingless):** Patients with WNT-activated tumors exhibit the best prognosis in neuro-oncology. In most contemporary cohorts, survival approaches 100% with standard therapy \[1.2\].

### The TP53 Impact in SHH-Activated Medulloblastoma

In the landscape of medulloblastoma, the *TP53* mutation is arguably the most significant molecular marker for treatment failure within the **SHH subgroup**. Crucially, its presence turns an otherwise intermediate-risk tumor into a "very high-risk" clinical entity \[1.1\].

#### \[Empirical Fact\]

- **Survival Divergence:** The 5-year Overall Survival (OS) for SHH medulloblastoma is roughly **81%** in *TP53* -wildtype cases, but drops precipitously to **41%** in *TP53* -mutant cases \[1.1\]\[5.1\].
- **Age Specificity:***TP53* mutations are almost exclusively found in children aged **5 to 18** within the SHH subgroup. They are rare in infants (<3 years) and adults \[5.1\]\[5.2\].
- **Germline Association:** Approximately **56%** of children with SHH/TP53-mutant tumors carry a germline mutation, often diagnostic of **Li-Fraumeni Syndrome** \[5.1\].
- **Subgroup Paradox:** Interestingly, *TP53* mutations also occur in the **WNT subgroup** (~16%) but have **no negative impact** on their excellent prognosis (>95% survival) \[5.1\]\[5.3\].

#### \[Informed Speculation\]

**Why *TP53* is lethal in SHH but not WNT:**The WNT pathway appears to biologically bypass or "abrogate" the radiation resistance typically conferred by *TP53* loss. In contrast, SHH tumors rely on genomic stability to survive therapeutic insult; when *TP53* is gone, these tumors accumulate massive chromosomal rearrangements (chromothripsis), leading to rapid, treatment-resistant relapse.

**Step-by-Step Logic:**

1. **Observation:** Radiation causes DNA double-strand breaks.
2. **Wildtype Response:** Normal *TP53* pauses the cell cycle for repair or triggers apoptosis.
3. **Mutant SHH Response:** Without *TP53*, the cell continues to divide with broken DNA, leading to extreme genomic instability and survival of the "fittest" (most aggressive) clones.
4. **WNT Exception:** High levels of -catenin (WNT hallmark) may interfere with the cell's ability to repair even minor damage, making the tumor hypersensitive to radiation regardless of *TP53* status \[1.3\].

### The Chang Staging System (M-Stage)

In Medulloblastoma, "M-stage" refers to the extent of metastatic spread within the Central Nervous System (CNS), as defined by the **Chang Staging System**:

- **M0:** No evidence of gross metastasis.
- **M1:** Microscopic tumor cells found in the Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF).
- **M2:** Gross nodular seeding in the brain (e.g., cerebellar/cerebral subarachnoid space).
- **M3:** Gross nodular seeding in the spinal subarachnoid space ("drop metastases").
- **M4:** Extra-axial metastasis (rare; e.g., to lungs or bone).

## Part I: Academic Essay

### The Molecular Architecture and Clinical Evolution of Medulloblastoma: A 2026 Perspective

**Abstract** Medulloblastoma (MB) has undergone a profound transformation from a histologically defined pediatric tumor to a complex molecular entity categorized by distinct genetic drivers and clinical trajectories. This essay examines the epidemiological profile of MB in the Republic of Ireland, the biological significance of the four primary molecular subgroups and their 12 constituent subtypes, and the divergent outcomes between pediatric and adult cohorts. Central to this discussion is the prognostic impact of the *TP53* mutation and the mechanisms of metastatic dissemination.

**Introduction** Medulloblastoma is a Grade 4 neuroepithelial tumor that primarily arises in the cerebellum. Historically treated as a single disease, the integration of DNA methylation profiling has necessitated a move toward precision oncology. In Ireland, the disease remains rare but stable, requiring a specialized multidisciplinary approach to navigate the narrow therapeutic window between cure and toxicity.

**The Irish Landscape and Epidemiology** Epidemiological data from the National Cancer Registry Ireland (NCRI) indicate that approximately 8 to 14 cases of medulloblastoma are diagnosed annually across all age groups. Within the pediatric population (ages 0–19), brain and central nervous system (CNS) tumors are the most prevalent solid malignancies. While 5-year survival rates have improved to approximately 87%, the rarity of the adult presentation (approx. 0.05 per 100,000) complicates the establishment of independent adult-centric treatment protocols.

**Molecular Subgrouping and Genomic Drivers** The 2021 WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System (CNS5) established four consensus molecular subgroups: WNT-activated, SHH-activated, Group 3, and Group 4. These subgroups are further stratified into 12 distinct subtypes based on transcriptomic and epigenetic clustering.The WNT-activated subgroup, defined by *CTNNB1* mutations and Monosomy 6, exhibits the most favorable prognosis, with survival frequently approaching 100%. Conversely, Group 3 tumors, often associated with *MYC* amplification, represent the most aggressive clinical phenotype. A critical marker in the SHH-activated subgroup is the *TP53* mutation; its presence in pediatric SHH cases correlates with massive genomic instability (chromothripsis) and a reduction in 5-year survival from 81% to 41%.

**Comparative Outcomes: The Age Divide** The clinical presentation of medulloblastoma differs significantly between children and adults. Pediatric cases are more likely to involve Group 3 or Group 4 tumors, whereas 60% of adult cases fall within the SHH-activated subgroup. While children generally experience higher cure rates, they are also more susceptible to severe long-term sequelae from craniospinal irradiation (CSI). In contrast, adults exhibit a higher risk of late recurrence, with approximately 29% of relapses occurring more than five years post-diagnosis.

**Metastatic Potential and Dissemination** Metastatic spread via the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)—leptomeningeal dissemination—is a hallmark of high-risk disease. Group 3 and Group 4 subtypes show the highest metastatic frequency, often reaching 40–50% at diagnosis. In the adult population, metastatic disease (M2–M4) is the strongest predictor of mortality, with a hazard ratio exceeding 3.0.

**Conclusion** The future of medulloblastoma management lies in the continued refinement of molecular risk-stratification. By moving beyond traditional clinical staging and into the 12-subtype architecture, clinicians can better identify patients who may benefit from treatment de-escalation (WNT) versus those requiring intensified, novel agents (*MYC* -amplified or *TP53* -mutant SHH).
