## What “PRC2 loss” means in MPNST (and why IHC works at all)

### Facts (well-supported)

- **PRC2** (Polycomb Repressive Complex 2) is an epigenetic writer complex. In many MPNSTs, **loss-of-function alterations** in PRC2 core components—especially **SUZ12** or **EED** —are recurrent.
- PRC2 loss typically leads to **global reduction/loss of H3K27 trimethylation (H3K27me3)**, which is why **H3K27me3 IHC** became a practical surrogate marker.

### Key clinical-pathology implication (fact)

- **Complete loss** of **H3K27me3** staining in tumor cells (with intact internal positive controls) is often supportive of **MPNST**, but it is **not universally present** and is **not perfectly specific** across all differentials.

## The main IHC surrogates: what the PubMed studies actually show

## 1) H3K27me3 IHC (the workhorse surrogate)

### What large/important cohorts concluded (facts)

- **PRC2 genetic inactivation is common**, motivating use of H3K27me3 as a biomarker.
- Multiple pathology cohort studies show **moderate sensitivity** for **complete loss** of H3K27me3 in MPNST, and **better specificity** when the differential is *not* melanoma-heavy (details below).
- **Partial / “mosaic” loss** is repeatedly flagged as **less reliable** diagnostically.

### Specific pitfall that matters in practice (fact)

- When the key differential includes **melanoma**, at least one very large study warns that **H3K27me3 loss (including complete loss) is not safe as a discriminator**, because melanomas may show loss patterns too.

### Small biopsies / cytology (fact)

- Performance has been specifically studied in **FNA/small biopsy contexts**: complete loss can be highly specific in those series, but sensitivity remains moderate (so a “retained” stain cannot exclude MPNST).

## How to use these stains in a real diagnostic workflow (pathologist-facing)

## Facts you can safely operationalize

- **Best single supportive pattern:****complete loss of H3K27me3** in tumor cells **with intact internal positive controls** (endothelial cells/benign elements).
- **Do not overcall “mosaic/partial” loss** as diagnostic by itself.
- **Retention of H3K27me3 does not exclude MPNST**, especially in certain clinicopathologic contexts and subtypes (several series show substantial retained cases).

## Working theory / informed speculation (clearly labeled)

Here’s a **working diagnostic logic** that many teams converge on, even when they disagree on exact specificity:

1. **Start with morphology + clinical setting** (NF1 history, nerve association, pre-existing neurofibroma, radiation field, etc.).
2. **Run a “nerve sheath vs melanoma vs synovial” panel** (typical core: S100, SOX10, ±EMA/claudin-1, ±pan-cytokeratin, ±Melan-A/HMB45/PRAME, ±SS18-SSX surrogate tests or molecular).
3. Use **H3K27me3** as an *ancillary* marker:
	- **Complete loss** pushes probability toward MPNST **only after** melanoma is reasonably excluded (because melanoma can also lose it in large series).
	- **Retained** staining keeps MPNST in play; you then lean more heavily on clinicopathologic correlation and (when needed) molecular tools.
4. Consider **molecular confirmation** (targeted sequencing for **SUZ12/EED/NF1/CDKN2A**, fusion testing for synovial sarcoma, or methylation-based classification in difficult cases).

That logic is an inference from the pattern of evidence: moderate sensitivity + context-dependent specificity + known melanoma pitfall.
