## 1\. From 十二生肖 to 干支: a layered object and a discipline of witnesses

The object commonly glossed as the “Chinese zodiac” is not a single unitary invention but a composite—an accretion in which mnemonic imagery, calendrical indexing, technical procedure, and cosmological theorization progressively lock together. For analytical purposes, this paper treats 十二生肖 as an overlay that becomes socially salient only because it attaches to a pre-existing time-indexing device: 十天干 (甲乙丙丁戊己庚辛壬癸) and 十二地支 (子丑寅卯辰巳午未申酉戌亥), whose combinatorics yield the 六十甲子 cycle used for naming days and later years. The first task, then, is not to repeat the familiar animal list, but to specify what kind of “meaning” we are tracking when we track “zodiac origins.”

To prevent category errors, the paper adopts a four-layer taxonomy of “semantic layers.” Each layer corresponds to a different mode of sign-function, a different style of textual articulation, and—crucially—a different class of evidence that can legitimately support claims about it. The layers are: **indexical**, **procedural**, **correlative**, and **mnemonic**. Indexical semantics refers to the use of 干支 elements as discrete labels in a finite ordered set: their “meaning” is their position and their contrast with other positions. Procedural semantics refers to rule-based attachments—tables, routines, and decision procedures—where specific slots (e.g., a given 支) trigger actions or predictions in technical practice. Correlative semantics refers to explicit theorization that maps slots onto cosmological domains (months, directions, 五行, political geography), thereby making the system discursively “about” the world. Mnemonic semantics refers to imagery designed for recall and social circulation—most visibly the animal mapping that allows the 支-cycle to be carried as a story rather than as an abstract grid.

An expanded schematic makes the stratigraphy operational:

| Layer (semantic mode) | System component | What “meaning” is doing | Typical genres / registers | Evidentiary anchors (primary habitats) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Indexical** | 十天干 / 十二地支 / 六十甲子 | Slot-labeling in a cycle; discrimination and recordability | Dating, scheduling, formulaic reference | 甲骨文 (divination records), 金文 (bronze inscriptions), early administrative/ritual formulae |
| **Procedural** | 地支-anchored routines; sometimes 干支 pairs | Slot triggers rules: auspices, prohibitions, theft-profiling, etc. | 術數 texts, hemerology, “daybooks” | 出土日書 (e.g., 睡虎地、放馬灘、孔家坡), related excavated technical manuscripts |
| **Correlative** | 干/支 embedded in cosmological mapping | Slots mapped to seasons, directions, qi dynamics, 五行, political space | Han learned discourse; commentarial systems | 《說文解字》 (lexicographic cosmology), correlative treatises; later encyclopedic compilations |
| **Mnemonic** | 十二生肖 mapped to 十二地支 | Imagery compresses the cycle; identity and folk pedagogy | Almanacs, popular divination, social self-description | Later transmitted tradition; popular materials; (occasionally) technical manuscripts that list animal sets |

The practical payoff of this taxonomy is that “zodiac-relevant evidence” can be defined without ambiguity. In this paper, a **zodiac-relevant witness** is any datable text or inscription that supports one of the following claim-types, each tied to a layer: (i) **Indexical witness**: an attestation of 干/支 used as cycle tokens (including but not limited to dates), establishing the existence and stability of the indexing backbone; (ii) **Procedural witness**: an attestation where specific 干/支 slots govern a rule, table, or divinatory inference, establishing that the cycle carries operational semantics in technical practice; (iii) **Correlative witness**: an explicit mapping of 干/支 to cosmological or systematic domains (months, directions, 五行, etc.), establishing the presence of theorized meaning; (iv) **Mnemonic-animal witness**: an explicit pairing or mapping between 地支 and animal labels (or a clearly presented animal set functioning as the 支 mnemonic), establishing the emergence and form of the 十二生肖 overlay.

Two further constraints prevent slippage. First, **implicit calendrical use is not treated as evidence for animal mapping**: a date like 甲子 is zodiac-relevant only as an indexical witness, not as proof of 十二生肖. Second, **animal language without explicit 支 linkage is not automatically zodiac evidence**: a bestiary motif is only relevant when it demonstrably functions as a mnemonic for the 支-cycle or as part of a structured technical routine keyed to 支 slots. With these constraints, later sections can compare manuscripts, inscriptions, and dictionaries without silently changing the question midstream: the evidence is always evaluated against the layer of meaning it can actually bear.

## 2\. Excavated manuals as the operational substrate: 《日書》 and procedural semantics in Qin–Han hemerology

If the sexagenary backbone (天干地支/干支) is the skeleton of early Chinese temporal indexing, the excavated 《日書》 are where the skeleton acquires musculature: rules, conditionals, and operational consequences. What makes these texts analytically privileged is not that they “explain” the zodiac in discursive prose—often they do not—but that they *use* branch slots (and sometimes stem slots) as levers in an inferential machine. The question that matters here is therefore not “what does 子 mean?” but “what does the system let you *do* with 子?” In Qin–Han hemerological practice, branch positions are treated as actionable inputs: they parameterize the likely appearance of a thief, the hiding place of stolen goods, the direction of flight, or the auspiciousness of action. This is procedural semantics in its purest form: meaning as rule-governed outcome.

The best-known window into this layer is the cluster of “theft divination” materials—variously titled 《盜者》/《盜》/《盜日》 in modern scholarship—preserved in multiple caches, allowing comparison across time and region. Studies that collate 雲夢睡虎地秦簡、甘肅天水放馬灘秦簡、湖北隨州孔家坡漢簡 show that “zodiac-like” pairings (十二支配十二獸/日肖) appear not as identity folklore but as forensic-cum-ritual technology: the day (or branch slot) becomes an index into a profile of the 盜者 and sometimes into instructions about response.

Two points are structurally important. First, these texts demonstrate that branch slots already function as more than neutral counters; they are treated as *typed positions* with attached properties, even where those properties are locally variable. Second, the variation itself is informative: early “animal mappings” are not uniformly the later canonical 十二生肖. In the comparative literature, Sleep Tiger (睡虎地) material is famous for irregular pairings (including extra or unexpected items and absences), while other caches exhibit different mismatches or duplications, underscoring that what later becomes a stable mnemonic set may have passed through a phase of “procedural bricolage,” in which local terminologies, graphic confusions, and differing technical lineages coexisted.

A compact comparative table makes the evidentiary geometry visible:

| Cache (tomb/corpus) | Approx. date | Text type | “Zodiac” layer present? | Procedural target | Notable signal for system history |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 雲夢睡虎地秦簡《日書》 | 秦 | 術數《日書》 | 十二支 + “十二獸/日肖” (non-canonical set) | 占盜：盜者體貌/藏匿/方位 | Early instability: mappings vary; suggests procedure-first, mnemonic later |
| 放馬灘秦簡《日書》 | 秦 | 術數《日書》 | 十二支 + “十二獸” with local anomalies | 占盜 / hemerological constraints | Regional/lineage variation; possible scribal/graphic confusions highlighted in scholarship |
| 孔家坡漢簡《日書》 | 西漢 | 術數《日書》 | 十二支 + idiosyncratic labels in 盜篇 | 占盜：profiling + situational inference | Shows persistence of technical use into Han with continued non-uniformity |
| 張家山漢簡（作對照） | 西漢 | 律令/奏讞與術數相關材料 | Not a primary “animal mapping” witness | “占盜” discourse intersects legal-administrative practice | Anchors how “procedural knowledge” circulates alongside governance genres |

What emerges from this layer is a productive inversion of the usual story. Rather than imagining that animals are primary and branches are an abstract codification, the Qin–Han 《日書》 invite us to view “animalization” as one of several representational strategies for making a calendrical grid legible to rapid inference. The technical problem is: given a day-marker (支 or 干支), how does one compress a large set of expectations into a short lookup? Animal labels (or animal-like labels) are cognitively efficient, but the manuscripts show that efficiency can be achieved even with unstable or locally inflected labels, so long as the procedure is internally consistent within the text’s own ecosystem. This is precisely why these caches matter: they document the zodiac not as a static list but as an evolving interface between temporal indexing and applied decision-making.

## 3\. Building an archaeology-first dossier: how to cite tomb context, date range, and “zodiac-relevant” lines

A review that attempts to trace 十二生肖 and the 干支 complex through early evidence must solve a prosaic but decisive problem: how to cite artifacts so that claims remain falsifiable when editions change, reconstructions are revised, or a single graphic ambiguity flips an animal label. The temptation in secondary discussions is to cite “睡虎地 says…” or “放馬灘 records…” and then move immediately to interpretive synthesis. That move is precisely what makes the evidentiary layer unstable, because “sleeping tiger” and “horse pasture” are not texts; they are excavation contexts that generated multiple manuscripts, edited by different teams, with different slip numbering systems and different degrees of lacunae. The dossier is therefore not a rhetorical accessory; it is the paper’s epistemic control surface.

The first step is to define what counts as a witness at a level that matches the paper’s semantic-layer taxonomy. A single 干支 date (e.g., 甲子) is a witness for indexical usage but not a witness for animal mapping. Conversely, a list of animals is not automatically a zodiac witness unless it is explicitly keyed to 十二地支 (or functions inside a procedural table whose inputs are 支 slots). In practice, the dossier needs to tag each excerpt by claim-type before interpretation: “支-slot procedural rule,” “explicit 支↔獸 mapping,” “correlative explanation,” and so forth. Without that tagging, the review will silently commute between different kinds of meaning, and the narrative will look smoother than the evidence warrants.

A second constraint is that “exactness” must be engineered at the level of editorial coordinates. For excavated manuscripts, the relevant coordinate is not page number but 簡號/牘號 (slip number) and, where available, the line segmentation in the edition’s 釋文. For epigraphy, the coordinate is the standard corpus identifier (e.g., 甲骨合集編號; 《殷周金文集成》器號) plus provenance class (controlled excavation vs old collection vs market). The point is not fetishistic cataloging; it is that semantic claims often hinge on a single graph—巳 vs 已, or a damaged animal name—and only edition-stable coordinates permit re-checking.

The dossier format can be standardized as follows:

| Field | Excavated manuscripts (e.g., 睡虎地、放馬灘、孔家坡) | Inscriptions (甲骨文 / 金文) | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Provenience | 墓葬編號、出土位置、隨葬關係 | 出土地/傳世來源、出土紀錄 | Separates controlled context from floating text |
| Date range | 墓葬斷代（考古分期 + 年代範圍） | 器物/卜辭分期 | Prevents anachronistic layering |
| Text unit | 篇名/類別（如《日書》《盜者》） | 器名/卜辭類型 | Keeps genre constraints visible |
| Editorial anchor | 簡號、釋文行次、圖版號 | 合集/集成號、拓片/摹本 | Enables re-collation across editions |
| Zodiac-relevant locus | 精確引文（短）+ 斷句標記 | 精確引文（短）+ 斷句標記 | Pins claims to the smallest checkable unit |
| Claim-type tag | 支↔獸、支-slot rule、干支 index | 干支 index、親屬稱謂+干 | Prevents slippage across semantic layers |
| Variant register | 異文、殘缺、釋讀分歧 | 釋文歧見、形體分歧 | Records uncertainty rather than smoothing it away |

Two further problems recur often enough to warrant explicit handling. One is “edition drift”: different publications renumber slips, rejoin fragments, or change readings after new joins; a dossier must therefore store both the edition used and any crosswalk to alternative numbering when available. The second is “graph-to-gloss inflation”: later scholarship (or popular summaries) can retroject standardized 十二生肖 labels onto earlier, locally variable “十二獸/日肖” vocabularies. The dossier should preserve the manuscript’s own terminology and only then, in commentary, propose equivalences, with the equivalence treated as an argument rather than a normalization.

Section 3 thus functions as a discipline of restraint. It does not reduce interpretation; it makes interpretation legible by forcing every move to pass through coordinates, claim-types, and variant logs. Once that infrastructure is in place, the paper can safely scale from single slips to cross-site comparison without converting the archaeological record into a single imagined “text” called “the zodiac.”

## 4\. Philology of 十二地支: 《說文解字》 as Han reception-history, not automatic Bronze Age etymology

Any attempt to write a “meaning history” of 十二地支 (子丑寅卯辰巳午未申酉戌亥) has to decide what kind of object a “meaning” is. In early usage, a 支 can function as a purely **indexical** token—its semantic payload is simply “the 4th slot” or “the 10th slot,” with no necessity for imagery or cosmology. 《說文解字》, by contrast, is explicitly invested in turning indexical tokens into **interpretable signs** by embedding them in a seasonal–qi narrative: each 支 is glossed through a month, the motion of 陽氣/陰氣, and a motivating iconic reading of the graph. This makes 《說文》 invaluable, but also treacherous if treated as a direct window onto Shang–Zhou sign origins. It is best read as a Han-era “semantic stabilization project”: not only recording inherited explanations, but actively producing a coherent correlative package in which the 支-cycle becomes discursively *about* cosmology, agriculture, and bodily time.

A quick way to see the systematizing impulse is to line up the twelve entries as a single seasonal argument. The glosses (as transmitted in common digital witnesses) form a chain of verbs and states—滋, 紐, 動/欲上出, 冒, 震, 出/藏, 逆/交, 味/木老, 申束, 就, 滅, 荄—each pinning a branch to a phase transition in the annual cycle. That chain does not merely explain isolated characters; it narrativizes the cycle itself.

| 地支 | 《說文》gloss “hook” (compressed) | Month frame | Qi / process motif | Graphic motivation offered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 子 | 「十一月，陽氣動，萬物滋」 | 11th | incipient growth | 象形 (child/germination frame) |
| 丑 | 「紐也。十二月…用事」 | 12th | binding/turning | 象手之形 |
| 寅 | 「正月…欲上出…象宀不達」 | 1st | blocked emergence | “hairpin/obstruction” story |
| 卯 | 「冒也。二月…冒地而出」 | 2nd | sprouting through earth | 象開門之形；二月為天門 |
| 辰 | 「震也。三月…靁電振」 | 3rd | thunder, mobilization | composite graph analysis + 房星 |
| 巳 | 「四月…陽氣巳出，陰氣巳藏…故巳為蛇」 | 4th | visibility, patterning | explicitly links to 蛇 |
| 午 | 「啎也。五月…陰气午逆陽」 | 5th | opposition/crossing | graphic as “交/逆” |
| 未 | 「味也。六月…滋味…木老於未」 | 6th | maturation/taste | 象木重枝葉 |
| 申 | 「神也。七月…體自申束」 | 7th | constriction/ordering | from 臼, “self-holding” |
| 酉 | 「就也。八月黍成，可為酎酒」 | 8th | completion/harvest | 古文酉之形; wine cluster |
| 戌 | 「滅也。九月…陽下入地」 | 9th | decline/withdrawal | 戊含一; 五行土盛 |
| 亥 | 「荄也。十月，微陽起，接盛陰」 | 10th | root, renewal | 二 + 乙; gestation imagery |

(Entries as given in《說文解字》pages for each 支.)

Two philological cautions follow immediately. First, the “graphic motivations” in 《說文》 are often *interpretations of inherited shapes* rather than reliable reconstructions of original graph formation. The famous 卯 gloss—“象開門之形”—is a good example of how a later script form can invite a tidy iconic story; but paleographic discussions note that earlier forms relate to actions like splitting/killing sacrificial animals, and that the “door” reading may reflect a later shape-driven reinterpretation rather than the earliest function. Second, even within the received tradition, the 支 entries are not text-critically inert: different recensions and annotation strata (e.g., 大徐本 versus 段注本, and the long afterlife of 段玉裁《說文解字注》) can affect small but meaningful details—particularly where a single graph boundary (巳/已, 卯/戼, 酉/丣) shifts the implied iconic argument.

For the purposes of a zodiac-history review, the payoff is methodological rather than merely lexical. 《說文》 is strongest as an evidentiary anchor for the **correlative layer**: it shows how a Han intellectual system makes the 支-cycle semantically thick by welding it to month-by-month qi dynamics and to a graph-iconicity rhetoric. It is weaker as a direct witness to Shang–Zhou “original meanings,” unless it can be triangulated against 甲骨文/金文 usage and against the variant ecology visible in 出土文獻. Read this way, 《說文》 does not “solve” the 支; it documents the moment when the 支 become legible as a cosmological narrative—precisely the kind of stabilization that later makes 十二生肖 feel inevitable.

## 5\. Philology of 十天干: parallelizing the stems with the same “function → explanation → codification” ladder

If 十二地支 can be made to look “naturally meaningful” by narrating an agricultural year, 十天干 are even more revealing as a test case for philological method, because their early usefulness does not depend on any intuitive imagery at all. The ten signs—甲乙丙丁戊己庚辛壬癸—are perfectly serviceable as a finite index-set: they generate contrast, enable enumeration, and (in combination with 地支) produce the 六十甲子 cycle. Yet the later tradition does not leave them as bare indices. It insists on making them *legible* —as a seasonal procession, as a cosmological map, and as a set of graphically motivated forms. The crucial point for a review article is that these are not mutually exclusive “meanings,” but stacked semantic regimes: the same graph can be a slot-label in one genre, a ritual-person label in another (through kin-title compounds), and an object of cosmological explanation in lexicography.

The Han lexicographic moment is especially visible in 《說文解字》, which treats the stems as components of an ordered system and embeds each in an explanatory frame that is at once seasonal and iconic. The entries do not merely gloss; they theorize. They repeatedly situate a stem by direction and seasonal phase (east/spring stirring; south flourishing; west harvesting; north gestation), and they frequently advance a graphic rationale that reads the shape through the body (e.g., “象人…” sequences) or through a concrete object (e.g., 甲 as an armored shell). This is precisely the kind of evidence that is strongest as reception-history—showing how Later Han scholarship stabilized a correlative semantics for a pre-existing indexing device—while remaining risky as a direct reconstruction of Shang–Zhou graph coinage.

A compact alignment of the ten entries makes the systematizing logic explicit. What follows compresses each entry into its interpretive “hook”: the seasonal/directional frame plus the iconic motif that makes the graph feel motivated.

| 天干 | Correlative frame (direction/season motif) | Iconic / analytic hook in 《說文》 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 甲 | 東方、春之始；陽氣萌動 | “從木” + 介/甲冑 imagery (“著甲”) |
| 乙 | 春木屈曲而出；陰猶強 | “艸木冤曲而出” / “象…曲” |
| 丙 | 南方；萬物成而明 | bright/manifestation frame; structural analysis |
| 丁 | 夏時丁實；成就與堅實 | ripening/solidity (“丁實”) motif |
| 戊 | 中宮；樞紐之位 | centrality + 土/樞 axis rhetoric |
| 己 | 中宮；收藏、曲屈 | withdrawal/storage + bent form motif |
| 庚 | 西方；秋肅、收斂 | firmness/harvest; form read through “𪗇” imagery |
| 辛 | 秋成而孰；金剛與辛味 | “辛” linked to pungency/metal; sometimes pain/tears chain |
| 壬 | 北方；陰極陽生；妊象 | pregnancy image; “妊/褢妊” shape reading |
| 癸 | 冬；可揆度；象水 | measurement/standard (“揆度”) + water imagery |

Seen as a set, the stems are being recruited into a single explanatory narrative: an annual and cosmological circuit that makes a tenfold index feel “about” growth, consolidation, harvesting, and hidden renewal. The philological payoff is not that the story must be “false” because it is coherent; rather, coherence itself becomes a datum. A highly regular explanatory template is precisely what we expect when a scholarly tradition is systematizing an inherited technical apparatus—especially one already entrenched in administrative and ritual practice—into a cosmological pedagogy. In other words, the ten stems become meaningful not only through usage, but through the *institution of explanation*: the ability of a learned discourse to supply reasons why a sign must occupy its slot, and why the slot must correspond to a stage in the world’s cycle.

This also clarifies why 十天干 and 十二地支, though paired in practice, often diverge in their cultural “stickiness.” The branches readily attract concrete overlays (animals, hours, directions) because a 12-cycle has immediate affordances for month-counting and diurnal partition. The stems, a 10-cycle, more naturally invite alignment to abstract schemata (centrality, measurement, metal pungency, pregnancy) that are legible inside a correlative cosmology and a lexicographic tradition. Parallelization, then, should aim for symmetry in method—track function, then procedural attachment, then correlative explanation, then medieval codification—without forcing symmetry in the kind of imagery that each subsystem tends to accumulate.

## 6\. Shang–Zhou epigraphy: non-dating stem usage and the kin-title axis as the cleanest diagnostic

The epigraphic record forces a conceptual correction that is easy to miss if one approaches 天干 (甲乙丙丁戊己庚辛壬癸) only through later calendrical tables. In the late Shang dynasty milieu, stems are not merely elements inside date strings; they are also deployed as name-like designators for ancestral spirits, typically governed by kin-titles. The key point is syntactic rather than speculative: the construction **「親屬稱謂 + 天干」** (e.g., 父丁、祖甲、妣辛、母辛) behaves as a **person-reference** —the recipient of sacrifice, the subject of divinatory inquiry, or the dedicatee of a vessel—whereas **「天干 + 地支」** (e.g., 甲子、丁卯) behaves as a **time-reference**. This is why the kin-title lexicon is so methodologically powerful: it separates two functions that use the same ten graphs but live in different grammatical niches.

Oracle-bone divination inscriptions (甲骨文) are densely ritual and therefore densely pronominal: they need stable ways to talk about whom one is feeding, placating, or petitioning. A widely noted feature of late Shang cult practice is that ancestors are designated by **stem-names** that often appear with kin titles—“father,” “grandfather/ancestor,” and related status appellations—and that these ancestors receive offerings according to a fixed schedule keyed to their stem designation. The implication is not merely that stems can name people, but that a stem-name actively participates in ritual time: “name day” is not metaphorical but procedural, because the stem token determines the day-class on which the cult is serviced.

Bronze inscriptions (金文) extend this logic into commemorative and dedicatory registers. In a controlled archaeological context like the tomb of Fu Hao (婦好), scholars repeatedly emphasize posthumous titles such as **母辛**, and the presence of bronzes cast for her cult under such designations; this provides a concrete anchor for the “kin-title + stem” mechanism as a mortuary and post-mortuary naming technology, not merely a divinatory shorthand. At the same time, bronze dedicatory formulae make the syntax visible in a different genre: rather than “divining about X,” the vessel says (in effect) “made for X,” with X expressed as a kin-title compound. Even when the full phrase varies, the structural signal is stable: the dedicatee slot is filled by kin-title + stem, and that slot is not a date.

The diagnostic becomes sharper when we map kin-title types against media and period. Late Shang usage tends to foreground **父 / 母 / 妣 / 祖** within sacrificial scheduling and ancestral designation; Western Zhou inscriptions increasingly foreground a filial commemorative rhetoric in which **考** (“deceased father”) and **妣** (“deceased mother”) become highly productive as dedication targets, often with honorific stacking and lineage self-presentation. This does not erase the stem-name technology; it reframes it, shifting the balance from a divination-centered cult bureaucracy to a broader aristocratic memorial economy in which vessels perform kinship and legitimacy.

A compact “two-axis” view—stem function by kin-title governance—captures the method:

| Axis B: 親屬稱謂 trigger | Typical form | Function of 天干 | Primary evidence stream | Notes for falsifiability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 父 + 干 | 父丁 / 父甲 | Ancestral designate; cult recipient | 甲骨文 | If followed by 祭/祀/貞-frames, securely non-dating |
| 祖 + 干 | 祖甲 / 祖乙 | Higher ancestor label | 甲骨文 + 金文 | Often indexes dynastic cult hierarchy |
| 母/妣 + 干 | 母辛 / 妣辛 | Female ancestor designation | 金文 + tomb assemblages | Strong because the compound is a name-slot, not an adverbial date |
| 考/妣 + 干 | 皇考某干 / 皇妣某干 | Filial commemorative dedicatee | 西周金文 | Marks Zhou-style dedication rhetoric; stem may be explicit or implicit |

What matters for the larger argument of this paper is that the earliest “semantic thickening” of 天干 is not primarily cosmological explanation (that is vivid in 《說文解字》), but **social reference**. The stems become semantically sticky because they name persons in ritual grammar; the kin-title lexicon is the hinge that makes that semantic mode visible and machine-classifiable. A dossier built on this axis can therefore do more than collect examples: it can measure how ritual address evolves, how naming conventions distribute across male/female ancestor categories, and how the same ten graphs shuttle between “time token” and “person token” without ambiguity once syntax is respected.

## 7\. Medieval stabilization through sound: using 《《廣韻》》 to model phonological neighborhoods and semantic clustering

If Qin–Han 出土《日書》 show how 干支 acquire “procedural semantics,” medieval lexicography shows how those semantics are later *packaged* for learned transmission. The decisive shift is that rhyme dictionaries in the 《切韻》 –《廣韻》 lineage impose a phonology-first ordering: characters are encountered as members of a pronunciation cell before they are encountered as meaning-bearers. In practical terms, this produces “phonological neighborhoods”—historically defined groupings of homophones—and these neighborhoods constrain how semantic associations form, persist, and sometimes bleed across graphs. For a review article on 十二地支/十天干, this matters because the signs are neither rare nor hermetically technical: many are ordinary graphs with everyday senses, and the label “支名/干名/辰名” must coexist in the same lexicographic slot with whatever else that syllable does in the language.

The most defensible neighborhood unit in 《廣韻》 is the **小韻**: a headword with a 反切 spelling that anchors a cluster of characters treated as sharing the “same sound” within the Qieyun system. Neighborhoods can be defined more coarsely (韻目, or even tone +韻), but 小韻 is the unit that is both granular enough to be meaningful and explicit enough to be extracted reproducibly. Once each of the 22 signs (10 干 + 12 支) is assigned to its 小韻, the question becomes: what sort of “semantic clustering” exists inside that sound-cell?

To keep this measurable, the paper can treat gloss overlap as two distinct channels. The first is **formula-channel overlap**: do multiple members of the neighborhood share the calendrical tag (“又辰名/又干名/又支名”) or its associated citation boilerplate? The second is **lexical-channel overlap**: do members share non-formula content words (e.g., 明/光/亮; 伸/身; 酒/酎) that would suggest a semantically coherent phonetic series, rather than mere phonological cohabitation. The point is not to decide whether medieval lexicographers “believed” the zodiac; it is to model how a reader’s semantic map is shaped by repeated adjacency inside the dictionary’s phonological grid.

A compact methodological table clarifies the moving parts:

| Step | Object | Output | Why it matters for 干支 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Map each sign to a 《廣韻》小韻 (via headword + 反切) | 22 neighborhood IDs | Prevents “near-sound” handwaving; keeps categories historical |
| 2 | Extract neighborhood membership | Character list per 小韻 | Defines what counts as “neighbors” without modern intuition |
| 3 | Segment gloss text | (a) formula tags; (b) lexical content tokens | Avoids the trivial result that only the sign has “辰名” |
| 4 | Compute overlap | Jaccard or cosine similarity within channel | Turns “clustering” into an inspectable statistic |
| 5 | Interpret with paleographic control | flag phonetic series vs accidental homophony | Stops us from mistaking sound-packaging for etymology |

Two expectations follow. First, **formula-channel overlap will usually be near-zero**: calendrical identity is typically anchored on the sign-character itself, not distributed across its homophones. Second, **lexical-channel overlap will be bimodal**: some neighborhoods will look semantically tight because they are phonetic families (e.g., 丙 adjacent to graphs glossed with brightness, or 申 adjacent to 伸/身-type senses), while others will be semantically heterogeneous (a common outcome when a syllable’s graph inventory is historically crowded). This bimodality is itself the “constraint”: medieval users learn meanings inside sound-cells, so signs embedded in semantically rich neighborhoods are more likely to accrete associative glossing, while signs in heterogeneous neighborhoods are more likely to have their calendrical sense quarantined as a tagged sub-sense (“又…名”) rather than integrated into broader semantic fields.

What this phonology layer adds to the paper’s central thesis is a transmission mechanism. Early evidence (甲骨文/金文, 出土日書) tells us how the system is used; Han lexicography (《說文解字》) tells us how it is theorized; medieval 韻書 tell us how it is *learned and stabilized* in literate practice, by pinning signs into a reproducible pronunciation lattice that both enables and limits semantic clustering.

## Conclusion
The argument of this paper has insisted on an awkward but productive distinction: what modern discourse calls “the Chinese zodiac” is not a single invention but a composite whose unity is historically achieved. 十二生肖 is the most visible face of that composite, yet it is methodologically safer to treat it as a mnemonic layer mapped onto an older indexing substrate—十天干 (甲乙丙丁戊己庚辛壬癸) and 十二地支 (子丑寅卯辰巳午未申酉戌亥)—than to treat animals as the system’s generative core. Once that separation is enforced, “origin” ceases to be a folk-historical question (“who first named the animals?”) and becomes a set of stratigraphic questions keyed to evidence streams: when do we see indexical use; when do we see procedural attachment; when do we see explicit correlative theorization; when do we see mnemonic canonicalization.

Across these layers, the early record looks less like a pristine list and more like a working technology. The Qin–Han 出土《日書》—especially theft-divination clusters conventionally labeled 《盜者》/《盜日》—are exemplary not because they provide a clean 十二生肖 chart, but because they demonstrate how 支-slots function as inputs to procedures: branch positions trigger predictions, profiles, and constraints. That procedural semantics can tolerate variation—local vocabulary, non-canonical animal labels, scribal noise—because what needs to be stable is the *internal mapping within a text lineage*, not an empire-wide mnemonic standard. In that sense, instability is not a defect in the evidence; it is itself evidence for an intermediate phase in which branch-indexing is already operationally meaningful while animalization remains one among several representational strategies.

The dossier discipline proposed in Section 3 is therefore not merely pedantic. It is the mechanism that keeps claims aligned with their proper witness class. A 支 in a date string is not, by itself, a zodiac-animal witness; a bestiary motif is not, by itself, a 支-mnemonic; a Han dictionary gloss is not, by itself, a Bronze Age etymology. By forcing every citation to carry provenience, date range, editorial anchors (簡號/釋文行次; 合集/集成號), and a claim-type tag, the review makes the inevitable uncertainties visible: variant readings, lacunae, recensional drift, and the temptation to normalize early labels into later standard forms.

Within this disciplined frame, Han lexicography—above all 《說文解字》—can be appreciated for what it is strongest at showing: a correlative stabilization project that renders indexical signs discursively meaningful. The 支 and 干 are reorganized as a seasonal–qi narrative, with iconic motivations that make the cycle feel inevitable. The philological caution is not to dismiss these explanations, but to situate them: they document how a learned tradition teaches and rationalizes a technical apparatus already entrenched in practice, rather than providing a transparent record of original graph coinage. Parallelizing 十天干 with 十二地支 under the same ladder—function → procedural attachment → correlative explanation → later codification—prevents the common asymmetry in which branches are granted “real meaning” while stems are treated as mere counters. Both can be counters; both can be thickened; they are thickened by different institutions.

The epigraphic dossier adds a further correction by showing that the earliest semantic thickening of 天干 is not necessarily cosmological but social and ritual. In late Shang–Zhou materials, stems appear as person-references embedded in kin-title compounds (父/母/妣/祖/考/公 + 干), a usage that is syntactically and functionally distinct from the stem–branch dating formula. The kin-title lexicon thus becomes a powerful diagnostic axis: it turns “non-dating stem usage” into a corpus-queryable, falsifiable category and allows ritual address to be tracked diachronically, including shifts in preferred kin rhetoric between Shang divination contexts and Western Zhou dedication registers.

Finally, the phonology layer introduced through 《廣韻》 supplies a mechanism of learned stabilization that is easy to overlook if one focuses only on content. By defining “phonological neighborhoods” at the 小韻 level (反切-defined homophone cells), we can model how later readers encounter the 22 signs not as isolated technical tokens but as members of sound-sets in which calendrical sense is typically quarantined as a tagged sub-sense (“又辰名/又干名/又支名”) while other lexical senses and citation traditions press in from adjacent homophones. This is not merely an add-on; it explains how semantic clustering can arise (or fail to arise) as a function of dictionary architecture, and why some signs may become semantically “sticky” in medieval literacy for reasons only partially continuous with early practice.

Taken together, these layers recast “the zodiac” as a history of interfaces: between time-indexing and ritual scheduling, between technical procedure and mnemonic compression, between inherited sign-sets and institutions of explanation, and between semantic learning and phonological ordering. The agenda that follows from this conclusion is not a new origin myth but a research program: build the dossier exhaustively, keep claim-types aligned with witness-types, and treat coherence—whether in Han correlative narratives or medieval韻書 neighborhoods—as historical evidence of stabilization work rather than as proof of primordial design. The result is a system that becomes more intelligible precisely when we allow it to have a history: not a single beginning, but multiple moments when different layers become visible, actionable, and finally canonical.
