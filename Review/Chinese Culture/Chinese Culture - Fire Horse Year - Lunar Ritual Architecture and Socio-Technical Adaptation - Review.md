### Section III: The Dietary Timeline: Nutritional Phasing and Symbolic Consumption (Days 1–15)

#### 3.1 Phase I: Purity and The Great Reset (Days 1–2)

The commencement of the lunar year is governed by the principle of **Abstinence as Preservation**. On the first day (*Zhèngyuè Chūyī* - 正月初一), the consumption of meat is traditionally eschewed in favor of *Zhāi* (斋), or Buddha’s Delight. This vegetarian mandate serves a dual purpose: it honors the Buddhist/Taoist sanctity of life at the year's genesis and provides a critical fiber-load to the gastrointestinal tract following the New Year’s Eve (*Chúxī*) feast.

A significant dietary taboo on Day 1 is the prohibition of **Rice Porridge** (*Zhōu* - 粥). In the socio-economic architecture of ancient China, thin porridge was the hallmark of the impoverished. To consume it at the "Head of the Year" (*Niántóu*) is viewed as a **Psychological Priming** for scarcity. Instead, households prioritize *Niángāo* (年糕), whose glutinous density symbolizes "Year-on-Year Elevation."

#### 3.2 Phase II: Reintegration and Kinetic Energy (Days 3–7)

As the festival progresses, the dietary rules shift from purity to **Social Maintenance**. By Day 2 (*Kāinián* - 开年), the reintroduction of protein through "Longevity Noodles" (*Chángshòumiàn* - 长寿面) facilitates the return of physical vigor required for the *Bàinián* (拜年) visiting rituals.

In the 2026 Fire Horse year, these dishes are often augmented with "Fire" elements—such as chili-infused oils or red-pigmented grains—to synchronize the practitioner's metabolism with the year's high-velocity energy.

#### 3.3 Phase III: Spiritual Completion and Harmonization (Days 8–15)

The final phase of the timeline focuses on the **Homeostasis of the Family Unit**.
