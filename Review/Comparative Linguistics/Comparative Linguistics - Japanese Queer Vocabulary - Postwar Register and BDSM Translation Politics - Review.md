## Section 1 (English version) — Introduction: Research Questions, Sites, and an Analytic Toolkit

### 1.1 Research problem: when “register” becomes platform-shaped engineering

This review treats a set of seemingly heterogeneous questions as manifestations of a shared mechanism: how postwar Japanese written–spoken distance is reallocated under new media constraints; how “gendered language” (sentence-final particles, pronouns, self-reference, politeness strategies, profanity/mitigation) weakens or reconfigures across workplaces, romantic interaction, and anonymous online spaces; how gay community lexicons—especially bear body-type vocabularies—toggle between face-saving in nightlife talk and searchable categorization on dating apps; and how BDSM/kink terms (e.g., ドS/ドM, 女王様, 調教, 緊縛／縛り) drift in meaning as they circulate across Japanese, English, and Chinese. The unifying claim is that language is not merely reflective but actively re-authored by affordances of visibility, searchability, and interaction tempo. Registers become interface objects: speakers continually trade off efficiency, interpretability, and social risk. To track how a single form acquires different social meanings across contexts, we adopt an indexicality framework—especially “indexical order,” which links micro-interactional usage to macro-social value regimes (1).

### 1.2 Sites and materials: Ni-chōme vs apps; news prose vs messaging; public talk vs private codes

To avoid treating “online Japanese” or “community language” as homogeneous, we stratify by communicative site. First, we contrast institutionally anchored public prose (news and bureaucratic styles) with short-form and instant messaging genres that reward compression and stance-marking. Second, we analyze a dual-register ecology within queer communities: nightlife interaction (e.g., Ni-chōme) prioritizes repairability and face management, while dating apps prioritize searchability, standardized tags, and self-presentation as structured fields. Third, we treat BDSM/kink discourse as inherently double-layered: in-group ethics/procedural language (consent, boundaries, risk management) coexists with out-group mainstreamed “meme-ified” talk. A crucial methodological point is that disclosure itself is a pragmatic condition: empirical work on BDSM stigma and disclosure highlights situational strategies and the risks attached to being “out,” which in turn encourages hedging, ambiguity, and deniable formulations (2).

### 1.3 Analytic toolkit: indexicality, visibility, stigma management, and translational anchoring

We integrate four complementary lenses. (i) Indexicality and indexical order capture layered semantic drift: e.g., how 〜寄り functions as a classificatory buffer (from categorical assertion to tendency), or how ドS/ドM shift from sexual preference labels to personality descriptors. (ii) Communication visibility theory characterizes how platforms reshape interaction: when messages become observable, searchable, and amplifiable, language tends toward tagging, templating, and standardization (3). (iii) Stigma and depathologization literatures provide the ethical background: BDSM has been historically pathologized in public understanding, while depathologization efforts and empirical studies foreground consent as a central organizing principle of community self-description (4,5). (iv) Translation politics treats lexical choice as a probabilistic “routing device” for interpretation. For example, rendering *discipline* as a Foucauldian-leaning term in Chinese is more likely to trigger power/violence frames; rendering it as “training/conditioning” or “調教” more often routes readers back to negotiated, scene-bound technique and agreement.

## 第 2 節　戰後到智慧型手機時代的日語語體變遷：規範、媒介與壓縮的再配置

### 2.1 戰後書寫改革與「公共書面語」的再定錨：正字法不是中立技術

戰後日語語體變遷若只從「口語是否變了」來衡量，容易低估真正關鍵的變動： **書寫系統與公共規範的重新校準** 。1940–50 年代的改革（例如現代假名遣い、漢字使用的制度化限制與教育政策）不只是拼寫便利化，而是把「可讀性」「民主化」「國民教育」綁成一個語言治理計畫，從根本上影響了書面語的權威來源與可接受形式。(1,2) 書面語因而獲得更強的可教性與可編輯性：新聞文體、教材文體與官僚文本在同一套正字法框架下運作，形成跨場域可移植的「標準書面語」。同時，這種制度化也製造了持久張力：一方面降低識字門檻，另一方面使「非標準」更容易被標記為偏差，間接擴大了書面語與方言/俗語的社會距離。(2) 因此，戰後的語體變遷首先是 **可見性（visibility）與可規範性** 的重分配：哪些形式能進入公共文本、哪些只能停留在私域口語，並非自然演化，而是政策—媒介—教育共同塑形的結果。(1–3)

### 2.2 1950s–1990s：大眾媒體與編輯制度把語體「模板化」

在戰後至泡沫經濟前後，日語語體的關鍵變化往往不是「語法革命」，而是 **編輯制度驅動的模板化** ：報紙、雜誌與廣播（如 NHK ）透過風格指南、用字規範與校閱流程，把書面語生產變成可複製的工藝。這種工藝化帶來兩個後果。其一，公共文本偏好高資訊密度的名詞鏈、明確的論證標記與固定的報導框架，使「新聞式書面語」在形式上更可預測、在社會上更具權威；其二，口語中大量依賴的省略、含混、語氣助詞與情緒指標，被推向非正式語體或被改寫成較可公開的替代形式。這也解釋了為何許多使用者會感覺戰後到 1990s 的「變化很大」：變的不是日語骨架，而是「公共可見的日語」被編輯制度收斂成一套高度規格化的產物。(2,3) 這段時期的變化通常是漸進的，但它為後來的平台語言提供了對照組：當短文平台興起時，使用者其實是在兩套模板之間切換——編輯模板與互動模板。

### 2.3 2010s–：短文平台與即時通訊的壓縮邏輯——從「可讀性」轉向「可互動性」

智慧型手機時代的陡峭轉折，體現在語體被平台的互動機制直接塑形：短文平台將表達壓縮為可快速消化、可轉發擴散的單位；即時通訊則把語言推向連續對話的微回合（micro-turns），鼓勵省略、情緒標記與圖像化符號。以 Twitter 為例，研究者整理其字符限制政策與語言差異時指出：日文推文平均字數遠短於英文，且觸頂比例顯著較低，這使平台「擴字」策略在日文語境的壓力較小，反而凸顯日語在單字（字元）層級的資訊密度與使用者的自我壓縮習慣。(4–6) 另一方面， LINE 在日本社會的滲透，使「訊息＝日常主要書寫管道」成為常態；其普及被多家媒體報導為覆蓋相當高比例人口。(7) 更重要的是，這些平台把語言的成功指標從「語法正確」轉為「回應速度」「情緒對齊」「可搜尋標籤」與「可見性風險最小化」：同一句話是否被誤讀、是否可被截圖擴散、是否留下可追溯痕跡，成為語體選擇的核心條件。(3) 因此，2010s–的變化之所以「看起來最陡」，常常不是因為日語本體變得陌生，而是因為 **書寫的社會功能改道** ：從單向出版轉為多向互動，語體在平台約束下被重新工程化。

## Section 2 (English) — Postwar to Smartphone-Era Japanese: Regulation, Media, and the Reallocation of Compression

### 2.1 Postwar script reform and the re-anchoring of “public written Japanese”

If postwar change is measured only as “has spoken Japanese changed?”, the most consequential transformation is missed: the recalibration of orthography and public norms. Reforms such as modern kana usage and state-linked guidance on kanji usage were not merely technical convenience; they bundled readability, democratization, and mass education into a language-governance project, reshaping what counted as legitimate public writing and how it could be taught, edited, and reproduced (1,2). A standardized written variety became more portable across sites—schools, newspapers, bureaucratic documents—while simultaneously sharpening the boundary between “publicly visible” forms and those relegated to private speech. In this sense, postwar change is a redistribution of visibility and normativity: which forms are allowed into public text is not purely endogenous evolution but a policy–media–education co-production (1–3).

### 2.2 1950s–1990s: editorial regimes and the templating of register

From the early postwar decades through the bubble era, the most salient shifts were rarely “syntactic revolutions” but editorial templating. Newspapers, magazines, and broadcasters (e.g., NHK) routinized language production through style rules, house conventions, and copyediting workflows, producing a predictable, high-authority public prose. Two consequences followed. First, public text favored dense nominal packaging, explicit logical marking, and stable reporting frames—features that increased predictability and institutional authority. Second, speech-like resources—ellipsis, ambiguity, affective particles, and stance cues—were either suppressed or rewritten into publicly acceptable substitutes. This is why many users perceive large postwar change: the “core grammar” may be continuous, but “publicly visible Japanese” is progressively engineered into an editorial product (2,3). That editorial template later becomes the comparison class against which platform language is experienced as a sharp rupture.

### 2.3 2010s–: platform-driven compression and the shift from readability to interaction

The smartphone era’s steepness is best understood as platforms directly shaping register through interactional mechanics. Short-form feeds compress expression into units optimized for rapid consumption and resharing; messaging pushes language into micro-turn sequences that reward ellipsis, affect marking, and semiotic hybridity. For Twitter, analyses of the character-limit debate highlight that Japanese posts are shorter on average and far less likely to hit the limit, underscoring both higher information density per character and learned user-side compression (4–6). Meanwhile, LINE has entrenched messaging as a dominant everyday writing channel in Japan, with widely reported population-level penetration (7). Crucially, these platforms redefine success metrics: not grammatical correctness but response speed, affect alignment, searchability (tags), and the minimization of visibility risk—what can be screenshotted, amplified, or left as an auditable trace (3). The “steepness” of the 2010s onward thus often reflects not a sudden mutation of Japanese itself, but a functional rerouting of writing from one-way publication to multi-directional interaction, with register increasingly treated as an engineered interface.

## 第 3 節　性別語的弱化與再分化：特徵集拆解、場域壓力與風格化資源

### 3.1 從「女言葉/男言葉」到索引資源：性別語必須被拆成可運算的特徵集

「性別語弱化」之所以常被誤判為單向消退，是因為大眾討論往往把性別語當成整體套件（package）而非可拆解的索引資源（indexical resources）。在社會語言學脈絡中，性別化形式更像「可被拿來做風格化（styling）與角色化（enregisterment）」的工具箱：同一個終助詞、代名詞或敬語策略，在不同場域可指向截然不同的身份姿態，而不必對應生理性別。(1,2) 早期針對年輕世代的研究已指出，年輕女性在自然會話中使用較少刻板「女性」終助詞，顯示「規範式女言葉」並非口語的原樣。(3) 進一步，教材/教科書對性別語的再現常誇大其性別對立，造成學習者以為日常會話更「二元化」；教科書對話與自然會話在性別關聯終助詞的分布差異即是一例。(4) 近年的語料研究更直接挑戰「終助詞＝性別專屬」的預設：例如針對青年會話的分析發現，被視為「女性」的終助詞 *wa/no* 可能由男性更頻繁使用，顯示其功能已可被重新索引為態度或風格資源而非性別標記。(5)

### 3.2 職場：性別語並非消失，而是被「禮貌技術」重組

職場語境的關鍵不是「更中性」這句口號，而是互動風險與責任可歸屬性增加：在可見性高、後果可追溯的環境中，說話者更傾向動用敬語、迂迴與間接指示來管理對上對下的面子與權力關係。日本企業的「商務禮儀訓練」研究顯示，禮貌在職場被教成一套可操作的策略，而非單純的形式選擇；敬語、態度、姿勢與話語框架共同構成「可被評分」的表現。(6) 這種訓練化與標準化，會把原本被視為性別差異的部分資源（例如柔化、婉轉）重新定位為「專業」與「合宜」；同時，某些粗礪或直接的形式，即便被部分女性在私域採用，在職場仍可能被評價為不適切。結果是：性別語的「弱化」在職場更多呈現為 **特徵的功能轉移** （gender → professionalism）與 **策略的分工** （終助詞/代名詞下降，敬語與語用策略上升），而非整體消散。

### 3.3 戀愛與匿名網路：去性別化與再性別化並存，平台加速風格化的極端值

若說職場強化的是一致性與可預測性，戀愛與匿名網路則提供更高的風格化空間。戀愛互動允許更密集的情緒協調，因而更容易保留「角色語」式的語氣資源；匿名網路（特別是可扮演/可偽裝的場域）則可能同時出現兩種方向：一方面為避免被辨識而採取去性別化、去地域化的中性寫法；另一方面又會為了戲仿、挑釁或社群內梗而使用高度性別化的「刻板語氣」。大型推特語料研究顯示，性別化終助詞與第一人稱代名詞在平台上仍呈現可量化的差異分布，但同時也顯示其使用與用戶類型/公開身份等因素交織，而非單純按性別二分。(7) 更早針對網路「偽裝性別」的討論亦指出，性別化語言可被用作線上身份建構工具，並可能在網路空間再製既有性別秩序。(8) 因此，智慧型手機時代的關鍵不是「性別語沒了」，而是平台把性別語推向兩個端點： **一端是可撤回、低風險的中性化** ；另一端是高辨識度、可表演的角色化。這種兩極化本身，就是第 2 節所述「可見性—可擴散性—可追溯性」壓力在性別索引資源上的投影。(2)

## Section 3 (English) — The “Weakening” and Re-Differentiation of Japanese Gendered Language: Feature Bundles, Site Pressures, and Stylization

### 3.1 From “women’s/men’s language” to indexical resources: operationalizing gendered language

Claims that Japanese gendered language is “disappearing” often treat it as a single package rather than a bundle of indexical resources. Contemporary sociolinguistic work frames gendered forms as tools for styling and enregisterment: the same particle, pronoun, or politeness strategy can index different stances across contexts without mapping neatly onto biological sex (1,2). Early work on generational patterns already suggested that younger women use fewer stereotypically “feminine” sentence-final particles in natural conversation, indicating that prescriptive “onna kotoba” is not a transparent reflection of everyday speech (3). The representational problem is compounded by pedagogical materials: textbook dialogues frequently exaggerate binary gender contrasts, and comparisons between textbook language and natural conversation show mismatches in the distribution of gender-associated particles (4). More recent corpus-based findings further destabilize the “gender-exclusive particle” premise: analyses of youth conversations report that particles classified as “female,” such as *wa/no*, can be used more frequently by male speakers, implying that these forms are being re-indexed as affective or stylistic resources rather than fixed gender markers (5).

### 3.2 Workplace speech: not disappearance but functional reassembly into “professional” politeness

In workplace interaction, the driving force is not an abstract move toward neutrality but elevated visibility, accountability, and reputational risk. Speakers rely more heavily on honorifics, indirectness, and mitigation to manage face and institutional hierarchies. Ethnographic work on Japanese business etiquette training shows politeness being taught as an operational toolkit—where linguistic forms, embodied comportment, and interactional framing are evaluated together (6). This institutionalization can re-route features once interpreted as gendered (e.g., mitigation, deference) into the indexical field of “professional appropriateness.” Meanwhile, forms perceived as rough or too direct may remain costly regardless of private-domain shifts. Hence, “weakening” in workplace contexts often takes the form of functional transfer (gender → professionalism) and strategic redistribution (reduced reliance on particles/pronouns, increased reliance on honorific and pragmatic strategies), rather than wholesale erosion.

### 3.3 Romance and anonymous online spaces: simultaneous de-gendering and re-gendering under platform pressure

Romantic interaction and anonymous online environments provide greater affordances for stylization. Romance often intensifies affect alignment and role-play, allowing gendered resources to persist in softer, relational ways. Anonymous online contexts, by contrast, can drive two opposing tendencies: de-gendering to reduce identifiability and exposure, and hyper-gendered stylization for parody, provocation, or in-group signaling. Large-scale Twitter analyses show measurable differences in the distribution of gendered particles and first-person pronouns, but also demonstrate that usage interacts with user type, public persona, and platform genres rather than tracking a simple binary (7). Earlier discussions of gender masquerade online similarly highlight how gendered Japanese can be mobilized to construct identities and can reproduce gender order in cyberspace (8). Under the smartphone-era conditions of visibility, searchability, and traceability emphasized in Section 2, gendered language is best understood as being pushed toward two poles: low-risk neutralization on one end, and high-recognition performative stylization on the other—each strategically selected to manage exposure while maintaining interactional goals (2).

## 第 4 節　日本同志語彙的媒介路徑：從夜場口耳到 APP 標籤化

### 4.1 「場所—媒介」雙軌生成的社群語體：線下修復性 vs 線上可檢索性

承接第 3 節的結論（性別/風格資源在平台壓力下兩極化），同志社群語彙也呈現類似的「雙軌語體」：一端是以面對面互動為核心的場所語體，另一端是被介面與搜尋邏輯塑形的標籤語體。以 新宿二丁目 作為象徵性場域，其歷史形成本身與戰後都市與社群可見性的變化相關，並在「LGBT 社群空間」的公共論述中反覆被定位與再敘事。(1) 這類場域的語言任務，首要是維持互動可修復性（repair）：說錯能補、冒犯能緩和、界線能試探後撤；因此分類與評價往往採用梯度、暗示與迂迴。相對地，交友 APP 的語言任務是高匹配性（matching）與高可檢索性：自我描述必須被濃縮成可篩選欄位與可搜尋標籤。關鍵在於：兩種語體並非「真實/不真實」對立，而是同一社群在不同媒介條件下對同一社交風險函數的最適化解。

### 4.2 二丁目與夜場對話：面子管理、互動倫理與「灰度分類」

夜場互動的社會技術包含面子工作（facework）、禮貌策略與默契式的界線測試。相關研究指出，日本的同志社群空間研究（包含二丁目人際關係與親密性）往往難以將「性」與「社會關係」切割，因為場域本身同時是交友、互助、資訊流通與身份確認的節點。(2) 另一條研究路徑則直接觀察日本年輕同志在現實空間與網路空間之間的互動：線下關係如何延伸到線上，線上分類又如何回流塑造線下互動。(3) 這種雙向回路使得夜場語體出現一個核心傾向：以「不把話說死」作為倫理性姿態。分類語彙（例如體型、年齡感、風格）常以「大概」「比較」「偏…」等緩衝結構包裹，讓對方保留改寫與拒絕的空間。從語用學角度，這可視為一種和らげ表現（attenuation）體系：透過降低斷言力、提升可撤回性來避免社交損失。(4)

### 4.3 交友 APP：標籤化、欄位化與「自我商品化」的語彙工程

在 APP 介面中，「自我」被拆成可搜尋的向量：身高體重、體型標籤、角色偏好、喜好等。這種拆解必然提高詞彙的名詞化與標準化，並鼓勵使用者採用社群內已慣例化的縮寫與梯度碼（例如把連續的體型光譜壓縮為少數可選項）。其效果是雙重的：一方面降低溝通成本並提高配對效率；另一方面也把社群內原本可在對話中細緻協商的差異，提前固定為「可見、可被篩除」的特徵，強化了分類的可監督性與可比較性。若結合「溝通可見性」理論，平台讓互動變得可旁觀、可擴散、可追溯，於是使用者更傾向採取可辯護、可否認、可切換的表述，並將高風險詞彙外包給代碼與欄位。(5)

### 4.4 「〜寄り」作為分類緩衝器：把類型斷言改寫成可撤回的傾向陳述

「〜寄り」的關鍵不在字面「靠近」，而在其語用功能：它把分類從二元判斷改寫為連續光譜上的定位，並降低對他人施加標籤的力道。這種緩衝與「ヘッジ（hedge）」概念相容：透過修飾命題強度以維持良好人際關係。(6) 更一般地，日本語用研究對「和らげ表現」指出，緩和並非只靠單一詞，而是可由句末形式、語氣副詞與評價框架共同完成。(4) 因此，在夜場語體中，「〜寄り」往往與其他緩衝資源共現（例如「ちょっと」「まあ」「感じ」等），形成一套可快速部署的「灰度分類語法」；在 APP 語體中，它則可作為介於標籤與敘述之間的折衷，允許使用者同時滿足可檢索性與自我詮釋權。

## Section 4 (English) — Media Pathways of Japanese Gay Lexicon: From Nightlife Talk to App Tagging

### 4.1 A dual-register ecology shaped by place and platform

Building on Section 3’s claim that smartphone-era visibility pressures polarize stylization, Japanese gay lexicon likewise develops along two coupled registers: a place-based nightlife register optimized for repairable, face-managed interaction, and a platform register engineered for searchability and matching. As a symbolic site, Shinjuku Ni-chōme is repeatedly framed in scholarship as a community space whose postwar emergence is tied to changing urban formations and public visibility of LGBT communities (1). In such spaces, language prioritizes repair: missteps can be softened, boundaries can be tested and withdrawn, and classification can remain negotiable. Dating apps invert these priorities. Profiles are designed for filtering, comparability, and discoverability; speakers compress selfhood into fields and tags. The contrast is not authenticity versus performance, but two solutions to the same social-risk optimization problem under different media constraints.

### 4.2 Nightlife discourse: facework, interactional ethics, and “graded categorization”

Nightlife interaction relies on facework and politeness strategies that keep social contact viable. Work on Japanese gay intimacy and social relations in community spaces emphasizes that sexuality cannot be cleanly separated from relational life because venues function as hubs for friendship, mutual support, information exchange, and identity confirmation (2). A complementary line of research explicitly examines feedback loops between offline and online communication among younger gay men in Japan: how offline ties extend online, and how online categorization flows back into offline interaction (3). These dynamics motivate an interactional ethic of not “locking in” categories too hard. Speakers often wrap potentially risky descriptions—body type, vibe, style—in hedges and gradients, making categorizations retractable. From a pragmatic standpoint, this aligns with Japanese attenuation (warage) systems that reduce assertive force to limit social damage (4).

### 4.3 Dating apps: tagging, fieldification, and the engineering of self-presentation

On apps, the self is decomposed into searchable vectors: height/weight, body-type tags, preferences, and tastes. This encourages nominalization and standardization, and it rewards conventional abbreviations and coarse-grained gradients that fit menus and filters. The result is double-edged: communication costs decrease and matching efficiency increases, but differences that could have been negotiated in conversation become pre-fixed, visible traits subject to filtering and surveillance. Communication-visibility theory helps explain why: when messages are observable, amplifiable, and traceable, speakers adopt defensible, deniable formulations and offload riskier meanings onto codes, dropdown fields, and standardized tags (5).

### 4.4 〜yori as a classificatory buffer: shifting categorical assertion into retractable tendency

The pragmatic power of 〜yori (“leaning toward…”) is less its literal proximity meaning than its buffering function. It reframes classification as a position on a continuum and lowers the imposition of labeling others. This aligns with “hedging,” defined as expressions that soften propositional content or speech acts to maintain good relations (6). Japanese pragmatic scholarship on attenuation further shows that mitigation is typically compositional—built via sentence-final forms, adverbials, and evaluative framing rather than a single token (4). In nightlife talk, 〜yori often co-occurs with other softeners (e.g., “a bit,” “kind of,” “well”), forming a deployable “grammar of gray categorization.” On apps, it can function as a compromise between tag and narrative, enabling speakers to satisfy searchability while retaining interpretive control over self-description.

## 第 5 節　「熊圈／GMPD」體型詞彙的社會語言學：從雜誌想像到 APP 向量化

### 5.1 雜誌—夜場—APP 的譜系：體型不是「描述」，而是社群分類技術

延續第 4 節「場所語體 vs 平台語體」的結論，熊系（クマ系（くまけい））與 GMPD 的體型詞彙可被視為一套 **社群內部的分類技術** ：它不只描述身體，還同時在做「市場切分」「親密互動的風險管理」與「可見性治理」。日本同志媒體史中，雜誌長期以「type」分眾：例如以壯碩、年長或特定職業幻想作為核心的刊物與欄目，促成了「身體—慾望—生活方式」的穩定連結。(1,2) 近年的研究進一步顯示，像 *Sabu* 這類刊物不僅是情色媒體，也提供身份敘事、社群連帶與對抗汙名的文本空間；讀者來信/互動欄位尤其是「類型語彙」的實驗場。(3) 當這套分眾邏輯進入交友 APP，類型不再只是敘事資源，而被介面直接「欄位化」：使用者必須把身體與偏好壓縮成可搜尋、可篩選的標籤向量。針對 新宿二丁目 及其「ゲイシーン」的研究，已指出線下場域與線上平台之間存在互相回饋的循環；而 9monsters（ナイモン）等應用更把這種循環制度化為日常配對機制。(4)

### 5.2 GMPD 的「梯度語法」：字母縮寫、混成詞與「可撤回的精準」

GMPD（ガッチリ（がっちり）／ムッチリ（むっちり）／ぽっちゃり（ぽっちゃり）／デブ（でぶ））作為縮寫，最重要的不是四分類本身，而是它提供了一種 **可延展的梯度語法** ：使用者可用單字母（G、P 等）、可用組合（GP、MP、GMP）、也可用混成詞細分（例：ガチムチ（がちむち）、ガチポチャ（がちぽちゃ）、ガチデブ（がちでぶ））。(5,6) 這種「可撤回的精準」特別適合平台語境：它足夠精準以利匹配，又足夠模糊以保留自我詮釋權。詞源層面，ガチムチ（がちむち）的流行史常被描述為從雜誌與圈內運動（如「SG（スーパーがっちり）」等）演變而來，並逐漸把「原先被歸為デブ」的一部分身體重新命名為「受歡迎的（モテスジ）」。(7) 換言之，這些詞不是中立描述，而是對身體資本的再分配：把某些脂肪/肌肉比例重新框架為魅力。

更細微的是，GMPD 詞彙系統高度依賴「邊界管理」：ぽっちゃり（ぽっちゃり）與デブ（でぶ）的邊界、ムッチリ（むっちり）與ガチムチ（がちむち）的邊界，往往不是量化指標，而是 **社群互動中的協商結果** 。因此它天然會與第 4 節提到的緩衝器共現，例如「〜寄り（より）」把類型斷言改寫成傾向（例：P寄り、M寄り），降低標籤的施壓感，提升可修復性。(6,8)

### 5.3 與英語 bear 詞彙的「錯位對譯」：同一身體，不同分類坐標

英語圈的 bear/cub/otter 等分類常同時編碼「體毛」「年齡感」「氣質/角色」；相對地，日本的 GMPD 更像以「肌肉×脂肪」作主軸的連續光譜，再以クマ系（くまけい）補上「體毛與粗獷男性氣質」的索引。這造成一種常見的翻譯錯位：把 bear 直譯為クマ（くま）會漏掉「胖熊/小熊/水獺」那套英語內部的次分類；反過來把 GMPD 直譯為 bear 又會錯過其核心其實是「體組成梯度」。(5,7) 在平台條件下（可見、可擴散、可追溯），這種錯位更容易引發誤讀：同一標籤在跨語社群間被截圖搬運時，讀者會用自己熟悉的分類坐標去解碼，於是產生「被誤貼」的感受。此處可把 GMPD 視為一種 **介面友善的本地分類法** ：它將身體差異壓縮為可運算的匹配特徵，並透過縮寫、混成與「〜寄り」提供必要的模糊度來降低社交風險。(8)

## Section 5 (English) — The Sociolinguistics of “Bear/GMPD” Body-Type Lexicon in Japan: From Magazine Imaginaries to App Vectorization

### 5.1 A media genealogy: body types as community classification technology

Following Section 4’s place–platform contrast, Japanese “bear-adjacent” lexicon (クマ系 *kuma-kei*) and the GMPD system can be treated as a **community classification technology** rather than mere description. In Japanese gay media history, magazines have long segmented audiences by “type,” stabilizing links between body imaginaries, desire, and lifestyle fantasies (1,2). Recent scholarship on *Sabu* argues that it functioned not only as erotic media but also as a venue for identity articulation, solidarity, and stigma resistance—especially through interactive reader-correspondence genres where type terms circulate and mutate (3). When this typological logic migrates into dating apps, “type” becomes interface-enforced: bodies and preferences are fieldified into searchable tags and filters. Work on app-mediated gay communication in Japan—explicitly addressing 9monsters (ナイモン)—highlights feedback loops between offline “gay scene” spaces and online categorization, suggesting a systematic institutionalization of these loops in everyday matching (4).

### 5.2 The “grammar of gradient categorization”: acronyms, blends, and retractable precision

GMPD—ガッチリ (*gacchiri*), ムッチリ (*mucchiri*), ぽっちゃり (*pocchari*), デブ (*debu*)—matters less as a four-way taxonomy than as a **productive grammar for gradient categorization**. Users can deploy single letters (G, P), combinations (GP, MP, GMP), and blended refinements (ガチムチ *gachimuchi*, ガチポチャ *gachipocha*, ガチデブ *gachidebu*) to tune classification granularity (5,6). This yields “retractable precision,” especially useful on platforms: precise enough for matching, ambiguous enough to preserve interpretive control. Lexicographic accounts of ガチムチ describe a trajectory in which earlier intra-scene movements (e.g., “SG”) helped reframe certain bodies previously lumped as “debu” into a valorized desirability lane—i.e., a redistribution of bodily capital through naming (7). Boundaries between categories are rarely quantitative; they are interactionally negotiated, and therefore naturally pair with hedging buffers such as 〜寄り (“leaning toward”), which shifts categorical assertion into tendency and reduces face-threat (6,8).

### 5.3 Translation misalignment with English “bear” vocabularies

English bear/cub/otter labels often co-encode hairiness, age-feel, and vibe/role, whereas Japanese GMPD primarily encodes a muscle–fat continuum, with クマ系 adding hairiness and rugged masculinity as an index. This produces a recurrent translation misfit: translating bear as クマ risks collapsing English internal subtypes; translating GMPD as bear misses GMPD’s core logic as body-composition gradience (5,7). Under platform conditions of visibility, amplification, and traceability, misalignment becomes socially consequential—screenshots travel, and readers decode labels using their own local coordinate systems. In this sense, GMPD is best described as an **interface-friendly local taxonomy**: it compresses embodied difference into computationally tractable matching features while maintaining a built-in margin of ambiguity through abbreviation, blending, and “〜yori” hedging (8).

在日本語境中，「SM」長期作為涵蓋式門牌，把多樣實作（綁縛、權力交換、角色扮演等）以兩個心理學借源符碼（S/M）統攝，並在大眾媒體與日常話語中逐步發生「去情境化」：詞彙從特定實作或偏好標記，漂移為人格特質與互動風格的評語。此過程最具代表性者為「ドS／ドM」。日文百科條目已將「ドM」界定為指涉「極端偏 M 的性格/行為」的俗語，並提及其與大眾媒體人物的自我歸因敘事相連。 (1) 重要的不是「誰發明」的版本學，而是：當「度（ど）」成為強度前綴時，S/M 便可在非性語境中被重新索引為「刻薄/強勢」與「能忍/被動」等互動風格，並因此降低了入門門檻，卻也提高了圈內外語義錯位的機率。相同機制亦作用於「女王様」與「調教」：前者可在圈內是角色稱謂或職業稱呼，但圈外常被讀為對強勢女性的符號化刻板；後者在圈內可被使用為規則、訓練、日常秩序的技術語，圈外卻更容易被喚起「馴化/物化」的評價層。這些漂移顯示：日語 BDSM 詞彙的社會生命，常在「類型名（type label）」與「道德想像（moral imagination）」之間來回擺盪，而平台化可見性會加速此擺盪（第 2、4 節之邏輯延伸）。 (2)

## Section 6 (English version) — Semantic Drift and Cross-Linguistic Circulation in BDSM/Kink Lexicon: From Type Labels to Ethical Frameworks

### 6.1 Japanese: “SM” as an umbrella, and the decontextualization of ドS/ドM, 女王様, 調教

In Japanese discourse, “SM” has long functioned as an umbrella signboard that collapses diverse practices into the S/M dyad, and it has progressively undergone **decontextualization** in mainstream circulation: terms shift from practice- or preference-linked labels into personality descriptors and interaction-style evaluations. The most salient case is ドS/ドM. Japanese reference discourse treats ドM as a slang label for “extremely M-like” character or behavior, and popular narratives connect its spread to mass-media self-attribution and comedic uptake. The analytic point is not authorship but re-indexing: once ド- becomes an intensifier, S/M can be recruited outside sexual contexts to index “harsh/assertive” versus “enduring/passive,” lowering entry barriers while increasing mismatch between in-group precision and out-group interpretation. Similar dynamics apply to 女王様 and 調教. Within kink communities these can operate as role terms or technical vocabularies for rules, training, and daily-ordering; outside, they are more likely to trigger stereotypical readings (e.g., caricatured dominance, animalizing/objectifying connotations). Under conditions of platform visibility and traceability, such oscillation between “type label” and “moral imagination” tends to accelerate.

### 6.2 Kinbaku/Shibari: from restraint techniques to aesthetic borrowings and brand-like genres

Unlike the abstractability of S/M, 緊縛/縛り centers on a concrete technique and then travels cross-culturally through **aestheticization and branding**. English-language discourse often uses *kinbaku* or *shibari* to denote “Japanese rope bondage,” increasingly framed in photography, performance, and contemporary art. Japanese and English reference descriptions foreground historical linkages to restraint methods (hojōjutsu) and narrate a transition—from punitive/constraint contexts toward erotic and artistic tying in the late 19th to early 20th century. A journalistic but historically oriented account in *The Guardian* similarly notes origins in captive restraint and later transformation into erotic *kinbaku*, emphasizing “the beauty of tight binding.” Linguistically, cross-language borrowing can background violence-adjacent histories while foregrounding craft and aesthetics: *shibari* in English often behaves like a stylistic genre rather than a subset within a broader BDSM ethical vocabulary. This creates structural misalignment between Japanese “kinbaku within SM frames” and English “shibari as an independent aesthetic category.”

### 6.3 English: SSC/RACK and the institutionalization of consent talk as portable negotiation language

A distinct English-language lexical trajectory turns ethics and negotiation into transportable acronyms. SSC (safe, sane, consensual) has been widely adopted since the 1980s as a public-facing boundary marker separating consensual kink from abuse/violence, and historical narratives often associate it with educational community environments such as GMSMA. Critiques of “safe” and “sane” then motivated RACK (risk-aware consensual kink), shifting emphasis from guaranteed safety to informed, negotiated risk. SSC/RACK comparison has entered scholarly and practice-facing discourse: Williams and colleagues proposed the 4Cs (caring, communication, consent, caution) to enrich negotiation dimensions, while recent ethics writing frames RACK as a corrective to SSC by acknowledging risk and avoiding stigmatizing mental-stability assumptions. Importantly, SSC/RACK functions as (i) public legitimacy talk, (ii) internal negotiation protocol, and (iii) platform-portable compression—exactly the triad that made GMPD-style taxonomies interface-friendly in Section 5. This sets up Section 7’s translation-politics problem: what travels cross-linguistically is often not a single word, but the capacity of a target language to carry an equivalent **frameworked ethics**.

## 第 7 節　翻譯政治與平台治理：discipline→「調教/規訓/紀律」的分岔路徑

### 7.1 多義詞的「定錨競爭」：discipline 在 BDSM 語用更靠近 training 還是 punishment？

第 6 節指出，跨語流動真正關鍵是「框架能否被承載」。在此視角下， *discipline* 的翻譯爭議不是字典對應，而是 **語用定錨** ：在 BDSM 圈內， *discipline* 經常與「規則設定、行為訓練、獎懲結構、日常秩序（protocol）」共構；它可以包含 punishment，但並不等同於 punishment。這種多義性在一般英語也存在： *discipline* 同時可指訓練/管教、紀律/自律、以及懲戒等義群，語境決定哪個義群被前景化。  
因此，翻譯時真正的問題變成：譯詞是否把讀者預設帶往「可協商的訓練/規則」框架，還是帶往「不對等權力的懲戒/矯正」框架。若譯詞在第一眼就讓圈外讀者把互動想成「施暴/制服」，那麼即便後文補上同意與界線，誤讀路徑已被啟動（path dependence）。這也解釋了為何同一社群在不同平台會偏好不同說法：可見性越高、擴散風險越大，就越需要選擇能自帶「非暴力、可撤回、可協商」預設的語彙組合（承接第 2、4、5 節的介面壓力邏輯）。

### 7.2 為何中文常選「調教」：把 discipline 拉向「玩法技術層」的低摩擦通道

「調教」在中文 BDSM/戀物語境中常被用來指涉一套 **技術—程序** ：規則、任務、獎懲、行為塑形、以及長期動態中的日常秩序。它的優勢在於：即使圈外讀者未必熟悉 BDSM，也容易把「調教」先讀成「訓練/養成」而非立即等同暴力。換言之，它在語感上提供一條 **較低摩擦的入門通道** ，把 *discipline* 的中心義拉向 training/conditioning，而把 punishment 放在可選分支。這與英語社群將倫理與協商程序框架化（如 SSC/RACK/4Cs）並可被平台壓縮的傳統，形成某種「結構相似」：先確立可協商性，再處理強度與邊界。  
然而，「調教」同時帶有第二層陰影語感：馴化、物化、支配性人格政治。第 6 節已指出日語「調教」也存在「中性技術層 vs 評價羞辱層」的分裂；中文同樣如此，只是分裂常被平台條件放大：在公開平台偏向技術層的可辯護用法，在私域/暗碼則可能保留更強烈的評價層。這種「雙層承載」使「調教」成為高度可移植的譯項，但也使研究者必須把它拆成可操作的兩層來測量（例如在讀者實驗中分別評估「技術聯想」與「暴力/物化聯想」）。

### 7.3 「規訓」的高風險：規訓與懲罰的影子如何把 BDSM 拉向「權力與暴力」的誤讀

「規訓」在中文知識語境極容易被 米歇爾·傅柯 的《規訓與懲罰》定錨：它召喚的是制度性權力、身體治理、監視與常模化。 當 *discipline* 被譯為「規訓」時，讀者很可能把 BDSM 的 discipline（作為合意關係中的規則/訓練）直接投影成「體制暴力的微觀機制」，於是「合意」「可撤回」「照護」等框架語反而被讀成權力話語的粉飾。這不是說「規訓」不能用；而是它需要更高密度的同意框架與語境釘死（例如同句綁定協商程序、風險知情、事後照護），否則誤讀路徑的先驗機率過高。  
在學術寫作裡，「規訓」當然可用來做分析（例如把 protocol 視為日常秩序的治理技術）；但在翻譯/科普/平台傳播中，它往往不是中性載體，而是強力的理論觸發器，等同把讀者預設帶往「權力＝暴力」的解碼模式。這正是第 6 節所謂「搬運框架」的反面例子：譯詞本身先搬運了另一套框架。

### 7.4 中文平台的「敏感詞文化」：審查—隱語工程如何再加工「調教/羞辱/綑綁」等詞

一旦進入中文平台生態，翻譯政治會與治理技術耦合。研究已提出「敏感詞文化（Sensitive Word Culture）」概念：網民在關鍵詞過濾與不確定執法下，透過改寫、替代、諧音、拆字與縮寫來達成可傳播但不易被機器捕捉的表達。 這不只是逃避審查，更會反向塑造語義：一個詞被迫以諧音/縮寫形式流通時，原本的倫理框架（同意、界線、照護）可能被「省略成本」擠壓掉，只剩高刺激、低語境的核心詞根，從而提高外部誤讀率。  
同時，技術研究顯示同音替換能有效延緩內容被刪除的時間，說明「諧音化」確實是可度量的規避策略。 語言學/計算語言學亦把中文社媒的編碼語言系統化分類（含諧音、隱喻、符號轉寫），指出其既承擔政治表達，也承擔社群自我保護與圈內互認。  
因此，中文 BDSM 詞彙的演化不能只看翻譯對應，更要看「可見性壓力」如何把詞彙推向兩端：公開端更傾向技術化、去暴力的可辯護語（例如把 discipline 說成規則/訓練/任務），私域端則可能保留更高強度、更評價化的隱語。這個分化結構，與第 3 節性別語在平台上呈現的兩極化（中性化 vs 角色化）是同一種動力學。

## Section 7 (English) — Translation Politics and Platform Governance: How Discipline Routes Readers toward “Training” or “Power/Violence”

### 7.1 Polysemy as “anchoring competition”: in BDSM, is discipline closer to training or punishment?

Section 6 argued that what truly travels cross-linguistically is not a single lexical item but an **ethical frame**. The dispute over translating *discipline* is therefore an anchoring problem: in BDSM usage, *discipline* commonly co-occurs with rule-setting, behavioral training, reward–consequence structures, and everyday order/protocol. Punishment may appear, but it is not the semantic center by default. This aligns with general English polysemy: *discipline* can mean training/instruction, rule-based self-control, and punitive correction, with context selecting the dominant sense.  
Translation choices thus determine the reader’s “default parse.” If the target term makes outsiders immediately picture coercion or institutional subjugation, later additions of consent/boundaries must fight an uphill battle against a now-activated misreading path. Under the visibility, amplification, and traceability pressures discussed in Sections 2–5, this anchoring effect becomes stronger: high-visibility contexts incentivize terms that carry a built-in “negotiable, retractable, consensual” presupposition.

### 7.2 Why Chinese often picks 「調教」: a low-friction route to the technical layer of play

In Chinese kink discourse, 「調教」 frequently functions as a **technique–procedure** label: rules, tasks, reward/penalty logic, shaping behavior, and long-term dynamics of everyday order. Its pragmatic advantage is that many outsiders will first map it onto “training/conditioning” rather than immediate violence, effectively routing *discipline* toward the training-centered reading while leaving punishment as an optional branch. This structurally resembles the English community tendency to compress negotiation ethics into portable frameworks (SSC/RACK/4Cs): establish negotiability first, then talk intensity and limits.  
Yet 「調教」 carries a second evaluative shadow (animalization/objectification). As noted in Section 6 for Japanese as well, the term can split into a neutral technical layer and a charged evaluative layer. Platform conditions often amplify this split: public discourse gravitates toward the defensible technical layer, while private channels may retain stronger evaluative codes. This makes 「調教」 highly portable but analytically non-trivial—empirical work should operationalize its two layers separately (e.g., measuring “technical association” vs “violence/objectification association” in reader studies).

### 7.3 The high-risk load of 「規訓」: 規訓與懲罰 as a powerful trigger for “power/violence” decoding

「規訓」 in Chinese intellectual contexts is strongly anchored to 米歇爾·傅柯 and *Discipline and Punish*, invoking institutional power, bodily governance, surveillance, and normalization. When *discipline* is rendered as 「規訓」 in kink translation, many readers will map consensual rule/training dynamics onto a pre-existing “institutional violence” schema; consent language can then be reinterpreted as rhetorical cover rather than ethical infrastructure. This does not mean 「規訓」 is unusable—within academic analysis it can be precise. But in public-facing translation, it demands dense, explicit consent/procedure co-text to prevent the default inference from collapsing into “power = violence.” In other words, the term itself imports a competing frame before the kink frame can be established.

### 7.4 “Sensitive word culture”: how censorship-driven recoding reshapes kink lexicon in Chinese platforms

Once translation enters Chinese platform ecologies, governance and lexicon co-evolve. Research conceptualizes a “Sensitive Word Culture” in which users respond to keyword filtering and uncertain enforcement via recoding—substitution, homophones, abbreviations, split-characters, and euphemistic rerouting. This is not merely evasion; it feeds back into meaning: when words circulate primarily as coded variants, ethical scaffolding (consent, boundaries, care) can be squeezed out by compression pressures, leaving high-salience roots that are easier to misread out of context.  
Technically oriented studies show homophone substitution measurably delays censorship removal on Sina Weibo, demonstrating that “homophonic recoding” is an effective, quantifiable tactic. Linguistic and computational work also classifies coding strategies (homophones, metaphors, symbolic transcriptions) as systematic infrastructures of communication under censorship.  
Therefore, Chinese BDSM vocabulary change cannot be modeled as translation alone. It is translation **plus** a platform-driven bifurcation: public-facing terms become more technical, defensible, and de-violentized; private-domain codes may preserve higher-intensity evaluative layers. This mirrors Section 3’s finding that platform visibility polarizes linguistic resources into low-risk neutralization and high-recognition performative stylization.

## 第 8 節　整合模型與研究議程：把「詞彙漂移」當成可測量的社會—技術動力系統

### 8.1 從索引序到「語體註冊化」：類型詞與倫理詞如何被做成可搬運的 register

承接第 7 節的結論，本綜述提出一個更強的主張： **跨語流動時真正被搬運的不是單字，而是「可重複部署的語體單位」** 。Silverstein 的索引序理論提示我們，形式一旦可反覆在互動中指向社會價值，就會在不同層級產生再定義與再評價的鏈式轉換。(1) Agha 所謂「註冊化（enregisterment）」則更進一步指出：某套形式能否成為被社會辨識的 register，取決於它是否被話語性地「命名、對比、教導、模仿與批評」。  
把這兩者疊合，可以解釋第 4–7 節看到的現象：GMPD、〜寄り、SSC/RACK/4Cs、乃至於「調教/規訓」的譯詞分歧，並不是零散詞條，而是可攜、可壓縮、可自我複製的 register 元件。其關鍵指標包括：是否能被縮寫、是否能嵌入模板、是否能以最小語境負荷觸發一套可預期的推論。這也讓「翻譯政治」變成可操作問題：譯詞之爭，實際上是 **哪一套推論鏈被預設為默認** 。

### 8.2 平台可見性與敏感詞文化：治理壓力如何把語彙推向「向量化 + 隱語化」的雙極

Treem 等人提出的「溝通可見性」框架強調：數位環境使交流更容易被旁觀、被回溯、被擴散，從而改寫人們對語言風險的估算。(2) 在中文平台上，Ye 與 Zhao 將此推向更具制度性的層次：關鍵詞審查與不確定執法催生「敏感詞文化」，使用者發展出諧音、拆字、縮寫等再編碼慣例，而這些慣例本身會反過來塑造亞文化詞彙的語義與可見性。(3) 技術研究亦證實諧音替換能系統性延緩內容被刪除，顯示「隱語化」不只是象徵抵抗，而是可量化的策略選擇。(4) 相關 NLP 工作進一步把這些策略視為可分類、可建模的「創意編碼」現象。(5)  
因此，本綜述把平台效應刻畫為雙極：一端是 **向量化（vectorization）** ——把身分/偏好拆成可檢索欄位；另一端是 **隱語化（obfuscation）** ——把高風險概念包裝為機器不易抓取的形式。第 7 節的 *discipline* →「調教/規訓」分歧，正是這個雙極系統在「倫理框架能否被默認」上的表現：越需要公共辯護，越傾向選擇能自帶「可協商」預設的譯項；越受審查/汙名壓力，越可能在私域保留更高強度、更評價化的代碼。

### 8.3 可檢驗的研究設計：把「誤讀路徑」與「框架承載度」量化

若要把上述模型落地，至少需要三類互補方法。第一，跨場域語料：夜場/訪談文本與 APP 自介、短文平台貼文並置，標註（a）分類詞密度、（b）緩衝器（〜寄り等）共現、（c）同意/協商框架語出現率。第二，翻譯—推論實驗：操弄 *discipline* 的譯項（調教/紀律/規訓等）與同意框架共文（如 4Cs），測量讀者對「可協商性」「暴力聯想」「物化聯想」的推論機率。 第三，審查—再編碼網絡分析：追蹤敏感詞替代鏈（諧音/縮寫/替代詞）如何壓縮倫理語境，並評估哪一類詞最容易在傳播中「只剩刺激詞根」。  
在倫理層面，BDSM 研究明確指出同意是核心，但同意違反亦可能發生，因此研究設計需避免把「框架詞」當成保證，並在資料蒐集與匿名化上採取更高標準。

## Section 8 (English version) — An Integrative Model and Research Agenda: Treating Lexical Drift as a Measurable Sociotechnical System

### 8.1 From indexical order to enregisterment: how “type words” and “ethics words” become portable registers

Building on Section 7, this review advances a stronger claim: what travels across languages is less a word-to-word mapping than a **deployable register unit**. Silverstein’s indexical order explains how forms acquire layered social values through repeated interactional pointing, producing chains of re-evaluation across levels of social meaning. Agha’s notion of enregisterment specifies the sociocultural mechanics through which repertoires become recognizable registers—named, contrasted, taught, imitated, and criticized.  
When combined, these frameworks illuminate the patterns observed in Sections 4–7: GMPD, 〜yori hedging, SSC/RACK/4Cs, and even the branching translations of *discipline* are not isolated lexemes but **portable register components**. Their key diagnostic features are compressibility (acronyms, blends), template-compatibility (profiles, checklists), and inferential efficiency (minimal context required to trigger predictable interpretations). “Translation politics,” in this view, becomes experimentally tractable: the dispute is about which inferential chain is installed as the default.

### 8.2 Communication visibility and “sensitive word culture”: governance pressure polarizes language into vectorization and obfuscation

Treem et al.’s communication visibility framework emphasizes that digital infrastructures make messages more observable, traceable, and amplifiable, reshaping users’ risk calculations and linguistic choices. In Chinese platform ecologies, Ye and Zhao conceptualize “Sensitive Word Culture,” where keyword filtering and uncertain enforcement generate systematic recoding practices (homophones, abbreviations, character-splitting) that feed back into subcultural semantics and public visibility. Technical work demonstrates that homophonic substitution can measurably delay censorship removal, confirming that obfuscation is not merely symbolic but strategically effective. NLP research further treats such tactics as classifiable “creative encoding” phenomena.  
Accordingly, this review models platform effects as a polarized system: **vectorization** (turning identities/preferences into searchable fields) and **obfuscation** (encoding high-risk meanings into machine-resistant forms). The *discipline* →「調教/規訓」 divergence exemplifies how this system operates on ethical frames: the higher the need for public defensibility, the more a community favors terms that presuppose negotiability; the higher the censorship/stigma pressure, the more evaluative intensity may retreat into private codes.

### 8.3 Testable designs: quantifying “misreading paths” and “frame-carrying capacity”

To operationalize the model, at least three complementary approaches are needed. First, multi-sited corpora: juxtapose nightlife/interview materials with app bios and short-form posts; annotate (a) category-label density, (b) hedge co-occurrence (e.g., 〜yori), and (c) consent/negotiation frame language. Second, translation–inference experiments: manipulate *discipline* renderings (training-like vs governance-like) crossed with explicit negotiation frames (e.g., 4Cs), then measure perceived negotiability, violence association, and objectification association. Third, censorship–recoding network analysis: trace substitution chains (homophones/abbreviations) and test whether ethical scaffolding (consent, boundaries, care) gets compressed out during circulation, leaving high-salience roots that amplify misreadings.  
Ethically, BDSM scholarship emphasizes consent as central while also acknowledging that consent violations can occur; researchers should not treat “frame words” as guarantees and must adopt heightened standards for anonymization and participant safety.
