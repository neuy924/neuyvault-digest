### Main topics

This conversation built a “semantic ecology” of Chinese words for dirt, filth, and moral taint. We started from core everyday items (**污、垢、髒**) and expanded into a larger inventory including **穢、臊、臭、腌臢、邋遢、齷齪、骯髒、污穢、腥、羶/膻**. The project was not just definitional. You asked for a structured typology of how “dirty” vocabulary migrates across domains: from **olfaction and physical uncleanliness** into **morality, reputation, stigma, and emotion**.

We progressively tightened the framework by forcing every item into the same descriptive template— **core niche / extrapolation direction** —so that comparisons stayed principled rather than impressionistic.

### Key themes

A central theme was that “dirty” is not a single axis but a system of competing, cooperating semantic “species.” We treated “smell → morality” as a helpful spine, yet repeatedly showed it’s better modeled as a network with bridges and forks.

Another theme was **diachronic ordering**. You asked to re-sort the continuum by “historical precedence” (which abstract uses appear earliest), yielding a time-sequenced narrative of how sensory terms become moralized and then routinized in modern pragmatics (insults, profanity, stigma).

A third theme was **institutional stabilization**. We incorporated Buddhist and ritual/礼制 vocabularies—especially **觸穢/污觸**, and purification/regulation systems (戒律, 禊/祓, 齊戒, etc.)—to explain why the “normative end” of the lexicon becomes stable: once “dirt” is encoded as a repeatable rule system (trigger → consequence → remediation), it becomes administratively usable, teachable, and easy to extend into moral condemnation.

### The biggest takeaway is a typology of cross-domain mappings, not a single “dirty scale”

Across your successive refinements, the core conclusion hardened into a typology of three (and then arguably four) mapping routes:

1. **Smell → emotion**: the “臊” pathway bifurcating into outward ugliness/taint vs inward embarrassment/shame (害臊).
2. **Smell → reputation**: the “臭” chain you highlighted—(流芳↔遺臭) → 臭名 → 臭罵—showing how historiographic evaluation can sink into everyday interactional aggression.
3. **Form/appearance → character → uncleanliness**: “齷齪” as a different sensory anchor (visual/bodily form) that becomes a character attack and then a hygiene/moral taint label.
4. **Smell → suspicion/risk intuition** (a later comparative insight): especially strong in Japanese/Korean where “smell” readily grammaticalizes into “this seems fishy.”

The point of the ecology is that these routes are not interchangeable: “negative” is not one thing. Each route has different triggers, constructions, and social functions.

- **Assumptions:** Lexical change is structured; stable extensions leave constructional and register traces.
- **Logic:** Different sensory anchors preferentially map into different endpoints (emotion vs reputation vs character).
- **Implications:** A robust theory of “dirty” needs both a continuum and a typology.
- **What would falsify it:** Large corpora showing no systematic route differences in collocations/targets/registers.

### Cross–Sinosphere comparison supports “mechanism-level isomorphism, carrier-level mismatch”

When we extended beyond Chinese to Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese, a pattern emerged: the **institutional triangle** (uncleanliness/purity management – shame – stigma/infamy) can be reconstructed across languages, but the **lexical carriers** differ. Chinese uniquely welds some olfactory material into shame (臊→害臊) and into historiographic reputation chains (臭→遺臭/臭名). Japanese/Korean often route olfaction more strongly into suspicion (“fishy”) while allocating stigma to “汚名/오명” type constructions. Vietnamese preserves the triangle strongly through Sino-Vietnamese strata (不潔/不淨/汙名/羞恥-type resources), even without Chinese-character writing as a daily system.

This made the comparative conclusion sharper: what travels across the region is the **mapping mechanism** (how societies make bodies police morals), while which word family “gets the job” is historically contingent.

- **Assumptions:** Prestige registers (ritual, religion, law, formal writing) stabilize normative vocabulary.
- **Logic:** If the triangle exists across languages but with different carriers, isomorphism is functional rather than lexical.
- **Implications:** Translation should preserve mapping type (shame-route vs stigma-route), not just polarity.
- **What would falsify it:** Evidence that carriers align one-to-one across languages, or that some corners of the triangle are missing/unstable.

### Major takeaways

Methodologically, the conversation’s deliverable is a reusable analytic toolkit: (a) **core niche / extrapolation direction**, (b) **diachronic sequencing by earliest abstract attestation**, (c) **construction chains** (especially for 臭), and (d) a **cross-domain typology** to prevent collapsing all negative evaluations into one bucket. Conceptually, the strongest “hinges” were **臊** (a rare smell→shame weld in Chinese) and **臭** (a historiographic-to-pragmatic chain), while institutional vocabularies (Buddhist/ritual purity systems) explain why the normative end becomes stable and highly extensible.

Develop the “臊” branching: outward condemnation vs inward shame/embarrassment (害臊). Add the psychological model explicitly (shame vs guilt; face/visibility dynamics) as an explanatory layer for why this branch is “sticky” in interactional contexts and how it differs from guilt-repair scripts.

### 5\. Route II — Smell → Reputation: 臭 and the Historiographic Construction Chain

Lay out the chain: 流芳↔遺臭 → 臭名 → 臭罵, emphasizing constructional portability (elite evaluative templates becoming reusable) and pragmatic downshifting (into everyday insult intensity). Connect to modern stigmatizing speech as “contamination rhetoric” in public moralization.

### 6\. Route III — Form/Appearance → Character → Uncleanliness: 齷齪 and Character Contamination

Treat 齷齪 as a different sensory anchor (visual/bodily form). Show the typical moralization trajectory: constrained form → constrained character → moral/physical uncleanliness. Compare with competing items (骯髒/邋遢/腌臢) to show niche partitioning (messiness vs baseness vs hypocrisy vs sordidness).

### 7\. Purity Regimes as Social Infrastructure: A Religion/Anthropology Account of 淨/穢, Taboos, and Ritual Repair

Make this a full-on religion/anthropology section. Start from the claim that “dirty” becomes governable when embedded in systems of **classification + taboo + repair**. Bring in classic purity/pollution theory (e.g., **Mary Douglas** and later ritual/process approaches like **Victor Turner** as interpretive scaffolding), then ground it in East Asian institutional cases: Buddhist vinaya-style purity management, state/ritual regimes, Shinto-style kegare logics, Korean folk/ritual impurity and avoidance, Vietnamese Buddhist “not-pure” discourse. Emphasize that impurity terms are not merely descriptors but **operators**: they trigger procedural consequences, authorize exclusion, and define remediation (purification, abstention, confession, expiation, ritual washing).

Close by explicitly tying this back to lexicon stabilization: institutions create repeated contexts that “train” stable collocations and constructions, making impurity vocabulary reproductively durable across centuries.

### 8\. Diachronic Semantics and Construction Grammar: Testing Isomorphism, Carrier Mismatch, and the Thai Stress-Test

Now switch to linguistics mode. Re-state the typology as hypotheses about **constructional pathways**: how sensory nouns/adjectives become evaluative predicates, how binaries become templates, how “污名/汚名/오명/ô danh” behave as a cross-language stigma construction family, and how “臭/臭い/…/mùi…” distribute into (i) reputational condemnation vs (ii) suspicion/risk intuition. Present the “isomorphism matrix” as a linguistic object: mechanism-level similarity vs carrier-level mismatch. Then specify what counts as evidence: earliest attestations, frequency shifts by register, collocation signatures, productivity of templates (X+臭, 汚名をそそぐ-type repair frames, etc.), and network measures (semantic clustering, competition/displacement).

End with the Thai stress-test framed linguistically: can the institutional triangle be reconstructed via non-Chinese prestige strata (often Pali/Sanskrit-derived lexicon), and what does that imply about universals vs contact-specific pathways? The point is to separate “shared mechanism” from “shared etymological carrier.”

## Section 1 (English)

## Why “Dirty” Is Not One Concept: From Continuum to Ecology

Everyday speech makes “dirty” feel like a simple idea. Something is clean, then it becomes dirty, and if we push the word harder it starts to sound moral: dirty jokes, dirty money, dirty politics. This slide from grime to blame is so familiar that it tempts us to treat “dirty” as a single scale—more dirt, more disgust, more immorality. The problem is that languages do not actually behave as if there were only one kind of “dirty.” They distribute the labor across multiple words, multiple registers, and multiple social purposes. What looks like one concept from a distance turns out, up close, to be a small ecosystem.

This review adopts a “semantic ecology” approach. The metaphor is practical rather than poetic: words occupy niches, compete with near-neighbors, specialize under pressure, and sometimes colonize new semantic territory. Dirt vocabulary is ideal for this because it sits at the intersection of three domains that constantly borrow from each other: sensory experience (smell, grime, bodily fluids), social order (taboo, purity rules, hygiene regimes), and moral evaluation (shame, stigma, condemnation). Once these domains are in contact, it becomes easy for a term that began as a bodily description to turn into an operator of social judgment.

A one-dimensional continuum struggles to capture this. Consider three negative statements that feel related, yet do different work. “The kitchen is dirty” describes a physical state and invites cleaning. “That was a dirty trick” condemns a method and invites blame. “He has a dirty reputation” targets social standing and invites avoidance. These uses do not simply differ in intensity; they differ in implied causes, appropriate remedies, and social consequences. The same language user can move among them effortlessly because the surrounding norms tell us what “dirty” is doing in that moment.

The ecology perspective helps because it makes room for functional differentiation. It encourages us to ask: which terms are built for material grime, which are built for social contamination, and which thrive as moral weapons? In Chinese, for example, everyday words for uncleanliness coexist with vocabularies of institutional impurity, odor-coded disgust, characterological “sordidness,” and self-conscious embarrassment. They overlap, but they are not redundant. They partition the semantic space in ways that reflect everyday life, ritual discipline, social surveillance, and the management of face.

To keep the ecological metaphor analytically sharp, this review uses two simple instruments throughout. First is the idea of a core niche: the least context-dependent job a word reliably performs. Second is an extrapolation direction: the characteristic route by which a word expands into new domains, especially from sensory description into normative evaluation. Together, they let us compare items without collapsing them into a mere list of synonyms. A term whose core niche is “physical filth” and whose extrapolation direction is “filth → moral condemnation” behaves differently from a term whose core niche is “odor” and whose extrapolation direction is “odor → suspicion,” even if both can be translated as “dirty” in some contexts.

Institutions matter here as much as cognition. Dirt vocabulary becomes especially stable when it is repeatedly required in rule-governed situations. Ritual and disciplinary systems create recurring trigger conditions (“contact with X,” “entering Y state”), predictable consequences (avoidance, exclusion, abstention), and prescribed remedies (purification, repair, expiation). These repeated scenarios act like a training environment for language: they encourage fixed collocations, conventional scripts, and portable constructions. By contrast, purely conversational insults can shift faster, because their enforcement is informal and their prestige cycles quickly. Many “dirty” words occupy both worlds at once, which is precisely why their histories are so revealing.

The educational payoff of this framing is straightforward. It prevents a common analytical mistake: treating all negative vocabulary as the same kind of negativity. Disgust, shame, stigma, and condemnation can travel together, but they are not interchangeable. When we later examine odor terms that branch into embarrassment, or reputation terms that crystallize into historiographic templates, we will need a model that distinguishes pathways, not just degrees.

The tables below present the conceptual equipment that will be used in later sections.

| Modeling stance | What it emphasizes | What it explains well | Typical blind spot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Continuum (“more dirty → more immoral”) | intensity, scalar judgments | quick comparisons, rhetorical force | route differences (emotion vs reputation vs character), register stability, why some words become templates |
| Semantic ecology (niches + routes + pressures) | functional differentiation, competition, migration | specialization, cross-domain transfer, institutional stabilization | requires structure; needs corpus checks for claims about “strength” |

With these tools in hand, the rest of the article will treat “dirty” not as a single slider but as a structured system. We will first build the lexical habitat, then trace major cross-domain routes (odor to emotion, odor to reputation, form to character), and finally show how institutional purity regimes stabilize the normative edge while cross-language comparison reveals shared mechanisms alongside local specializations.

## 第一節（繁體中文）

## 為何「髒」不是單一概念：從連續譜到語義生態系

日常語言很容易讓「髒」看起來是一個簡單概念：乾淨變髒，然後詞義一加重，就開始帶道德色彩——髒笑話、髒錢、髒政治。這種從污垢滑向譴責的路徑太熟悉了，以至於人會自然地把「髒」想成一條刻度：越髒越噁心，越噁心越不道德。但語言實際上的運作方式並不支持這種單線模型。不同語言往往把「髒」的工作拆分給多個詞、不同語域、以及不同的社會用途。遠看像一個概念，近看更像一個小型生態系。

本綜述採用「語義生態系」的取徑。這個比喻的目的不是文學修辭，而是分析上的便利：詞彙會佔據棲位、與近義詞競爭、在社會壓力下專門化，甚至跨域“入侵”新的語義領地。髒的詞彙特別適合做這種分析，因為它恰好位於三個高度互滲的領域交界：感官經驗（氣味、污垢、體液）、社會秩序（禁忌、淨穢規則、衛生治理）、以及道德評價（羞恥、污名、譴責）。一旦這三者緊密接觸，原本只是描述身體狀態的詞，就很容易變成執行社會判斷的工具。

單一連續譜之所以不夠，是因為它難以處理功能差異。想想三句聽起來都“負面”的話：「廚房很髒」主要在描述物理狀態，推向清理；「手段很髒」是在譴責方法，推向責備；「名聲很髒（有污點）」是在處理社會評價，推向迴避與排除。這些用法不只是強度不同，而是隱含原因、補救方式、以及社會後果都不同。使用者之所以能自然切換，是因為語境中的規範與期待告訴我們此刻「髒」在做什麼工作。

生態系視角能把這些工作拆開來看。它鼓勵我們提出更精準的問題：哪些詞擅長描述物質污垢，哪些詞擅長標記社會污染，哪些詞最容易長成道德武器？以中文為例，日常不潔詞彙與制度化淨穢詞彙並存，嗅覺厭惡詞群、人格髒污詞群、自我羞恥詞群也各有資源。它們彼此重疊，但並非冗餘；它們在語義空間中形成分工，反映的是日常生活、儀式紀律、社會監看與面子管理的不同需求。

為了讓“生態系”不只是一個漂亮比喻，本文會反覆使用兩個簡單而可操作的分析工具。第一是「核心棲位」：某個詞最不依賴語境、最穩定的本職工作。第二是「外推方向」：它最常見的跨域走向，尤其是從感官/身體描述走向規範/道德評價。這兩個工具使得比較不會退化成近義詞堆疊：一個核心棲位在“物理污垢”、外推方向偏“污垢→道德譴責”的詞，和一個核心棲位在“氣味”、外推方向偏“氣味→可疑直覺”的詞，即便有時都可被粗略翻成“髒”，它們在系統裡的行為也截然不同。

制度因素在這裡和認知同等重要。髒的詞彙之所以能穩定，往往因為它被反覆需要於規則化場景。宗教或禮制的淨穢系統會持續生成可預期的流程：什麼算觸發（接觸某物/進入某狀態）、會造成什麼後果（避忌、隔離、禁入）、以及該如何補救（禊、祓、齊戒、淨化、修復）。這些反覆出現的腳手架像是一個語言訓練環境，會促成固定搭配、慣例腳本、可搬運構式，使某些不潔詞彙跨世代保持生命力。相反地，純口語辱罵的變動通常更快，因為它的社會制裁更非正式，也更受流行循環影響。許多“髒”詞彙同時棲居兩個世界，這也正是它們最值得追蹤的原因。

這種框架的教學價值很直接：它避免把所有負面詞都視為同一種負面。厭惡、羞恥、污名、譴責可能會同時出現、彼此借力，但它們不是可以互換的同義品。後文討論嗅覺詞如何分岔成害臊，或名聲詞如何結晶成史評模板時，需要的不是一把“更髒”的尺，而是一張能辨識路徑的地圖。

## Section 2 (English)

## Method: “Core Niche / Extrapolation Direction” as a Comparative Instrument

A review article that claims “dirty” vocabulary forms an ecology needs more than clever metaphors. It needs a method that can be applied consistently across items, across time, and across languages. This section presents the working instrument used throughout the paper: identify each term’s **core niche** (its least context-dependent job), then map its **extrapolation direction** (its characteristic pathways of semantic expansion), while tracking the **registers** and **constructions** that make those expansions socially real. The aim is not to freeze a word into a single definition; it is to describe how it behaves as a tool in a community.

### 2.1 Core niche: finding the “home job” of a word

The core niche is the meaning that remains stable when you strip away the most specialized contexts. Practically, it is the sense that can survive in short, low-context utterances without sounding odd. For “dirty” vocabulary, core niches often cluster into a few broad zones: physical grime, odor, institutional impurity, and character evaluation. Identifying the core niche matters because it anchors comparison: two words may both be negative, but one may be “about bodies” while the other is “about reputations.”

A simple diagnostic is to test the word in minimal frames:

- **Object-state frame:** “X is \_\_\_.” (kitchen, clothes, hands)
- **Person-evaluation frame:** “He/She is \_\_\_.”
- **Method frame:** “That was a \_\_\_ trick/act.”
- **Institutional frame:** “This is \_\_\_ / leads to \_\_\_; it requires \_\_\_.” (taboo, purification, prohibition)

The frames are not truth conditions; they are probes for typicality. If a term feels natural mainly in object-state frames, its core niche is likely material. If it feels natural mainly as a person evaluation, its core niche likely includes character.

### 2.2 Extrapolation direction: mapping predictable routes of extension

Extrapolation direction describes how a word tends to travel from its home job into other domains. Many expansions begin as contextual metaphors (“moral stain”), then become repeatable scripts, and finally harden into productive patterns. For our topic, a small set of routes recurs across items:

- **Material grime → moral condemnation** (“dirty money,” “dirty politics”)
- **Odor → suspicion/risk intuition** (“something smells fishy”)
- **Odor → reputation history** (infamy templates, “stink of…”)
- **Impurity (ritual) → social exclusion** (avoidance, taboo)
- **Messiness → character baseness** (sordid, petty, mean)

The key methodological move is to treat these as **routes**, not as a single slide toward “more negative.” Routes have different endpoints, and they recruit different linguistic machinery.

### 2.3 Register tracking: where meanings live and how they stabilize

Register is not an afterthought. A word’s expansion becomes durable when it is repeatedly used in a stable social context. For “dirty” lexicons, the important registers include:

- **Ritual/disciplinary:** taboo, purity rules, prescribed repairs
- **Historiographic/elite evaluation:** reputation templates, binary judgments
- **Everyday vernacular:** household hygiene, interpersonal insults
- **Modern public discourse:** media moralization, stigma politics
- **Religious practice:** impurity triggers and remediation scripts

Tracking register does two things. It prevents category errors (e.g., reading an institutional operator as if it were a casual insult), and it provides an explanation for stability: ritual systems reproduce usage contexts across generations, while slang cycles faster.

### 2.4 Constructional diagnostics: when a meaning becomes a reusable tool

Words often become influential when they enter **constructions** —semi-fixed patterns that can be reused with new content. A “dirty” term that becomes part of a template can scale socially in a way a free-floating adjective cannot. Constructional diagnostics ask:

- Does the item appear in **fixed phrases** with conventional meaning?
- Does it participate in a **productive pattern** (X + “dirty”, “dirty + N”)?
- Does it support **repair frames** (wash away stigma, cleanse impurity, restore name)?
- Does it form **binary templates** (fragrance vs stench; purity vs impurity)?

In the later sections, “臭” and “污名/汚名/오명/ô danh” are treated not merely as words but as constructional hubs that permit chaining, intensification, and institutional uptake.

### 2.5 Diachronic protocol: attestation, branching, and route timing

A historical argument needs a disciplined approach to time. The basic protocol here is:

1. **Earliest attestation of the core niche.** Identify the earliest clear examples of the home job.
2. **First appearance of each extrapolation route.** Locate the earliest instances where the word clearly operates in a new domain.
3. **Branching points.** When a route splits (e.g., odor → shame vs odor → condemnation), treat each branch as separate and track its earliest secure evidence.
4. **Register migration.** Note when an expansion moves from elite texts to everyday interaction, or from ritual texts to general public discourse.
5. **Stabilization markers.** Look for repeated collocations, idioms, and productive templates as signals that the route has hardened.

In practice, “earliest” is always “earliest found so far under defined search conditions.” This is why the method insists on transparent corpora, query patterns, and versioned notes.

### 2.6 A reusable worksheet for each lexical item

The tables below provide a worksheet format that can be applied to any term in the ecology. The goal is consistency: the same questions asked of 污, 垢, 髒, 穢, 臊, 臭, 齷齪, and their cross-language analogues.

| 4 | What constructions does it join? | idioms, templates, repair frames | predicts productivity |
| 5 | What are the earliest attestations per route? | dated/periodized citations | supports diachronic claims |
| 6 | What are near-neighbors and competitors? | substitution tests + niche partitioning | models ecological pressure |

### 2.7 Minimal evidence standards for claims of “strength”

Because later sections compare routes across languages, we need a minimal standard for calling a mapping “strong” or “weak.” This article treats a route as stronger when it shows several of the following: broad register spread, stable collocations, productive templates, and persistence across time. It treats a route as weaker when it appears only as occasional poetic metaphor, highly local slang, or isolated quotations without pattern.

The method, then, is a disciplined way to turn interpretive insight into testable structure. It keeps the ecology model honest: every claim about a route can, in principle, be checked against attestation and constructional evidence, and every comparison across languages can be grounded in comparable diagnostics rather than loose translation.

## 方法論：用「核心棲位／外推方向」作為可比較的分析工具

若要主張「髒」詞彙是一個語義生態系，光靠比喻是不夠的；必須有一套可重複、可跨詞、可跨時代、甚至可跨語言操作的方法。本節提出本文反覆使用的分析工具：先確定每個詞的 **核心棲位** （最不依賴語境的本職工作），再描繪它的 **外推方向** （典型的語義擴張路徑），同時追蹤支撐這些擴張的 **語域** 與 **構式** 。重點不在於替詞下唯一正確定義，而在於描述它如何在社群中被當作一個可用的工具。

### 2.1 核心棲位：找出詞的「本職工作」

核心棲位指的是：當你拿掉最專門、最依賴情境的使用場合後，仍能保持穩定的那個義項。就髒的詞彙而言，核心棲位常聚集在幾個區域：物理污垢、氣味、制度化不潔（淨穢/禁忌）、以及人格評價。辨認核心棲位的價值在於，它為比較提供錨點：兩個詞都帶負面，但一個可能主要處理“身體/物質”，另一個主要處理“名聲/人格”。

操作上可以用「最小框架測試」來探針典型性：

- **物件狀態框架：** 「X 很 \_\_\_。」（廚房、衣服、手）
- **人物評價框架：** 「他/她很 \_\_\_。」
- **方法手段框架：** 「那是很 \_\_\_ 的手段/作法。」
- **制度語境框架：** 「這屬於 \_\_\_；會導致 \_\_\_；需要 \_\_\_。」（避忌、淨化、禁入）

這些測試不是在判真假，而是在看“自然度”：若某詞最自然地落在物件狀態框架，核心棲位多半偏物質；若它更自然地用來評價人或手段，核心棲位就更靠近人格/道德端。

### 2.2 外推方向：描繪可預測的跨域路徑

外推方向描述詞如何從本職工作移動到其他領域。許多擴張起初只是情境化的隱喻（如“道德污點”），但若反覆被使用，就可能形成腳本，進一步硬化為可生產的模式。就本文主題而言，一些常見路徑會反覆出現：

- **物理污垢 → 道德譴責** （髒錢、髒政治）
- **氣味 → 可疑/風險直覺** （聞起來不對勁）
- **氣味 → 名聲史評模板** （惡名、遺臭型的定讞）
- **制度化不潔 → 社會排除** （避忌、禁入、隔離）
- **凌亂/難堪 → 人格卑劣** （卑鄙、下作、齷齪一類）

方法上的關鍵是：把它們當作“路徑”，而不是同一條“越來越負面”的滑坡。不同路徑有不同終點，也會動員不同的語言機械（搭配、慣用語、模板、修復框架）。

### 2.3 語域追蹤：意義住在哪裡、如何被固定

語域不是附註，而是解釋的一部分。詞義擴張之所以能持久，常因為它被反覆需要於穩定的社會情境。髒的詞彙尤其要關注以下語域：

- \*\*儀式/紀律語域：\*\*禁忌、淨穢規則、補救程序
- \*\*史評/精英評價語域：\*\*名聲模板、二元定讞
- \*\*日常白話：\*\*家務衛生、互動辱罵
- \*\*現代公共語篇：\*\*媒體道德化、污名政治
- \*\*宗教實作語境：\*\*觸發條件與修復腳本（齊戒、禊祓、懺悔等）

語域追蹤一方面避免誤讀（把制度操作符當成口語辱罵來理解），另一方面提供穩定性的機制解釋：儀式系統能跨世代重複生成使用場景，因此更容易固定搭配與構式；口語流行語則更易漂移。

### 2.4 構式診斷：何時詞變成可搬運的社會工具

很多詞真正變得有力，是因為它們進入了 **構式** ：半固定、可重複套用的模式。一個能進入模板的髒詞，擴散能力往往比一個只作自由形容詞的詞強。構式診斷會問：

- 它是否出現在 **固定語/慣用語** ，且意義約定俗成？
- 它是否參與 **可生產模式** （例如 X+某詞、某詞+名詞）？
- 它是否支援 **修復框架** （洗刷污名、淨化不潔、恢復名譽）？
- 它是否形成 **二元模板** （芳/臭、淨/穢一類）？

在後續章節中，像“臭”的鏈條與“污名/汚名/오명/ô danh”的跨語家族，都會被當作構式樞紐來處理，而不只是單一詞義。

### 2.5 歷時操作流程：例證、分岔、路徑定時

歷史語義的論證需要一套可複現的時間處理方式。本文採取的基本流程如下：

1. \*\*核心棲位最早例證：\*\*先確定本職義最早可證的用法。
2. \*\*各外推路徑的首次出現：\*\*找出清楚跨域運作的最早例子。
3. \*\*分岔點：\*\*路徑一旦分裂（如氣味→羞恥 vs 氣味→譴責），每一枝獨立追蹤。
4. \*\*語域遷移：\*\*注意擴張何時從精英文本下沉到日常互動，或從宗教/禮制語境外溢到公共語篇。
5. \*\*穩定化指標：\*\*用固定搭配、慣用語、模板生產力作為“硬化”的信號。

“最早”在實務上永遠是“在明確搜尋條件下目前找到的最早”。因此本文強調透明的語料來源、檢索式與可追溯的筆記版本。

因為後文會做跨語比較，需要一個最低標準判定某條映射是“強”還是“弱”。本文傾向把一條路徑視為更強，若它同時呈現多項特徵：跨語域分布廣、固定搭配穩定、模板可生產、並在時間上持續。相對地，若某路徑只零星出現於詩化比喻、地方性俚語、或孤立引文而缺乏模式，它就更像弱路徑。

## Section 3 (English)

## The Lexical Habitat: Inventory, Competition, and Niche Partitioning

Before tracing the major “dirty” routes—odor to emotion, odor to reputation, form to character—we need a map of the terrain itself. A semantic ecology begins with habitat: which items exist, where they tend to live (register and genre), what they readily modify, and how they divide labor. The goal of this section is not to treat the lexicon as a bag of synonyms, but to show how a community’s vocabulary organizes recurring problems: cleaning matter, regulating bodies, describing sensory aversion, policing moral boundaries, and stylizing insults.

A useful first move is to group the inventory by **core niche** rather than by dictionary gloss. In the Chinese “dirty” habitat, five clusters are especially instructive.

### 3.1 Cluster A: Physical uncleanliness and contamination (basic household ecology)

Words like **污、垢、髒** anchor the everyday end of the field. They thrive in low-context settings and attach easily to objects, surfaces, liquids, and clothing. Their strength is flexibility: they can describe a state (“dirty”), a substance (“filth”), or a result (“stained”). Their weakness, ecologically speaking, is that their broadness invites competition: when a speaker wants a more specific kind of “dirty,” specialized items rush in to do the job.

Within this cluster, niche partitioning often tracks *material vs residue*. **垢** tends to evoke stubborn residue or crust—something adhered, accumulated, and difficult to remove. **污** can feel more like “contamination” or “taint,” often with a sense of defilement or stain. **髒** is the everyday generalist, the term that can cover both the kitchen sink and a person’s “dirty hands” in a moralized frame. Even without committing to fixed boundaries, we can already see the ecological logic: generalists provide coverage; specialists provide precision.

### 3.2 Cluster B: Institutional impurity and normative operators (ritual and disciplinary habitats)

A second cluster includes **穢、污穢**, and related institutional phrases such as **觸穢/污觸**. These items are not merely descriptive; they are often used as operators that imply rules: what counts as contact, what consequences follow, what repairs are needed. This cluster has high stability because it is anchored in repeatable institutional contexts (ritual practice, religious discipline, formal moral discourse). The core niche is not “dirt on an object” but “a condition that reorders social permissions.”

Importantly, this cluster can coexist with modern hygiene talk without collapsing into it. Institutional impurity is about boundary management—who may enter, touch, eat, officiate, or participate—whereas hygiene is about health and cleanliness. The overlap is real, but the ecological niches differ, and speakers often maintain the difference through register.

### 3.3 Cluster C: Odor and food-coded disgust (olfactory habitat)

The olfactory zone is densely populated because smell is an efficient social signal: it is immediate, visceral, and hard to argue with. Here we find **臭、臊、腥、羶/膻**. These terms readily modify bodies, foods, spaces, and sometimes social atmospheres. Odor words are prime candidates for cross-domain expansion because they already carry an evaluation component: a smell is rarely “neutral” in ordinary talk. Even when odor is literal, it pulls attention toward the relationship between the body and the social world (who is tolerable to be near, who is offensive, what is “safe” to consume).

This cluster will later supply two major bridges: odor to shame/emotion (especially with 臊 and 害臊) and odor to reputation (臭名, 遺臭). It also supports a quieter but pervasive bridge: odor to suspicion (“something smells off”), which becomes particularly visible in cross-language comparison.

### 3.4 Cluster D: Mess, neglect, and “low-class” grime (squalor habitat)

Words like **腌臢、邋遢** live in a habitat that is partly physical and partly social: messy spaces, poor upkeep, sloppy presentation, and the implied judgments that follow. These terms do not merely report dirt; they often index neglect, disorder, or a lack of self-management. Their typical targets are environments (rooms, kitchens), bodies (appearance), and lifestyles. This gives them a distinct niche from “污/髒” because they imply a narrative: dirt as a symptom of carelessness or degradation.

### 3.5 Cluster E: Character contamination and moral smallness (personhood habitat)

Finally, we have items whose home job is not physical at all, even if they can be metaphorically linked to it. **齷齪** and **骯髒** often function as person-evaluation terms, condemning someone as sordid, ignoble, or morally unclean. The path from “unclean” to “untrustworthy” is not automatic; it is cultivated by social norms and frequently stabilized by recurring moral discourse (accusations, denunciation genres, political rhetoric).

This cluster is where “dirty” becomes a tool of social sorting. The ecological stakes are higher: calling a room dirty invites cleaning; calling a person “dirty” invites exclusion, distrust, or contempt. The lexicon reflects that difference by maintaining specialized terms for character attack.

### 3.6 Competition: how near-neighbors divide labor

In an ecology, near-neighbors cannot remain perfectly synonymous for long. They either split into niches or one displaces the other in some register. For the Chinese “dirty” habitat, three axes are especially important for explaining partitioning:

1. **Material state vs moral operator:** is the term about grime, or about permission and defilement?
2. **Literal sensory trigger vs evaluative template:** does it require an odor/grime trigger, or can it be used abstractly?
3. **Object-focused vs person-focused:** is the default target a thing, a place, a practice, or a person?

The table below organizes the inventory as an ecological map. It is not a dictionary; it is a guide to typical habitats and likely bridges.

| Cluster (habitat) | Core niche (typical “home job”) | Typical targets | Likely bridge(s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 污 / 垢 / 髒 | physical uncleanliness, stain, residue | objects, surfaces, clothes; sometimes actions | grime → moral condemnation; stain → reputation |
| 穢 / 污穢 / 觸穢 | institutional impurity; norm operator | bodies, events, spaces, ritual states | impurity → exclusion; impurity → moral order |
| 臭 / 臊 / 腥 / 羶(膻) | odor/disgust coding | bodies, foods, spaces; atmospheres | odor → shame; odor → reputation; odor → suspicion |
| 腌臢 / 邋遢 | squalor, neglect, disordered presentation | rooms, appearance, lifestyle | disorder → character judgment |
| 齷齪 / 骯髒 | personhood contamination; sordidness | persons, motives, methods | character → moral stain; person → exclusion |

A final methodological note: this habitat map is intentionally provisional. Later sections will sharpen it by examining constructions, historical branching, and register migration. Even at this stage, however, the ecological perspective reveals why “dirty” vocabulary is so productive: it provides multiple handles for managing different social problems. Where hygiene meets taboo, where disgust meets shame, where stains become reputations, and where messy rooms become moralizing stories about people—there the lexicon multiplies.

## 第三節（繁體中文）

## 詞彙棲地：全量清單、競爭關係與棲位分工

在追蹤主要跨域路徑（嗅覺→情緒、嗅覺→名聲、形貌→性格）之前，需要先把整個地形畫出來。語義生態系首先要有“棲地圖”：有哪些詞，通常住在哪些語域與體裁裡，最容易修飾哪些對象，彼此如何分工，何時互相擠壓、互相取代。本節的目的不是把詞彙當成同義詞堆，而是展示一個社群如何用詞彙來處理反覆出現的問題：清理物質、規範身體邊界、表達感官厭惡、執行道德秩序、以及風格化的辱罵。

一個有效的起手式，是按「核心棲位」來分群，而不是按字典英譯或單一“髒”的泛義。在中文的髒詞彙棲地中，至少有五個群落特別值得分開看。

### 3.1 群落A：物理不潔與污染（家務型棲地）

**污、垢、髒** 是這個語場的日常錨點，低語境就能運作，最容易黏在物件、表面、液體、衣物上。它們的優勢在於彈性：可以描述狀態、物質、或結果；它們的生態弱點也在於過度寬泛，因此一旦說話者需要更精準的“髒”，其他更專門的詞就會湧入搶位。

在這個群落內部，分工往往沿著“物質狀態 vs 附著殘留”展開。 **垢** 容易喚起頑固附著的污垢、積累的殘渣； **污** 更像“沾染、污染、玷污”的語感，帶有“被弄髒/被污損”的方向性； **髒** 則是最常用的泛化詞，既能說廚房很髒，也能在道德化語境裡指“髒手”。即便不需要劃死邊界，這種“泛化者提供覆蓋、專門者提供精準”的生態邏輯已很清楚。

### 3.2 群落B：制度化淨穢與規範操作符（儀式/紀律棲地）

第二個群落包括 **穢、污穢** ，以及像 **觸穢/污觸** 這種偏制度語境的搭配。它們往往不只是描述，而是帶出規則：什麼算接觸、會導致什麼後果、如何補救。這個群落之所以穩，是因為它依附於可重複的制度情境（宗教實作、禮制紀律、正式道德語體）。它的核心棲位不是“物件上有髒”，而更像“某種狀態改寫了社會允許的邊界”。

這個群落與現代衛生話語可以共存而不必合併。淨穢制度主要處理的是邊界管理：誰可以進入、接觸、食用、主持、參與；而衛生話語主要處理健康與清潔。兩者會重疊，但棲位不同，說話者也常透過語域差異維持這個區別。

### 3.3 群落C：嗅覺與食物式厭惡（氣味棲地）

嗅覺區域詞彙密度高，因為氣味是高效的社會訊號：立即、強烈、難以辯駁。此處包含 **臭、臊、腥、羶/膻** 等。它們容易修飾身體、食物、空間，也能修飾某種社會氛圍。嗅覺詞特別容易跨域外推，因為它們即使在字面用法裡也常自帶評價色彩：日常說“臭”通常不只是中性描述，而是帶出拒斥、距離與警覺。嗅覺把身體與社會關係綁在一起：誰能靠近、誰令人不適、什麼可入口、什麼讓人退避。

這個群落稍後會提供兩條重要橋接：臊與害臊牽動的“嗅覺→情緒”，以及臭名、遺臭牽動的“嗅覺→名聲”。此外，它也支撐一條常被忽略但很普遍的橋接：嗅覺→可疑（聞起來不對勁），跨語比較時會更顯眼。

### 3.4 群落D：凌亂、怠惰與“低階”髒亂（邋遢棲地）

**腌臢、邋遢** 住在一個半物理、半社會的棲地：空間凌亂、維護不良、外表潦草，以及隨之而來的價值判斷。這些詞不只在報告“有髒”，而常在指向“失序/怠惰/缺乏自我管理”。它們常落在環境（房間、廚房）、身體呈現（儀容）、與生活方式上，因而與“污/髒”這類泛化不潔詞形成清楚分工：前者更像帶敘事的髒（髒作為某種生活失序的症狀）。

### 3.5 群落E：人格污染與道德卑小（人物棲地）

最後是一組核心棲位偏“人”而非偏“物”的詞。 **齷齪、骯髒** 常用作人格評價，指人卑劣、下作、道德不潔。從“不潔”到“不可信”的連結並非自動，而是由社會規範不斷栽培，並在指控、譴責、政治修辭等語類中被穩定化。

這個群落把“髒”變成社會排序工具。稱一個房間髒，通常邀請清理；稱一個人髒，則更常邀請排除、猜忌、鄙視。詞彙棲地之所以需要專門詞，就是因為社會後果不同。

### 3.6 競爭與分工：近鄰如何避免長期同義

在生態系裡，近鄰很難長期保持完全同義；要嘛分工，要嘛某個語域裡被取代。中文的髒詞彙棲地，至少有三條重要分工軸：

1. \*\*物質狀態 vs 規範操作符：\*\*這個詞是在說污垢，還是在改寫允許/不允許？
2. \*\*字面感官觸發 vs 可抽象模板：\*\*必須真的有臭/髒才用，還是可直接抽象指控？
3. \*\*物件中心 vs 人物中心：\*\*默認修飾物、空間、行為，還是默認評價人與動機？

下表把本文的“棲地圖”做成可用的導覽，而非字典定義。它也提示後文可能出現的橋接方向。

| 群落（棲地） | 核心棲位（典型本職） | 常見修飾對象 | 可能橋接方向 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 污 / 垢 / 髒 | 物理不潔、沾染、殘留 | 物件、表面、衣物；亦可指行為 | 污垢→道德譴責；沾染→名聲污點 |
| 穢 / 污穢 / 觸穢 | 制度化不潔；規範操作符 | 身體、事件、空間、儀式狀態 | 不潔→排除；不潔→道德秩序 |
| 臭 / 臊 / 腥 / 羶(膻) | 氣味與厭惡編碼 | 身體、食物、空間、氛圍 | 氣味→情緒；氣味→名聲；氣味→可疑 |
| 腌臢 / 邋遢 | 髒亂、失序、怠惰式髒 | 房間、外表、生活方式 | 失序→人格評價 |
| 齷齪 / 骯髒 | 人格污染；下作卑劣 | 人、動機、手段 | 性格→道德污點；人物→排除 |

最後補一點方法上的提醒：這張棲地圖刻意保留“可修正性”。後文會用構式、歷時分岔、語域遷移把它變得更尖銳。但即便在此階段，生態系視角已揭示髒詞彙為何如此高產：它為不同的社會問題提供不同把手。衛生與禁忌相遇、厭惡與羞恥串聯、污點變成名聲、凌亂變成關於人的道德敘事——在這些交界處，詞彙自然增殖並分工。

## Section 4 (English)

## Route I — Smell → Emotion: 臊 as a Bifurcation Engine (Odor → Ugliness / Odor → Shame)

Few items in the Chinese “dirty” ecology are as structurally revealing as **臊**. At first glance it looks like a straightforward odor word: a sharp, animal, bodily smell, often unpleasant. Yet in modern usage its most conspicuous descendant is not a smell at all but a feeling: **害臊**, “to be embarrassed / ashamed.” This pairing is not just a cute lexical accident. It is a window into how sensory vocabulary can split into two divergent social functions—one outward-facing (condemnation, ugliness, taint), the other inward-facing (self-conscious emotion, face threat). In ecological terms, 臊 does not merely “extend” from smell to shame; it forks into two habitats and becomes productive in both.

### 4.1 The sensory base: what kind of smell is 臊?

臊 is not a generic “stinky.” It tends to evoke a bodily, animal, or fatty scent—something that clings, rises, and feels intrusive. This specificity matters because the more embodied and intimate a sensation is, the more easily it can be recruited into social boundary work. Smell is already a distance-regulating sense: it tells you who is safe to approach, what is edible, what is decaying, what is “out of place.” When a word encodes a bodily odor, it arrives preloaded with social implications about proximity and exposure.

From that base, the ecology supports two extrapolation routes:

1. **Odor → ugliness/taint (other-directed evaluation).**
2. **Odor → shame/embarrassment (self-directed emotion).**

The first treats odor as a property of an object or person that can be judged, mocked, or condemned. The second treats odor as a metaphorical model for the feeling of being exposed: you “give off” something socially unacceptable, and others can “sense” it.

### 4.2 Branch A: Odor → ugliness/taint (condemnation as sensory verdict)

In this branch, 臊 aligns with the broader “dirty” machinery that turns sensory repulsion into social repulsion. The implied logic is simple: what reeks is what should be distanced from. Because odor is experienced as intrusive and contagious (it spreads through air; it enters your body through breathing), it becomes a persuasive resource for condemnation. Speakers can frame a target as not merely wrong but contaminating.

Linguistically, this branch often appears in evaluative descriptions where the odor sense remains available, even if partially bleached. It clusters with talk about bodies, foods, and spaces, but can also be extended to actions or styles as “vulgar,” “crude,” or “disgusting” in a way that feels bodily rather than abstract.

The ecological point is that this branch competes with other condemnation-capable items (臭, 髒, 污穢, 齷齪). 臊’s niche advantage is its bodily intimacy: it can make condemnation feel visceral and immediate. Its niche disadvantage is that it can sound colloquial or embodied in contexts where more institutional operators (穢/污穢) carry greater authority. So it specializes: it thrives where physicality, embarrassment, sexuality, or bodily proximity are salient.

### 4.3 Branch B: Odor → shame (害臊 as an emotion of exposure)

The second branch is the star of the show: **害臊**. To understand why odor would become embarrassment, it helps to treat shame not as “feeling bad about a wrong action” but as a social-perceptual state. Shame is centrally about **visibility**: being seen in a diminished way, being exposed, being placed under a judging gaze. Guilt, by contrast, is more about **action and repair**: a wrong deed, an obligation to make amends, and a script of confession/apology/compensation.

This difference explains why odor is a good metaphorical substrate for shame. Smell is the sense of involuntary broadcasting. You cannot easily hide it once it exists; you often cannot detect it as well as others can; and it leaks across boundaries. “I am embarrassed” can be conceptualized as “something about me is leaking into the social air.” In other words, odor models shame as **unwanted emanation**.

The compound 害臊 intensifies this. 害 introduces a sense of being afflicted or harmed by the state: embarrassment is not chosen; it happens to you. The result is an emotion word that captures the felt loss of control over one’s social presentation—precisely the core phenomenology of shame.

Face dynamics sharpen the picture. In face-oriented interaction, what matters is not only moral wrongdoing but the management of public self-image: dignity, propriety, appropriateness, timing. Odor metaphors are well suited to this because they bypass argument. If something “smells,” it triggers avoidance and discomfort before rational evaluation. In shame episodes, the body often responds similarly: heat, flushing, withdrawal, the urge to disappear—behaviors that reduce exposure.

### 4.4 Constructions and diagnostics: how to tell the branches apart in texts

To treat 臊 as a bifurcation rather than a vague “metaphor,” we need diagnostics that a reader (or a corpus study) can apply.

- **Target diagnostics:** Branch A often targets objects, bodies, foods, spaces, or other people; Branch B targets the self (or a character’s internal state).
- **Trigger diagnostics:** Branch A is triggered by physical odor or by bodily/crude content; Branch B is triggered by social exposure (being watched, teased, praised inappropriately, caught unprepared).
- **Remedy diagnostics:** Branch A invites cleansing/removal/avoidance; Branch B invites concealment, repair of face, topic shift, withdrawal.
- **Co-text diagnostics:** Branch A co-occurs with disgust terms; Branch B co-occurs with gaze/face terms (見笑, 丟臉, 不好意思) and interactional settings.

The table below packages these contrasts.

| Feature | Branch A: Odor → ugliness/taint | Branch B: Odor → shame (害臊) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Default target | external object/person/action | the self (internal state) |
| Typical trigger | bodily smell, crude bodily content | attention, exposure, being evaluated |
| Social function | condemnation, distancing, ridicule | self-regulation, face management |
| Typical remedy | remove/avoid the “source” | hide, withdraw, repair face |
| Likely co-text | disgust/filth lexicon | face/gaze lexicon; politeness markers |
| Competition | overlaps with 臭/髒/齷齪 | overlaps with 羞恥/不好意思/丟臉 |

### 4.5 Why this bifurcation matters for the larger ecology

The 臊 bifurcation demonstrates a general principle: “dirty” vocabulary does not moralize in only one way. It can produce outward condemnation, but it can also produce inward emotional discipline. These are different social technologies. Outward condemnation sorts others; inward shame regulates the self. Odor is unusually capable of feeding both technologies because it is simultaneously sensory (thus persuasive) and social (thus normative).

In later sections, the contrast with **臭** will be instructive. 臭 is well suited to reputational machinery and public denunciation chains; 臊, by comparison, is unusually well suited to the micro-politics of face. That division of labor is exactly what a semantic ecology model is designed to capture.

## 第四節（繁體中文）

## 路徑一：嗅覺→情緒——「臊」作為分岔引擎（氣味→醜惡／氣味→羞恥）

在中文的「髒」語義生態系裡， **臊** 是最能暴露結構的詞之一。表面看，它是氣味詞：一種尖銳、動物性、帶身體感的味道，常令人不快。然而在現代語用裡，它最醒目的後裔卻不是味道，而是一種情緒： **害臊** （尷尬、難為情、羞怯）。這並非偶然的“好玩延伸”，而是一個典型的語義分岔：同一個感官詞可以裂成兩條不同的社會功能線——一條外向（譴責、醜惡、污損），一條內向（自我感、面子受威脅）。用生態系語言說，臊不是單純從“臭”走到“羞”，而是在兩個棲地都能繁殖，並在各自棲地形成可預期的用法。

### 4.1 感官底座：臊是什麼樣的味道？

臊不是泛泛的“臭”。它常指帶身體、動物、油脂或腥膻邊緣的氣味：會黏附、會上竄、讓人覺得侵入。這個具體性很關鍵，因為感官越貼身、越私密，就越容易被用來做社會邊界工作。嗅覺本來就是一種距離管理的感官：它告訴你誰能靠近、什麼可入口、什麼在腐敗、什麼“不對勁”。當一個詞把身體氣味編碼進去，它天然就帶著關於接近、暴露、與不適的社會含義。

從這個底座出發，本節把臊的外推拆成兩條路徑：

1. **氣味 → 醜惡/污損（他者導向的評價）**
2. **氣味 → 羞恥/尷尬（自我導向的情緒）**

第一條把氣味視為可被評價、可被嘲弄、可被譴責的屬性；第二條把氣味視為“暴露感”的模型：彷彿你散發出某種不合宜的訊號，別人能感覺到。

### 4.2 分枝A：氣味→醜惡/污損（以感官定讞推動譴責）

在這條分枝裡，臊與整個“髒詞彙機械”同向運作：把感官厭惡轉成社會厭惡。其隱含邏輯很直白：難聞的，就該保持距離。由於氣味常被體驗為侵入與擴散（在空氣中飄散、經呼吸進入身體），它成為譴責時非常有力的資源：把對象描寫成不只是“錯”，而是“令人避之唯恐不及”。

語言上，這條分枝常出現在帶評價色彩的描述中：即便氣味義部分淡化，它仍可保留“身體性”的感覺。它容易與身體、飲食、空間相連，也能外推到行為或風格，表示“粗鄙、下作、令人作嘔”的那種不抽象的厭惡。

生態系角度的重點是：這條分枝會與其他譴責力強的詞競爭（如臭、髒、污穢、齷齪）。臊的棲位優勢在於“貼身的身體感”，能讓譴責立即變得生理化；棲位劣勢則在於它在某些語境裡可能顯得過於口語或過於肉身，相比之下，穢/污穢之類的制度詞更具權威。因此臊會專門化：它更擅長在身體、尷尬、性、近距離互動等情境裡發揮。

### 4.3 分枝B：氣味→羞恥（害臊是“被暴露”的情緒）

第二條分枝最關鍵： **害臊** 。理解“為何氣味會變成尷尬”，可以先把羞恥看成一種社會知覺狀態，而不只是“做錯事而難過”。羞恥的核心是 **可見性** ：被看見、被評價、以某種貶抑方式暴露在他人的目光下。與之相比，罪疚更像是 **行為—修復** 的劇本：做錯一件事，承認、道歉、補償、修正。

這個差異讓氣味成為羞恥的理想底材。嗅覺是一種“不由自主的廣播”：一旦存在就不易遮掩；你自己常不如旁人敏感；它會穿越距離與邊界。“我很害臊”可被理解為“有什麼關於我正在漏到社會空氣裡”，而且別人能“聞到”。換句話說，氣味把羞恥建模成 **不合宜的外溢** 。

複合詞中的「害」又把這種外溢感強化成“受害/受困”：尷尬不是你選的，而是降臨在你身上的狀態。這非常貼合羞恥的現象學：失去對自我呈現的控制，想縮小、想消失、想把自己從他人的視線中撤回。

面子動力使這條分枝更清晰。在重視體面與互動得體的場景中，問題往往不是道德罪錯，而是公共自我形象的管理：何時被注意、以何種方式被注意、是否失了分寸。氣味隱喻在此特別好用，因為它繞過辯論：只要“聞起來不對”，就先引發不適與退避。羞恥時的身體反應（發熱、臉紅、退縮、躲避）也正是降低暴露的行為。

### 4.4 構式與診斷：如何在文本中辨認兩條分枝

若要把臊視為“分岔”而非鬆散比喻，需要可操作的辨識指標，讓讀者或語料研究都能使用：

- \*\*目標指標：\*\*分枝A多指向外部對象（物、身體、他者、行為）；分枝B多指向自我內在狀態。
- \*\*觸發指標：\*\*分枝A常由字面氣味或粗鄙內容觸發；分枝B常由社會暴露觸發（被盯、被調侃、被不合時宜地稱讚、被抓到失態）。
- \*\*補救指標：\*\*分枝A常導向清除/避開；分枝B導向遮掩、退場、修復面子、轉移話題。
- \*\*共現指標：\*\*分枝A常與厭惡/污穢詞群共現；分枝B常與面子/目光詞群（丟臉、見笑、不好意思）與互動場景共現。

下表把兩條分枝的對比壓縮成可用的分析卡片。

| 特徵 | 分枝A：氣味→醜惡/污損 | 分枝B：氣味→羞恥（害臊） |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 默認目標 | 外部物/人/行為 | 自我（內在狀態） |
| 常見觸發 | 身體氣味、粗鄙身體內容 | 注意、暴露、被評價 |
| 社會功能 | 譴責、距離化、嘲弄 | 自我規訓、面子管理 |
| 常見補救 | 去除來源、避開 | 遮掩、退縮、修復體面 |
| 常見共現 | 厭惡/髒污詞群 | 面子/目光詞群、禮貌標記 |
| 競爭關係 | 與臭/髒/齷齪重疊 | 與羞恥/不好意思/丟臉重疊 |

### 4.5 這個分岔對整體生態系意味著什麼

臊的分岔揭示一個總原理：髒詞彙的道德化不是單一路徑。它既能產生外向譴責，也能產生內向的情緒紀律；兩者是不同的社會技術。外向譴責用來排序他人，內向羞恥用來管束自我。嗅覺之所以能同時餵養兩者，是因為它兼具感官說服力與社會規範性：既是身體經驗，又能立即召喚距離、暴露與不適的秩序。

後文與 **臭** 的對照會更凸顯分工：臭更容易長成名聲機器與公開譴責鏈；臊則異常擅長面子微政治。這種分工正是語義生態系視角要捕捉的核心。

## Section 5 (English)

## Route II — Smell → Reputation: 臭 and the Historiographic Construction Chain

If **臊** shows how odor can become an inward emotion, **臭** shows how odor can become a public verdict. In the Chinese “dirty” ecology, 臭 is not merely an adjective for a bad smell. It is a reputational instrument: a way of making moral evaluation portable, repeatable, and socially enforceable. Its most important contribution is not a single meaning but a **construction chain** —a sequence of increasingly abstract patterns that link sensory disgust to historical memory and then to everyday interactional aggression.

A useful way to approach 臭 is to treat it as a device for moving evaluation across three scales:

1. **Immediate sensory judgment** (this smells bad).
2. **Long-range reputational judgment** (this will stink forever).
3. **Real-time social attack** (I will stink you, your name, and your speech).

This scaling is exactly what makes 臭 central to “moral language.” Many moral accusations are contestable: you can argue about intention, harm, context, and mitigation. Smell metaphors are harder to argue with because they recruit a bodily response. If something is “臭,” it invites a non-negotiable reaction—distance, disgust, avoidance. That embodied force is then transferred to the social target.

### 5.1 The evaluative binary: 流芳 vs 遺臭

The classic pair **流芳** (“fragrance that spreads”) and **遺臭** (“stench left behind”) crystallizes a powerful template: moral value is a sensory trace that persists beyond the person or event. What matters here is not the literal odor; it is the logic of **afterlife evaluation**. The pair belongs to a discourse style that treats history as a moral court. Actions are not merely recorded; they are sentenced. The sensory metaphor makes that sentencing feel natural: good deeds perfume the world; bad deeds rot into stench.

This binary matters methodologically because it provides the earliest stable scaffold for later constructions. Once a language has a clean, memorable opposition like fragrance/stench, it can be reused as a rhetorical machine. Speakers can attach new targets to the template while preserving the moral force of the opposition.

### 5.2 From template to noun: 臭名 and the objectification of stigma

A second step in the chain is **臭名**: the “stinky name,” a reputational object that can be acquired, carried, spread, and sometimes repaired. This is a crucial semantic shift. A smell is an event (it happens in a moment); a “name” is a social object (it persists across contexts). By merging 臭 with 名, the language turns evaluation into property. Stigma becomes something that “belongs” to a person and can outlast the original action.

Once stigma is objectified, it becomes administratively and rhetorically usable. People can warn others, justify exclusion, narrate moral lessons, and construct social boundaries. Notice how this differs from direct condemnation: saying “you are bad” is confrontational and local; saying “you have a臭名” positions the judgment as socially distributed and already recognized.

In ecological terms, 臭名 competes with other stigma carriers such as 污名. The coexistence is instructive. “污” tends to emphasize stain/taint and is comfortable in formal or institutional registers; “臭” carries a visceral, sensory aggression that can feel more immediate and punishing. One can see niche partitioning: 污名 is good for official discourse and repair frames; 臭名 is good for moral storytelling and denunciation.

### 5.3 From stigma to speech act: 臭罵 and the pragmatics of attack

The third step is **臭罵**: a “stinking scolding,” a speech act characterized by aggression, humiliation, and often public display. Here 臭 does not describe a target’s property; it describes the *act itself* as saturated with negativity. This is the point where the chain sinks into everyday interaction. The odor metaphor becomes a way to intensify blame and to frame the interaction as contaminating: the scolding is not just harsh, it is “filthy,” “polluting,” “unfit.”

This move is important because it shows a shift from evaluation as record (historiography) to evaluation as weapon (pragmatics). The same sensory logic is repurposed: stench is not only what you leave behind; it is what you can inflict in the moment. The addressee is placed under a cloud of social contamination.

### 5.4 How construction chains travel: genre migration and downshifting

Construction chains are not purely linguistic; they are sociological. The fragrance/stench binary is at home in elite moral narration. 臭名 lives comfortably in public moral discourse (didactic writing, journalism, denunciation, gossip). 臭罵 lives in interactional heat. The migration can be described as a **downshifting** across genres:

- **Elite evaluation:** moralized history, epigrammatic binaries, stylized judgments.
- **Public discourse:** reputation talk, social warnings, stereotype reinforcement.
- **Everyday interaction:** insult grammar, scolding scripts, intensification.

In many cases, the same term can appear in multiple genres, but its function shifts. In historiography, 臭 is about legacy; in interaction, 臭 is about dominance and humiliation. The ecology model predicts this: once a construction becomes portable, it will be recruited into new habitats that value its force.

### 5.5 Diagnostics: identifying the chain in texts

To keep the analysis teachable, it helps to treat each link as a recognizable construction type with its own signals.

| Link in the chain | Construction type | Default target | Social function | Typical habitat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 流芳↔遺臭 | evaluative binary template | deeds, persons, regimes | moral sentencing; legacy framing | historiography; moral essays |
| 臭名 | noun phrase (stigma object) | persons, groups, institutions | warning, exclusion, moral narrative | public discourse; didactic genres |
| 臭罵 | event noun / speech-act label | acts of scolding; speakers | aggression; humiliation; moral theater | vernacular; media; online discourse |

A reader can also apply a few quick tests. If 臭 attaches to **trace and afterlife** language (留/遺/傳/千古), it is operating in the legacy register. If it attaches to **name and recognition** language (名聲、名譽、眾所周知), it is operating as stigma objectification. If it attaches to **speech-act** language (罵、罵人、辱、噴), it is operating in the pragmatic attack habitat.

### 5.6 Why 臭 matters for the overall ecology

臭 demonstrates how moral language can become scalable. Sensory disgust provides the force; constructions provide the portability; genre migration provides the reach. Once evaluation is packaged as a binary template, then objectified as a “name,” and finally deployed as an interactional weapon, it can circulate from elite texts to street talk to online outrage without losing its core affective charge.

In later sections, this chain will be compared to other routes. 臊 produces a tight link between odor and self-conscious emotion. 臭, by contrast, builds infrastructure for collective judgment: reputation, stigma, and public condemnation. Together they show why “dirty” cannot be modeled as a single slider: different sensory anchors support different moral technologies.

## 第五節（繁體中文）

## 路徑二：嗅覺→名聲——「臭」的史評構式鏈

如果說 **臊** 展示了氣味如何長成內向的情緒，那麼 **臭** 展示的則是：氣味如何長成公共定讞。在中文的「髒」語義生態系裡，臭不只是“不好聞”的形容詞；它是一種名聲工具，一種能把道德評價做成可攜帶、可重複、可執行的語言機械。臭最關鍵的貢獻不在單一義項，而在一條 **構式鏈** ：把感官厭惡串到歷史記憶，再串到日常互動攻擊的序列。

理解臭最有效的方法，是把它看成一個能跨三種尺度搬運評價的裝置：

1. **即時感官判斷** （聞起來臭）。
2. **長時名聲判斷** （會遺臭千古）。
3. **即刻社會攻擊** （用臭去打擊對方、名聲與話語）。

這種尺度搬運正是臭在“道德語言”中的中心性來源。許多道德指控其實可以爭辯：動機、傷害、情境、補救都可討論；但嗅覺隱喻更難被辯論，因為它動員的是身體反應。只要說“臭”，就召喚距離、厭惡與避開的本能；而那股不可協商的力，會被轉移到社會目標上。

### 5.1 評價二元模板：流芳 vs 遺臭

**流芳** （芳香流傳）與 **遺臭** （臭名遺留）這組對偶，是一台非常強的修辭引擎：道德價值被描寫成一種感官痕跡，會在人物或事件之外持續存在。重點不在字面氣味，而在\*\*“後世評價”\*\*的邏輯：歷史像道德法庭，行為不只是被記錄，而是被宣判。感官隱喻讓宣判顯得自然：善行如香，惡行如腐臭。

這組二元模板之所以重要，在於它提供後續構式的穩定腳手架。一旦有了“香/臭”這種記憶友善又強對比的框架，語言就能把新的對象掛上去，保留同樣的道德張力。

### 5.2 從模板到名物化：臭名與污名對象的生成

構式鏈的第二步是 **臭名** ：把臭與名結合，使“壞評價”變成一個可攜帶的社會物件。這是關鍵的語義轉換。氣味是事件性（當下發生），而“名”是社會性（跨情境持續）。當臭黏到名上，評價被物件化：一個人“帶著”臭名，臭名可以散播、可以傳聞、也可能被修復或洗刷。

一旦污名被名物化，它就更容易被社會管理與修辭利用：可以用來警告、用來正當化排除、用來講道德故事、用來劃邊界。這也解釋了臭名與直接罵人之間的差異：直接說“你很壞”是近距離、對抗性強；說“你有臭名”則把判斷放到社會共同體裡，暗示它是眾所周知、已經形成共識的評價。

生態系角度上，臭名會與 **污名** 等詞競爭。這個共存很有啟發： **污** 更像污點/玷污，較容易在正式或制度語域中運作，也較容易進入“修復框架”（洗刷、澄清、恢復名譽）； **臭** 則自帶更強烈的感官攻擊性，語氣更直接、更懲罰性。兩者因此形成棲位分工：污名偏官方與修復，臭名偏道德敘事與譴責。

### 5.3 從名聲到言語行動：臭罵與互動攻擊的語用學

構式鏈的第三步是 **臭罵** 。此時臭不再主要描述對方的性質，而是描述 *行為本身* ：一場充滿攻擊性、羞辱性、且常帶公開展示的斥罵。臭在這裡成為強度與道德戲劇性的標記：罵不只是狠，而是“髒”“污染”“不堪”。臭把互動事件框進“污染語言”的場景，讓受害者像被罩在一團社會臭雲裡。

這一步非常重要，因為它顯示了從“史評紀錄”到“語用武器”的轉換：同一套感官邏輯被重新部署。臭不只是在後世留下的氣味，也可以成為此刻施加的社會傷害。

### 5.4 構式鏈如何旅行：體裁遷移與下沉

構式鏈不只是語言現象，也是社會現象。香/臭二元模板最適合住在精英的道德敘事與史評體；臭名最適合在公共道德語篇裡運作（訓誡、新聞、檄文式譴責、流言與八卦）；臭罵最適合在互動熱區出現。可以把它理解為跨體裁的“下沉”：

- \*\*精英評價：\*\*史評、格言化對偶、定讞式判語。
- \*\*公共語篇：\*\*名聲敘事、社會警示、道德教化與排除。
- \*\*日常互動：\*\*辱罵腳本、強度標記、羞辱與支配。

同一詞在不同體裁中會改變功能：在史評裡臭關乎“遺留”；在互動裡臭關乎“壓制與羞辱”。一旦構式變得可攜帶，它就會被拉進需要其力量的新棲地——這正是生態系模型預測的行為。

### 5.5 文本診斷：如何辨認構式鏈的三個環節

為了讓分析可教、可操作，可以把每一環當作一種可辨識的構式類型，並觀察其訊號。

| 構式鏈環節 | 構式類型 | 默認目標 | 社會功能 | 常見棲地 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 流芳↔遺臭 | 評價二元模板 | 事功、人物、政權 | 道德宣判；遺留框架 | 史評、道德文章 |
| 臭名 | 名物化名聲片語 | 人、群體、機構 | 警告、排除、道德敘事 | 公共語篇、教化體裁 |
| 臭罵 | 事件名詞/言語行動標記 | 斥罵行為、說話者 | 攻擊、羞辱、道德劇場 | 白話互動、媒體/網路 |

也可以用更快的線索：若臭與“留/遺/傳/千古”等痕跡詞共現，多半在遺留/史評棲地；若與“名聲/名譽/眾所周知”等名物化語彙共現，多半在臭名棲地；若與“罵/辱/噴”等言語行動共現，多半在臭罵棲地。

### 5.6 臭對整體生態系的意義

臭讓道德語言具備“可擴張性”。感官厭惡提供情緒力量；構式提供可搬運的模板；體裁遷移提供擴散的路徑。當評價先被包裝成二元模板，再被名物化成“名”，最後被部署成互動武器，它就能從精英文本走到街談巷議，再走到線上公審，而不失去核心的情緒衝擊。

後文與其他路徑對照時，這條構式鏈會成為關鍵參照：臊把嗅覺焊到面子與害臊；臭則搭建集體判斷的基礎設施——名聲、污名、公開譴責。兩者合在一起，正好證明“髒”不是單一刻度，而是多種道德技術的集合。

## Section 6 (English)

## Route III — Form/Appearance → Character → Uncleanliness: 齷齪 and Character Contamination

In the “dirty” ecology, not every route begins with smell. Some begin with what can be seen: posture, crampedness, awkward arrangement, a sense of bodily or spatial constraint. **齷齪** is an especially instructive case because it shows how visual and form-based cues can become moralized, then loop back into a language of cleanliness. The result is a term that does more than call someone “unclean.” It frames them as **small, sordid, and morally cramped** —a character judgment that can be projected onto motives, methods, and social identity.

This route matters because it clarifies a broader point: moralization does not always require the visceral punch of odor. It can begin with **shape**, with how a body or action is imagined as arranged in space. Visual metaphors are powerful because they offer a different kind of “evidence.” Smell convinces through involuntary bodily reaction; form convinces through intelligibility—things look “crooked,” “narrow,” “twisted,” “constricted.” Those spatial intuitions are easily recruited for moral sorting.

### 6.1 Core niche: from cramped form to cramped personhood

齷齪 is commonly used today as an evaluative term for a person, an intention, or a way of doing things. Its default target is not dirt on a surface but a perceived **moral ugliness** of conduct: petty, sneaky, ignoble, lacking openness. Even when speakers do not consciously picture bodily form, the word carries a residue of spatial constriction: something tight, awkward, not free. That residue is not decorative; it is the bridge that allows the word to operate as a character label. A cramped form becomes a cramped soul.

This helps explain why 齷齪 is often uncomfortable as a purely literal description of mess. If a room is untidy, speakers usually prefer 邋遢 or 髒. If a person is morally objectionable, 齷齪 becomes available, because it is already tuned to personhood evaluation.

### 6.2 The loop back to uncleanliness: why “character dirt” still feels like dirt

A striking feature of 齷齪 is that it can feel “dirty” even when it is not literally about dirt. This is the ecological loop: form-based moralization often reattaches to cleanliness language because cleanliness offers a ready-made social logic—contamination, avoidance, exclusion, repair. Once a person is framed as sordid, it becomes easy to speak as if their presence, speech, or motives are contaminating.

This is why terms like 齷齪 can slide into the same pragmatic neighborhood as 污穢 or 骯髒 without being equivalent. It shares the social function (condemnation and distancing) but arrives there by a different sensory path. Smell words often target proximity and disgust; form words often target trust and moral readability. Both can produce exclusion, but the justifications differ.

### 6.3 Comparing near-neighbors: 齷齪 vs 骯髒 vs 邋遢 vs 腌臢

A semantic ecology is clearest when we watch near-neighbors compete and specialize. The cluster around “dirty person” includes at least four items with different niche preferences.

- **邋遢** tends to attach to appearance and upkeep: messy hair, sloppy dress, a neglected room. It often implies lack of self-management rather than moral corruption.
- **腌臢** often evokes grimy unpleasantness and squalor; it can carry a classed or taste-based judgment (unrefined, gross), and it sticks well to environments and bodily states.
- **骯髒** is closer to an explicit “dirty/filthy” condemnation; it can be physical or moral, and it often pairs with talk of money, deals, politics, and “dirty hands.”
- **齷齪** specializes as a character attack: petty, despicable, underhanded, morally cramped. It is less about the mess you can wash away and more about the kind of person you distrust.

These differences show why translation is difficult. In English, “sordid,” “sleazy,” “dirty,” “slovenly,” and “grimy” each cover parts of the space. 齷齪 leans toward “sleazy/sordid” rather than “slovenly.”

The table below turns these contrasts into a practical diagnostic.

| Term | Default target | Core niche | Typical implication | Common “remedy” implied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 邋遢 | appearance, room | neglect/disorder | lack of care, low self-management | tidy up; improve habits |
| 腌臢 | environment/body | grimy squalor | unpleasantness; sometimes class/taste judgment | clean; avoid; sanitize |
| 骯髒 | actions/money/people | filth + moral taint | corruption, dishonor, contamination | expose, cleanse, distance |
| 齷齪 | motives/methods/personhood | character contamination | pettiness, underhandedness, distrust | condemn; exclude; moral distancing |

### 6.4 Constructions and co-text: how 齷齪 behaves in discourse

齷齪 often appears in constructions that make it a lens on intention: “齷齪的想法,” “齷齪的手段,” “內心齷齪.” These pairings matter because they keep the term anchored in inner character rather than external mess. The term also fits well in denunciation genres—editorials, polemics, moral commentary—where character attributions do social work. By contrast, it is less natural in neutral household description, which is exactly what we should expect if its core niche is personhood evaluation.

Another diagnostic is the co-text it attracts. 齷齪 tends to cluster with words about deceit, pettiness, and moral smallness (卑鄙, 下作, 猥瑣, 小人). It is less likely to cluster with purely hygienic vocabulary unless the speaker is deliberately blending physical and moral disgust.

### 6.5 Why this route matters for “odor–morality” narratives

The presence of 齷齪 complicates any story that makes smell the privileged pathway into moral language. Odor is important, but it is not the only sensory gateway. Spatial and visual metaphors can moralize just as effectively, especially in societies where moral judgment is linked to ideals of openness, uprightness, and proper form.

In the overall ecology, 齷齪 occupies a complementary niche to 臊 and 臭. Where 臊 excels at the micro-politics of face and embarrassment, and 臭 excels at scalable reputation and public denunciation, 齷齪 excels at character condemnation grounded in an intuition of cramped, crooked, or ignoble form. That division of labor is not accidental; it reflects multiple ways communities convert perception into normativity.

## 第六節（繁體中文）

## 路徑三：形貌→性格→不潔——「齷齪」與人格污染

在「髒」的語義生態系裡，並非所有路徑都從氣味出發。有些路徑從“看得見的形貌”出發：姿態、局促、佈局不順、以及一種空間上的不舒展。 **齷齪** 特別值得細看，因為它展示了視覺與形式線索如何被道德化，接著又回扣到“不潔”的語感，形成一個帶回路的評價詞。它不只是說某人“不乾淨”，而是把人框成 **卑小、下作、心胸狹隘、行事猥瑣** 的類型；這種人格判斷可以外推到動機、手段與社會身份。

這條路徑的重要性在於：它提醒我們，道德化不必依賴嗅覺那種生理衝擊。道德化也可以從 **形狀與空間直覺** 開始。視覺/形式隱喻之所以有力，是因為它提供另一種“證據感”：東西看起來歪、窄、扭、縮、不舒展。這些空間直覺很容易被招募來做道德排序。嗅覺靠不由自主的身體反應說服你；形貌靠“可理解性”說服你——他看起來就不正、不大方、不光明。

### 6.1 核心棲位：從局促之形到局促之人

現代語用裡，齷齪常被用作評價人、意圖或作法的詞。它的默認目標不是桌面上的灰塵，而是行為背後的 **道德醜陋** ：卑鄙、猥瑣、鬼祟、不大方、不磊落。即使說話者不會意識到“形貌”意象，詞本身仍殘留一種空間上的收縮感：緊、擠、彆扭、不自由。這個殘留不是裝飾，而是橋接點，讓它能穩定地運作成“人格標籤”。局促的形，轉寫為局促的心與行事風格。

因此，齷齪往往不適合作為純粹的“房間很亂”描述。要說凌亂，多數情況下邋遢或髒更自然；要說人品可鄙，齷齪才是它的主場。這正是核心棲位在語感上的反映。

### 6.2 回扣到不潔：為何“人格髒”仍像髒

齷齪有一個引人注目的特徵：即便不談物理污垢，它仍帶“髒”的感覺。這是一種生態系回路：以形貌/性格起家的道德化，常會重新掛回清潔語言，因為清潔語言提供現成的社會邏輯——污染、避開、排除、修復。當一個人被框成猥瑣下作時，就很容易順勢把他的存在、言語、動機說成“會污染”的東西。

這也解釋了齷齪為何能與污穢或骯髒落在相近的語用鄰里，卻又不等價。它共享的是社會功能（譴責與距離化），但進入這個功能的感官路徑不同。嗅覺詞往往先處理“靠近是否令人不適”；形貌詞往往先處理“是否可信、是否正直、是否可被道德理解”。兩者都可能導向排除，但其正當化策略不同。

### 6.3 與近鄰比較：齷齪 vs 骯髒 vs 邋遢 vs 腌臢

生態系最清晰的時刻，是近義詞在競爭中被迫分工。圍繞“人很髒/不堪”的語義空間，至少有四個常見競爭者，棲位偏好不同：

- **邋遢** 多黏在外表與打理：頭髮、衣著、房間維護。它常暗示缺乏自我管理，而不必然指向道德腐敗。
- **腌臢** 更偏向“髒得難堪”的髒亂與污膩，容易黏在環境與身體狀態上，也常帶品味/階層化評價（不體面、不講究）。
- **骯髒** 更接近直指“污穢/腐敗”的譴責，可物理亦可道德，常和錢、交易、政治、髒手等題材相連。
- **齷齪** 最專門化為人格攻擊：卑小、下作、鬼祟、心術不正。它不是“洗一洗就好”的髒，而是“你這個人就不可信”的髒。

這也說明了為何英譯常會分裂成多個詞：sordid/sleazy（齷齪的核心）、slovenly（邋遢的核心）、grimy/squalid（腌臢的核心）、dirty/corrupt（骯髒的核心）各只覆蓋部分區域。

下表把差異整理成可用的診斷卡片。

| 詞 | 默認目標 | 核心棲位 | 常見含意 | 隱含的“補救”方向 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 邋遢 | 外表、房間 | 失序/怠惰式髒 | 缺乏照料、自我管理差 | 整理、改習慣 |
| 腌臢 | 環境/身體 | 污膩難堪 | 不舒適；常帶品味/體面評價 | 清理、避開、消毒 |
| 骯髒 | 行為/金錢/人 | 髒＋道德沾染 | 腐敗、可恥、污染 | 揭露、洗刷、距離化 |
| 齷齪 | 動機/手段/人格 | 人格污染 | 卑鄙鬼祟、不可信 | 譴責、排除、道德距離 |

### 6.4 構式與共現：齷齪在語篇中的行為

齷齪常出現在把它直接對準“內在”的搭配中，例如「齷齪的想法」「齷齪的手段」「內心齷齪」。這些搭配很關鍵，因為它們把齷齪穩定地錨在“人格/意圖”而非“外在凌亂”。它也特別適合住在譴責語類（社論、檄文式評論、道德批判、政治口水）之中，因為那裡需要的正是人格歸因。相反地，它在中性的家務描述裡顯得不自然，正好印證其核心棲位。

另一个可操作的指標是共現詞。齷齪往往與卑鄙、下作、猥瑣、小人等“道德卑小”詞群聚集；除非說話者刻意把物理厭惡與道德譴責混搭，它較少與純衛生詞彙自然共現。

### 6.5 這條路徑如何改寫“嗅覺→道德”的敘事

齷齪的存在使任何把嗅覺當作唯一道德化通道的敘事都不完整。嗅覺確實重要，但它不是唯一的感官入口。空間與形式隱喻同樣能有效地道德化，尤其在道德評價與“正直/磊落/大方”的理想相連的社會裡。

放回整體生態系看，齷齪與臊、臭形成互補分工：臊擅長面子微政治與害臊，臭擅長名聲機器與公開譴責鏈，齷齪擅長以“局促/不正/不光明”的直覺支撐人格譴責。這種分工並非偶然，而是社群把感知轉成規範的多條技術路徑在詞彙層面的投影。

## Section 7 (English)

## Purity Regimes as Social Infrastructure: How “Impurity” Vocabulary Becomes Stable

“Dirty” words do not become powerful only because bodies feel disgust. They also become powerful because institutions teach communities how to treat certain states as consequential. In religion and ritual systems, impurity is rarely just a description of grime. It is a condition that reorganizes permissions—who may enter, touch, eat, officiate, marry, mourn, or participate. This section argues that the normative edge of the “dirty” ecology becomes unusually stable when it is embedded in a purity regime: a repeatable package of classification, taboo, and repair.

A purity regime can be understood as a three-part mechanism. First, it classifies the world into categories that are not merely descriptive but moralized: clean/unclean, proper/improper, auspicious/inauspicious, inside/outside. Second, it defines triggers that place a person, object, or space into a restricted state (contact, time periods, bodily processes, death, sex, blood, certain foods, certain locations). Third, it prescribes remedies that reverse or manage the restricted state (washing, abstention, confession, offerings, avoidance periods, ritual specialists). This is why impurity vocabulary is so often procedural. It does not just say “this is bad”; it implies “therefore do X.”

Classic anthropological accounts—especially Mary Douglas —help clarify why impurity talk feels so “non-negotiable.” The point is not that dirt is objectively dangerous in every case; it is that “dirt” often names matter (or behavior) that violates a socially enforced map of where things belong. The emotional power of disgust, then, is recruited to defend a classification system. Complementary ritual-process perspectives—often associated with Victor Turner —show how impurity states can be transitional and socially productive: restrictions and repairs do not merely punish; they stage transformation, boundary reinforcement, and reintegration.

For our purposes, the key linguistic insight is simple: institutions create **repetition**. When the same triggers and remedies recur across generations, the language used to name them becomes predictable, collocationally tight, and resistant to drift. In everyday talk, “dirty” slang can shift quickly because the social enforcement is informal. In purity regimes, however, enforcement is patterned: texts, liturgies, etiquette, specialists, and communal memory keep the script intact. Vocabulary at the normative edge—terms like 穢, 污穢, and constructions that encode “contact-with-impurity”—thus gains a durable niche.

### 7.1 Impurity as an operator, not an adjective

A helpful way to teach this is to treat institutional “impurity” terms as **operators**. An operator is a word that, once invoked, implies a chain of consequences. In many East Asian contexts, impurity discourse is tightly associated with contact logic: touching, entering, or mixing becomes the central concern. That contact logic is exactly the bridge from sensory dirt to social order. It is also why the lexicon tends to stabilize around a small set of high-authority items (穢/污穢) that can be plugged into many procedural frames, rather than infinitely proliferating synonyms.

The operator function becomes visible when we look at typical “slots” in ritual discourse: *trigger → status → restriction → remedy*. Even when the surface wording varies, the slots remain stable.

| Slot in the ritual script | Typical question it answers | Linguistic footprint (what tends to stabilize) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | “What caused the restricted state?” | contact verbs; “touch/encounter/enter” frames |
| Status | “What state are we now in?” | impurity operators (穢/污穢-type items) |
| Restriction | “What is forbidden or avoided?” | prohibitive frames; access/participation terms |
| Remedy | “How is the state managed or reversed?” | cleansing/abstinence/repair constructions |
| Reintegration | “When is normal permission restored?” | completion markers; time/sequence frames |

This script-like structure is one reason impurity vocabulary remains legible even to non-specialists. People may not know the full theology, but they recognize the procedural shape: “if X happened, then Y is required.”

### 7.2 Boundary management: bodies, spaces, and times

Purity regimes are frequently organized around boundary events: birth, sex, menstruation, illness, death, food preparation, travel between spaces, and participation in sacred or official arenas. These events matter anthropologically because they are points where private bodies intersect public order. They matter linguistically because they generate stable phraseology. A community needs reliable ways to say: “this state affects access,” “this contact is consequential,” “this remedy is required.”

This is also where the “dirty” ecology gains its normative authority. Household dirt is annoying; ritual impurity is consequential. Once the lexicon can express consequentiality, it can be borrowed for secular moralization. Terms anchored in ritual authority can later be redeployed in political denunciation or social stigma, not because speakers are consciously importing doctrine, but because the words already carry a shadow of procedure and seriousness.

### 7.3 Stabilization through institutions: why some words endure

We can now state a linguistic mechanism in religion-studies terms. Purity regimes stabilize vocabulary through four channels:

1. **Textualization:** rules and exemplars are written, quoted, and taught.
2. **Specialization:** ritual experts and institutions preserve formality and correct usage.
3. **Periodicity:** the same events recur (calendars, life cycles), refreshing the script.
4. **Public salience:** violations are socially visible, making naming socially necessary.

When these channels are active, the lexicon shows predictable symptoms: fixed collocations, durable constructions, and register stratification (high-authority terms in formal contexts; simplified analogues in casual speech). The normative edge of “dirty,” then, is not just an emotional reflex; it is a maintained infrastructure.

The table below summarizes how a purity regime interacts with the broader “dirty” ecology.

| Function in the ecology | What institutions supply | Resulting linguistic tendency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Making impurity consequential | explicit restrictions and permissions | operator-like impurity terms |
| Making impurity transmissible | contact logics and boundary rules | productive “contact-with-X” frames |
| Making impurity repairable | standardized remedies | stable repair constructions |
| Making impurity memorable | public ritual and repetition | fixed phrases and durable registers |

This section positions purity regimes as the stabilizing backbone of the normative lexicon. Later, when we turn to cross-language triangulation and diachronic construction analysis, this institutional account will act as the causal background: the route patterns are not floating metaphors; they are fed and disciplined by recurring social technologies.

## 第七節（繁體中文）

## 淨穢制度作為社會基礎設施：為何「不潔」詞彙特別穩定

「髒」詞彙之所以有力量，不只因為身體會覺得噁心，還因為制度會教人哪些狀態“有後果”。在宗教與禮制系統裡，不潔往往不是描述灰塵的形容詞，而是一種會改寫許可的狀態：誰可以進入、觸碰、食用、主持、交往、參與。這一節主張，髒的語義生態系之所以能在規範端形成高度穩定的詞彙棲位，是因為它被嵌入了淨穢制度——一套可重複運作的分類、禁忌與修復機制。

淨穢制度可以用三個部件來理解。第一，它把世界分類成帶規範性的類別：淨/不淨、正/不正、吉/凶、內/外；這些分類不是純描述，而是帶“該如何對待”的指令。第二，它定義觸發條件，把人、物、空間或事件置入受限狀態：接觸、時段、身體過程、死亡、性、血、某些食物、某些場所。第三，它提供修復程序，讓受限狀態被管理或逆轉：洗滌、齋戒、避忌期、懺悔、供獻、祓除、由專業者主持的儀式。這就是為何不潔詞彙常帶程序性：它不只說“這不好”，還暗示“所以要做某事”。

人類學對淨穢的經典討論（以 Mary Douglas 一系的觀點最具代表性）提醒我們：不潔的關鍵不必是客觀危險，而常是“東西出現在不該出現的位置”，也就是對社會分類圖的違規。厭惡的情緒力量因此被用來守護分類系統。另一類儀式過程取向（常與 Victor Turner 相關）則強調不潔狀態往往是過渡性的：限制與修復不只是在懲罰，更是在安排轉換、強化邊界、並完成再整合。

就本文的語言學關懷而言，最重要的點其實很簡單：制度會製造 **重複** 。當同樣的觸發與修復在世代間反覆出現，用來命名它們的語言就會變得可預期、搭配緊密、而且不容易漂移。日常口語的髒話或羞辱詞可以跟著流行快速變化，因為制裁機制比較非正式；淨穢制度的語言卻有模式化的執行：文本、禮儀、規範、專門者、共同記憶都在維持腳本。因此，規範端詞彙（如穢、污穢，以及能表達“觸碰—染污”邏輯的框架）會獲得極穩固的棲位。

### 7.1 把不潔視為“操作符”

教學上很有用的一個轉換，是把制度語域中的“不潔”詞當作 **操作符** 。操作符一旦被啟動，就隱含一串後果。許多東亞情境裡，不潔話語與“接觸邏輯”高度綁定：摸到、碰到、進入、混入成為核心關切。這種接觸邏輯正是從感官髒到社會秩序的橋接點，也解釋了為何詞彙會圍繞少數高權威項穩定下來：它們可以被插入多種程序框架，不需要靠無限同義詞擴張。

操作符功能最清楚的地方，是儀式語篇常見的“槽位結構”： *觸發 → 狀態 → 限制 → 修復* 。措辭可以變，槽位卻穩定。

| 儀式腳本槽位 | 它回答的問題 | 容易被固定下來的語言形態 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 觸發 | “是什麼造成受限狀態？” | 接觸動詞；“遇/觸/入”框架 |
| 狀態 | “現在處於什麼狀態？” | 不潔操作符（穢/污穢類） |
| 限制 | “哪些行為需要避免或禁止？” | 禁止句式；出入/參與詞彙 |
| 修復 | “如何管理或逆轉？” | 洗滌/齋戒/補救構式 |
| 再整合 | “何時恢復正常許可？” | 完成標記；時間序列框架 |

這種腳本形狀使得淨穢語言即便在非專家之間也容易被識別：即使不懂完整神學，人仍能辨認“如果發生X，就需要Y”的程序感。

### 7.2 邊界管理：身體、空間、時間

淨穢制度常圍繞邊界事件組織：出生、性、月經、疾病、死亡、飲食處理、空間移動、進出神聖或官方場域。人類學上，這些事件重要，因為它們是私密身體與公共秩序的交會點；語言上，它們重要，因為它們會生成穩定片語與固定句式。社群需要可靠的說法來表達：“這個狀態影響許可”“這種接觸有後果”“這種修復是必要的”。

也正是在這裡，髒的語義生態系獲得規範權威。家務型髒多半只是麻煩；制度型不潔則是後果性。一旦詞彙能表達後果性，它就可能被世俗語篇借用來做道德化：原本被宗教/禮制權威支撐的詞，會在政治譴責、社會污名化中被重新部署，不必是因為說話者刻意輸入教義，而是因為這些詞本來就帶著程序陰影與嚴重性。

### 7.3 制度如何讓詞彙耐久

把宗教研究語言翻成語言機制，可以得到一個清楚的穩定化模型。淨穢制度主要透過四個通道固定詞彙：

1. \*\*文本化：\*\*規則與範例被書寫、引用、教導。
2. \*\*專門化：\*\*儀式專家與機構保存正式用法與“正確性”。
3. \*\*週期性：\*\*生命週期與曆法事件反覆出現，不斷刷新腳本。
4. \*\*公共可見性：\*\*違規會被看見，命名變得社會必要。

當這些通道運作時，語言會呈現可預期的症狀：固定搭配、耐久構式、語域分層（高權威詞在正式情境；簡化版本在日常）。因此，髒的規範端不是單純情緒反射，而是一套被維護的社會基礎設施。

下表把淨穢制度如何支撐整體生態系整理為幾個功能連結。

| 生態系功能 | 制度提供什麼 | 產生的語言傾向 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 讓不潔“有後果” | 明確限制與許可 | 操作符式不潔詞 |
| 讓不潔“可傳遞” | 接觸邏輯與邊界規則 | 高產的“觸碰—染污”框架 |
| 讓不潔“可修復” | 標準化補救程序 | 穩定的修復構式 |
| 讓不潔“可記憶” | 公共儀式與重複 | 固定片語與耐久語域 |

本節把淨穢制度定位為規範端詞彙的穩定骨架。下一節轉向歷時語義與構式語法時，這個制度解釋將提供因果背景：路徑不是漂浮的隱喻，而是被一套反覆運作的社會技術餵養、訓練與固定下來的。

## Section 8 (English)

## Diachronic Semantics and Construction Grammar: Testing Isomorphism, Carrier Mismatch, and the Thai Stress-Test

By this point, the “dirty” ecology has shown three major cross-domain routes—odor to emotion (臊 → 害臊), odor to reputation (臭 → 臭名/遺臭/臭罵), and form to character (齷齪). Section 7 added the institutional backbone: purity regimes stabilize the normative edge of the lexicon. Section 8 now turns that whole story into a linguistic research program. The central claim is not merely that meanings shift, but that shifts become socially consequential when they crystallize into **constructions**: reusable patterns that speakers can plug new content into. Once a “dirty” term enters a constructional template, it gains scale—across topics, across speakers, across genres, and often across centuries.

### 8.1 From meaning shift to constructionalization

A construction, in this sense, is more than a phrase. It is a partly fixed form–meaning pairing that behaves like a tool. “臭名” is not just “臭 + 名”; it is a stigma-object template (“bad evaluation becomes a portable ‘name’”). “遺臭” is not just “leave + stink”; it is a legacy template (“evaluation persists after the person/event”). “害臊” is not just “harm + odor”; it is an emotion template (“embarrassment as involuntary exposure”). When these templates become productive—when speakers extend them to new targets without needing a fresh metaphor each time—we can say the system has undergone constructionalization.

Constructionalization gives us a concrete way to track “strength” without relying on intuition. A route is stronger when it generates stable templates, spreads across registers, and persists over time; weaker when it remains an occasional poetic flourish.

The table below lays out a practical diagnostic set for identifying constructionalization in historical and contemporary data.

| Diagnostic | What you look for in texts | Why it matters for “dirty” routes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collocation rigidity | recurring pairings that resist substitution | signals a stabilized template (e.g., legacy/stigma frames) |
| Productivity | new fillers appear in the same pattern | shows the pattern is a tool, not a one-off metaphor |
| Register migration | pattern moves from elite to public to vernacular (or vice versa) | reveals social scaling and genre uptake |
| Repair framing | constructions for cleansing/undoing stigma/impurity | indicates institutional integration of evaluation |
| Semantic bleaching + retention | literal sense fades but evaluative force remains | typical of odor → moral machinery |
| Paradigmatic competition | a rival template crowds out another in a register | maps ecological pressure among near-neighbors |

### 8.2 A cross-language question: mechanism isomorphism vs carrier mismatch

Cross-language comparison is often derailed by a simple trap: assuming that shared characters guarantee shared mechanisms, or that different words imply different mechanisms. This section separates two levels.

- **Mechanism isomorphism** means the *same kind of mapping* is strong across languages: odor can support stigma templates; impurity can support procedural operators; “dirty” can support repair scripts.
- **Carrier mismatch** means the *lexical vehicle* differs: one language may use an odor word for a stigma template, another may prefer “stain/taint,” and a third may borrow a Sino-Xenic compound (e.g., “污名/汚名/오명/ô danh”) while keeping native adjectives for everyday “dirty.”

This distinction lets us test the “Sinosphere triangulation” cleanly. Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese have different mixtures of Sino-derived compounds and native vocabulary. That makes them ideal for asking whether the same social-linguistic mechanisms recur even when the carriers differ.

The matrix below is a teaching-friendly sketch of what we would test. It does not claim final measurements; it specifies the comparison targets.

| Mapping / template family | Chinese (typical carriers) | Japanese (typical carriers) | Korean (typical carriers) | Vietnamese (typical carriers) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Odor → emotion (embarrassment) | 臊 → 害臊 | often uses native emotion vocabulary; odor words exist but may not map the same way | shame lexicon often via Sino words (수치) plus native forms | shame lexicon largely native + Sino layers; check odor linkage |
| Odor → reputation (infamy/legacy) | 臭名 / 遺臭 | 臭い can form evaluative patterns; Sino “汚名” exists | 오명 (Sino) strongly reputational; native insult patterns too | ô danh (Sino) reputational; native insult patterns |
| Stain/taint → stigma object | 污名 (also 臭名) | 汚名 strongly available; repair scripts common | 오명 strongly available; repair scripts in formal register | ô danh available in formal register |
| Impurity operator (ritual/procedural) | 穢 / 污穢 / 觸穢-type frames | 穢れ and ritual vocabulary; strong procedural feel | 불결 (Sino) plus ritual terms; procedurality depends on domain | bất/不潔-type Sino compounds + ritual vocabulary |
| Repair (wash away / cleanse) | 洗刷/澄清/除… | “汚名をそそぐ”/cleanse frames | “오명을 씻다”/wash away frames | “rửa …” frames; check Sino compound integration |

The pedagogical point is that the “triangle” can survive even if the corners are carried by different words: uncleanliness/impurity, shame, and stigma/reputation can remain structurally linked across languages while the surface vocabulary shifts. That is what a genuine areal typology would look like: shared pathways, varied vehicles.

### 8.3 How to make the comparison real: a minimal corpus plan

A reader-friendly comparison still needs a disciplined method. The minimal plan is to build small, register-diverse corpora in each language (classical/modern where relevant), then test for: (i) earliest attestations of each template family, (ii) productivity (new fillers), (iii) register spread, and (iv) repair scripts. “Strength” can be operationalized as a composite of frequency, dispersion across genres, and template productivity.

### 8.4 The Thai stress-test: can the institutional triangle be reconstructed without Han-script writing?

Thai is useful not because it is “the same,” but because it is plausibly “adjacent” in a different way: it has long-standing contact with Chinese communities and Chinese commercial vocabulary in some domains, but it does not use Han characters as its primary writing system, and its high-register religious vocabulary is heavily shaped by Pali/Sanskrit. That makes it a pressure-test for the institutional side of the model.

The stress-test question is: can we still reconstruct the same functional triangle—impurity (procedural), shame (face/social exposure), stigma (reputation object)—even if the carriers are different and the religious register is not Sino-derived? If the triangle reappears with different lexical materials, that supports a mechanism-level generalization: institutions plus interactional face dynamics naturally generate similar construction families. If it does not reappear, we learn something equally valuable: the “triangle” may depend on specific historical packaging in the Sinosphere (textual traditions, compounds, bureaucratic and ritual genres).

A practical way to run the Thai test is to search not for direct “translations,” but for the same constructional jobs: “X causes restricted participation,” “wash away bad name,” “public scolding as contaminating,” “embarrassment as exposure.” The carriers may not look familiar; the mechanism is the target.

Section 8 therefore closes the loop: the ecology is not just a descriptive metaphor. It generates testable predictions about which templates should exist, where they should live (register), how they should spread (genre migration), and how much of the system is portable across languages versus bound to particular historical carriers.

## 歷時語義與構式語法：同型性檢驗、載體錯位、與泰語壓力測試

前面幾節已經把三條主要跨域路徑做成可教的模型：臊把嗅覺帶向害臊的情緒機制，臭把嗅覺帶向名聲與譴責鏈，齷齪把形貌直覺帶向人格污染；第七節則指出淨穢制度如何穩定規範端詞彙。第八節把這整套敘事轉成語言學的研究方案：重點不只是“義項改變”，而是“改變何時變成可重複使用的工具”。這個轉折點就是 **構式** ：半固定、可套用的形式—意義配對。一旦髒詞彙進入構式模板，它就能跨題材、跨說話者、跨語域擴散，甚至跨世紀保存。

### 8.1 從語義延伸到構式化

構式不等於隨口搭配，而是一種可攜帶的語言工具。“臭名”不只是“臭＋名”，而是一個把負評物件化的模板（壞評價變成可攜帶的“名”）；“遺臭”不只是“留下臭”，而是一個遺留定讞模板（評價超越當事者而持續）；“害臊”也不只是“害＋臊”，而是一個把尷尬建模成“非自願暴露”的情緒模板。當這些模板變得可生產（能換填充物、能跨語域運作），就可以說它們完成了構式化。

構式化讓“路徑強弱”不再只是直覺，而可用可觀察指標來界定：是否形成固定搭配、是否可生產、是否跨語域遷移、是否出現修復框架、是否在字面義淡化後仍保留規範力量。

| 指標 | 在文本中看什麼 | 對髒路徑的意義 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 搭配僵硬度 | 反覆出現且難替換的搭配 | 顯示模板已穩定（如名聲/遺留框架） |
| 生產力 | 同一模式可容納新填充物 | 顯示它是工具而非一次性比喻 |
| 語域遷移 | 從精英到公共到白話（或反向） | 顯示社會擴張與體裁吸納 |
| 修復框架 | 洗刷/澄清/除去污名與不潔 | 顯示制度化整合與程序感 |
| 字面淡化＋力量保留 | 氣味/污垢義弱化但評價力仍強 | 常見於嗅覺→道德機械 |
| 競爭與位移 | 某模板在某語域擠壓另一模板 | 呈現生態壓力與分工 |

### 8.2 跨語比較的關鍵：機制同型 vs 載體錯位

跨語比較最容易踩的坑，是把“同字”當成“同機制”，或把“不同詞”當成“不同機制”。本節把比較拆成兩層。

第一層是 **機制同型性** ：同一種映射在多語都強，例如嗅覺可支撐污名/惡名模板，不潔可支撐程序性操作符，髒可支撐“修復/洗刷”的語言腳本。第二層是 **載體錯位** ：負責承載同一機制的詞不必相同；某語言可能用氣味詞來做名聲模板，另一語言更偏好“污/汚/汚名”這種“污點—名物化”載體，第三語言則以漢字圈借詞（污名/汚名/오명/ô danh）承擔正式語域，而日常“髒/臭”仍由本族語形容詞主導。

這樣拆分後，中日韓越的“漢字圈三角測量”就變得可操作：四語的載體混合不同，反而能檢驗機制是否真的穩定重現。

| 映射／模板家族 | 中文（常見載體） | 日語（常見載體） | 韓語（常見載體） | 越南語（常見載體） |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 嗅覺→情緒（尷尬/羞怯） | 臊→害臊 | 多由本族情緒詞承擔；需檢驗嗅覺鏈結強度 | 羞恥詞彙（如 수치）＋本族形式；檢驗嗅覺分岔 | 情緒詞彙多層；檢驗是否存在“嗅覺→尷尬”強鏈 |
| 嗅覺→名聲（惡名/遺臭） | 臭名/遺臭 | 臭い可形成評價模式；另有汚名 | 오명（漢字語）名聲載體強；另有本族辱罵模式 | ô danh（漢越）偏正式名聲載體 |
| 污點→污名物件 | 污名（亦有臭名） | 汚名可用且常見修復框架 | 오명可用且偏正式 | ô danh 可用且偏正式 |

| 修復（洗刷/澄清） | 洗刷/澄清/除… | 汚名をそそぐ類 | 오명을 씻다 類 | rửa… 類；檢驗與漢越載體整合度 |

教學上的重點是：即便載體不同，三角仍可能成立。只要“不潔（程序性）—羞恥（暴露/面子）—污名（名聲物件）”這三種功能能互相借力並形成模板家族，就可稱之為區域同型；若只有一兩角強、另一角弱或另起載體，也同樣提供重要的類型學訊息。

### 8.3 最小可行語料方案：把比較做實

要讓這張矩陣從概念變成結果，最小方案是：各語建立小型但語域多樣的語料集合（經典/近代/現代視研究範圍而定），以一致指標檢驗：最早例證、模板生產力、語域分布、修復框架。路徑強弱可用頻率＋體裁分散度＋模板生產力做合成指標，而不必靠印象。

### 8.4 泰語壓力測試：在非漢字書寫下三角是否仍可重建？

泰語之所以適合做壓力測試，不是因為它“等同漢字圈”，而是因為它可能在某些領域接觸漢語社群與商業詞彙，卻不以漢字作主要書寫體系；同時其高語域宗教詞彙又深受巴利/梵語影響。這使它成為制度端三角的一個關鍵反問：在載體大幅不同的情況下，“不潔操作符—羞恥暴露—名聲污名物件”是否仍會以相似構式家族出現？

若能以不同詞料重建同樣的構式工作（例如“某狀態導致受限參與”“洗去惡名”“公開斥罵被框成污染性”“尷尬被建模成暴露”），那就支持機制層面的概括：制度與面子互動自然會生產相似模板。若不能重建，也同樣有啟發：這個三角可能依賴漢字圈特定的歷史包裝（文本傳統、複合詞工藝、官僚/禮制語類），屬於區域特殊的語言—制度共構結果。

因此，第八節把整體生態系收束為一套可檢驗的語言學計畫：不只描述哪些詞很髒，而是指出哪些模板必然出現、會住在哪些語域、會如何跨體裁遷移、以及哪些機制可跨語同型、哪些則受載體與制度史約束。
