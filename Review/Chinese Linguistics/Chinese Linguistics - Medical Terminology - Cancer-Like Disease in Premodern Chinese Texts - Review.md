### Summary

**What we covered:**  
This conversation built a research trail through **premodern Chinese medical and non-medical texts** to answer a deceptively modern question: *“Where do we see ‘cancer-like’ disease concepts, how were they named, and how did the vocabulary evolve across dynasties and genres?”* We started broad (Qin–Tang “any mention of cancer”) and then zoomed into specific **terminology systems** (瘤 / 癥瘕 / 積聚 vs 癌/巖/岩 + 翻花), **site-specific narratives** (乳、唇、舌、喉), and finally **character history and morphology** (癌 as a graph; radical choice; modifier ecology like 石癰/骨疽/血瘤/肉瘤).

**Major themes:**

1. **“Cancer” is not one ancient word—it’s an evolving semantic field.**  
	Early texts discuss lumps, swellings, sores, and internal masses using multiple systems:
- **內科-style “mass as accumulation”**: 積聚、癥瘕 (often classified by *movable vs fixed*, *form vs formless*).
- **外科-style “destructive lesion”**: 癰疽、惡瘡, later strongly associated with “岩穴/巖壑” imagery and end-stage events like ulceration, fetor, bleeding, and “翻花.”
1. **“Terminal” is encoded as prognosis language and event chains, not pathology.**  
	Because classical authors lacked histology, “late stage” appears as **fixed rhetorical and clinical markers**:
- Prognosis formulas: **百無一救 / 百無一生 / 四大不救 / 百必死**
- Systemic collapse: **五臟俱衰**, wasting: **形體日削**
- Local end-stage events: **臭血 / 汙水 / 腐爛 / 翻花 / 怒出血**  
	乳巖 in particular is heavily “terminal-coded” in canonical teaching texts, but other sites (舌、喉) also get terminal framing—often via **functional obstruction** (難食、難語) and explicit death outcomes (遂卒), even if the exact slogan differs.
1. **Two books became the anchor comparison: 《外科正宗》 vs 《醫宗金鑑》.**  
	We distinguished **“clinical staging-like” phrasing** (clear stepwise progression markers: initial nodule → pain → discoloration → ulceration → bleeding → collapse) from **rhetorical amplification** (absolute fatality claims; vivid metaphor like 岩穴/泛蓮). The take-home: **《醫宗金鑑》 reads more like a staged teaching algorithm; 《外科正宗》 mixes staging with moral/genre-driven warning rhetoric.**
2. **The graph “癌” is late, but its design is telling.**  
	We traced that 癌 is structurally **疒 + 嵒/喦/岩**:
- **疒** tags it as a disease category (nosological sorting).
- **嵒/岩** contributes sound and a “rock/cavern” image that matches classical descriptions like “上高下深、岩穴之狀、毒孔透裏.”  
	Modern pronunciation standardization (yán → ái in Putonghua) reflects **communication-driven norming** rather than original phonetic intent.
1. **Radical and modifier ecologies reveal how Chinese medical naming works.**  
	We saw that disease “head morphemes” overwhelmingly live in **疒**, while **石/肉/骨/血** often function as **qualifiers** attached to shared 疒-heads (石癰, 骨疽, 血瘤, 肉瘤). Genre matters: **內科 typologies** favor composition/appearance modifiers (血/肉/石 × 瘤/癭), while **外科/瘍科** favors behavior/depth modifiers (石/骨 × 癰/疽).

**Take-home messages:**

- Don’t force a 1:1 mapping from ancient labels to modern oncology; instead, track **narrative patterns + prognosis language + morphology metaphors**.
- “Cancer-like” discourse becomes clearest where texts standardize **end-stage event chains** and **categorical fatal prognosis**, especially in breast and oral/throat lesions.
- Character structure and word-formation patterns (radicals/modifiers) are not decorative—they encode how medical knowledge was categorized and taught.

## 一、部位延伸：鼻 / 齦 / 頰 的「終末標記」聚類

> 先講結論（可被文本推翻、但目前樣本支持）： **鼻/齦/頰** 在古典材料裡更常落在「局部破壞、出血、成漏、阻塞」語彙；而「五臟俱衰、四大不救、百人百必死」這種 **系統性崩解＋必死宣告** ，在你要比較的這組部位裡 **並不典型** ，更集中出現在（例如）乳部「乳巖」的敘事傳統中。

### 1) 鼻（bí）—以「阻塞/息肉」敘事更常見（非必然惡性，但符合“阻塞群”）

- **例證（鼻息肉、鼻塞）** ：  
	《先哲醫話》提到鼻中生「息肉」並以是否鼻塞作辨：
	> 「鼻中點滴連綿不止…甚者…鼻塞者，息肉也。」
	- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：鼻（bí）、息肉（xī ròu）、鼻塞（bí sè）
- **聚類判讀** ：偏 **阻塞/局部病變** （鼻塞、流涕、局部增生），而非「五臟俱衰」式終末語。

### 2) 齦（yín）—以「齦疽/出血」更常見（偏“出血/感染破壞群”）

- **例證（齦疽出血不止）** ：  
	《皇漢醫學》載：
	> 「走馬疳，齦疽，出血不止者…」
	- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：齦疽（yín jū）、出血不止（chū xuè bù zhǐ）、走馬疳（zǒu mǎ gān）
- **聚類判讀** ：偏 **出血/潰爛/感染性壞死** 語彙；可致重症甚至死亡，但不像乳巖傳統那樣用「五臟俱衰」定型描述。

### 3) 頰（jiá）—以「附骨、成漏、難愈」的局部破壞為核心（偏“局部侵蝕群”）

- **例證（頰疽＝附骨疽，久則成漏）** ：  
	《瘍醫大全》引述：
	> 「頰疽…亦名附骨疽…久則成漏。難愈。」
	- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：頰疽（jiá jū）、附骨疽（fù gǔ jū）、成漏（chéng lòu）、難愈（nán yù）
- **聚類判讀** ：偏 **局部侵蝕/骨旁破壞/瘻管** ，仍更像“局部毀損群”，不是典型「系統性崩解群」。

## 二、「翻花 / 反花」作為終末標記：最早出現與跨部位遷移

### A) 最早可核對用例：隋《諸病源候論》「反花瘡候」（約 614）

《諸病源候論》直接把「反花」定義成 **惡肉外翻如花** ：

> 「肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡。」

- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：反花瘡（fǎn huā chuāng）、惡肉（è ròu）

✅ **要點（事實層）** ：到隋代，「反花/翻花」已是 **瘡瘍晚期外翻肉芽** 的命名框架，且可泛用於「諸惡瘡久不瘥」。

### B) 明清外科系統化：翻花＝「瘡口努肉突出如菌、易出血」

《瘍科心得集》說得很“病理形態學”：

> 「瘡口肉突出如菌…揩損每流鮮血。」

- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：翻花瘡（fān huā chuāng）、突出如菌（tū chū rú jūn）、流鮮血（liú xiān xuè）

### C) 向「特定部位惡性」敘事靠攏：乳部（乳巖）吸收「翻花/泛蓮」作終末形容

《外科大成》在「乳巖」條下用「突如泛蓮」＋「百無一救」：

> 「突如泛蓮…百無一救。」

- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：乳巖（rǔ yán）、泛蓮（fàn lián）、百無一救（bǎi wú yī jiù）

### D) 口唇部位也吸收「翻花」：繭唇病例以「翻花瘤」收束於死亡

《口齒類要》病例：

> 「狀如翻花瘤而死。」

- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：翻花瘤（fān huā liú）

### E) 泌尿生殖部位也出現「腎巖翻花絕證」的搭配

《外科證治秘要》條文直接把「腎巖翻花」標作絕證：

> 「腎巖翻花絕證…」

- **關鍵詞 pinyin** ：腎巖（shèn yán）、翻花（fān huā）、絕證（jué zhèng）

## 2) 《瘍醫大全》轉引《外科正宗》時變成「百人百死」——更像「引文省略」而非版本差

《瘍醫大全》段落：

> 「凡犯此者，百人百死…（《正宗》）」

這裡少了「必」。但它自己明示是引《正宗》，而《正宗》多底本又穩定是「百人百必死」。因此更合理的判斷是： **後出書在轉引時做了口語化/省筆** ，不必然表示《正宗》版本分歧。

同段「乳巖」都有：

> 「…百無一救。」

我這次抓到的「百無一生」多出現在其他病證（例如脈症論述、咳證等），並非穩定地和「巖/癌」部位敘事綁定。

> 我不把它說成真理，只說成「目前文本最省力的解釋」。

### 500-word conversation summary

Across this conversation we treated “cancer” not as a single ancient diagnosis but as a **moving target in a premodern semantic ecosystem**. We began by asking whether Qin–Tang sources mention cancer-like conditions, then built a structured research program: (1) map the core disease terms (癰疽 / 瘤 / 積聚 / 癥瘕 / 惡肉 / 石癰 / 肉瘤), (2) locate “cancer narrative” language in Tang non-medical genres (墓誌銘、筆記、史書), (3) trace the entry and stabilization of **癌/嵒/岩/巖** vocabulary, (4) compare classic lump systems (積聚/癥瘕) with later surgical malignancy systems (巖/癌/翻花), and (5) build site maps (乳、唇、舌、喉—and later 鼻、齦、頰) to see how end-stage markers cluster.

A major theme was methodological: **terminal cancer in classical texts is encoded as prognosis and event chains**, not histology. We identified “end-stage” markers like **五臟俱衰、四大不救、百人百必死、百無一救、形體日削、臭血、翻花、怒出血**, and used these to distinguish “difficult but survivable lumps” from “categorically fatal/destructive lesions.” In particular, we compared **《外科正宗》** and **《醫宗金鑑》**: both narrate a natural history from small nodule to pain, discoloration, ulceration, bleeding, and collapse, but **《醫宗金鑑》 reads more like staged teaching/algorithmic triage**, while **《外科正宗》 mixes staging with stronger rhetorical warning and social-moral framing**. We also tested whether fatal slogans are stable across editions: “百人百必死” in 《外科正宗》 appeared consistent across multiple accessible witnesses, while later works sometimes quote it in shortened form, suggesting **citation drift** more than true textual variance.

Another theme was **site-specific ecology**. Breast (乳巖) tends to carry the densest package of systemic collapse + fatal certainty, whereas tongue/throat narratives often cluster with **functional obstruction** (難食、難語) and explicit death outcomes, and lip/gum/cheek sources frequently emphasize **local destruction** (bleeding, ulceration, fistula, bone involvement) rather than “五臟俱衰”-style formulas. Extending to 鼻/齦/頰 reinforced this: these sites more readily fall into obstruction/bleeding/local invasion vocabularies.

Finally, we zoomed out to **graph history and word formation**. 癌 is structurally **疒 + 嵒/巖**: 疒 tags “disease class,” while the rocky/cavern imagery supports the lesion’s described morphology. This led to a “radical ecology” study: **疒 dominates as disease-head radical**, while 石/肉/骨/血 more often act as **modifiers** attached to shared 疒-heads (石癰、骨疽、血瘤、肉瘤). Genre matters: **內科** favors accumulation typologies (積聚/癥瘕) and composition/appearance modifiers for lumps, while **外科/瘍科** favors depth/behavior modifiers (stone-hard, bone-involving, ulcerative, “翻花”) and terminal rhetoric.

**Take-home messages:** treat “cancer” as a historically layered semantic field; use prognosis/event markers rather than modern back-diagnosis; and pay attention to radicals/modifiers and genre, because they reveal how medical knowledge was categorized and taught.

## 1\. Introduction: Why “Cancer” Is a Moving Target in Premodern Chinese Texts

### 1.1 Problem statement and scope (Qin–Qing; medical + selected non-medical genres)

### 1.2 Key definitional stance: avoiding retrospective diagnosis

### 1.3 Review questions and contributions

**Section summary (≈340 words):**  
This review addresses a recurring methodological trap: treating “cancer” as a stable biomedical entity that can be cleanly mapped onto premodern Chinese disease names. Instead, we frame “cancer” as a **semantic field** spanning lumps, hard masses, ulcerative destructive lesions, and terminal wasting—described through the resources available to premodern writers: palpation, appearance, discharge, pain, functional obstruction, and prognostic rhetoric. We define the review’s scope as (i) canonical medical corpora from early classics through late imperial surgical compendia, and (ii) targeted non-medical genres (墓誌銘, 筆記, 史書) only insofar as they preserve symptom narratives that plausibly intersect with medical templates. The central claim is not “the ancients knew cancer,” but that **distinct lexical systems** emerged to organize chronic destructive lesions, with increasing attention to “late-stage” markers and fatal certainty in certain site-based categories.  
We pose three guiding questions. First, what were the major **terminology systems** for growths and destructive sores, and how did they divide labor (e.g., 瘤 vs 積聚/癥瘕 vs 癌/巖/翻花)? Second, how did “terminality” get encoded—through slogans (百無一救, 百必死), systemic collapse (五臟俱衰), or event chains (腐爛→臭血→翻花→出血)? Third, how did character formation and word-formation—radicals (疒 vs 肉/月) and modifiers (石-, 骨-, 血-, 肉-)—shape nosological classification?  
The section closes by stating what the review contributes: a reproducible framework for reading premodern evidence without smuggling in modern pathology, while still allowing rigorous comparison across dynasties, sites, and genres.

## 2\. Materials and Methods: Corpus Design, Extraction Rules, and Evidence Grading

### 2.1 Corpus selection by dynasty and genre (內科 vs 外科/瘍科; plus non-medical)

### 2.2 Lexical extraction: head terms, modifiers, and event markers

### 2.3 Evidence grading and uncertainty management

**Section summary (≈360 words):**  
This section defines a method that is strict enough to resist retrospective diagnosis yet flexible enough to track conceptual evolution. We propose a tiered corpus: (A) “core medical canon” (early classics and major nosological compendia), (B) “surgical/sore-specialist” texts that amplify ulcerative and terminal rhetoric, and (C) “adjacent narrative corpora” (epigraphy and miscellanies) that offer symptom sequences outside medical taxonomies. We split medical sources by genre because genre predicts vocabulary: 內科 discourse tends toward process nouns (積聚, 癥瘕) and internal constraint models, whereas 外科/瘍科 favors lesion morphology, discharge, and crisis markers (癰疽, 惡肉, 翻花).  
Extraction proceeds on three linked layers. Layer 1 is **head-term inventory**: the “named disease heads” (e.g., 瘤, 癰, 疽, 瘡, 癥, 瘕, 癭, 癌, 巖). Layer 2 is **modifier ecology**: productive qualifiers attached to shared heads (石癰, 骨疽, 血瘤, 肉瘤, 石癭). Layer 3 is **event-marker lexicon** used to encode progression and terminality: obstruction (難食, 難語), destructive morphology (腐爛, 潰, 惡肉), fetor/bleeding (臭血, 鮮血), exophytic “flowering” (翻花/反花), wasting (形體日削), and systemic collapse (五臟俱衰).  
We recommend evidence grading to keep claims proportional: Grade I (explicit prognosis formulas or death endpoints), Grade II (structured event chains compatible with terminal narratives), Grade III (ambiguous lump language without prognosis), and Grade IV (modern retrospective mapping only, used sparingly and labeled as hypothesis). Finally, we discuss reproducibility: concordance tables (term, dynasty, genre, site, markers), plus a variant/edition check protocol for high-impact slogans to separate textual variance from later quotation drift.

## 3\. The Pre-癌 Landscape: Growth and Mass Systems Before “Rock-Cave” Malignancy Language

### 3.1 Lumps as retention/concretion: 瘤 as “留結不散”

### 3.2 Accumulation and fixation: 積聚 and 癥瘕 as process categories

### 3.3 Suppurative/destructive sores: 癰疽 as an alternative pathway into “cancer-like” narratives

**Section summary (≈350 words):**  
Before 癌 becomes a stable headword in late imperial discourse, premodern Chinese medicine already had rich ways to speak about growths and masses. One major pathway is the **lump-as-retention** model, crystallized in definitions of 瘤 as a persistent, localized “staying” entity (留結不散). This is semantically different from malignant transformation: it captures chronicity and boundedness, and in some influential formulations carries a comparatively non-fatal expectation. A second pathway is the **accumulation/fixation** model: 積聚 and 癥瘕 frame masses as products of long-term internal dynamics—constraint, congealing fluids, or persistent pathogenic lodging—often classified by palpatory behavior (movable vs fixed) and locus (abdominal, gynecologic, etc.). These terms function less as “tumor labels” and more as **process nouns** that organize a wide range of internal masses, benign or lethal, without a built-in malignant criterion.  
A third pathway, often overlooked in modern retrodictions, is **sore/destruction discourse**. The 癰疽 tradition provides vocabulary for deep, ulcerative lesions characterized by “toxicity,” necrosis, discharge, and progressive tissue breakdown. This pathway becomes crucial for understanding why later malignant-like categories borrow heavily from external-surgery morphology: the language of腐爛, 潰, 臭, 血, and later 翻花 is already native to sore literature.  
This section argues that what later readers call “cancer” is frequently assembled from **overlapping older systems**: a mass framework (瘤, 癥瘕) plus a destructive sore framework (癰疽) plus increasingly explicit terminal rhetoric. The result is not a single lineage but a braided semantic history in which “growth” terms can remain non-terminal while “sore” terms carry the dramatic end-stage markers. The section sets up later chapters by clarifying the baseline ecology against which 癌/巖 vocabulary will appear as a re-sorting of existing clinical perceptions into sharper prognostic categories.

## 4\. Graph History and Radical Logic: 癌 as Morphological Argument (疒 + 嵒/巖)

### 4.1 When 癌 stabilizes in dictionaries and medical writing

### 4.2 Why 疒 wins: nosological tagging vs anatomical radicals (肉/月)

### 4.3 “Rock-cave” imagery: 嵒/巖 semantics and malignancy-style morphology

**Section summary (≈360 words):**  
This section treats the character 癌 as an artifact of medical categorization: it is not merely a label but an argument about what kind of thing the lesion is. We review lexicographic evidence that 癌 is absent from very early dictionary strata and becomes prominent later, alongside related graphs and definitions that emphasize “rocky/cavernous” morphology and deep-rooted penetration. The core structural point is that 癌 is built as **疒 + 嵒/巖-family component**: the sickness radical (疒) marks membership in the disease domain, while the phonetic/imagistic component contributes hardness and crater-like topography. This is consistent with classical descriptive formulas that portray cancer-like lesions as “up-high down-deep,” “cave-like,” and “rooted” (毒根深藏, 穿孔透裏).  
We then contrast **radical roles**. 肉/月 radicals tend to index body substances and anatomical entities; they are ideal for generic swelling (腫) or tissue terms but less suited for naming a disease kind intended to travel across sites and contexts. 疒, by contrast, is a stable classifier for disease heads across the medical lexicon (瘤, 癰, 疽, 瘡, 癥, 瘕, 癭). The formation of 癌 therefore follows the dominant morphological grammar of medical naming: category-tagging radical plus differentiating component.  
Finally, we discuss how “rock” imagery shifts from metaphor to nosology. Terms like 巖/岩 begin as shape metaphors but, in late imperial surgery, become integrated into structured disease categories (乳巖, 喉巖, 腎巖), often linked with late-stage markers (翻花, 出血, 臭, 不救). The section’s take-home message is that graph formation and radical choice encode a historical move from describing masses as “accumulations” toward describing certain lesions as **structurally malignant** in behavior—penetrating, ulcerating, and prognostically terminal—even before modern pathology.

## 5\. Terminality as Language: Slogans, System Collapse, and Event-Chain Markers

### 5.1 Prognostic slogans: 百無一救 / 百必死 and their rhetorical function

### 5.2 Systemic collapse: 五臟俱衰 and wasting (形體日削)

### 5.3 Late-stage morphology: 臭血, 翻花, 怒出血 as a canonical end-stage package

**Section summary (≈350 words):**  
Premodern texts rarely define malignancy by microscopic criteria; instead they define “end-stage” by **what happens next** and **whether anyone can be saved**. This section constructs a typology of terminality language. The first class is **slogan-like prognosis**: absolute formulas such as “百無一救” or “百人百必死” function as didactic compression. They demarcate a boundary between treatable disease space and “敗證/絕證” space, serving both clinical triage and rhetorical warning. Because slogans are high-impact, we treat them as priority targets for variant/edition checking: slight differences (“百死” vs “百必死”) may reflect quotation drift rather than true authorial variance.  
The second class is **systemic collapse and wasting**. Phrases like “五臟俱衰” and explicit wasting markers (形體日削) translate local disease into whole-body failure, approximating a premodern articulation of terminal cachexia and multi-organ decline. These markers are especially salient in late imperial surgical narratives of certain site lesions, where local ulceration is portrayed as consuming the body.  
The third class is **event-chain morphology**: a recurring sequence—discoloration, necrosis, ulceration, foul discharge, fetid blood (臭血), exophytic “flowering” (翻花/反花), and hemorrhage (怒出血). This chain is portable across lesion sites and helps explain how a general sore vocabulary (翻花 originally as a “bad sore” marker) migrates into specific “cancer-like” site categories.  
The section’s methodological takeaway is that terminality is best operationalized as a **marker bundle**, not a single phrase. A lesion narrative that co-activates foul bleeding + flowering + wasting + no-rescue prognosis is strongly terminal-coded, whereas a narrative limited to “lump present for years” is not. This marker-based approach allows careful cross-dynasty comparison without forcing modern diagnosis.

## 6\. Site-Based Nosology: Breast, Oral Cavity, Throat, and Beyond

### 6.1 Breast: 乳巖 as the densest terminal-coded template

### 6.2 Oral cavity and throat: 唇/舌/喉 clusters around obstruction (難食難語) + bleeding

### 6.3 Extension sites: 鼻/齦/頰 as obstruction/bleeding/local invasion ecologies

**Section summary (≈360 words):**  
Here we test whether “cancer-like” language behaves similarly across anatomical sites. Breast lesions (乳巖) emerge as the most densely terminal-coded template in many late imperial surgical texts: they commonly combine staged natural history (small nucleus → pain → discoloration → ulceration) with end-stage marker bundles (臭血, 翻花/泛蓮 imagery, hemorrhage) and systemic collapse formulas. This produces a stable pedagogical narrative: breast “巖” is not merely a lump but a destructive, fatal progression with limited therapeutic optimism.  
By contrast, oral cavity and throat labels—whether framed as 唇-related lesions, 舌疳/舌菌 traditions, or explicit 喉巖—often cluster around **functional obstruction** markers: difficulty speaking and eating (難語難食), plus local bleeding and fetid discharges. Terminality may be coded narratively (explicit death endpoints in case records) or via survival nihilism (“百無一生”) rather than “五臟俱衰.” This suggests that site ecology shapes which terminal markers are most salient: airway and oral function make obstruction-language unavoidable, whereas breast narratives readily expand into systemic metaphors of collapse and wasting.  
Extending to 鼻/齦/頰 further supports site clustering. Nasal narratives frequently emphasize obstruction (nasal blockage, polyp-like growths) and chronic discharge. Gingival/gum lesions commonly highlight uncontrolled bleeding and necrotic processes (especially in “gan/疳” traditions). Cheek lesions (頰疽) often frame progression as deep local invasion (e.g., bone-adjacent destruction, fistula formation), which is “terminal-like” in morbidity but not necessarily expressed in the systemic-collapse slogan style.  
The section argues for a site-sensitive reading strategy: rather than asking “Is this cancer?”, we ask “Which terminal marker bundles are activated for this site?” The result is a comparative nosology that can explain both convergence (shared end-stage morphology like 翻花) and divergence (obstruction vs systemic collapse) across sites.

## 7\. Modifier Ecology: How 石-, 骨-, 血-, 肉- Partition Disease Space

### 7.1 Modifier + 疒-head productivity (石癰, 骨疽, 血瘤, 肉瘤, 石癭)

### 7.2 What modifiers “mean” clinically: hardness, depth, composition, vascularity

### 7.3 Diachronic shifts: from occasional labels to systematic typologies

**Section summary (≈340 words):**  
Modifier ecology reveals a second layer of historical medical reasoning: not just what lesions are called, but how they are subclassified. We focus on four high-yield qualifiers—stone (石-), bone (骨-), blood (血-), meat/flesh (肉-)—and examine how they attach to shared disease heads with the sickness radical (疒). The pattern is not random. Stone overwhelmingly signals a **hardness phenotype** and often attaches to abscess-like heads (石癰) or to immovable mass categories (石癭, 石瘤). Bone preferentially attaches to deep ulcerative heads (骨疽, 附骨疽), indexing anatomical depth and destructive involvement. Blood attaches readily to lump heads (血瘤, 血癭), marking vascular appearance or bleeding tendency. Flesh/meat attaches to lump heads (肉瘤, 肉癭), marking tissue-like composition and softer morphology.  
We argue that this modifier system functions as a premodern, low-tech version of phenotyping: palpation, appearance, discharge, and perceived substrate become encoded as lexical structure. Diachronically, these compounds appear sporadically in early practical texts but become systematically enumerated in later encyclopedic and specialist contexts, suggesting a maturation of classification grids. Importantly, “石” largely behaves as a **modifier ecology** rather than a head ecology: authors usually do not coin new stone-radical disease heads but instead use stone as a qualifier on an established 疒-head, preserving disease-domain tagging while adding phenotype.  
The clinical payoff of modifier ecology is interpretive: it helps separate “hard fixed mass” discourse (stone) from “deep destructive sore” discourse (bone) and from “composition/vascularity” discourse (flesh/blood). This becomes crucial when tracking the semantic boundary between “lump that persists” and “lesion that destroys,” and when mapping how malignancy-like narratives co-opt sore terminology (翻花) while still relying on mass phenotypes (stone-hard) to signal danger.

## 8\. Genre Ecology: 內科 vs 外科/瘍科 and the Migration of Vocabularies

### 8.1 內科 process nouns: 積聚/癥瘕 and constraint/retention etiologies

### 8.2 外科 morphology and terminal rhetoric: 癰疽→翻花→巖/癌

### 8.3 “Contamination” and fusion: when systems cross-talk

**Section summary (≈350 words):**  
Genre is a major hidden variable in this literature. 內科 writing tends to organize disease by internal dynamics—constraint, accumulation, congealing fluids, and long-duration lodging—producing process nouns and palpation-based categories (積聚, 癥瘕). These labels are semantically capacious: they can house diverse conditions, including ones that modern readers might consider malignant, without marking malignancy as a distinct kind. Prognosis is often graded, but categorical fatal slogans are less central.  
外科/瘍科 writing, by contrast, is morphology-forward: it privileges what lesions look like, smell like, exude, and do to surrounding tissues. Here, the vocabulary of suppuration and destruction (癰疽, 瘡) provides a ready-made template for terminal narratives. “Flowering” language (翻花/反花), initially a general marker of bad ulcers with exophytic tissue and bleeding, becomes an end-stage signal that can migrate into site-based “巖/癌” categories. This genre more readily produces compressed prognostic formulas (“no rescue,” “must die”), partly because it teaches triage and cautions against dangerous interventions.  
The key analytic payoff is the ability to track **vocabulary migration**: when do internal mass systems begin to borrow external-surgery imagery (e.g., ulceration metaphors, “flowering” tissue), and when do sore systems borrow internal-mass concepts (e.g., “rootedness,” “fixedness”)? We propose operational tests for fusion: the co-occurrence of internal head terms (積聚/癥瘕) with external event markers (翻花, 臭血, 惡肉) within the same passage or subchapter; or the appearance of “巖/癌” labels in contexts previously dominated by process nouns.  
This section sets up the review’s central narrative: malignancy-like conceptualization in late imperial China is less a sudden discovery than a **genre-mediated reconfiguration** —older mass vocabularies and older sore vocabularies were always available; what changes is how often they are explicitly fused, how strongly they are terminal-coded, and how widely the fused templates travel across sites.

## 9\. Synthesis and Future Directions: From Lexical Systems to Historical Clinical Epistemology

### 9.1 A diachronic model: braided lineages (mass, sore, rock-cave malignancy)

### 9.2 Variant control: edition checks and quotation drift as a methodological necessity

### 9.3 Research agenda: corpora, quantitative concordances, and cross-genre triangulation

**Section summary (≈360 words):**  
The concluding synthesis proposes a braided model of historical development. One braid is the internal mass tradition (瘤, 積聚, 癥瘕), which provides durable tools for describing persistence, fixation, and bodily accumulation without requiring a malignant/benign dichotomy. Another braid is the sore/destruction tradition (癰疽, 惡肉, 翻花), which supplies event-chain language for necrosis, fetor, bleeding, and exophytic breakdown—features that modern readers associate with late-stage malignancy. A third braid is the “rock-cave” malignancy tradition (巖/癌), which re-sorts earlier perceptions into categories defined by rootedness, penetration, ulceration, and terminal rhetoric, and which becomes increasingly site-based in late imperial surgery.  
We emphasize methodological guardrails: variant/edition checking is not an optional flourish when dealing with high-impact prognosis slogans. Differences such as “百死” vs “百必死” may reflect later quotation drift, abridgement, or editorial smoothing rather than a genuine shift in clinical stance. The review therefore recommends a minimal critical apparatus for any claim built on categorical prognosis phrases: at least two independent witnesses (ideally one image-based scan) and explicit labeling of whether a phrase is authorial or quoted.  
Finally, we lay out a future research agenda that bridges philology and digital humanities without sacrificing interpretive caution. Priority projects include building dynasty- and genre-stratified concordances of end-stage marker bundles; running quantitative co-occurrence analyses to map how 翻花 migrates across sites and labels; and triangulating medical templates with non-medical narratives to identify when lay writing adopts medical terminal markers. The ultimate take-home message is epistemic: premodern Chinese medical authors were not doing modern oncology, but they were doing **structured risk recognition** —encoded in radicals, modifiers, site templates, and prognosis rhetoric. A careful lexical ecology approach can recover that structure and make cross-dynasty comparison possible without anachronism.

## 1\. Introduction: Why “Cancer” Is a Moving Target in Premodern Chinese Texts

The category “cancer” is a modern biomedical abstraction with diagnostic anchoring in histopathology, imaging, and population epidemiology. Premodern Chinese sources—medical and non-medical alike—operate with a different evidentiary economy: **palpable form**, **visible change**, **discharge/odor**, **pain**, **functional obstruction**, and, crucially, **prognostic certainty**. A review that simply equates 癌 with “cancer” and then retrofits earlier disease names into oncology is methodologically brittle; it confuses *lexical equivalence* with *pathological identity*. The more faithful approach is to treat “cancer-like disease” as a **semantic field** whose boundaries shift as genres, clinical priorities, and classificatory habits evolve.

Two early, enduring conceptual engines already exist long before 癌 becomes a stable headword. First is the **accumulation/retention engine**, in which masses emerge through prolonged lodging and congealing. In 《黃帝內經·靈樞》, a compact etiological chain links persistence to lump formation: “ **津液久留，合而為腸溜** ”. Whatever this describes in modern terms, the ancient logic is clear: time + retention → concreted form. Second is the **ulcerative/destructive engine** of sore literature (癰疽/惡瘡), which supplies a morphology-rich template for later malignant-coded narratives. In 《諸病源候論》, “flowering” tissue is already defined as a late-stage morphology of bad sores: “ **肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡** ”. This matters because 翻花/反花 later becomes one of the most portable end-stage markers across sites.

By late imperial surgery, some site-based entities are narrated with an explicit terminal register: not just a lesion that persists, but one that **predictably progresses to systemic failure**. In 《外科正宗》, breast “rock-cave” disease is framed via systemic collapse and categorical fatality: “ **其時五臟俱衰，四大不救** ” and “ **凡犯此者，百人百必死** ”. This sort of language is not a trivial flourish; it is a nosological operator that partitions disease space into treatable vs “敗證/絕證.”

Character formation itself encodes this historical re-sorting. 《說文解字》 does not record 癌; the later lexicographic tradition states bluntly: “ **「癌」《說文》不錄** ” and analyzes it as a sickness-tagged graph with a “rock” component: “ **「疒」下作「嵒」** ”. In other words, 癌 is not merely a label for a thing; it is an **argument** that certain “rocky/cavernous” lesions belong to the disease domain (疒) as a distinct kind, rather than remaining absorbed into broader lump vocabularies.

### Operational constructs used in this review

| Construct | What counts as evidence | Why it helps avoid anachronism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mass-field terms** (瘤, 積聚, 癥瘕) | definitions, etiologies, palpation descriptors | captures “growth” without forcing malignancy |
| **Destructive-sore terms** (癰疽, 惡瘡, 反花/翻花) | ulceration, fetor, bleeding, exophytic tissue | tracks late-stage morphology as described |
| **Terminal coding** | slogans (必死/不救), systemic collapse (五臟俱衰), obstruction (難食難語) | models “end-stage” as language + event chains |

This review therefore treats “cancer-like” not as a single ancient diagnosis but as a **convergence zone**: (i) mass concepts that explain persistence, (ii) sore concepts that explain destruction, and (iii) terminal rhetoric that encodes irreversible trajectory. The goal is not to “discover modern cancer” in ancient texts, but to reconstruct how premodern writers **recognized, named, and forecast** the worst kinds of growths and ulcers using the tools their epistemic world made available.

## 2\. Materials and Methods: Corpus Design, Extraction Rules, and Evidence Control

### 2.1 Corpus architecture and genre stratification

This review treats “cancer-like” as a **diachronic semantic field**, so the corpus must be designed to preserve (i) time, (ii) genre, and (iii) site. We therefore sample texts along two orthogonal axes:

1. **Dynasty/time (Han → Sui/Tang → Ming/Qing):** to track when certain labels (e.g., 癌/巖) and end-stage marker bundles become salient.
2. **Genre (內科 vs 外科/瘍科 vs narrative-adjacent):** because genre predicts lexical choice. 內科 discourse preferentially organizes mass phenomena through process nouns (e.g., 積聚/癥瘕), while 外科/瘍科 foregrounds morphology, discharge, and “catastrophe coding” (e.g., 翻花、臭血、必死). This is treated as a design feature, not noise.

The core medical stratum includes: **黃帝內經** (especially **靈樞**), **諸病源候論**, and specialist late-imperial surgical texts such as **外科正宗**. For graph-history constraints, we consult **教育部《異體字字典》** for structure and attestation notes on 癌.

### 2.2 Lexical extraction: heads, modifiers, and end-stage markers

Extraction proceeds in three linked layers.

**Layer A: head-term inventory (disease “heads”).** We treat characters functioning as nosological heads as the primary units (e.g., 瘤, 癰, 疽, 瘡, 癥, 瘕, 癭, 癌, 巖). This choice is justified by the radical grammar of medical naming: 癌 is explicitly analyzed as **疒 + 嵒**, and the dictionary notes it is not recorded in *Shuowen* (“「癌」《說文》不錄”)—a signal that the graph is a later disease-tagged construction rather than an ancient common noun.

**Layer B: modifier ecology (X-head compounds).** We register productive qualifiers that attach to shared heads (石-, 骨-, 血-, 肉-), because they partition lesion space by phenotype (hardness, depth, bleeding tendency, fleshy composition) without requiring modern pathology. In analysis, modifiers are treated as **typed edges** (modifier → head) enabling cross-dynasty comparison of classificatory habits.

**Layer C: end-stage marker lexicon (event chains + prognosis).** We operationalize “terminal coding” via marker bundles rather than retrospective diagnosis. Two canonical anchors illustrate this logic: (i) “flowering” tissue as a late-stage sore morphology is explicitly defined in **《諸病源候論》**: “肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡,” establishing 翻花/反花 as a portable morphological marker. (ii) categorical terminality and systemic collapse appear in **《外科正宗》** breast “巖” discourse: “五臟俱衰，四大不救…百人百必死,” justifying the inclusion of slogans and collapse language as high-grade end-stage operators.

### 2.3 Evidence grading, variant control, and reproducibility

Because high-impact phrases are prone to quotation drift, we grade evidence and enforce a minimal variant-check protocol.

| Evidence grade | Operational criterion | Example of admissible signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I (explicit terminality)** | categorical prognosis or systemic-collapse clause | “四大不救 / 百必死 / 五臟俱衰” |
| **II (end-stage event chain)** | ≥2 destructive morphology markers co-occur | 翻花/反花 + bleeding/fetor/ulceration |
| **III (mass without terminal coding)** | lump/process language lacking prognosis | retention/concretion narratives in classics |
| **IV (hypothesized mapping)** | modern inference only; must be labeled | used only as discussion, not as “proof” |

Variant control: any Grade I slogan must be witnessed in **≥2 independent representations** (e.g., a curated transcription plus a separate repository) before it is treated as stable. Reproducibility is ensured by a concordance table schema (term, dynasty, genre, site, markers, citation locus), enabling re-extraction and dispute resolution without appealing to modern diagnostic authority.

## 3\. The Pre-癌 Landscape: Growth and Mass Systems Before “Rock-Cave” Malignancy Language

### 3.1 Retention and concreted form: 瘤 as a “staying” entity

A striking feature of early and medieval nosology is that “lump” language often encodes **persistence** rather than “malignancy.” In the canonical etiological grammar of 《黃帝內經》 —particularly the 《靈樞》 —mass formation is described as a time-dependent consequence of lodging and congealing: “ **津液久留，合而為腸溜** ” and, with chronicity, “ **久者，數歲乃成**.” The text then explicitly transitions from “溜” to “瘤,” linking palpation to categorical recognition: “ **連以聚居，為昔瘤……以手按之堅**.” The semiotic point is not microscopic “tumor biology,” but a tactile-phenomenological threshold: a persistent, localized form that hardens over years becomes thinkable as a named kind.

The Sui nosological tradition renders this “staying” logic as definition. In 《諸病源候論》, the definition is programmatically semantic: “ **瘤者……言留結不散，謂之為瘤**.” This is not a description of invasion or metastasis; it is a claim about **failure to disperse** —a classification that can include many modern entities (benign and malignant alike) but, in its own terms, is organized around persistence, boundedness, and dispersion-resistance. That internal logic becomes important later when “cancer-like” discourse sharpens: the semantic field already has a stable “lump” pole.

### 3.2 Process nouns and internal dynamics: 積聚／癥瘕 as mass-making engines

Alongside “lump” heads, classical and medieval medicine develops a second system built around **process nouns** —not “a lump,” but “accumulation and concretion.” Terms such as 積聚 and 癥瘕 encode a physiology of **aggregation** and **fixation**. The analytic advantage of this system is its flexibility: it can subsume abdominal, gynecologic, and constitutional mass phenomena under a common causal grammar (constraint, lodging, congealing), without demanding a stable external morphology like ulceration or fetor.

This is where genre begins to matter. 內科 discourse tends to treat masses as the emergent products of internal dynamics; what matters is whether a phenomenon behaves like “accumulation” (gradual, often deep, sometimes intermittent) or “fixed concretion” (stubborn, resistant, relatively immobile). Even when pain or wasting appears, it is often described as downstream consequence rather than a defining nosological feature. As a result, 積聚／癥瘕 can remain semantically “agnostic” with respect to terminality: they name a process-space that may be compatible with recovery, chronicity, or death depending on context.

### 3.3 Ulcerative destruction as an alternative lineage: 癰疽 and “flowering” morphology

A third pre-癌 lineage is not mass-centered but **destruction-centered**: the sore/ulcer tradition (癰疽, 惡瘡) that foregrounds suppuration, necrosis, discharge, and crisis dynamics. Crucially, this lineage supplies the morphological vocabulary that later becomes diagnostic of terminal “cancer narratives.” The Sui text defines “flowering” tissue as a named late-stage configuration: “ **肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡**.” Here, “flowering” is not yet site-bound to breast or oral disease; it is a **portable end-stage morphology** within a sore ecology.

Taken together, these three systems—(i) “staying lumps” (瘤), (ii) “accumulation/fixation processes” (積聚／癥瘕), and (iii) “ulcerative destruction” (癰疽/反花)—constitute the pre-癌 conceptual substrate onto which later “rock-cave” malignancy language is grafted. They do not converge automatically; convergence requires later texts to fuse mass semantics with destructive morphology and, eventually, categorical prognosis.

| System (head terms) | Primary organizing idea | Typical descriptive signals | “Terminal” coding tendency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 瘤 (liú) | persistence / non-dispersion | “留結不散,” hardness on palpation | low unless paired with destructive markers |
| 積聚／癥瘕 | process of accumulation/fixation | gradual formation; internal dynamics | variable; often non-categorical (genre-dependent) |
| 癰疽／反花 | destructive sore morphology | ulceration, necrosis, “flesh like a flower” | higher via event-chains rather than slogans |

## 4\. Graph History and Radical Logic: 「癌」 as a Morphological Argument (疒 + 嵒/巖)

### 4.1 From “rock-like lesions” to a disease-head graph: why 疒 matters

The late-imperial stabilization of 「癌」 (ái / yán) should be read less as the sudden birth of a new disease phenomenon than as the **re-coding of an existing descriptive tradition into a standardized disease-head**. The Ministry of Education’s variant dictionary is unusually explicit about the graph’s design: 「癌」 is built with the sickness classifier **疒** and an internal component written as 「嵒」 (“「疒」下作「嵒」…上作「品」，下作「山」”). It also flags a crucial lexicographic discontinuity: “「癌」《說文》不錄.”

This is not just paleographic trivia. In medical word-formation, **疒 functions as a categorical “this is a disease kind” tag**, allowing the same head term to travel across anatomical sites (e.g., 肝癌、胃癌、鼻咽癌) without needing to be re-derived as a body-part noun. The same source further notes that, judged by component, the “proper” reading aligns with **yán** (matching 嵒/巖), while **ái** is treated as a later “also-read” introduced to disambiguate from 「炎」 in modern usage: “此字从「嵒」，當音 ㄧㄢˊ…今亦讀 ㄞˊ.”  
**Graph formation, then, encodes a claim**: what was once describable as “rocky/cavernous lesion morphology” becomes legible as a stable disease category.

### 4.2 The older lexicographic anchor: 「𤸔」 in Kangxi and the “rock-cave” phenotype

Even when the exact modern graph 「癌」 is not the primary witness, the descriptive core is already present in earlier lexicographic strata under variant graphs. *Kangxi* records 「𤸔」 (pǐn) as a sore-type entity with a strikingly “architectural” morphology: “𤸔瘡，上高下深…毒孔透裏.” The passage continues with a concrete topical remedy and cross-references to pharmacopeial/recipe traditions, which is revealing: the lexicographer is not defining an abstract entity, but indexing a **recognizable lesion phenotype** and its therapeutic lore.

Two points follow. First, “上高下深” (raised surface + deep base) and “毒孔透裏” (toxic channels penetrating inward) are precisely the kind of **depth/invasion metaphors** that later become diagnostic of “malignant-coded” lesions in surgical writing. Second, the fact that this description sits under a **疒-head graph** again underscores that the tradition already leans toward disease-kind classification, even before the orthography converges on 「癌」.

### 4.3 Orthographic convergence: 癌 / 嵒 / 巖 as an ecology of graphs rather than a single etymon

It is therefore methodologically safer to speak of a **graph ecology** —「癌」, 「嵒/喦」, 「巖/岩」, and lexicographic 「𤸔」—that circles a shared descriptive nucleus (hardness, irregular surface, cave-like depth, inward penetration) than to insist on a single linear origin. The MOE entry’s form note (“疒下作嵒”) effectively documents a later moment of orthographic rationalization: the “rock/cave” imagery becomes internalized into the disease-head itself. Meanwhile, *Kangxi* ’s 「𤸔」 preserves a parallel track where the same phenotype is lexicalized without the now-standard 癌 graph.

#### Table 4.1. A minimal graph ecology for “malignant-coded” lesions (orthography ↔ semantics)

| Graph / family | Head radical | Internal cue (phonetic/imagery) | Core descriptive payload (as attested) | What it enables nosologically |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 「癌」 (ái / yán) | 疒 | 「嵒」 (rock/cave component) | “《說文》不錄”; “疒下作嵒”; yán as component-reading, ái as later also-read | Stable disease head usable across sites (肝癌…); explicit disease-category tagging |
| 「𤸔」 (pǐn) | 疒 | (graph-specific; *Kangxi* entry) | “上高下深…毒孔透裏” | Preserves phenotype in lexicographic tradition even without standard 癌 graph |
| 「嵒/巖/岩」 family | 山 (in the component ecology) | rock/cave imagery | (functions as the imagistic/phonetic base for 癌’s internal component) | Supplies the metaphorical morphology that later gets “medicalized” via 疒 |

What emerges is a historically specific mechanism: **malignancy-like behavior is first stabilized as a *morphology narrative* (hard, irregular, deep, penetrating)**; later, that narrative is compressed into graph structure through the productive medical grammar “疒 + differentiating component.” In this sense, 「癌」 is not merely a word that labels an already-fixed disease; it is a *late graphic crystallization* of a long-running descriptive logic.

## 5\. Terminality as Language: Slogans, System Collapse, and Event-Chain Markers

### 5.1 Prognostic slogans as nosological “hard stops”

If Section 4 argued that **癌** crystallizes a morphology into a disease-head graph (疒 + 嵒/巖), the complementary move in late imperial writing is the crystallization of **trajectory** into **categorical prognosis**. In 《外科正宗》, the breast “rock-cave” lesion is narrated through a staged morphology and then abruptly sealed with an absolute boundary marker: after describing progressive enlargement, discoloration, ulceration, and fetid bleeding, the text declares “其時五臟俱衰，四大不救…名曰乳岩。凡犯此者，百人百必死。” This is not merely emphatic style. It functions as a **classification operator**: once the lesion is placed on the “no-rescue” side of the boundary, therapeutic aims shift from cure to temporization (the text even frames treatment as “只可苟延歲月”).

Two methodological consequences follow. First, such slogans are high-value evidence for “terminal coding” because they explicitly declare irreversibility. Second, they are also high-risk evidence because later works may quote or compress them; thus, variant control matters. In our accessible witnesses, the slogan set in 《外科正宗》 is stable in at least one independent transcription stream.

### 5.2 System-collapse language: translating local lesions into whole-body failure

Terminality is also encoded by **systemic collapse** vocabulary that makes the lesion’s local morphology rhetorically inseparable from whole-body decline. The same 《外科正宗》 passage uses “五臟俱衰” as a bridge: the local signs (ulceration, fetid blood) become sufficient to infer total physiological failure. This differs from many 內科 discussions, where “mass” terminology (積聚/癥瘕) can remain process-oriented without categorical fatality language. In other words, “system collapse” phrases are not generic moral panic; they are **late-stage inference rules** within a surgical epistemology that privileges visible progression and crisis events.

A useful contrast appears in 《醫宗金鑑》, where terminality can be framed less as immediate systemic collapse and more as a recognized threshold into “inverse” (逆) disease course. In its “繭唇” entry, the text ties severity to function (obstructed eating) and then designates ulceration with “flowering” features as a turning point: “若潰後如翻花，時津血水者屬逆…久則變為…屬凶。” Here, “凶/逆” performs boundary work similar to “不救/必死,” but with a different rhetorical temperature and often a more conditional logic.

### 5.3 Event-chain markers: “flowering,” bleeding, and the portability of late-stage morphology

The most transportable terminality signals are not slogans but **event-chain morphology** —a reusable diagnostic module that migrates across diseases and sites. The Sui nosological tradition already defines “flowering” tissue as a recognizable end-stage configuration of bad sores: in 《諸病源候論》, “反花瘡” is explicitly named because “肉反散如花狀.” This is crucial: “flowering” begins as a **general late-stage sore morphology**, not as a site-specific cancer label. Later specialist texts refine it into clinically vivid descriptors. 《瘍科心得集》 describes “翻花瘡” as protruding tissue with bleeding and wasting effects: “瘡口肉突出如菌…揩損每流鮮血，久亦虛人,” and warns that injudicious procedures can worsen the course and precipitate relentless hemorrhage and systemic symptoms.

Taken together, these sources justify treating terminality as a **bundle** rather than a single word: “flowering” morphology + bleeding + chronic non-healing + wasting/system-collapse framing can be compared across dynasties without assuming modern pathology.

| Terminal coding type | Linguistic form | Primary function | Textual anchor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prognostic slogan | “不救 / 必死” | categorical boundary (irreversibility) | 《外科正宗》乳岩 |
| System-collapse clause | “五臟俱衰” | whole-body failure inference | 《外科正宗》乳岩 |
| Event-chain morphology | “反花/翻花” + bleeding | portable late-stage phenotype | 《諸病源候論》; 《瘍科心得集》 |
| Threshold coding | “屬逆 / 屬凶” | stage shift without absolute slogan | 《醫宗金鑑》繭唇 |

## 6\. Site-Based Nosology: How “Terminal Marker Bundles” Cluster by Anatomical Locale

### 6.1 Breast as the paradigm of “system-collapse terminality” in 《外科正宗》

Late-imperial surgical writing does not merely *name* breast “rock-cave” disease; it **stages** it and then **locks** it with systemic terminality. The breast passage in 《外科正宗》 is exemplary precisely because it fuses (i) slow enlargement (“半年一年…二載三載”), (ii) late destructive morphology (“紫色氣穢…潰爛…深者如巖穴，凸者若泛蓮”), (iii) fetid bleeding (“出血則臭”), and (iv) a categorical whole-body endpoint: “其時五臟俱衰，四大不救…凡犯此者，百人百必死。”  
What matters for site-ecology is that **the breast template recruits systemic-collapse language as the decisive terminal operator**. The lesion’s local surface events are treated as sufficient warrant for inferring body-wide failure, and the passage’s therapeutic stance follows: “只可苟延歲月.” This creates an unusually “hard” terminal boundary compared with many lump vocabularies (瘤/癥瘕), where prognosis is often graded rather than sealed.

### 6.2 Lip, tongue, and throat: obstruction and “inverse-course” thresholds in 《醫宗金鑑》 and 《醫門補要》

In oral and laryngeal sites, terminality is frequently encoded less as immediate system-collapse and more as **functional loss + stage-crossing**. 《醫宗金鑑》 frames “繭唇” by pairing morphology with function (“妨礙飲食”) and then treats “flowering” ulceration as a turning point: “若潰後如翻花，時津血水者，屬逆…久則變為…屬凶。” Here, “逆/凶” behaves like a terminal gate—less absolute than “百必死,” but still a categorical shift in trajectory once “翻花 + sero-bloody flow” appears.

The case-writing mode in 《醫門補要》 makes the obstruction logic even sharper: a throat lesion “狀似雞冠…攪塞要路，惟進米飲…症名喉巖,” followed by relapse with “難語難食，時流鮮血…未久遂卒。”  
Two site-specific regularities are visible in these texts: (i) **obstruction markers** (難語/難食/惟進米飲) become central severity signals because they track airway/oral function directly; (ii) **bleeding** appears as a late marker (“時流鮮血”) that upgrades urgency without necessarily invoking “五臟俱衰.”

Lip discourse also provides a clean example of the “flowering” module being absorbed into site narratives. In 《口齒類要》, an explicitly terminal outcome is attached to a “flowering tumor” descriptor: “彼執用前藥，狀如翻花瘤而死。” The key point is not etiological correctness, but how **翻花** functions as an end-stage phenotype that can terminate the narrative with death.

### 6.3 Extension sites: nose, gum, and cheek as “obstruction/bleeding/local invasion” ecologies

When the same marker framework is applied to non-classic “巖” sites, the dominant signals shift. 《先哲醫話》 distinguishes nasal polyp pathology via obstruction as the defining discriminator: “又似此證而鼻塞者，息肉也…漸逼鼻口…此證雖相似，以鼻塞與不塞為辨。”  
For gums, 《皇漢醫學》 provides an intensity marker keyed to hemorrhage: “走馬疳，齦疽，出血不止者…”  
For cheek-adjacent deep lesions, the “bone-attachment” axis dominates: 《外科精義》 defines 附骨疽 by depth and marrow-level pain: “毒氣深沉，附著於骨…痛深徹骨髓。”  
Across these sites, terminality is often legible as **obstruction, uncontrolled bleeding, and depth/invasion metaphors**, even when the canonical “system-collapse slogan” is absent.

| Site cluster | Dominant terminal operators | Representative textual signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breast | system-collapse + categorical fatality | “五臟俱衰，四大不救…百人百必死” |
| Lip/oral/throat | obstruction + threshold coding + bleeding | “妨礙飲食…屬逆…屬凶”; “難語難食…流鮮血…遂卒” |
| Nose/gum/cheek | obstruction / hemorrhage / depth-to-bone | “以鼻塞與不塞為辨”; “出血不止”; “痛深徹骨髓” |

## 7\. Modifier Ecology: How 石-, 骨-, 血-, 肉- Partition Disease Space Across Shared 疒-Heads

### 7.1 Modifiers as “phenotype switches” on stable disease heads

Once disease entities are stabilized as heads (癰/疽/瘤/癭/瘡), late-medieval and late-imperial writing repeatedly uses short modifiers to encode what is, in effect, a **phenotype claim** —hardness, depth, bleeding tendency, or tissue-likeness—without inventing a new head term. A paradigmatic taxonomy appears in 《普濟方》, which explicitly enumerates “five goiters” and “six tumors” by modifier class: “癭有五種、瘤有六證…石癭…肉癭…筋癭…血癭…氣癭…六瘤者：骨瘤、脂瘤、血瘤、石瘤、膿瘤、肉瘤….” The list is not casual vocabulary; it is an **index of classificatory legitimacy**: these modifier–head pairings are treated as standard types worth separate formulaic management.

Two observations follow from the structure of the list. First, the same modifier can attach to different heads (石癭 and 石瘤), indicating that “stone” is a cross-cutting phenotype rather than a site-locked diagnosis. Second, the same head accepts multiple modifiers (瘤: 骨/脂/血/石/膿/肉), suggesting a deliberate effort to partition the lump space by **substrate and behavior** rather than by a single etiologic story.

### 7.2 Stone and bone: hardness and depth as the surgical axis (石癰, 附骨疽)

In practical therapy literature, “stone” appears most prominently as a modifier for abscess-like swellings. 《外臺祕要方》 literally organizes treatment under the rubric “石癰方五首,” anchoring stone-hardness as a type-level discriminator rather than an incidental description. The older emergency-formulary tradition makes the phenotype even more explicit. In 《葛仙翁肘後備急方》, stone-typing is defined by palpation and persistence—“癰結腫堅如石…或作石癰不消…若發腫至堅而有根者，名曰石癰”—and is paired with procedural cautions about aggressive intervention: “癰、疽、瘤、石癰…皆不可就針角….” Here, the modifier does triple duty: it characterizes morphology (hard), implies trajectory (non-resolving/rooted), and shapes permissible technique (do not needle casually).

“Bone” behaves differently. Rather than indicating hardness alone, 骨- tends to encode **depth and attachment**, clustering with the ulcerative/destructive head 疽 (骨疽/附骨疽) and the clinical imagination of pain “into the bone.” Even when the term appears inside a lump taxonomy (骨瘤 in 《普濟方》), its most productive ecological niche is in sore-surgery discourse where “bone involvement” signals chronicity, fistulization, and difficult healing—features that later overlap with “terminal marker bundles” discussed in Section 6.

### 7.3 Blood and flesh: vascularity and tissue-likeness as the internal-lump axis (血瘤, 肉瘤)

If stone/bone modifiers dominate the surgical axis, blood/flesh modifiers dominate the lump-goiter axis. In the same 《普濟方》 enumeration, 血癭/血瘤 and 肉癭/肉瘤 sit beside each other as parallel phenotype claims—one invoking blood-associated appearance/bleeding propensity, the other invoking flesh-like substance and texture. The notable point is what the system does *not* require: it does not require a unique causal narrative for each subtype. The modifier itself is treated as sufficient to justify a different clinical handling pathway (formulas, cautions, prognosis rhetoric).

The same logic helps explain why “flowering” (翻花/反花) can migrate across sites without becoming a head: it is, in effect, an **event-state modifier** on a sore ecology. In 《瘍科心得集》, “翻花瘡” is defined by a protruding, vascular, easily bleeding phenotype—“瘡口肉突出如菌…揩損每流鮮血，久亦虛人”—linking local morphology (exophytic tissue, bleeding) to systemic depletion. This is modifier ecology operating at the level of progression: not “what it is” as a head, but “what it has become” as an end-stage configuration.

| Modifier | Preferred head ecology    | Encoded phenotype claim                   | Textual anchor      |
| -------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| 石-       | 癰, 癭/瘤                    | hardness, rooted/non-resolving            | “石癰…至堅而有根”; “石癰方五首” |
| 骨-       | 疽 (and sometimes 瘤 lists) | depth/attachment, difficult healing       | “骨瘤…” list          |
| 血-       | 癭/瘤                       | vascularity/bleeding-associated phenotype | “血癭…血瘤…”            |
| 肉-       | 癭/瘤                       | flesh-like substance/texture              | “肉癭…肉瘤…”            |

## 8\. Genre Ecology and Vocabulary Migration: 內科 Process Nouns vs 外科/瘍科 Morphology, and Their Fusion Zones

### 8.1 內科 discourse: process nouns, temporality, and “mass as accumulation”

In 內科-style writing, the decisive explanatory unit is often **a process**, not a lesion object. The point is not “what the growth is,” but *how* something becomes persistent and palpable through duration and retention. A representative physiological grammar appears in 《黃帝內經》, where long residence of fluids is sufficient to yield concreted form: “津液久留，合而為腸溜.” Even when the passage is not about “tumors” in any modern sense, it exemplifies an epistemic posture: chronicity + retention → morphological consequence. The mass vocabulary that later becomes central (積聚/癥瘕) inherits this stance: it is structurally agnostic about “benign vs malignant,” and instead partitions disease space by **fixation, mobility, depth, and duration**. Terminality is possible, but it tends to be inferred contextually rather than asserted as a categorical boundary.

### 8.2 外科/瘍科 discourse: visible morphology, crisis events, and categorical terminality

外科/瘍科 writing re-weights the evidence. It privileges **surface events** (ulceration, fetor, bleeding), “what happens next,” and a clinical pedagogy of thresholds. In 《諸病源候論》, “flowering” tissue is defined as a morphology-state of bad sores: “肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡,” with explicit linkage to chronic non-healing. This establishes 翻花/反花 not as a site-specific cancer word, but as a **portable late-stage phenotype** within a sore ecology.

Late imperial surgery then adds a second operator: **categorical prognosis**. 《外科正宗》 does not merely describe progression; it seals it with systemic collapse and an irreversibility slogan—“五臟俱衰，四大不救…百人百必死.” The functional role of such language is to create a hard nosological boundary: once a lesion is placed on the “不救/必死” side, treatment goals are explicitly downgraded (“只可苟延歲月”).

A parallel, slightly cooler threshold vocabulary appears in 《醫宗金鑑》, where ulceration “like flowering” is staged as a turning point: “若潰後如翻花…屬逆…屬凶,” while functional obstruction (“妨礙飲食”) is used as a severity pivot. The genre difference is not simply “internal vs external medicine,” but **object/process vs event/threshold**, and **graded prognosis vs categorical boundary-making**.

### 8.3 Fusion and cross-talk: typologies, cautions, and migration tests

The fusion zones are where vocabularies migrate: mass discourse imports sore morphology (ulceration/flowering), while sore discourse imports mass phenotypes (hardness/rootedness). 《普濟方》 makes the typological impulse explicit by listing modifier–head partitions across lumps/goiters: “石癭…肉癭…血癭…六瘤者：骨瘤、脂瘤、血瘤、石瘤、膿瘤、肉瘤.” This is classification as infrastructure: it creates interoperable slots into which later “terminal marker bundles” can be plugged.

Meanwhile, 《肘後備急方》 shows how phenotype and technique are bound together: “若發腫至堅而有根者，名曰石癰,” followed by a procedural prohibition: “癰，疽，瘤，石癰…皆不可就針角.” These cautions are an early form of risk stratification: hardness/rootedness pushes a lesion toward a high-risk handling category, even without later “癌/巖” terminology.

| Dimension              | 內科-leaning signature                       | 外科/瘍科-leaning signature                                  | Migration cue (fusion)                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary unit           | process noun (accumulation/retention)      | morphology + event chain (ulceration/bleeding/flowering) | 積聚/癥瘕 passages co-activate 翻花/臭血 markers                  |
| Prognosis style        | graded, context-dependent                  | threshold-coded (“逆/凶”) or categorical (“不救/必死”)         | hard slogans appear in formerly process-dominant contexts |
| Classification grammar | fewer fixed subtypes; etiologic narratives | typologies + technique rules (e.g., 石癰禁針)                | modifier ecologies become shared across genres            |

## 9\. Synthesis and Research Agenda: From Genre Ecology to Evidence-Governed Historical Inference

### 9.1 A braided model of semantic development: process → phenotype → terminal boundary

The genre ecology described in Section 8 implies a non-linear history: “cancer-like” meaning emerges not by replacement of one lexicon by another, but by **braiding** older explanatory engines into new boundary-making packages. One braid is the **process grammar** of internal medicine, where temporality and retention are sufficient causal primitives. In 《黃帝內經》, the logic is explicit: “津液久留，合而為腸溜…久者，數歲乃成…連以聚居，為昔瘤。” Here, “mass” is an endpoint of time + lodging; malignancy is not a required category.

A second braid is the **phenotype grammar** of sore literature, where morphology-state becomes nameable. In 《諸病源候論》, “flowering” tissue is defined by a specific configuration: “肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡。” This is crucial because “flowering” begins as a portable end-stage phenotype before it is recruited into site-based malignancy discourse.

A third braid is late surgical writing’s **terminal boundary language**, where prognosis is compressed into categorical hard stops. In 《外科正宗》, breast “rock-cave” disease is sealed by systemic collapse and irreversibility: “其時五臟俱衰，四大不救…凡犯此者，百人百必死…只可苟延歲月。” Together these braids explain why “癌/巖” cannot be read as a simple synonym for “tumor”: the semantic field is assembled from (i) process-noun mass formation, (ii) phenotype-state markers (翻花/反花), and (iii) boundary-making terminal rhetoric.

### 9.2 Evidence governance: variant control, quotation drift, and claim–evidence proportionality

Because late texts often circulate through excerpting and reprinting, the review adopts an evidence-governed posture: **high-impact claims require high-control witnesses**. This is especially true for fatal slogans (“必死/不救”), which are both interpretively powerful and transmission-prone. A minimal control standard is justified by the way quotation drift can silently soften or sharpen prognosis without changing the underlying clinical narrative.

The same logic applies to graph-history claims. The MOE variant dictionary’s explicit note—“「癌」《說文》不錄…『疒』下作『嵒』…今亦讀ㄞˊ”—is not ancillary; it is a primary witness that “癌” is a later orthographic crystallization of a rock-cave phenotype into a disease-head. Graph formation and prognosis rhetoric are, methodologically, the two places where small textual differences can yield large interpretive errors—hence the need for disciplined witness handling.

### 9.3 Operational research program: reproducible corpora, cross-genre triangulation, and migration tests

Future work should treat typologies as infrastructure. In 《普濟方》, “癭有五種、瘤有六證…六瘤者：骨瘤、脂瘤、血瘤、石瘤、膿瘤、肉瘤” formalizes modifier ecology as a stable partition. Those partitions then become searchable “slots” for tracking which modifier-head types preferentially co-occur with terminal marker bundles (翻花、臭血、出血、虛人、難食難語). Likewise, early procedural cautions encode risk stratification even before “癌” stabilizes: 《肘後備急方》 defines stone-hard rooted swelling (“發腫至堅而有根者，名曰石癰”) and warns against casual needling (“癰，疽，瘤，石癰…皆不可就針角”). These are precisely the kinds of cross-genre migration cues that can be quantified.

## Conclusion: Toward an Evidence-Governed Semantics of “Cancer-Like” Disease in Premodern Chinese Writing

### C.1 Terminality, not taxonomy alone, is the pivotal historical variable

The preceding sections imply that what late imperial authors increasingly stabilize is not “cancer” as a single nosological object, but **a rule-governed way of reading lesions as terminal trajectories**. The most historically consequential move is the coupling of (i) portable phenotype markers, (ii) site-templated natural histories, and (iii) categorical boundary language. Early sources provide the ingredients. The classical process grammar in 《黃帝內經》 allows chronic retention to harden into named form—“津液久留，合而為腸溜…久者，數歲乃成…連以聚居，為昔瘤。” ([ctext.org](https://ctext.org/huangdi-neijing/ci-jie-zhen-xie/zh?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) In parallel, Sui nosology defines a morphology-state whose later career will be decisive: “肉反散如花狀，因名反花瘡…凡諸惡瘡久不差者，亦惡肉反出。” ([ctext.org](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=851126&if=gb&utm_source=chatgpt.com)) This “flowering” module is already an end-stage package: non-healing duration, exophytic tissue, and a chronic destructive course.

Late imperial surgery then transforms these ingredients into **terminal boundary-making**. In 《外科正宗》, breast “rock-cave” disease is narrated through duration and local destruction and then sealed with irreversibility: “五臟俱衰，四大不救…百人百必死…只可苟延歲月。” ([jicheng.tw](https://jicheng.tw/tcm/book/%E5%A4%96%E7%A7%91%E6%AD%A3%E5%AE%97/index.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) This is not simply pessimism. It is a clinical pedagogy that constructs a hard edge between treatable and non-treatable space, with immediate implications for technique and ethics. The term ecology we traced (瘤/癥瘕/積聚 vs 癌/巖 vs 翻花) therefore reorganizes itself around a single, historically variable question: **when does a lesion become linguistically legible as irreversibly lethal?**

### C.2 “Cancer” emerges as a braided semantic field, and 癌 as a late graphic crystallization

A second implication is that **orthography and rhetoric co-evolve**. The graph 癌 is not recorded in *Shuowen* and is explicitly analyzed as disease-tagged 疒 plus a rock-cave component: “「癌」《說文》不錄…『疒』下作『嵒』…今亦讀ㄞˊ。” ([dict.variants.moe.edu.tw](https://dict.variants.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=29027&utm_source=chatgpt.com)) This is best understood as a late stage in a longer semiotic process: morphology metaphors (rocky, cavernous, deep, penetrating) become formalized into a disease-head graph, enabling cross-site portability in modern usage while preserving the earlier imagistic payload. Crucially, that portability does not erase genre ecology. The 內科 process nouns (積聚/癥瘕) remain capable of naming severe internal masses without categorical fatality language; the 外科/瘍科 traditions, conversely, tend to recruit phenotype and event-chain markers (ulceration, fetor, bleeding, “flowering”) and then mark thresholds (“逆/凶” or “不救/必死”). The historical “arrival” of cancer-like semantics is therefore less a lexical invention than a **fusion**: process → phenotype → terminal boundary, braided across genres and then stabilized in headings, graphs, and teaching templates.

### C.3 Methodological implications: what counts as a robust historical claim

Because prognosis slogans and graph analyses are high-impact—and therefore high-risk—this review’s central methodological recommendation is evidence governance. Terminal boundary claims should be treated as **text-critical objects**, not merely quotations: they must be stabilized across independent witnesses to avoid over-interpreting citation drift. Likewise, “flowering” should be traced from its early generic sore context into later site templates to distinguish **phenotype migration** from **disease reclassification**.

## 摘要（繁體中文）

本綜述以「語義場」而非單一病名的角度重建中國前近代（漢至清）文本中「癌樣病證」的可辨結構。本文主張：古醫籍並非缺乏對惡性疾病的識別，而是以不同於現代腫瘤學（病理、影像、分子分型）的證據經濟來進行分類與預後推論。早期經典與內科論述偏向以「過程名詞」組織病象：如留滯、凝結、積聚、癥瘕等，著重時間性（久留、數歲乃成）、觸診行為（固定/可移）、及內在動力（氣血津液之失衡），使「腫塊」能被描述而不必然被賦予“必死”的類別地位。與之平行，瘡瘍—外科傳統則提供一套以外觀事件為中心的形態學語彙：潰爛、惡肉、臭血、出血、以及「反花/翻花」等，構成可跨部位遷移的「終末標記套件」。其中《諸病源候論》對「反花瘡」之界定（惡肉外翻如花、久不瘥）可視為此套件在隋代已被明確命名與制度化的證據。

晚明以降的外科專書進一步將上述兩套系統「融合並加硬」：一方面吸收腫塊語彙的硬結、根蒂、固定等特徵（如石-、骨-等修飾語生態），另一方面以口號式預後（不救、必死）與系統崩解語（五臟俱衰）建立不可逆的分類邊界，尤其在乳部「乳巖」敘事中呈現最密集的終末化模板。本文並以字形—部首形態學補強語義史：教育部《異體字字典》所示「癌」為疒＋嵒/巖之構形，且《說文》不錄，顯示其為後起之疾病頭字，將「岩穴/嵒」的深陷、堅硬、透裏等形態想像內嵌於病名本身，遂得以跨部位移植並在近代醫學語境中穩定化。

方法上，本文提出以「終末性語言」作為比較核心：不以現代診斷回填，而以文本中可核對的事件鏈（翻花＋臭血/鮮血＋久不瘥＋消耗）與預後閾值（逆/凶、不救/必死）作為證據等級，並強調對高影響口號斷語進行版本/引文漂移控制。由此可在避免時代錯置的前提下，呈現古典醫家如何以體裁、詞彙與字形共同塑造對「惡性—終末」病程的辨識、教學與風險管理。
