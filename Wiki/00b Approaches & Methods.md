# 🔬 Approaches & Methods

## 1. Philology & Semantic History

Attention to the *layered history of words* — what a term meant in earlier textual strata, how it shifted across scripts, dynasties, and languages, and what sediment it carries into the present. Treats language as primary evidence, not secondary description.

**Methodological note:** Philology in this vault is consistently *stratographic* — not asking "what does this word mean?" but "what history has this word carried, and how does each layer modify the ones beneath?" The political stakes are rarely absent: to trace the layers of 罪, 髒, or a frontier place name is to uncover how concepts arrived at their current coercive or cosmological force.

## 2. Ritual Studies & Anthropology of Practice

Treats ritual as a primary mode of social action — not as symbolic decoration overlaid on "real" social processes but as the mechanism through which cosmological, political, and moral orders are produced and reproduced.

**Methodological note:** A recurring finding across these essays: no ritual tradition is a single coherent system. Each is a *sedimented stack* of historically distinct layers that have accumulated and been synchronized into a working whole. The question is never which layer is "really" in charge — it is how practitioners navigate all of them simultaneously.

## 4. Critical Theory

Draws on the Frankfurt tradition extended through Foucault, Butler, Bataille, Bersani, and the queer-of-color critique to examine how power operates through discourse, desire, and subject-formation. Applied where the question is not merely "what is this phenomenon?" but "what power relations produce and sustain it?"

**Methodological note:** Critical theory's contribution in this vault is consistently the same move: revealing that what appears as individual preference, deviance, or natural category is in fact produced by a specific arrangement of discourse and power. The BDSM ordeal is not aberrant desire; the gay Asian "type" is not a natural kind; Sino-Kink's secrecy is not mere social awkwardness — each is a formation produced by determinable historical conditions.

## 5. Biopsychosocial Modeling

A clinical-integrative framework that holds biological, psychological, and social determinants in simultaneous view without privilege to any single level. Applied in the vault primarily to BDSM/kink phenomena, where monocausal accounts (purely psychiatric, purely sociological) have historically dominated and distorted.

**Methodological note:** The biopsychosocial model's strength in these essays is negative as well as positive: it eliminates single-level explanations. ## 7. Sociolinguistics & Translation Studies

Examines how language functions as a social technology — carrying power relations, shaping communities, transmitting across borders, and being transformed in the crossing. Applied across film translation, food naming, queer social typing, and BDSM terminology.

**Methodological note:** The recurrent insight across these essays: language is not a neutral medium in which social facts are reported but the technology through which those facts are produced. Film titles don't describe a film — they constitute its public identity. Calling someone 三八 is not an observation — it is a social act. Verbal seals in TPE don't name a power relation — they enact it.
