## AI in Medicine

### [external] [[Science - Hopkins - AI Can Reason Like a Physician - External|AI Can Reason Like a Physician — What Comes Next?]]
*Science* · Ashley M. Hopkins & Erik Cornelisse · 2026-04-30

Reasoning-capable LLMs now match or exceed physician-level diagnostic performance on text-based clinical vignettes and real emergency cases, demonstrating that AI can replicate structured clinical thinking. The findings are framed not as a milestone for replacement but as a call for prospective real-world trials and careful integration alongside clinicians. The urgent question shifts from capability to governance: how do we verify safety, equity, and reliability before deployment at scale?

**Tags:** AI · clinical-reasoning · LLM · diagnostic-AI · medicine · healthcare

### [external] [[Substack - Topol - Paradox of Medical AI Implementation - External|The Paradox of Medical AI Implementation]]
*Ground Truths (Substack)* · Eric Topol · 2026

Despite strong evidence for AI performance in medical imaging and diagnostic tasks, clinical adoption remains negligible. Eric Topol diagnoses this as a structural paradox: the settings where AI works best (radiology, pathology) are not where most patients are seen, while the settings with highest demand (primary care, emergency medicine) lack sufficient trial evidence. The piece calls for expanding deployment where evidence is strong and accelerating randomized trials everywhere else.

**Tags:** AI · medicine · implementation · clinical-AI · Eric-Topol · healthcare-systems  
**URL:** [source](https://erictopol.substack.com/p/the-paradox-of-medical-ai-implementation)

## AI in Policy & Governance

### [external] [[The Economist - Legislating With AI - External|Legislating With AI]]
*The Economist* · 2026-04-25

State legislators with chronically understaffed offices are adopting AI tools for research and bill drafting, compressing work that once took days into hours. Human review remains essential — AI errors in legal language carry high stakes — and critics warn that the convenience may erode lawmakers' capacity for independent analytical judgment over time. The piece raises a quiet constitutional question: if AI writes the bill, who bears deliberative responsibility?

### [external] [[Newsweek - AI Governance Benefits Innovation - External|The Benefits of a Guiding Hand: AI Governance and Innovation]]
*Newsweek* · 2026

This essay reframes the innovation-versus-regulation binary as a false dilemma, arguing that well-designed AI governance does not suppress innovation but redirects it toward socially beneficial outcomes. Drawing on labor economics and industrial policy, it contends that the real question is not speed versus safeguards but what innovation is for and who captures the gains. Governance that aligns AI development with rising living standards and worker participation is positioned as the precondition for durable technological progress rather than its obstacle.

Joe Hagan's profile of Dario Amodei — CEO of Anthropic and architect of what may become the most consequential technology in human history — follows the writer's attempt to confront AI directly and find instead the human preoccupations that power it: fear, status, idealism, and unresolved childhood. ### [external] [[The Economist - How AI Predicts Wars - External|How AI Predicts Wars]]
*The Economist* · 2026-05-16

AI conflict-forecasting systems — including RAND's Integrated Strategic Forecasting (ISF) — are advancing rapidly after two decades of disappointing results, with machine learning and LLMs enabling models that integrate past violence, social media signals, and environmental data at scale. These systems show real promise but remain poor at predicting novel conflicts with no historical precedent, and are systematically vulnerable to dataset bias. A deeper problem is political: accurate conflict predictions may prompt preemptive government crackdowns, creating feedback loops that validate the forecast while worsening underlying conditions.

## AI in the Information Economy

### [external] [[The Economist - Fang - AI and the Information Economy - External|How AI Will Change the Information Economy]]
*The Economist* · Shuwei Fang · 2026-04-18

AI is shifting the information economy's organizing logic from supply-side publishing (what is produced and indexed) to demand-side query (what is asked and retrieved), creating new market structures for both machine and human knowledge consumption. Personalization replaces broadcast; successful information producers will need to model user intent rather than optimize for search ranking. The essay identifies redistribution problems: as AI mediates access, the economics of attention and attribution are restructured in ways that may concentrate value at the retrieval layer.

**Tags:** AI · information-economy · knowledge-markets · media · publishing · attention-economy

## Philosophy of Science & Cosmology

### [[Philosophy of Science - Fermi Paradox - Cosmic Silence Hypotheses and Intellectual History - Reference|Cosmic Silence and Its Explanations: From the Fermi Paradox and Great Filter to Dark Forest and Simulation]]
*Format: REFERENCE*

A comprehensive comparative survey of frameworks developed to explain the apparent absence of observable extraterrestrial intelligence, spanning the Fermi paradox, Drake equation, Great Filter, Rare Earth, Dark Forest, Zoo hypothesis, Berserker hypothesis, and Bostrom's simulation argument. The central methodological argument is that the modern discourse on cosmic silence is best understood as an overlapping field of scientific hypotheses, philosophical frameworks, and science-fiction imaginaries rather than a single coherent theory. The review reconstructs the intellectual history through key figures including Hart (1975 absence argument), Tipler (self-replicating probe logic), Brin (taxonomic synthesis and berserker scenarios), Ward and Brownlee (Rare Earth), Bostrom (simulation trilemma), Liu Cixin (Dark Forest popularisation), Hanson (Great Filter formalisation), Carter (anthropic principle), Sagan (SETI legitimation), and Ciirkovic (synthetic interdisciplinary treatment). Includes extended analysis of Bostrom's trilemma — distinguishing the extinction horn, abstinence horn, and simulation horn — and surveys the main objections: the consciousness problem, abstinence horn underappreciation, fragile observer-counting logic, and the 2011 published bug-patch.

**Key arguments:**
- Great Filter is the most parsimonious explanation for cosmic silence: it requires no hidden actors, coordination, or alien ethics, only that one or more transitions from matter to durable civilisation are very hard or lethal
- Bostrom's simulation argument is not a claim that we live in a simulation but a trilemma: at least one of (civilisations rarely reach posthuman maturity / posthuman civilisations rarely run ancestor simulations / we are probably simulated) must be true
- Dark Forest and Zoo hypothesis are much heavier hypotheses than Great Filter — they require not only alien civilisations but universal strategic doctrines or sustained galaxy-wide coordination
- The intellectual history is branching and non-linear: Hart and Tipler sharpened the paradox, Brin broadened the explanation menu, Ward/Brownlee biologised it, Bostrom metaphysicalised it, and Liu Cixin mythologised the strategic-silence branch

**Key concepts:** Fermi paradox · Great Filter · Drake equation · Rare Earth · Dark Forest · Zoo hypothesis · Berserker hypothesis · simulation trilemma · anthropic principle · observer selection · Hart-Tipler absence argument
