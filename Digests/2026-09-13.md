### 1. Queer Theory — Melancholic Subject — Butler, Passionate Attachment, and Buddhist Anatta
*Folder: Review / Queer Theory*

Argues that Buddhist anattā (non-self) and Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka two-truths framework offers a more radical ontological ground for queer theory than Butler's psychoanalytic melancholic subject. Where Butler's *The Psychic Life of Power* locates subjection in the passionate attachment (*upādāna*) to norms that simultaneously harm and constitute the self, Madhyamaka *śūnyatā* dissolves the very self-structure that makes such attachment operative. The essay proposes the bodhisattva ideal — compassionate political action without clinging — as a model for queer engagement that escapes the compulsory reproduction of the heterosexual matrix.

**Key concepts:** anattā, upādāna, Madhyamaka two-truths, śūnyatā, *Vimalakīrti Sūtra* (gender emptiness), bodhisattva ideal, melancholic subject, passionate attachment, ālayavijñāna

Tang-to-Qing vocabulary drift in these terms is itself evidence that the problem of moral authority under technological mimicry has a long Chinese philosophical history.

### 3. Philosophy — Skin-Ego Theory — Anzieu and the Architecture of the Self
*Folder: Review / Philosophies*

A structured pedagogical review of Didier Anzieu's *Moi-peau* (Skin-ego) across eight sections. The core thesis is that the ego is a psychic envelope — a surface and container derived from the skin's early experience — rather than a set of contents. Anzieu delineates nine skin-ego functions (containment, protection, Sign-Writing/individuation, intersensoriality, libidinal charge, sexual excitation, toxic attack surface, self-inscription, and self-destruction) and three primary envelopes (maternal mirror, acoustic envelope, thermal/olfactory). Clinical pathologies include the flayed ego (*Moi-écorché*), the sieve-ego (*Moi-tamis*), and vampire/parasitic transference. The essay extends the framework into aesthetics (Francis Bacon's haptic painting; Beckett's *Not I* as acoustic envelope collapse), group psychology (totalitarian skin/group illusion), and digital life (smartphone as tactile mirror; data breach as narcissistic castration).

**Key concepts:** Skin-ego (Moi-peau), psychic envelope, primary envelopes, sieve-ego, vampire ego, group illusion, haptic vs. optical, digital envelope, étayage (anaclisis)

## Cross-Note Thread

A single question threads through all ten notes today: what happens to the self when its boundaries are produced, maintained, or deliberately unmade by external forces?

Butler's melancholic subject is constituted by an attachment (*upādāna*) that simultaneously harms and sustains it — the self exists only by clinging to the norms that injure it. Anzieu's skin-ego makes this visceral: selfhood is literally a surface, and pathologies (the sieve-ego, the flayed ego) are what happens when that surface fails to hold. The Buddhist anattā essay's answer is to dissolve the problem from below — there is no bounded self for *upādāna* to fasten to — and the bodhisattva model shows how compassionate action survives that dissolution without requiring the melancholic structure. The TPE essay operationalises this from the opposite direction: the nominalization protocol and pro-drop weaponisation are engineered techniques for producing ego-dissolution under controlled conditions, with Default Mode Network suppression as the measurable correlate of what Buddhist practice calls non-self. The 起信論's 覺/不覺 framework (awakened vs. non-awakened cognition within a single mind-ground) from the Tang-Qing vocabulary essay maps strikingly onto TPE's architecture: the submissive's 覺 capacity is still present — that is what makes Ecstatic Aphasia an experience at all — but the dominant's linguistic apparatus has occupied the 不覺 channel.

Imperial naming (Hainan ethnonymy) and the Four Symbols sovereign grammar (前朱雀後玄武左青龍右白虎) show the same logic at the political scale: to name a people with a body-part term and then convert it to a territory is to dissolve person into place, to turn human self-designation into administrative coordinate. The code-switching theorist map (Bourdieu's symbolic violence, Bhabha's colonial mimicry) provides the intermediate register — the self is not abolished but deformed, forced to inhabit a linguistic habitus that was built for someone else's body. And the queer theory genealogy's central irony — that anti-identity queer theory still requires subject-positions from which to speak — closes the loop: the self that dissolves in Buddhist ontology or TPE protocol or colonial renaming is also the self that must be reconstituted, however provisionally, to make political speech possible at all. The oncology MOC sits at the edge of this thread: the patient's self is the one whose boundaries are most literally under assault, and the molecular subtyping apparatus (DNA methylation classes, AR/NEPC transcriptomic subtypes) is the clinical version of the same question — which self is this, and what does it take to persist?
